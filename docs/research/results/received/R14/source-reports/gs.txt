# Which text sources could add verifiable value to a MOEX futures-spread project: sources, export briefs, library plan and a leakage-aware ablation protocol

**Research date (cutoff for current facts):** 2026-10-22. The pack was prepared on 2026-10-07. No repository, channel export, Gemini account or other Rxx report was available to me. Everything below comes from public pages I fetched during this session. I ran no tests, backtests or Gemini calls, so nothing here claims measured text value.

---

## 1. Conclusions

1. **One hypothesis is worth preregistering, and it is narrow.** Bank of Russia key-rate press releases and the later *Summary of the Key Rate Discussion* may add something beyond the decision itself and price action. The candidate is forward-guidance wording and the alternatives discussed. Everything else on the shortlist is mainly numeric (Rosstat CPI, Minfin OFZ auctions, EIA storage, OPEC reports) or operational (MOEX/NCC risk-parameter notices). For those, a deterministic parser or event flag is the right tool, and an LLM adds cost and risk without clear gain.
2. **Historical LLM backtests are mostly uninformative here.** The newest Flash model card states a knowledge cutoff of March 2026, with some domains limited to January 2025 ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-3-8-flash/)). Since then the Bank of Russia has held only five key-rate meetings: 24 Apr, 19 Jun, 24 Jul, 11 Sep and 23 Oct 2026 ([cbr.ru calendar](https://www.cbr.ru/dkp/cal_mp/)). That is far too few for any statistical claim about P&L. Credible evidence can only come prospectively and will take years at about 8 meetings per year.
3. **Telegram should not be a text input for any AI processing.** Telegram's content-licensing terms "firmly [prohibit] the scraping, indexing, harvesting, aggregation or use of data obtained from its platform to train, fine-tune, validate or otherwise engage in the development, enhancement, benchmarking or deployment of artificial intelligence" ([telegram.org](https://telegram.org/tos/content-licensing)). Official channels mostly repeat website material anyway. Use the website originals.
4. **Free Gemini may be blocked before any study starts, on terms rather than technology.**
   - The terms say "You may only access the Services … within an available region" ([Gemini API Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)).
   - Russia is not in the published region list ([available regions, updated 2026-04-28](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/available-regions)). Using a VPN to get around this is likely a terms problem. I have not checked this legally.
   - On the unpaid tier, submitted content is used to improve Google products, human reviewers may read it, and users are told "Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information" ([Gemini API Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)).
   - If the region condition cannot be met lawfully, the fallback is deterministic plus lexical features only. A locally run open-weight model is a later, separate gate.
5. **The baselines will probably capture most of the value.** The expected decision is "no learned text feature". The protocol below is built so that this outcome is cheap to reach and well documented.

### 2.2 First-hand lineage findings on MOEX ISS site news

- **Retroactive modification.** The oldest rows have `modified_at` years after `published_at`. Item 4570 was published 2001-10-02 13:39 and modified 2013-12-17. Items 5476 and 5477 were modified 2014-05-16 ([sitenews start=63800](https://iss.moex.com/iss/sitenews.json?start=63800)). The current body text therefore cannot prove what was visible historically.
- **Timezone inconsistency.** Items 104046–104049 (9 Sep 2026) show `modified_at` exactly 3 hours *before* `published_at`, e.g. published 12:29:03, modified 09:29:03 ([sitenews start=1000](https://iss.moex.com/iss/sitenews.json?start=1000)). This points to mixed UTC and MSK (UTC+3) storage. Every timestamp needs a timezone check, not an assumed MSK.
- **Item body lacks timestamps.** `/iss/sitenews/105343.json` returned the body as an HTML table with no `published_at`/`modified_at` inside it ([ISS item](https://iss.moex.com/iss/sitenews/105343.json)). The list endpoint must be joined to the body.

### 2.4 Incentives and manipulation

- **Commentary channels.** On 13.04.2026 the Bank of Russia found market manipulation by individuals linked to PFL Advisors. That entity administered the Telegram channels "RynkiDengiVlast | RDV", "Signaly RCB" and "Volk s Mosbirzhi", whose posts "encouraged market participants to buy/sell" ([cbr.ru press release](https://www.cbr.ru/eng/press/pr/?file=639118653205990149ENG_PNP.htm)). This is direct regulator evidence against promotional or commentary channels.
- **Official exchange channels.** MOEX's own channel mixes facts with marketing, for example a cocoa-futures promotion and ticket giveaways ([t.me/s/moex_derivatives](https://t.me/s/moex_derivatives)). MOEX earns more when turnover rises, so treat its content as promotional unless it is a rules or risk-parameter notice.

### 2.5 Literature on text and timing

- **Look-ahead bias vs distraction (Glasserman & Lin).** Inside the training window, anonymized headlines *outperformed* the originals, which means the distraction effect was larger than look-ahead bias. Out of sample, "look-ahead bias is not a concern but distraction remains possible" ([arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322)).
- **Tests for look-ahead bias (Sarkar & Vafa).** They found evidence of the bias and discuss "limitations of prompting-based approaches to counteract this bias" ([SSRN 4754678](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4754678)).
- **Chronologically consistent models.** He, Lv, Manela & Wu build LLMs trained only on data available up to a given date, published in *Journal of Financial Economics* 185, 104374 (2026) ([Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=plIPHrQAAAAJ&hl=en); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21206)).
- **Russia-specific.** Erokhin & Klachkova (*Russian Journal of Money and Finance* 83(4), pp. 27–47, 2024) measured tone with ChatGPT-4 and found that press-release tone has "a small but statistically significant impact" on *market inflation expectations* ([RJMF](https://rjmf.econs.online/en/2024/4/readability-tone-bank-of-russia-text-inflation-expectations/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bkr/journl/v83y2024i4p27-47.html)). The authors call it a correlation. It concerns expectations, not trading P&L.
- **Lexical baseline.** The Loughran-McDonald dictionary (updated March 2026) is "free for use in academic research", with commercial licences on request. It is built from 10-K filings and earnings calls, so it is English ([SRAF](https://sraf.nd.edu/loughranmcdonald-master-dictionary/)). A Russian central-bank lexicon has to be built and frozen inside each training fold.

## 4. Export briefs

**A. Bank of Russia website documents (justified, small).**
- **Identity.** cbr.ru, the official domain (also the source of the RSS list).
- **Documents.** Key-rate press releases and Summaries for meetings from Feb 2024, when Summaries began ([cbr.ru](https://www.cbr.ru/eng/dkp/how_dec/)), to the present.
- **Fields per document:** URL, meeting date, scheduled time (13:30 MSK), page-footer timestamp, retrieval time in UTC, SHA-256 of the raw HTML/PDF, extracted text, language, and a version counter on each re-fetch.
- **Lineage rule.** Pre-cutoff documents (before Mar 2026) are used **only** for deterministic and lexical baselines and parser development, never for LLM evaluation. From now on, poll after 13:30 MSK on each calendar date and record first-seen time.
- **Do not use** RSS pubDate as availability time (§2.1).

**B. MOEX ISS sitenews (justified for H3 risk flags only).**
- **Endpoint.** `/iss/sitenews.json?start=…`, plus item bodies via `/iss/sitenews/[id]`.
- **Scope.** Derivatives risk-parameter and limit notices only, filtered by title keywords such as "риск-параметр" and "ставк" (an assumption to validate).
- **Fields:** id, published_at, modified_at, inferred timezone, effective time parsed from the body, and first-seen time for prospective items.
- **Rule.** Rows with modified_at earlier than published_at, or with large gaps between them, are flagged and excluded from latency analysis.

**C. `@moex_derivatives` Telegram (a deeper export is not worth it).**
- **Identity:** verified through the moex.com derivatives page link and the channel description.
- **The only question worth asking** is whether posts appear before the website notice. One manual check pair is enough: posts around IDs 590–606 (late 2022 / early 2023) include the risk-rate change effective 19:00 09.01.2023, which links to moex.com n53820 ([t.me/s archive](https://t.me/s/moex_derivatives?before=610)).
- **Fields:** post ID, `date`, `edit_date`, link, matching website id.
- **Restrictions.** Read by a human only, with no AI processing, because of Telegram's terms.
- **Stop condition.** If the post is no earlier than the website notice, stop.

**D. `@centralbank_russia` Telegram, YouTube/VK/Rutube video and transcripts: not worth exporting.** The content is repackaged from cbr.ru, AI processing is barred by Telegram's terms, transcripts have no verified availability timestamps, and the YouTube identity is unverified.

## 6. Leakage-aware ablation protocol

### 6.1 Gates before any text work
- **G0, legal and terms.** Confirm that Gemini can be accessed from an available region without breaching the terms. If not, run only the deterministic and lexical arms (A0–A2). Upload only public official documents, never account or broker material.
- **G1, preregistration.** Fix H1 and H2, the contract set, the reaction windows (for example, enter at release time + D, with D drawn from 3, 5, 15, 30 and 90 minutes), the cost scenarios, and the primary metric: net RUB excess over the matched LQDT counterfactual.

### 6.2 Arms, all on identical splits and costs
| Arm | Content |
|---|---|
| A0 | Price-only reference (pre-release trend and volatility) |
| A1 | Deterministic event flags: decision size vs previous rate; scheduled-date dummy; NCC margin-change flag |
| A2 | A1 plus a fold-frozen lexical guidance score (Russian phrase list built only from earlier releases) |
| A3 | A2 plus Gemini structured extraction (enum fields only, temperature 0, no free-text opinions, no instruction to "predict the market") |
| A3-anon | A3 with the issuer name and dates masked, as a distraction check ([Glasserman & Lin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322)) |

### 6.3 Leakage controls
- **Input and model rules.**
  - The prompt contains only the as-of document, with no URLs and no retrieval or search tools.
  - The model ID must be a pinned stable version, never `latest` ([models](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models)).
  - Store the prompt, the raw response, a response hash, the model ID and the call time.
  - Results are never regenerated after a model change. A new model means a new arm, re-scored only from that date forward.
- **Historical use.** Documents before the model cutoff (Mar 2026; Jan 2025 for some domains) are used only to check extraction agreement against hand labels, which tests accuracy rather than predictive value. Run a contamination probe on each one: ask for the decision that *followed* a masked release, and if the model answers better than chance, treat it as contaminated.
- **Repeat calls.** Repeat each call 3 times on a sample of documents. Report the agreement rate, and use the majority vote or set ties to "unspecified".

### 6.4 Quota, latency and cost assessment
- **Documents.** About 8 press releases and 8 Summaries per year, plus perhaps 20–40 margin notices if the LLM were used for parser QA. That is under 100 calls per year even with 3× repeats on the main documents. It fits even the reported 20-requests-per-day stress case, with no batch or caching needed (neither is available free).
- **Timing.** The model takes seconds. The human reaction time of 3–90 minutes, with a median of about 5–15, is the binding constraint.
- **Untested risk.** The initial price move after 13:30 MSK may be complete before a manual order is placed. Test this with A0/A1 timing curves before spending any effort on A3.
- **Data caveat.** The ISS manual v1.4 says unauthenticated data is delayed 15 minutes and that historical data is for subscribers. This conflicts with common free use of candle data and needs checking separately ([MOEX ISS manual](https://www.moex.com/files/4be999zbzp80bx2bgmwayrtyx0)).

### 6.5 Rejection criteria (any one rejects A3)
1. A3 does not beat **both** A1 and A2 on net RUB excess vs LQDT under the *conservative* cost scenario in the prospective holdout.
2. Extraction agreement with hand labels is below a preregistered threshold (for example, 90% on the guidance enum), or the repeat-call agreement rate is unstable.
3. A3-anon differs materially from A3, which indicates distraction.
4. The result depends on a single meeting. With about 8 events per year, require a preregistered minimum number of events, judged by the Deflated Sharpe Ratio using the full trial log ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)), before any claim.
5. Any terms, region or privacy condition fails.
6. The improvement disappears at D ≥ 15 minutes, the realistic manual median.

### 6.6 Expected outcome and project changes
- **Default plan.** Adopt A1 (calendar flags, decision size and NCC margin-change flags) as risk and no-trade filters. Keep A2 as an optional lexical guidance diff. Run A3 only prospectively, as a logged shadow column, and do not let it drive the virtual portfolio until the rejection criteria have been checked over a multi-year sample.
- **Changes to the project.** Add NCC margin-notice parsing to the risk stream, since it is relevant to collateral strain on a RUB 30k test. Drop all Telegram and commentary ingestion.
