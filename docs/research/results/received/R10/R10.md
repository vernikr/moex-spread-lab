## Front matter

**What this document is.** A merged, de-duplicated synthesis of nine separate research reports produced independently against the same R10 prompt (the "source reports"). Each source report was written without access to the others; all of them worked from the prompt alone. This document reorganizes their combined content into a single sequential argument, states each finding once in the place where it belongs, and reconciles the points on which the sources disagree. Nothing here is new research: every claim, number and citation is traceable to at least one source report, and where the sources conflict the conflict is shown rather than silently resolved.

**What this document is not.** Not a backtest. Not a parameter selection. Not a finding that any strategy beats `LQDT`. Not investment advice, a broker/account check, a legal or tax opinion, or a declaration of trading feasibility. No trade has been taken, no order simulated, no historical dataset assembled, no tariff or account eligibility verified, no performance measured.

**Dates — four distinct timestamps, never interchangeable.**

| Timestamp | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Pack date (context) | 2026-10-07 | Date the R10 prompt pack was prepared. Context, not the facts cutoff. |
| Research / access cutoff | 2026-10-22 (eight reports), 2026-10-23 (one report) | Date public sources were read and live market data pulled. |
| Document *effective* date | Varies per source | The interval during which a specification, tariff or rule applied. |
| Today | 2026-10-23 | Date of this synthesis. |

A current, edited web page establishes neither historical content nor historical availability. Where an accessible source lacked a date, this document says so rather than assigning one.

**Inputs actually read.** The complete R10 prompt, including its embedded working assumptions; public web pages and public ISS endpoints, cited with URL, section and access date; published tables of contents, publisher metadata and one public author article for the three requested books. **No** repository, prior R-series report, broker statement, tariff confirmation, account record, historical data file, MOEX archive download, or paid database was supplied or read. Book *contents* were inspected; **no book chapter text was read**, and nothing below is attributed to unread text.

**Language.** English body, per the source prompt. Russian terms are quoted verbatim for MOEX/broker economics because translation loses the operative meaning (e.g. «календарные спреды», «гарантийное обеспечение», «вечные фьючерсы»).

**Chinese-language extension: not activated.** All four reports that addressed it reached the same conclusion. MOEX copper futures settle against the preceding day's LME price, not a Chinese benchmark; the MOEX copper page mentions China only in historical global-demand discussion; CNY currency exposure alone does not satisfy the prompt's condition (a commodity whose economics are demonstrably tied to a Chinese benchmark price/settlement or a specific export flow). No qualifying MOEX contract was found within these bounded searches. This is a scoped negative, not a claim that no such contract could ever exist. If one is later admitted, register the new lead, translate the terminology at source level (现货/期货基差 spot/futures basis; 跨期价差 term spread; 仓储成本 storage cost; 便利收益 convenience yield), and keep Chinese contract economics separate from MOEX rules.

**How to read every number below.**

- Verified contract facts (lot, tick, tick value, initial margin, exchange fee, last trade date, open interest, observed bid/ask, settlement price, RUSFAR, `LQDT` close) are live observations on the stated date — they are facts about that day, not about history.
- Cost figures, hurdle rates, round-trip costs and LQDT drags are **deterministic arithmetic on those observed inputs under stated scenarios**. They are modeled cost scenarios, not measured trading results.
- Every threshold, lookback, band, cap and buffer is a **preregistered candidate range for testing**, not a calibrated rule and not a risk limit.
- No numerical "survival range" or break-even cost frontier can be reported, because no historical execution results exist.

## 1. Executive summary and consolidated research queue

### 1.1 The one-line verdict all nine reports converge on

The only defensible path to positive net RUB excess over a matched `LQDT` counterfactual — on RUB 30,000, with manual 3–90 minute reaction, and **zero emergency funding at entry** — is a **small, pre-admitted set of very low-turnover spreads whose mechanism is a contractual cash flow** (financing carry, funding accrual, settlement convergence, storage/convenience economics), not generic statistical co-movement. Generic statistical mean reversion is admitted only as a **falsification benchmark**, never as a candidate.

All nine reports independently reject the same starting move: screening MOEX for high-correlation or cointegrated pairs and trading their z-scores. All nine independently conclude that the first task is **not** a backtest but a **feasibility gate** on data rights, contract specifications, integer/cash affordability and costs.

**An empty feasible universe is a valid and expected-defensible result.** Zero-trade days are a legitimate outcome, not an operational failure.

### 1.3 Dissents on the ordering, preserved rather than smoothed away

The sources did not agree unanimously on rank order. The disagreement is informative and is recorded here instead of being averaged out.

- **Two reports put an equity-index calendar first** (RTS card A; MIX/IMOEXF card B), on the grounds of multi-year depth, high liquidity, and the existence of **directly listed MOEX calendar-spread products** with their own archives. The consolidated ranking places index calendars at rank 3 because the dividend point-in-time input is an unresolved unknown that can silently convert the test into a hindsight result; if a free, dated, historically-available dividend series is found, this family legitimately moves to rank 1–2.
- **One report puts a Brent commodity calendar first**, citing the Theory of Storage as the most explicit economic anchor. The consolidated ranking demotes it to rank 4 because convenience yield is unobservable, the near leg expires monthly, and commodity calendar spreads demonstrably trend through supply shocks.
- **One report puts USD/RUB (`Si`) inter-expiry first.** The consolidated ranking prefers CNY/RUB first on the live-verified margin and liquidity comparison (CNY quarterly IM ≈ RUB 1,185–1,281 vs Si ≈ RUB 13,173; CNY quarterly OI ≈ 33 m vs Si ≈ 9.8 m contracts), which is decisive at a RUB 30,000 book.
- **One report leaves the choice open between "conservative curve/carry deviation" and "carry/convenience-yield stress,"** deliberately declining to rank beyond a gating sequence. That caution is preserved in the rank-0 gate.

### 1.4 The five-stage decision architecture

Every source converged on the same falsification hierarchy, expressed slightly differently. Consolidated:

1. **Is there an economic mechanism?** Carry, funding accrual, storage/convenience yield, dividend/financing, settlement convergence, or deliverable supply. If the story is "correlation is 0.8, therefore it reverts," reject without trading.
2. **Can end-of-day bars define the signal?** If the hypothesis needs minute-level queues, live spread books, or simultaneous two-leg depth, park it until the EOD version is rejected and the upgrade cost is justified.
3. **Does net excess survive cost?** Base → Conservative → Stress. Failure at Base is a hard reject. Survival at Base only, with failure at Conservative, earns the tag "fragile, manual-latency-sensitive" and nothing more.
4. **Does one portfolio beat `LQDT` with finite capital and a queue?** A single virtual portfolio, no oracle, respecting initial margin, variation-margin calls, rejected fills, staleness and the latency distribution. If only cherry-picked individual spreads beat `LQDT`, reject the architecture.
5. **Does simplicity beat complexity?** Fixed-minimum sizing plus a ranked queue must be beaten by adaptive sizing, scale-ins and opportunity-aware exits — on identical splits, delays and costs — before any of them are adopted.

### 1.5 The decisive sequence

> **specification and permission → integer/cash feasibility → simple delayed-execution baseline → net `LQDT` excess → continuation and sizing ablations → untouched evaluation.**

Until those tests are run, the justified conclusion is neither "spreads will beat `LQDT`" nor "holding capital in `LQDT` is wasteful." It is that **every new entry, every continuation, every addition and every switch must earn its place against the same feasible `LQDT` alternative, under the same finite authority, and without hindsight.**

## 3. Evidence base: what the public record establishes — and what it does not

This section consolidates every externally sourced fact the nine reports verified, and every gap they documented. Each item states the source, the location, the date, and the consequence for R10.

### 3.1 Data foundations: what free end-of-day history actually contains

**ISS history endpoint (verified live, 2026-10-21/22).** One report tested the endpoint directly rather than relying on documentation:

```
GET https://iss.moex.com/iss/history/engines/futures/markets/forts/securities/<SECID>.json
    ?from=YYYY-MM-DD&till=YYYY-MM-DD
```

Returned columns (verified on `CRZ6` 2026-10-21 and on the **expired** `CRU6` at its 2026-09-17 last trade date): `BOARDID, TRADEDATE, SECID, OPEN, LOW, HIGH, CLOSE, OPENPOSITIONVALUE, VALUE, VOLUME, OPENPOSITION, SETTLEPRICE, SWAPRATE, WAPRICE, CHANGE, QTY, NUMTRADES, SHORTNAME, ASSETCODE, SWAPRATE_CURR`. `SETTLEPRICE` and `OPENPOSITION` give daily settlement and open interest; **`SWAPRATE` (RUB per contract per day) carries the funding cash flow for perpetual futures — already inside free EOD.**

**Verified coverage findings.**

- Active contracts return full OHLCV + OI (e.g. `CRZ6` 2026-10-21: CLOSE 12.404, SETTLE 12.419, OI 33.9 m contracts, volume 6.59 m).
- **Expired contracts remain queryable after last trade** (`CRU6` last day 2026-09-17: SETTLE 12.5876, OPENPOSITION 0, volume 2.29 m). Delisted history is available through this route.
- Perpetual futures are infinite-dated in the feed (`LASTTRADEDATE = 2100-01-01` for `CNYRUBF`, `GLDRUBF`, `USDRUBF`, `IMOEXF`).
- MOEX's derivatives archive offers a separate CSV route for daily trading results and for calendar-spread archives (`spread-info.aspx`, `archive.aspx`), and listed calendar spreads exist as **separate securities with their own archives** (Last, Bid, Ask, High, Low, Volume, Trades).

**What this source does not contain — and must not be assumed.** No intraday OHLCV; no spread-book history; **no per-contract historical initial-margin, tick, or specification series in one call** — point-in-time lot/step/IM must come from daily securities snapshots that have to be scraped and archived going forward; no corporate-action-adjusted stock-future series flag; no bid/ask history.

**The conflicting evidence on "free history," stated honestly.** Two reports read the MOEX ISS Developer Manual v1.4 (©2016) and quote it directly: *"It is possible to get delayed market data for free or subscribe for real-time"* and *"Orderbooks and historical data are only available to the paid subscribers"*, with p. 8 adding that `/iss/history` data except indices are subscriber-only — [MOEX ISS Developer Manual v1.4, https://www.moex.com/files/4be999zbzp80bx2bgmwayrtyx0]. A separate subscription product exists — [Historical Data service, https://www.moex.com/a2863]. Those reports concluded this **rejects any unverified assumption of a complete free EOD dataset**.

The live endpoint test above is stronger, more recent evidence and resolves the narrow factual question in the affirmative for the specific contracts tested. It does **not** resolve: (a) completeness of depth for every contract and date, (b) delisted-coverage completeness across all families, (c) open-interest publication completeness, or (d) **permission**. Treat these as three separate data hypotheses, all still open:

- **Free-EOD gate** — can the required expiry-specific series be lawfully and reproducibly obtained free, including expired contracts and point-in-time instrument metadata?
- **Execution-resolution gate** — does a candidate survive at the lowest freely available interval with realistic 3, 5–15 and 90-minute reaction delays?
- **Margin/collateral gate** — can historical initial margin, risk-parameter changes and intraday variation-margin demands be reconstructed? **If not, historical performance may be screened but cannot be declared feasible for the RUB 30,000 pilot.**

**Permission is a separate gate from accessibility.** The ISS page distinguishes delayed access without subscription from real-time subscription, and states that information is for familiarisation and that other use requires an agreement: *"Любое использование информации (кроме ознакомления) возможно только по договору"* — [MOEX, «Программный интерфейс АПИ к ИСС», https://www.moex.com/a2193, accessed 2026-10-22]. Delayed accessibility therefore does not establish depth, completeness, or permission for a screener or a trading workflow. "Free EOD research input" remains a **conditional planning assumption**.

### 3.3 Margin, calendar-spread and portfolio-margin privileges — where they help and where they do not

Verified via the MOEX parameters page [https://www.moex.com/a215, accessed 2026-10-22].

**Межмесячные спреды (MMS) — same underlying, different expiries.** Maximum eligible maturity differs by underlying:

| Underlying | Max eligible maturity |
|---|---|
| CNY | **second quarterly** (current quarter + next) |
| Si (USD/RUB) | 4th quarterly |
| NG / NGM | 6th monthly |
| BR / BRM | 2nd monthly |
| Gold (physical) | 2nd quarterly |

Consequence: F1 (`CRZ6` vs `CRH7`) **is** inside the MMS window, so clearing grants a spread discount. A commodity calendar's second leg must be the *next month only* — a far calendar (e.g. 11-26 vs 01-27) would **not** qualify.

**Межконтрактные спреды (MKS) — 21 groups.** Group 1 index family (`RTS`/`MIX`/`MXI`/`MOEXCNY` + perpetuals); Group 2 gold (perp + quarterly); **Group 3 FX (`Si` + `Eu` + CNY + perpetuals cross)**; Group 4 precious physical (`GOLD`↔`GOLDM`↔`SILV`↔`SILVM`); Groups 9–10 `NG`/`NGM` and `BR`/`BRM` families. Consequence: F2 (perpetual ↔ quarterly) sits in MKS Group 3 for FX and Group 2 for gold and also receives a discount.

**Netting rules, quoted verbatim from the MOEX page:** *«Все сроки исполнения, не превышающие максимальный, включаются в межмесячный спред»* ("all terms not exceeding the maximum are included in the inter-month spread"). For `PLZL`/`PLT`/`PLD` families with полунеттинг, GO is computed by the «большой ноги» (large leg) rule pre-expiry.

**Calendar-spread fee formula.** Fee_CS = T × F × (1 − K), with K = 0.2 during the marketing period (6 months per underlying, 18 for the OFZ basket) — [MOEX «Календарные спреды», https://www.moex.com/ru/spreads; tariff page https://www.moex.com/s93; tariff document https://fs.moex.com/files/3838]. **But this discount applies only to orders submitted via the «Календарный спред» order type, not to two atomic legs.** A manual pilot at VTB web/mobile probably cannot use that order type (it needs QUIK/TWIME/Spectra), so **baseline costing must assume atomic legs**; the discount is upside memo only.

**Recorded conflict — the size of the margin benefit.** One report read the same calendar-spread page as saying margin is reserved *«в таком же размере»* as execution of the two constituent atomic orders — i.e. **no automatic extra margin discount** merely because the order is submitted as a calendar spread. Another report modelled an MMS/MKS "large leg" estimate roughly half the gross sum. A third stated that MOEX provides reduced-margin treatment for calendar spreads but that this does not make capital free.

**Resolution adopted here:** do **not** assign a margin discount in any baseline. Model the **gross sum of leg margins** as the conservative ceiling, note the MMS/MKS-modelled figure as a sensitivity, and treat any realised discount as upside. Real SPAN and portfolio-margin charges must be read from a daily `go_futures` snapshot, which was **not** available historically and is therefore an open gate. Reduced margin lowers funding cost; it is **never** a return enhancement.

## 4. Measurement framework: the matched `LQDT` counterfactual and the excess metric X(t)

This is the accounting spine of the whole plan. Every performance claim, every drawdown trigger and every continuation decision is expressed in it. All nine reports converged on the same structure; this section states it once, in full.

### 4.1 Definition

Let:

- **Wᴬₜ** = actual-policy account wealth: retained `LQDT`, cash, futures variation margin and other assets, less liabilities and incurred costs;
- **Wᴸₜ** = matched counterfactual wealth;
- **Fₜ** = true external cash flows, **identical in both paths**.

Then the excess is

```
X(t) = (Wᴬₜ − Wᴸₜ) − (Wᴬ₀ − Wᴸ₀)
```

**The counterfactual follows the stated core `LQDT` policy and keeps any `LQDT` diverted to the pilot invested.** Approved emergency external contributions enter **both** paths at the same time, and the counterfactual invests them according to its predefined `LQDT` policy. Internal sales of core `LQDT` are **not** external contributions.

This **full-account** comparison is necessary because the pilot and the core holding share one account. The unchanged core position should largely cancel economically, but the ledger must retain any differences caused by collateral use, asset sales, fees, taxes or liquidation order.

A reconciliation identity (useful for reporting, **not** a substitute for the matched ledgers):

```
X(t) = futures P&L
     + other actual-minus-counterfactual asset returns
     − incremental costs
     − incremental tax effects
```

### 4.2 The no-double-count rule

**Do not calculate excess as futures P&L minus interest on all posted collateral.** If eligible collateral remains invested in `LQDT` and continues earning its return, that return is already inside Wᴬ. Deducting the same return again double-charges opportunity cost — it manufactures a fictitious loss.

Correct procedure: **subtract foregone `LQDT` return only for capital actually diverted from the counterfactual.** Common core holdings cancel in the comparison unless they were actually sold, pledged or otherwise changed.

### 4.3 Two collateral modes — publish both

| Mode | Definition | Meaning | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 — Segregated** | The test book is fenced. The `LQDT` core stays fully invested; the RUB 30,000 test cash is the **only** capital whose `LQDT` return is foregone. Excess = test P&L − (30k × `LQDT` return) − costs. | Isolates strategy skill. | **Primary pilot metric.** |
| **C0 — Full-account (with EDP)** | A single settlement account where `LQDT` *can* be posted as collateral and continues earning. Yield on collateral still posted is retained in **both** paths, so it cancels; only the *incremental* cash diverted to IM/VM buffer loses accrual. | Reflects the actual account if the broker's Single Cash Position (ЕДП / единый денежный пул) allows `LQDT`-as-collateral with a haircut. | Secondary, and strictly conditional on broker confirmation. |

**C0 excess will be higher than C1 by the retained yield. That gap is collateral mechanics, not strategy alpha, and must never be reported as edge.**

### 4.4 Required accounting conventions

- Use identical valuation times and consistent marking conventions on both paths.
- Include costs of opening, rehedging, rolling, closing, failed execution and forced de-risking.
- Report terminal results after liquidation.
- Track a separate **liquidation-adjusted excess** estimate for risk monitoring.
- **Do not charge estimated closing costs twice** — once in liquidation marking and again as an additional expense.
- Do not double-count: if wealth is already liquidation-adjusted, reconcile that adjustment rather than re-charging it.
- Record every flow and every collateral movement; **never assume core collateral is pilot capital.**
- Distinguish four different quantities at all times: **performance shortfall**, **drawdown from the excess high-water mark**, **gross funding**, and **margin usage**. They are not interchangeable and conflating them produces false triggers.

**Tax scope limitation.** Only the loss of the `LQDT` long-term holding deduction is excluded, per instruction. Residence, account type/treatment, timing of taxes and loss-offset rules are unknown. Until resolved, label results **"net of modeled trading costs, before unresolved investor-specific tax effects"** — not after-tax excess. Other tax effects (e.g. on variation margin and futures P&L) are identical in both paths and cancel at pilot scale.

**The `LQDT` counterfactual must be reconstructible.** If the counterfactual itself cannot be rebuilt from actual flow, price and fee records, do not compute strategy "excess" at all until it can be.

## 5. Cost model: three scenarios — not three forecasts

Unknown fees are unknown, not zero. Every hypothesis is evaluated under all three scenarios, and the **break-even cost frontier** is the required output.

### 5.1 Cost formula

For a fill *j* in leg *i* with *n* contracts:

```
C_ij = |n_ij| × [ b_ij + e_ij + v_ij × (h_ij + s_ij) ]
```

where **b** = broker fee per contract, **e** = applicable exchange/clearing fee, **h** = adverse half-spread distance in ticks, **s** = additional slippage in ticks, **v** = RUB tick value.

**If actual executable bid/ask prices are used, do not add the same half-spread again.**

Round-trip cost for a two-leg spread with *n₁* and *n₂* contracts:

```
RTC = 2 × (n₁·fee_ex₁ + n₂·fee_ex₂)
    + (n₁ + n₂) × 2 × fee_broker
    + spread_entry + spread_exit          [= 1.0 × (n₁·sp₁·tv₁ + n₂·sp₂·tv₂) × mult]
    + slippage_entry + slippage_exit      [= 2 × (n₁·tv₁ + n₂·tv₂) × ticks × mult]
```

The half-spread convention: crossing half the spread entering and half exiting equals **one full spread total**. Slippage is extra ticks beyond BBO mid.

Add **separately** where applicable: settlement/execution charges at expiry; forced-liquidation charges; failed-leg unwinds; rehedging and rolls; funding cash flows; collateral/cash financing; incremental `LQDT` trading costs; unresolved tax effects. Any contract count above 1 scales costs and **may change impact and slippage — do not assume linearity without data.**

Equivalent full-form statement used by one source:

```
C_total = Σ_i ∈ {near, far} [ fees_i,entry + fees_i,exit
                            + tick_value_i × (half_spread_i,entry + slip_i,entry
                                            + half_spread_i,exit  + slip_i,exit) ]
```

### 5.2 The three scenarios

Two slightly different calibrations appeared in the sources; both are preserved because they answer different questions.

**Calibration A — anchored on observed BBO and specific broker reference points** (deterministic, RUB-denominated):

| Scenario | Broker (RUB/contract/leg) | Exchange | Spread multiplier | Slippage (ticks/leg/side) | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | **0.30** (BCS «Трейдер» 30–80 m tier) | 100 % taker, from §3.2 | **1.0×** observed BBO | **0.5** | Favourable but plausible: manual taker on both legs, bulk tier, clean fill |
| **Conservative** | **1.00** (VTB «Профессиональный» post-promo / BCS default) | 100 % taker | **1.25×** | **1.0** | Routine friction, post-promo fees, mild latency |
| **Stress** | **3.00** (voice/urgent/second attempt, or a 0.1 % short-leg trap if triggered) | 100 % taker | **2.0×** | **2.0** | 30–90 minute delay into a volatile book |

Memo, **not** base case: VTB's 0.00 RUB/contract promotion (to 2026-12-31) would cut Base by RUB 1.2–6.6; the calendar-spread order type's K = 0.2 discount would cut the exchange fee by ~20 % *if* accessible via Spectra/QUIK. Neither may be assumed.

**Calibration B — distribution-based, for use once fills are measured:**

| Scenario | Required assumption | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Base** | Exact dated MOEX/NCC and actual broker tariffs; taker fills unless passive-fill modelling is supported; median measured per-side spread/slippage; empirical delay and fill rates | Central decision case **only after every input exists** |
| **Conservative** | Same actual non-zero fees; **p90** spread and slippage; measured high delay/staleness and partial/no-fill rates; less favourable feasible fill prices | **Primary gate for ranking and reject/advance** |
| **Stress** | Actual fees plus **at least 2× the measured p90** spread/slippage; 90-minute response; a gap/one-leg stress; elevated variation-margin need | Operational survival test — **not** a claim that 2×p90 bounds the worst loss |

A fourth, coarser calibration used by one source for quick triage: Conservative = RUB 3.0/contract all-in, 1 tick spread, 0 slippage / Base = RUB 5.0, 1 tick, 1 tick / Stress = RUB 10.0, 2 ticks, 2 ticks. It is retained only as an order-of-magnitude sanity check.

**Rules common to all calibrations.**

- An "exchange-fees-only" computation may be reported as a **lower-bound diagnostic**, never as a base case.
- If the broker/NCC tariff or execution cost is missing, mark the candidate **cost-incomplete** and **block any survival claim**.
- Observed worse conditions override these values; a wide or illiquid leg must not be capped at synthetic stress.

### 5.4 Triage rules derived from cost, not from backtests

Two independent cost-based screens emerged; both are adopted.

1. **Absolute cost ceiling.** Any hypothesis whose Base-scenario round-trip cost exceeds **≈ RUB 35** on a RUB 30 k book, or whose required mean-reversion amplitude is **< 2× Base RTC**, is queued last — it cannot survive Conservative or Stress. This immediately demotes F6 (Sberbank ord/pref, Base RTC 36.8), F8 (cross-metal, 34.7) and the extended equity-pair variant (222.0) to negative-control status.
2. **Break-even frontier (required output per candidate).** `C* = gross expected edge − `LQDT` opportunity cost − other incremental costs`, estimated from the untouched holdout and expressed **both in RUB per completed spread and in ticks per leg**. Report also the fee range, half-spread/slippage range, delay conditions and fill assumptions, and **the boundary at which the hypothesis ceases to pass**.

**No numerical survival range can be reported now**: no historical execution results and no verified tariffs were supplied or measured. The decomposition for sensitivity work is:

```
X(θ) = G − OC − C(θ)        and        C_max = G − OC
```

where **G** includes the appropriate futures/funding cash flows and **OC** is the actual-minus-counterfactual `LQDT` effect. In practice the frontier must be estimated over **full portfolio paths**, because costs also change sizing, rejections and subsequent opportunities.

### 5.5 What end-of-day data can and cannot establish

This determines whether EOD is the right research resolution, and it is a research question rather than an assumption.

**EOD can support:** specification and unit checks; broad residual and curve hypotheses; low-frequency holding-time analysis; preliminary integer feasibility; and **rejection** of effects too small to survive coarse costs.

**EOD OHLCV cannot establish:** simultaneous executable two- or three-leg prices; bid/ask spreads; fill probability or partial fills; the path between high and low; performance after a 3–90 minute reaction delay; or availability of margin offsets during legging.

**Defensible first proxy:** form signals only after the required EOD information is available, then attempt execution at a predefined point in the **next joint trading session**. A next-session open is still a proxy, not a demonstrated fill. **Do not construct an executable spread high from one leg's daily high and the other leg's daily low.** With later synchronised quote data, test fixed delays of 3, 15 and 90 minutes; a one-day delay test is **not** a mathematical upper bound on every intraday-delay outcome.

## 6. Why z-scores, correlation and stationarity tests are not a trade

All nine reports independently derived the same critique. Stated once, in the strongest consolidated form.

### 6.1 The core argument

A **z-score** is a standardised distance from a reference mean. It does not encode a mechanism, a counterparty, a speed of recovery, or a reason why the distance should close. A **correlation** measures co-movement of returns, not a stable long-run price relationship — two series can be highly correlated and still diverge permanently in level. An **ADF or Johansen test** can reject a unit root in-sample and still be followed by a structural break that makes the estimated relationship useless out-of-sample; repeated testing also creates selection opportunities.

Three structural failure modes recur:

1. **Spurious stationarity and non-stationary drift.** Financial price series frequently exhibit spurious cointegration over finite samples. A stationary ADF result on historical price differences often reflects a temporary regime (matching monetary-policy cycles, synchronised inflation shocks) rather than a durable economic attractor.
2. **Absence of economic gravitational pull.** Statistical mean reversion assumes a restorative force. Without an explicit mechanism — arbitrage bounds governed by storage costs, financing rates, delivery mechanics or substitution elasticities — a wide z-score may simply represent a permanent structural break (regulatory shifts, sanctions, capital controls, permanent impairment of a delivery asset).
3. **Non-constant variance and regime shifts.** Spread volatility is time-varying and heteroskedastic. Fixed thresholds (e.g. ±2.0) applied to a heteroskedastic spread generate false breakouts in calm regimes and catastrophic drawdowns when volatility expands under stress.

**Two additional, MOEX-specific traps.**

- **A two-maturity spread does not necessarily converge to zero when the near contract expires** — the far contract still has remaining maturity. Convergence-to-expiry reasoning is only valid where both legs settle against the same reference at their own dates.
- **Unit-root tests on futures are contaminated by market microstructure**: price limits, data errors, non-synchronous closes and settlement conventions can produce misleading stationarity results. A spread that appears mean-reverting in unadjusted tests can lose that property after correcting for noise.

**Consequence:** statistical diagnostics are **filters and falsification tools**, never substitutes for an economic mechanism plus an executable cash-flow model. Every hypothesis card in §9 therefore carries an economic story *and* an invalidation rule. A test that only reports "z-score > 2 was profitable" is not evidence of a mechanism; it is evidence that a particular rule fitted a particular sample.

### 6.2 The five gates a purely statistical candidate must pass

Candidates that rely solely on correlation > 0.8, cointegration p < 0.05, |z| > 2, Hurst < 0.5, KPSS stationarity, or "half-life = 5 days therefore hold 5 days" are flagged **non-economic** and must clear five extra gates before any cost logic runs.

| Gate | Why the statistic alone fails | What economics must add |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Mechanism** | Correlation is not a deliverable linkage; two instruments can co-move in risk-on and diverge on an issuer event. | Carry, convenience yield, funding accrual, dividend/financing forecast, index arbitrage, substitution. **State the cash flow that enforces reversion or bounds the band.** |
| **2. Stationarity fragility** | ADF/KPSS are low-power on short, heteroskedastic samples; regime shifts (2022 sanctions, the 2024 rate regime, 2025 FX controls) break parameters. | Require walk-forward parameter stability; run a break test (Chow / Bai–Perron / CUSUM) on training data. A significant break ⇒ **invalidate, not re-fit**. |
| **3. Tradability gap** | A z-score at the close ignores the spread of the spread, integer lots, initial margin and expiry. A 2σ move worth RUB 8 that costs RUB 27 to enter and exit is noise. | Map to integer lots with an IM check; simulate a settlement-price fill plus slippage; **reject if amplitude < 1.5× Conservative RTC** (a stricter source suggests 2×). |
| **4. Horizon mismatch** | A half-life from AR(1) on daily closes assumes an AR(1) data-generating process — rarely true for calendar strips with roll jumps. | Estimate half-life **after** removing deterministic roll and carry; report its confidence interval; require holding period < ⅓ of time to expiry. |
| **5. Selection bias** | Testing 200 pairs × 5 thresholds × 3 windows = 3,000 chances; one p = 0.01 is expected by construction. | Preregister candidate ranges; log every trial; apply Benjamini–Hochberg or Deflated Sharpe; keep the hold-out untouched. |

**A worked rejection from the pairs literature:** an OU residual with a 120-day half-life on a contract expiring in 30 days is **untradable before expiry** even if the ADF p-value is 0.01. Tradability requires that the trade can complete within the hedge horizon.

### 6.3 What the evidence says, precisely

- Unit-root tests on futures are affected by price limits, data errors and microstructure noise; apparent mean reversion can vanish after correcting for noise.
- The cointegration framework assumes stationarity of idiosyncratic components and implicitly assumes frictionless execution; structural breaks, regime shifts and transaction costs can produce extended non-convergence and losses.
- Volatility-targeting frameworks treat forecasts as uncertain and scale positions by volatility — they do **not** treat a statistical signal as a certain edge.
- Commodity research finds basis **seasonality** and some predictive content, but risk-premium evidence is **inconclusive**.
- Published US-equity pairs results are **method leads only**; they do not transfer as return forecasts to MOEX futures.

## 8. Common machinery

Every hypothesis inherits this machinery. All thresholds are **preregistered candidate ranges**, fitted on training data only, frozen per evaluation fold, and never chosen ex post.

### 8.1 Separate the economic signal from the tradable P&L

For each leg *i*, retain: raw quote `P_i,t`; quote unit and underlying unit; contract quantity `Q_i,t`; tick size `τ_i,t`; **RUB tick value `v_i,t`**; settlement and variation-margin formula; any conversion rate and its fixing time; and the **effective specification version**.

For a locally linear contract:

```
ΔΠ_i ≈ n_i × (v_i,t / τ_i,t) × ΔP_i
```

This is a **diagnostic approximation only**. Actual P&L must use the applicable contract formula, including conversion, rounding and settlement adjustments. In particular, **a USD quotation is not permission to model the contract as a simple USD cash asset** — the RUB tick value already embeds the exchange USD/RUB rate used by clearing.

**Signals may use normalised economic prices, log ratios or curve slopes. Accounting must use actual contract cash flows.** A log spread is a signal, not the RUB P&L.

### 8.2 Three distinct hedge concepts — not interchangeable

| Concept | Definition | When used |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical-unit hedge** | Match units of the same underlying across maturities | Same underlying, same lot, same currency |
| **Value / delta hedge** | Match local economic sensitivities (RUB per unit move) | Different lots, different multipliers, currency conversion |
| **Statistical hedge** | Estimate a residual relation from training data | Only where no economic identity exists |

**Equal contract counts do not necessarily mean equal units, and a statistically quiet spread need not be economically neutral.** A fitted β is a price ratio, **not automatically a contract ratio**: it must be translated into economic quantities and then into integer contract counts.

For a log-spread `s_t = log p_A − α − β log p_B`, local sensitivities are `∂s/∂p_A = 1/p_A` and `∂s/∂p_B = −β/p_B`. For a log-price relation, a first-order sensitivity match is:

```
n_B ≈ β × n_A × (M_A · F_A) / (M_B · F_B)
```

adjusting signs, quote units and currency so that P&L sensitivity is expressed in RUB.

### 8.3 Hedge-estimation hierarchy — deliberately restricted

The sources disagreed here, and the disagreement is resolved conservatively.

*One source proposed dynamic hedging from the outset* (Kalman filter state-space, or total least squares to account for measurement error in both legs). *Three sources explicitly warned against it* at this stage: Kalman filters, continuously refitted betas and pair-specific bespoke models add turnover, add estimation choices, and — critically — **create additional paths for fitting away a structural break**. A fourth source allowed OLS or a state-space model but required the window length to be fixed before the holdout.

**Adopted hierarchy:**

1. **Identity / physical hedge** wherever the contract economics justify it (all same-underlying calendars).
2. **Carry-implied hedge** (β = 1, or the lot ratio) for perpetual-vs-dated and calendar structures — **no regression at all.**
3. **One robust static regression per training fold** (OLS, or TLS where measurement error in both legs is plausible), with **the hedge frozen during the trade** and re-estimated only at scheduled training updates while flat.
4. **Kalman / dynamic β** — permitted only as a **separately registered later variant**, with the noise ratio `Q/R` preregistered, never in the first pass.

**No hedge may be estimated on the evaluation period.** That includes using the full sample to choose the pair, the window, or the threshold. A stability check must compare the rolling β in training versus holdout; a large shift is a structural-break warning, not something to re-fit away.

### 8.4 Spread normalisation — three options, compared not assumed

| Normalisation | Formula | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Absolute RUB spread** | `S_t = P⁽²⁾ₜ·M₂ − P⁽¹⁾ₜ·M₁` | Most interpretable for cost comparison: already in P&L units | Level-dependent |
| **Percentage / log spread** | `S_t / P⁽¹⁾ₜ`; or `log(F_far/F_near)` | Comparable across price levels | Misleading near zero or when multipliers change; **invalid for non-positive prices** |
| **Robust z-score** | `(S_t − median_w(S)) / (1.4826 × MAD_w(S))` | Outlier-resistant; scale-free | Window *w* is a parameter; still not a mechanism |

Additional normalisations used by specific families:

- **Annualised carry diagnostic** (same underlying, positive prices, matched snapshots): `b_t = ln(F_far,t / F_near,t) × 365 / (T_far − T_near)`, or equivalently `c_t = ln(F_far/F_near) / ((T_far − T_near)/365)`.
- **Signed RUB pair value** (fallback when log form is inadmissible): `S_t = m_far·F_far,t − h·m_near·F_near,t`.
- **Currency-identity residual:** `r_t = log F_RUB/USD − log F_RUB/CNY − log F_CNY/USD`, with a zero benchmark admissible **only if contractual compatibility is demonstrated**.

**Robust scale with a floor.** For a training sample *x*: `scale = 1.4826 × MAD(x)`. Use median and scale from the **training window only**. If MAD is zero or the scale is unstable, use a **preregistered fallback** (e.g. the conventional training standard deviation, explicitly flagged) or mark the hypothesis non-estimable. **Do not silently change the normaliser after seeing test results**, and apply a residual-scale floor `σ_min` so that stale or near-constant prices cannot generate enormous artificial signals.

**A z-score is a unit conversion from history, not an expected-return forecast.** The normalisation must be identical across training and holdout; a full-sample normalisation is a look-ahead error.

### 8.5 Expiry, roll and settlement handling

**Recorded conflict on continuous series.** One source proposed constructing continuous back-adjusted series (volume-weighted or fixed-offset rolling) for signal generation. **Four sources explicitly forbade this** for signals and fills, because back-adjustment injects artificial history and hides roll jumps. **Adopted rule: price the actual pair of contracts. Never splice continuous series by hindsight for signals or fills.** Continuous series may be used only as a *display* diagnostic, clearly labelled.

**Required per hypothesis:**

- **Last trading day and settlement time** from the **dated** contract specification. MOEX expiry times differ by instrument and now occur at specified times (e.g. 14:00 and 19:00) rather than only during a clearing break.
- **Settlement price methodology.** RTS: the average index value over 15:00–16:00 Moscow time on the last trading day. FX: the fixing of the corresponding currency to RUB. Commodities: benchmark/foreign-market references. **A convergence trade that targets the spread but ignores the settlement average is exposed to final basis risk.**
- **Exit buffer.** Close each leg before the earlier required deadline, sized per contract from the dated last-trading/settlement specification, broker policy and liquidity observed near expiry. Candidate buffers: **3 or 5 eligible trading sessions**; one source uses a hard flat at **T−2 trading days**; another proposes **5 or 10 sessions** enlarged for broker cutoffs and closure risk.
- **Roll rule if continuing beyond the nearby expiry.** A roll is **not** automatic: it is a close plus an open, i.e. **two independent fully-costed exposure decisions**, each paying RTC again. Preregister whether signals are frozen during roll windows.
- **Margin and collateral treatment at expiry**, including the change in variation-margin timing under the unified trading session and any clearing execution fee charged for holding to settlement.
- **Never assume settlement is costless, cash-neutral, or available to the pilot** without checking specification, expiry fees, fixing timing, margin release and actual account treatment.

**Stock futures:** expire quarterly (third Thursday), with automatic delivery on EDS — the position becomes a stock position the next day and margin is released. Oil and gas expire monthly with last trade 2–3 days before calendar expiry.

### 8.6 Integer lot mapping

Futures cannot be traded fractionally. The mapping must:

1. Compute the RUB value of one tick for each leg from the current specification and applicable FX conversion.
2. Choose integers `(n₁, n₂)` minimising the absolute residual RUB exposure `|n₂·V₂ − β·n₁·V₁|`, where `V` is the RUB value of one contract at the current price.
3. Impose a **maximum contract count per leg**, a gross exposure cap, and a **minimum liquidity constraint** (the order must not exceed a preregistered fraction of average daily volume).
4. **Record the hedge error in RUB and include it in the cost model.** A large hedge error is an implicit cost routinely omitted from pairs-trading backtests.

General form used by one source — enumerate a small set of candidate integer baskets *n* (including **no trade**) and minimise:

```
‖D·n − a*‖²_W + λ · Var̂(ΔΠ(n))
```

subject to funding and stressed-liquidity limits, residual factor-exposure caps, maximum contracts and concurrent positions, permitted settlement/expiry exposure, minimum hedge quality, and human execution constraints. `D` maps contracts into economic sensitivities; `a*` is the desired exposure vector.

**Reject the signal if no acceptable integer basket exists. Do not round up merely to obtain a position**, and do not take a badly hedged "one contract each" position merely because it fits the nominal allocation. If the nearest integer pair is too unbalanced, reject the pair rather than silently using fractional contracts.

**Pilot-specific consequence:** at RUB 30,000 and КПУР-tier margin, most families bind at 1×. Brent full-lot and multi-leg equity structures bind at 0 and are **rejected for size, not for statistics**. One source flagged the exact same indivisibility problem from the systematic-trading literature: *"you can't own less than one futures contract"* — [Carver, "Diversification and small account size", March 2016, https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2016/03/diversification-and-small-account-size.html]. That is a methodological warning, not a MOEX capital estimate, and its conclusions must not be transplanted as numerical MOEX requirements.

### 8.7 Pre-entry filters, entry and structural-break logic

**Pre-entry filters (candidate values):** volume and open interest above contract-specific minimums (one source: `VOLTODAY > 5,000` contracts and `OI > 10,000` per leg; another: daily volume above a percentile of the last N days and OI above a contract-specific floor); spread BBO width below a tick limit (one source: < 10 ticks); no entry on the expiry day itself; both legs' liquidity and quote-quality gates fitted on **training data only**.

**Shared state machine:**

```
FLAT ──[ |z| ≥ z_entry  AND  |S| > cost_floor  AND  days_to_expiry > min
         AND  liquidity/OI/filters pass  AND  integer basket feasible
         AND  margin + VM reserve fits C_avail ]──►  OPEN

OPEN ──[ target reached | reverted to exit band | time stop
       | structural break | invalidation | margin trigger ]──►  FLAT

ANY  ──[ break test fires | expiry buffer reached | IM spike > 1.5×
       | OI collapse below preregistered percentile | loss trigger ]──►  FORCED FLAT
                                                                        (at market, stress slippage)
```

**Structural-break and invalidation checks.** Invalidation is **not** a stop-loss: it is a change in the economic premise. A price-only stop may exit a position that is economically more attractive; an invalidation rule may exit a position that is only mildly offside but no longer supported by the mechanism.

Candidate triggers (choose **one criterion per family** before the holdout; do not widen thresholds after a loss):

| Trigger | Candidate range | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| Residual beyond training robust scales | 3–4 scales for 2–3 consecutive closes (one source: 4.5σ; another: 3.5σ on oil, 4.0σ on FX) | Block new entries; alert; flatten subject to order/venue state; log trigger and slippage |
| Hedge-ratio drift | 25–50 % from the fitted training coefficient | Same |
| Open-interest collapse | > 50 % over 5 days, or below a preregistered percentile | Same |
| Contract specification / reference change | Any dated change to lot, tick, settlement reference, fixing, or margin | Same, plus invalidate the family until re-specified |
| Break test on training window | Chow / Bai–Perron / CUSUM significant ⇒ **invalidate, not re-fit** | Cool-down N days with no new entries |
| Family-specific | Policy-rate jump (one source: > 200 bp/day); dividend announcement or index rebalancing; inventory or benchmark shock; funding-formula change; corporate action | Same |

**Point-in-time inputs required daily (archived, not live-regenerated).**

- Contract table: `SECID, ASSETCODE, LOTVOLUME, MINSTEP, STEPPRICE, LASTTRADEDATE, SETTLEPRICE, INITIALMARGIN` (per risk tier), `BUYSELLFEE`.
- Market data: `BID, OFFER, BIDDEPTH, OFFERDEPTHT, SETTLEPRICE, OPENPOSITION, VOLTODAY`.
- Corporate actions and splits for stock-future underlyings, with availability times.
- `RUSFAR` ON and `LQDT` close for the hurdle; perpetual `SWAPRATE` history.
- Trading calendars, holidays, early closes, unified-session schedule, broker cutoffs.

**All "as of T" inputs must be timestamped ≤ T close; never use T+1 margin or a revised series.** An EOD settlement-derived signal enters **no earlier than the next executable session** — do not fill at the settlement price that generated the signal. Close, last trade, VWAP and settlement price are **not interchangeable**; prefer settlement/official daily reference consistently for model fitting, and account separately for likely entry/exit execution.

**Missingness is a test, not a gap to fill.** Data revisions, corrected candles and missing expired contracts are missingness tests. Do not forward-fill across them.

These are starting candidate ranges, **not a Cartesian grid to exhaust**. Values are the union of those proposed across the sources; the union is larger than any single source's menu and must be pruned by staged screening.

| Take-profit | Residual centre, or a fraction {0.5, 0.0} of the entry residual | |
| Maximum intended holding | 10, 20 or 40 joint trading sessions (alternatives: 5–20 days; 15 days; 1.5–3.0 × estimated half-life; ≥3 half-lives) | Time stop is failure control, not proof of equilibrium |
| No-entry expiry buffer | 5 or 10 applicable trading sessions (one source: hard flat at T−2) | Enlarged for broker cutoffs and closure risk |
| Hedge | Identity/carry-implied first; one robust static training-only regression second | See §8.3 |
| Structural-break checks | Residual displacement; hedge instability; reference-rule change; data-quality failure | One criterion per family |
| Liquidity gates | Volume and OI floors; spread-width cap | Training-only calibration |
| Switching / hysteresis buffer δ | Preregistered range; prevents churn from estimation noise and costs | See §11 |

**Trial budget: preregister at most ~12 complete configurations per family for the first pass**, chosen for interpretable contrasts. Do not silently test every combination above. Every discarded configuration, later change and family variant enters the trial ledger (§14). Hard financial and expiry limits **override** signal thresholds.

## 9. Candidate hypothesis cards

Nine cards. Every card is a **preregistered test design**, not a recommendation. Parameter ranges are candidates; they must be fixed on training data and held constant in the holdout. Each card ends with the labels used for it in the source reports so the merge is auditable.

All cards inherit the common funding, cost, counterfactual, validation and execution requirements of §§4, 5 and 8.

### H0 — No-trade / matched `LQDT` control

*Source labels: cg H0; the "no trade" / "remain in cash, retain `LQDT` yield" branch in every other source.*

- **Economic story.** The default is to preserve the baseline allocation when evidence is insufficient or when, after costs, the forecast edge is too small. Zero-trade days are legitimate outcomes, not operational failures.
- **Instruments / data.** No futures orders. Record the same external cash flows, `LQDT` units/prices/distributions, account fees and dates as the pilot scenario.
- **Rule.** Do not enter if data rights, contract-spec version, all-in costs, margin reserve, two-leg execution feasibility, expected edge, or available human response fails a hard gate.
- **Validation.** This is the benchmark and the safeguard against forced activity. Run the same account/flow ledger with zero futures exposure.
- **Reject / alter condition.** If the counterfactual itself cannot be accurately reconstructed from actual flow, price and fee records, do not compute strategy "excess" at all until it can be.
- **Cost ceiling rule.** Every other card must beat H0 **after** Conservative costs and the `LQDT` drag on capital actually committed.

### F1 — Same-underlying financial calendar / carry residual

*Source labels: ar H2a (CNY `CRZ6` vs `CRH7`); gs H1; cg H1; ds H1 (FX calendar carry, CNY/RUB and USD/RUB); ex rank 1 ("same-underlying calendar: conservative curve/carry deviation"); ge card 1 (`Si` inter-expiry basis); cr H3 (currency carry/FX spread).*

**Economic story.** Two futures on the **same** financial underlying (a currency pair, or an equity/index future) with different expiries differ by the financing differential over the interval, plus a basis. For a deliverable-free cash-settled FX future, the slope should reflect the interest-rate differential and expected funding; both legs cash-settle against the same fixing at their own expiries. A large deviation from a point-in-time, instrument-consistent fair-value curve **may** normalise — but basis, fixing differences, capital controls, dividend surprises and risk premia can persist.

**This is explicitly not "sell contango because it must disappear."** A currency forward slope may reflect interest differentials, market segmentation and other persistent conditions. It is a test of deviations from a **justified anchor**, not an arbitrage claim.

**Instruments.** First: CNY/RUB quarterly pairs inside the MMS window (`CRZ6` 2026-12-17 vs `CRH7` 2027-03-18), 1:1 (both 1,000 CNY lots). Second: USD/RUB (`SiZ6` vs next quarter) — note IM ≈ RUB 13,173 versus RUB 1,185–1,281 for CNY, which is decisive at RUB 30 k. **Never pool the funding/dividend mechanisms of different underlying families.**

**Data and calendar prerequisites.** Expiry-specific daily prices, volume and OI for both legs; overlapping history including expired contracts; quote units, settlement formula, fixing source and rule history; actual exchange, settlement and broker calendars and cutoffs; **point-in-time rate/financing inputs only if the model explicitly uses them**; synchronised quotes later, if execution sensitivity proves material.

**Formula and units.**

```
Observed calendar spread:   S_t = F_far,t − F_near,t            (RUB per unit; also record ticks)
Annualised log-slope:       b_t = ln(F_far,t / F_near,t) × 365 / (T_far − T_near)
Fair-value residual:        e_t = S_t − S_fair,t   (or e_t = b_t − m(tenor bucket))
Robust signal:              z_t = (e_t − median_train(e)) / max(1.4826 × MAD_train(e), σ_min)
```

The first baseline should **omit rate inputs** rather than manufacture historical forecasts. A rate-adjusted version is a **separate logged variant**, using only rates and publication times available at the time. Do not use the log form through zero or negative prices.

**Parameter selection (preregistered).** Residual estimation window L ∈ {45, 60, 90, 120}; entry |z| ∈ {1.8, 2.2, 2.8} (or the shared grid {1.5, 2.0, 2.5}); exit at z = 0 ± 0.3 (alternatives 0.25/0.5); maximum hold = min(20 sessions, τ_near − buffer); entry floor = Conservative RTC + 2 × daily carry.

**Entry.** Fade the fair-value residual only where (i) the conservative conditional forward payoff after costs is positive, (ii) the residual exceeds the cost-adjusted band, (iii) both legs pass liquidity and OI gates, (iv) an acceptable integer basket exists and margin plus a conservative VM reserve fits C_avail, and (v) no scheduled policy event inside the intended holding window invalidates the band. Entry no earlier than the next executable session after the fixing/settlement timestamp.

**Continue.** Under the forward-value test of §11, using current conditional value, remaining carry, remaining costs and the `LQDT` counterfactual. Recompute remaining theta (carry bleed/earn) each day; close early if the carry curve inverts and adverse theta flips sign.

**Exit.** Residual reaches the exit band; holding limit reached; expiry buffer reached; structural break; or a better feasible alternative clears the switching buffer.

**Invalidate.** Fixing or settlement-reference change; broken unit compatibility; reference-market discontinuity; a point-in-time curve input that is stale or missing; a policy-rate change that moves the carry band; severe hedge residuals; absence of usable overlapping history; margin/liquidity reserve failure; or one leg reaching its expiry buffer.

**Expected failure mode.** The apparent dislocation is a persistent financing premium; observed daily prices are asynchronous; the near-leg expiry does not deliver the supposed convergence; or the spread cost and legging exceed the predicted correction.

**Validation.** Chronological folds separated by actual signal/holding overlap. Report contribution by maturity bucket and market regime, and by whether the exit was a convergence, a time stop or an expiry-buffer exit. Compare against no-trade and against a simple fixed fair-value band without rolling parameter tuning.

**Decision to reject.** No positive net RUB excess in the untouched holdout under Base **and** Conservative; minimum integer sizing fails the funding/risk constraint; performance depends on one expiry episode; or the result disappears when the `LQDT` counterfactual is properly charged. **Insufficient independent events means inconclusive, not profitable.**

**Verified feasibility note (not P&L).** The observed `CRZ6`/`CRH7` spread on 2026-10-22 implies roughly RUB 0.205 over 91 days, i.e. about **RUB 2.25 per 1,000 CNY per day of carry**. A 20-day hold earns roughly RUB 45 of carry against a Base RTC of RUB 9.5 — a narrow net. **The model therefore predicts few trades**, which is consistent with the project's acceptance of zero-trade days.

### F2 — Perpetual (daily auto-prolonging) versus dated futures funding basis

*Source labels: ar H1a (CNY), H1b (gold), H1c (index); gs H5 ("dated-versus-daily-auto-prolonging"); mm card B (`MIX` vs `IMOEXF`); ds H3.*

**Economic story.** A dated quarterly future embeds implied carry to its expiry. A daily auto-prolonging ("perpetual") future is anchored to spot through a **daily funding transfer** — MOEX describes it as *«покупатели уплачивают фандинг, продавцы получают фандинг»* when positive funding applies — [MOEX, «Вечные фьючерсы на валютные пары», https://www.moex.com/a8141]. When the perpetual-versus-quarterly price difference deviates from fair cumulative funding by more than funding plus carry uncertainty, the spread mean-reverts as funding accrues or as roll-down realises.

**This family is unique among MOEX structures because the defining cash flow is directly observable in free EOD data** (`SWAPRATE`), not inferred from prices. That is why it ranks second despite being noisier than a pure calendar.

**Instruments.** First: `CNYRUBF` (lot 1,000 CNY, step RUB 0.001, tick value RUB 1.00, IM RUB 981.98) versus `CRZ6` (IM RUB 1,185.50), 1:1. MKS Group 3 (FX) applies. Second wave: `GLDRUBF` vs `GLZ6` (MKS Group 2); `IMOEXF` vs `MMZ6`.

**Data and calendar prerequisites.** Both price histories; **actual historical funding accruals/payments**; funding determination windows and payment timing; effective bands, caps, formulas and conversion rules; quarterly transition/termination provisions; broker support and cutoffs; and the cash-liquidity implications of funding plus variation margin.

**Formula and units.**

```
Implied daily carry (quarter):   c_imp(t) = (F_quarter(t) − S_perp_proxy(t)) / τ,   S_perp_proxy ≈ P_perp(t)
Fair funding:                    f_fair(t) = RUSFAR_RUB − foreign-rate proxy
Spread signal:                   S(t) = P_perp(t) − P_quarter(t) − f_fair × τ_remaining
Direct funding signal:           z_fund = (swaprate_observed − f_fair) / σ_fund
P&L over t→u:                    Π = Π_dated + Π_daily + Σ_d signed funding_d − C_{t→u}
Units: RUB per 1,000 CNY; thresholds in ticks.
```

**Forecasting must distinguish a change in the basis from future funding.** The realised funding sequence may be used to **score** a completed test, never to decide the earlier entry. The public explanatory page is **not** a substitute for the effective contract specification.

**Parameter selection.** L ∈ {30, 60, 90} days for σ; f_fair ∈ {1.0 %, 2.0 %} pa, or RUSFAR minus 2 %; entry |z| ∈ {1.5, 2.0, 2.5}; take-profit at z ∈ {0, ±0.5}; minimum |S| floor = Conservative RTC + 5-day `LQDT` drag; maximum hold {10, 15, 20} sessions; τ ≥ 10 days at entry.

**Entry.** |z| ≥ z_entry AND |S| > Conservative RTC + 0.5 × IM variation AND τ ≥ 10 days AND OI on both legs above the floor. Compare a simple persistence forecast of funding against a **zero-predictability benchmark**.

**Continue.** Hold while |z| remains on the favourable side and the conditional value test (§11) is positive, re-evaluated daily as funding conditions change. No averaging down.

**Exit.** z crosses 0 (or ±0.5); τ ≤ 5 sessions; time stop; or adverse by 2× the entry gap.

**Invalidate.** RUSFAR jump exceeding the preregistered threshold or an announced `SWAPRATE` formula change (cool-down 5 days); a funding history gap; ambiguous sign conventions; incomplete rule versions; broker limitations; convertibility-rule change; or a liquidity collapse.

**Expected failure mode.** Funding stays pinned at a floor or cap for weeks, producing slow bleed; funding reverses; caps alter the price-adjustment mechanism; the basis moves against the hedge; or central-bank intervention, capital controls or an NCC funding-parameter change (K1/K2 bands, L1/L2) makes carry fair jump so the spread walks away while variation margin drains the test book.

**Validation.** Walk-forward with quarterly refit; gap sized by actual signal/holding overlap; a stress branch using MOEX funding-specification change dates as known event dummies.

**Decision to reject.** **Reject the initial experiment outright if only daily OHLC bars are available** — a price-only backtest would omit a defining cash flow and is not an acceptable substitute. Also reject if net X median ≤ 0 on C1 after Conservative cost and `LQDT` drag, or if half-life > τ/2, or if the funding-persistence test shows the swaprate is regime-shifted (ADF on the swaprate residual with p > 0.10 across two regimes).

**Verified feasibility note (not P&L).** Observed funding on 2026-10-21 was RUB 0.00571 per 1,000 CNY per day = RUB 5.71 per contract-day, i.e. roughly **47 bp per fortnight** on RUB 12,230 notional. Closing an entry-level deviation must exceed the Base RTC of RUB 7.5 within about 1.3 days of funding accrual alone — plausible only if the dislocation exceeds 1.5–2σ **and** is held more than 10 days. **Expected frequency: low.**

**Why the gold and index variants are second-wave.** Gold: Base RTC RUB 11.3, but observed funding accrual is only about RUB 0.1–1.5/day, requiring 7–80 days of carry to cover cost against roughly 56 days remaining to expiry — the edge is thin. Index: Base RTC RUB 19.2 and a 15-day `LQDT` drag of RUB 14–42, implying a hurdle of roughly 0.7–1.3 % per fortnight against a perp-quarter funding edge that is small relative to equity volatility.

### F3 — Index calendar basis

*Source labels: ds H2 (RTS calendar basis); mm cards A (RTS calendar) and B (`MIX`/`IMOEXF`); ex rank 1 (MIX/MXI calendars); cr H4 (index future vs basket); gr card 3 (single-stock vs index).*

**Economic story.** A cash-settled index future has no physical delivery. The calendar spread between two expiries should reflect the risk-free RUB rate **less the expected dividend yield** of the index over the interval, plus a basis. If the spread deviates from the dividend-adjusted carry band and the deviation is not explained by a change in expected dividends or a scheduled rebalancing, a convergence trade has a contractual anchor at the deferred expiry.

**Instruments.** `RTS` / `RTSM` mini pairs, or `MIX` / `MXI` mini pairs. `MIX` tick 25, value RUB 25; `RTSM` tick 0.5 point, value ≈ RUB 8.36; `MXZ6` full has tick 25 and IM RUB 28,833 (too large for the pilot at 1×). The mini and full contracts have different multipliers and **must not be mixed without normalisation**.

**Data.** Daily settlement prices and OI for at least two expiries, plus a dividend forecast or proxy; index rebalancing dates; contract specifications.

**Formula.**

```
Carry-implied spread:  F_{t,T1} × [(r_RUB − q) × τ]     (q = expected dividend yield over the interval)
Residual:              index points → RUB per contract via the multiplier and any USD/RUB conversion
```

The hedge ratio between two expiries is **not necessarily 1:1 in contract count**, because multipliers and price levels differ; the integer mapping must solve for the number of deferred contracts matching the RUB value of one nearby contract within a preregistered tolerance.

**The dividend input is the gating unknown.** It must be treated as an explicit unknown, never as zero. Run the test in **two modes**: (a) with a point-in-time dividend forecast if a free, dated source can be found; (b) with a conservative constant dividend assumption, reporting sensitivity to the assumption. **If the result is positive only in mode (a) and the forecast source cannot be shown to have been available historically, the hypothesis is conditional and cannot be ranked highly.**

**Parameter selection.** L ∈ {60, 120, 250}; entry |z| ∈ {1.5, 2.0, 2.5} (one source allows up to 3.0); exit |z| ∈ {0.3, 1.0}; T_max ∈ {1.5, 3.0} × estimated half-life from an AR(1) fit on training data; minimum liquidity by volume percentile and OI floor.

**Entry / continue / exit.** As F1, with the invalidation set extended to include a dividend announcement, a change in the expected dividend calendar, and an index rebalancing.

**Invalidate.** Dividend or rebalancing event; index-composition change; contract specification change; structural break (CUSUM on the spread mean); liquidity cliff near roll dates; or repeated failures — more than K consecutive trades hitting T_max without reaching the exit band.

**Expected failure mode.** The dividend forecast is wrong or stale; the basis reflects a risk premium rather than a mispricing; the spread converges but the **settlement average** produces an adverse final mark; or liquidity in the deferred leg is insufficient to exit before expiry.

**Why this ranks third rather than first.** Depth is excellent and MOEX lists index calendar spreads directly with their own archives, which two sources used to argue for first place. The consolidated ranking demotes it **solely** because the dividend point-in-time input is unresolved; if that input is obtained, this family moves to rank 1–2.

**Decision to reject.** Positive result only under the hindsight dividend series; results concentrated in one regime; or failure to beat H0 after Conservative costs.

### F4 — CNY/RUB synthetic RUB-bond carry

*Source labels: ds H3.*

**Economic story.** The CNY/RUB futures curve has traded in contango reflecting the RUB interest rate. A long spot CNY / short CNY futures position is economically a **synthetic RUB-denominated bond**: the investor earns the implied RUB rate if the basis converges at expiry, with FX exposure hedged. This is a **carry story with contractual convergence at expiry**, not a mean-reversion story.

**Instruments and data.** Spot CNY/RUB, the futures settlement price, the contract lot (CNY 1,000 for the perpetual; verify the quarterly) and the RUB money-market rate. **The perpetual's funding mechanics differ and must not be conflated with the quarterly contract.**

**Formula.** `Annualised implied carry = (F/S − 1) × 365 / days to expiry`. Gross P&L at expiry is the difference between the futures price and the spot price at expiry, less the cost of funding the spot position. If the spot leg is not held (a pure futures calendar or basis trade), the carry is embedded in the spread and the same expiry logic applies.

**Entry / continue / exit.** Enter only when the implied carry exceeds the RUB funding cost plus a cost buffer and the position can be held to expiry or rolled under a preregistered roll rule. Continue only if the implied carry remains positive after remaining costs and the `LQDT` counterfactual. Exit on convergence, expiry, or invalidation.

**Expected failure mode — the critical one.** The carry is compensation for convertibility or settlement risk; the spot leg is unavailable or expensive; or the futures basis widens further before expiry, **producing a mark-to-market drawdown that breaches the pilot's governance even though the trade would be profitable if held to expiry**. An economically correct carry trade can still fail the pilot's drawdown rules. This distinction must appear in every report of this family.

**Decision to reject.** The carry does not exceed RUB funding plus costs after the drawdown limit; or spot execution is infeasible at the pilot scale.

### F5 — Commodity calendar with tenor and seasonal controls

*Source labels: ar H3a (Brent mini) and H3b (gas micro); gs H2; cg H2; ds H4; mm card D (Brent calendar, Brent/WTI exploratory); gr card 1 (Brent term structure); ge card 2 (Brent calendar); cr H1 (commodity calendar mean reversion).*

**Economic story.** For a storable commodity, the calendar spread reflects storage cost, insurance, financing cost and **convenience yield**, plus seasonal supply/demand and delivery optionality. In contango the deferred contract trades above the nearby; in backwardation, below. Near-term physical tightness can push the near-deferred difference temporarily rich, then normalise as tightness eases.

**Commodity research supports testing this — not assuming it.** Published work finds **stronger evidence of basis seasonality** and some basis forecasting power, while **risk-premium evidence is inconclusive**. A seasonal residual is therefore a reason to test, not a guarantee of reversion.

**Instruments.** First: Brent mini `BMX6` (2026-11-02) vs `BMZ6` (2026-12-01), 1 bbl lots, tick USD 0.01 = RUB 0.81974, IM RUB 1,875 / 2,080. Full `BR` (10 bbl, IM RUB 18,754) is a **scale-up cross-check only** — it does not fit the pilot comfortably. Second: gas micro `NRX6` vs `NRZ6`, 1 MMBtu lots, IM RUB 69 / 74, traded 5× to obtain tradable granularity (gross IM for 5× micro ≈ RUB 718). Full `NG` (100 MMBtu) is too large. Deliverable precious metals (`GOLD`, `SILV`, `PLT`, `PLD`) and `OF10` bond-futures calendars are alternative leads.

**Data and calendar prerequisites.** F1 inputs, **plus**: benchmark location, quality and settlement reference; contract month and season; benchmark publication calendar and time; point-in-time specification changes; **enough repeated seasonal cycles** to estimate a seasonal model; and an explicit treatment of non-positive prices if encountered.

**Formula and units.**

```
Spread:        s_t = log F_far,t − log F_near,t            (positive prices only)
Residual:      e_t = s_t − m(tenor bucket, contract-month pair)
Carry band:    F_near × [(r + c − y) × τ]                 (c = storage, y = convenience yield — unobservable)
Signal:        z_t = (S_t − seasonal_location_train(bucket)) / seasonal_scale_train(bucket)
```

**Use a no-seasonality robust baseline first.** Add a coarse seasonal effect only if the training history contains repeated cycles sufficient to estimate it. **Avoid twelve free monthly parameters on a short history.** Because *y* is unobservable, the residual is a joint test of the carry model and the convenience-yield estimate — which is what makes this family more fragile than F1–F4.

**Parameter selection.** L ∈ {30, 45, 60} (one source: 60/120/250); entry |z| ∈ {2.0, 2.5, 2.8} (alternatives 2.0–2.5, 1.5–2.5); exit at z = 0 ± 0.5; seasonal lookback ~3 years; maximum hold 15 sessions (one source: 10/20/40); OI floor 10,000.

**Entry.** Fade the residual only where the economic direction and month pair were **written down before inspecting backtest performance**, where the conditional forward payoff after costs is positive, and where inventory data or the curve shape supports a directional convenience-yield view. Fit seasonality on training data only; **do not select the best pair or month from the full history.**

**Continue.** Only while the original benchmark relationship remains valid and forward value clears the continuation hurdle. Carry slope is a **state variable, not a guaranteed cash receipt**.

**Exit.** Seasonal fair value, or halfway to fair value; the preregistered holding limit; or before the near-leg risk window unless settlement exposure was explicitly part of a later, separately approved test.

**Invalidate.** Benchmark replacement; changed delivery or settlement economics; missing seasonal history; insufficient overlapping maturities; contract-rule ambiguity; an inventory/OPEC/sanction shock that changes storage economics; a pattern that flips sign across training subperiods; or the GL-type specification conflict (§3.6) if a gold variant is tested.

**Expected failure mode.** A shortage or inventory regime permanently changes the curve; a recurring seasonal premium is mistaken for mispricing; the reference commodity does not behave like the MOEX contract; the far leg reprices without near-leg exit liquidity; or the spread **trends** through a supply shock — the published evidence is that calendar spreads generally do **not** mean-revert except inside a seasonal window.

**Competing control — mandatory.** Test reversion against a preregistered **curve-slope / roll-down continuation** control on the same candidate series. Do not assume every large spread is meant to revert. "Carry" and "mean reversion" must not become two names for whichever rule performed better; any distinct directional carry rule is a **separate registered family**.

**Validation.** Hold out entire seasonal/expiry episodes where possible. Compare three variants: (1) no seasonal adjustment; (2) one coarse seasonal adjustment; (3) the no-trade `LQDT` counterfactual. Report **per-month-pair** hit rates — aggregation must not hide that only two of twelve month-pairs ever work.

**Decision to reject.** Signs or magnitudes unstable across seasons; net cost-adjusted edge not positive in the untouched holdout; the result dominated by a rare shock; the required inventory/benchmark/contract data not accessible at the decision timestamp; or the result earned from a foreign-market backtest or an unverified settlement assumption.

**Pilot-specific note.** A pilot starting 2026-10-07 faces only about three weeks on the nearest Brent expiry, forcing a rapid test; the real hypothesis is a **recurring monthly pair**, not one fixed pair. Gas sits in shoulder season in the Oct–Dec window.

**Brent/WTI is exploratory and excluded from the first wave.** WTI launched 2026-07-14, so no statistical validation is possible at present.

### F6 — Same-issuer ordinary versus preferred equity futures

*Source labels: ar H4a (`SRZ6` vs `SPZ6`); gs H3 (same-issuer common/preferred); gr card 3 (`SBRF` vs `MXI`); cr (equity-equity pairs); ds H5 (relative value).*

**Economic story.** Matched-maturity futures on the ordinary and preferred shares of the same issuer share the same enterprise value. The relative price reflects the **voting/control premium** and dividend-class expectations. The relative valuation fluctuates with governance news and control expectations — it is **not** a contractual convergence.

**Common and preferred shares are not assumed fungible.** Their relative valuation can change persistently. This is a conditional relative-value mean-reversion hypothesis, and it is admitted **chiefly as a negative control** for the "equity-pairs hope".

**Instruments.** `SRZ6` (Sberbank ordinary, 100 shares, IM RUB 5,497.50) vs `SPZ6` (Sberbank preferred, 100 shares, IM RUB 5,499.16), 1:1. **No MKS/MMS discount across share classes** (separate asset codes), so gross IM is ≈ RUB 11 k, capping the pilot at one spread. Observed `SPZ6` bid/ask was **9 ticks** wide versus 1 tick for `SRZ6`.

**Data and calendar prerequisites.** Matched-maturity futures histories; point-in-time underlying share definitions and contract multipliers; **corporate-action notices and dividend announcements with availability times**; settlement method, corporate-action adjustment rules and expiry cutoffs; underlying share prices as diagnostics if lawfully available. **No future announced dividend may be inserted into an earlier observation.**

**Formula and units.**

```
Level residual:   S_t = P_ord,t − β · P_pref,t        (β = 1.0 primary baseline)
Log residual:     s_t = log p_ord,t − α − β log p_pref,t
Ratio form:       R_t = P_ord,t / P_pref,t ;  s = (R − median_train R) / σ_train
```

Primary baseline: **β = 1**, robust centre and scale. One additional variant: a robust training-only β, frozen during the position.

**Parameter selection.** β ∈ {1.0, OLS}; entry |z| ∈ {1.5, 2.0, 2.5}; exit at the residual centre or halfway to it; fixed maximum hold and expiry buffer.

**Entry / continue / exit.** Enter on a preregistered robust residual threshold only if the conservative expected excess is positive. **Compare including versus excluding predefined corporate-event windows — neither treatment may be chosen after seeing the event outcome.** Continue using current conditional value, dividend/event state and remaining risk. Exit on normalisation, time limit, impending expiry or an account trigger.

**Invalidate.** Changed share rights; corporate restructuring; incompatible corporate-action treatment; unstable hedge; a dividend announcement during the holding period; or any event that removes the original economic story.

**Expected failure mode.** A permanent relative repricing mistaken for temporary divergence; a missing dividend expectation driving the spread; the preferred leg being stale or costly; or dividend-capture distortion before a record date followed by structural re-rating.

**Validation.** Compare the fixed economic hedge (β = 1) against the robust statistical hedge under identical splits and costs. Record how much apparent alpha disappears once stale observations and event windows are handled honestly. Report **all** considered and rejected pairs.

**Decision to reject.** Profitability requires frequent hedge refits, one dividend event, continuous position averaging, or an unverified corporate-action adjustment; or median trade net X < 0 after Conservative costs; or annual turnover < 1 trade.

**Expected outcome.** The observed economics say this trade should be rare (11.9 k OI on the preferred versus 616 k on the ordinary), and the Base RTC of RUB 36.8 already breaches the RUB 35 triage ceiling of §5.4. **Expect rejection on cost; that is the point of including it.**

### F7 — Currency-identity basket

*Source labels: gs H4; cr H3 (cross-FX carry).*

**Economic story.** For consistent spot definitions, `RUB/USD = RUB/CNY × CNY/USD`. MOEX lists USD/RUB, CNY/RUB and USD/CNY futures families — [https://www.moex.com/s3811] — which supports investigating a three-leg relationship. **Listing does not prove that their futures settlement references, fixing times or sizes form a tradeable identity.**

**Instruments.** `Si` / `USDRUBF`, `CR` / `CNYRUBF`, and the USD/CNY family, at compatible maturities.

**Data and calendar prerequisites.** Compatible maturity dates and reference periods; each leg's fixing source, timestamp and fallback rules; correct quote orientation and contract quantity; RUB variation-margin conversion rules; synchronised executable quotes for later validation; and an explicit account of differences between the futures identity and the underlying spot identity.

**Formula and units.**

```
r_t = log F_RUB/USD,t − log F_RUB/CNY,t − log F_CNY/USD,t
```

The **zero benchmark is admissible only if contractual compatibility is demonstrated**; otherwise fit a restricted training-only residual centre and identify the remaining basis risks. Use the basket Jacobian to derive local hedge quantities — **three futures contracts do not automatically produce a currency-neutral basket.**

**Entry / continue / exit.** Start with the **identity hedge, not a regression search**. Enter only outside a full three-leg cost-and-uncertainty band. Continue while compatible reference economics remain and expected forward value exceeds flattening/reallocation value. Exit on normalisation, loss of a leg, fixing-window risk, expiry constraints or account rules.

**Invalidate.** Incompatible settlement references; one leg unavailable; or integer mapping leaving excessive residual currency exposure.

**Expected failure mode.** Daily timestamps create artificial deviations; a fixing basis is persistent; three-leg costs exceed the residual; or manual delay destroys the opportunity.

**Validation and rejection.** **EOD data can reject grossly incompatible economics; it cannot establish executable identity arbitrage.** Reject from the manual pilot if the advantage is concentrated in short-lived discrepancies, coordinated execution is unavailable, or legging stress would require core collateral.

**Deferred alternatives in the same structural class:** MOEX/RTS/FX baskets and RUB-gold/USD-gold/FX baskets. The 2017 index notice described MOEX and RTS as sharing a calculation base in different currencies; that historical statement does not establish current futures equivalence. These are **not** additional first-pass searches.

### F8 — Cross-asset and inter-commodity beta spreads

*Source labels: ar H5a (`GNZ6` vs `S1Z6`); ds H5 (relative value); mm card D (Brent/WTI); cr H2 (crack/conversion), H4 (index vs basket), H5 (petrocurrency); gr card 2 (`CY`/`CNYRUBF` basis).*

**Economic story.** Two instruments with a stable economic relationship — shared supply chain, quality/logistics differential, shared cash-flow or factor driver, or a macro linkage such as oil revenues supporting the ruble — may deviate from their historical relationship and revert.

**Admission is deliberately narrow.** A small ex-ante list of 2–4 pairs, selected from an **economic map written before any performance test**, each with the relationship, common risk drivers, counterexamples and an observable break condition documented. **A same-sector label alone is insufficient.** This is the family most exposed to pair mining, regime breaks, multiplier mismatch and apparent stationarity without a tradeable edge.

**Instruments.** Gold vs silver (`GNZ6` 2-unit mini ×2 vs `S1Z6` micro ×3, IM ≈ RUB 7,906); Brent vs WTI (blocked by WTI's 2026-07-14 launch); oil vs USD/RUB; index future vs a basket of single-stock futures; cross-currency implied-carry differences (e.g. CNY versus another FX-futures calendar's difference in implied RUB carry, only where FX conventions, settlement and common RUB funding exposure can be reconciled).

**Data.** Exact expiry-specific prices for both legs; corporate-action and settlement adjustments; point-in-time specifications and multipliers; OI/volume; fees, spreads and slippage; margin; and any point-in-time fundamentals needed to explain the relation. **A rolling-front continuous series is not sufficient if roll adjustments can generate a false residual.**

**Formula.** Pre-register **one** method: level residual `s_t = F_A,t − (α + β F_B,t)` where price units are economically comparable, or log-price residual `s_t = ln F_A,t − α − β ln F_B,t` where both prices are positive. Fit (α, β) on training data only and **freeze through the test fold**; estimate the reverse regression only if independently preregistered. Normalise with training-only median/MAD. **Do not use cointegration p-values as the trade signal.**

**Integer mapping.** As §8.6, with a predeclared maximum hedge-error limit. If the nearest integer pair is too unbalanced, **reject the pair**.

**Entry / continue / exit.** Fade a residual at |z| ∈ {1.5, 2.0, 2.5} only while relationship and liquidity checks pass. The eligible pair list is fixed before the test; **any post-hoc new pair is a new family in the candidate log.** Continue only if forward expected convergence exceeds remaining costs and the `LQDT` counterfactual. Exit on normalisation, holding limit, expiry buffer or invalidation.

**Invalidate.** Hedge ratio changing beyond a preregistered band; persistent residual extremes; structural/fundamental break; index-constituent or methodology change; incompatible dividend/settlement treatment; poor integer hedge; one leg unfillable.

**Expected failure mode.** Spurious cointegration; changing factor betas; corporate actions; roll artefacts; **survivor-biased pair choice**; a common shock becoming asymmetric; and cost plus integer rounding consuming the edge.

**Validation.** Hold the ex-ante universe fixed. Report **all** pairs considered and rejected, including those that failed tradability tests, so the multiple-testing burden is visible.

**Decision to reject.** A small change in training start date or hedge window changes the sign of the result; the integer residual-risk test fails; profitability comes from one pair or one period; or conservative net excess is not positive in the final holdout.

### F9 — Robust statistical mean reversion (falsification benchmark)

*Source labels: ds H6; the "raw z-score" comparator in gs, cg, mm, gr, ge, cr.*

**Economic story.** Some spreads exhibit statistical mean reversion even where the contractual carry story is weak. The hypothesis is that the reversion is strong enough to cover costs and is not merely an artefact of the estimation window.

**This is the weakest economic mechanism in the set and is included as a benchmark, not a core hypothesis.**

**Formula and cost filter.** Spread normalised by a rolling **median and MAD** rather than mean and standard deviation. Entry when the robust z-score exceeds the threshold. **A mandatory cost filter:** expected reversion from the entry level to the exit level, multiplied by the contract multiplier, must exceed the modeled round-trip cost by a preregistered multiple (1.5× to 3× across sources). Without that filter the strategy trades deviations too small to be profitable.

**Its real purpose.** F9 is the **A4 economic-filter ablation**. If the economic families (F1–F8) cannot beat F9 on the same data and the same costs, the economic story is not adding measurable value and the hypotheses must be rejected or reformulated. If F9 wins, that is a finding to report honestly, not to suppress.

**Decision to reject.** F9 is never "rejected" — it is the yardstick. It is reported alongside every other family so that the reader can see how much, if anything, economics bought.

## 11. Entry versus continuation: conditional value from now, not sunk cost

### 11.1 The principle

A trade's entry price and its past P&L are **sunk** for the *continue versus flatten now* decision. Holding a position is economically identical to entering it afresh at the current spread, minus the cost of closing and re-entering.

Two symmetric errors must both be avoided:

- **"Holding long" is not automatically bad.** A long-lived trade may be entirely justified if expected remaining excess is positive, liquidity needs are manageable and alternatives are weak. A long holding period per se is not a reason to exit.
- **A sunk loss is not a reason to continue.** Prior loss does not increase expected recovery value. Conversely, a recently opened trade may deserve immediate exit after a structural change.

There is one legitimate channel through which the past matters: **past losses reduce the pilot's remaining lifetime loss authority and funding headroom**, and they are Bayesian evidence about the regime. That restricts future actions; it never justifies continuation by breakeven arithmetic.

### 11.2 The formal comparison

At decision time *t*, define the feasible policies:

- **F** — flatten and follow the `LQDT`/cash policy;
- **H** — hold the current basket under its exit rules;
- **R** — flatten/reduce and allocate to a **currently observable** alternative;
- **W** — flatten and wait for future opportunities under a past-information arrival model.

All start from the **same present economic state** and are evaluated over a common horizon with a terminal liquidation convention. For action *a*:

```
V_t(a) = E_t[ ΔWᵃ − ΔWᴸ ] − risk/uncertainty penalty_t(a)
```

**Continue only if**

```
V_t(H) > max{ V_t(F), V_t(R), V_t(W) } + δ
```

where **δ** is a preregistered switching/estimation buffer that prevents churn from estimation noise and transaction costs. δ is a parameter to be tested within a preregistered range, not optimised per trade.

**The first implementation need not be an elaborate optimiser.** A conservative lookup keyed on coarse training-only state bins — residual magnitude, trade age, days to expiry, and normal-versus-stressed liquidity — is sufficient. Avoid finely partitioned state tables containing only a handful of completed trades.

An equivalent formulation, expressed relative to flattening now, uses a consistent mid-mark `M_t` and the future mark `M_τ` at the random exit time *τ* under the predeclared exit policy:

```
V_continue_vs_flat(t) = E[ M_τ − M_t | I_t ]
                      + C_exit(t)
                      − E[ C_exit(τ) + incremental hold/transaction/failure costs ]
                      − OC_LQDT(t, τ)
                      − risk/liquidity penalty
```

This **credits** continuation for not paying the current flattening cost while **charging** the expected future exit and incremental costs. Flatten-now is zero on this relative scale, before separately valuing the released capital. Use one consistent price/cost convention; **do not subtract the same bid/ask, slippage or fee twice, and do not ignore the exit half because the position already exists.** And against a competing new trade:

```
V_hold_vs_queue(t) = V_continue_vs_flat(t) − V_best_feasible_alternative(t)
```

where the alternative is valued by its **forecast at time t**, never by its realised future profit.

An alternative but equivalent statement, expressed as an incremental expected value per position:

```
IEV_i(t) = E[ P&L_to_exit | signal_t, τ_remaining ]
         − RTC_remaining (= exit cost + expected time × LQDT daily drag)
         − opportunity_cost_of_locked_margin(t)
```

with `opportunity_cost = bestAlternativeQueueExpectedExcessPerDay × E[time_to_exit]`, applied only when the queue is non-empty and the alternative could actually be funded by freeing the margin.

### 11.3 Decision rules that follow

- If `IEV < 0` **and** the queue holds a positive-expected trade that fits the freed margin → **flatten now**, even at a loss, free the margin, enter the alternative. This is *opportunity-aware exit*, not a stop-loss.
- If `IEV < 0` but the queue is empty → flatten if `E[P&L_to_exit] < exit_cost` (i.e. the expected loss from holding exceeds the cost of exiting) — effectively a time stop.
- If margin-call risk is present (VM buffer below one day of a high-percentile adverse move) → **flatten immediately**. The risk rule overrides the IEV comparison.
- **Never** count sunk entry cost or sunk P&L in the IEV — they cancel on both the "hold" and "close now" branches.

**Practical estimator.** For each residual-magnitude bucket, compute the empirical distribution of *future* spread P&L to exit (at target or time stop) **from training data only**. Take the median, or for conservatism the mean minus one standard deviation. This is what prevents "we lost RUB 200, so we must hold to win it back."

### 11.4 What the continuation forecast must contain

At each scheduled decision, report:

1. Conditional expected remaining RUB payoff.
2. Remaining cost range.
3. Expected `LQDT` wealth difference from funds still diverted.
4. **Distribution of time to exit** — see §11.5.
5. Probability of a loss-trigger exit, an expiry-buffer exit, and non-normalisation.
6. Remaining VM liquidity needs and possible margin increases.
7. Residual unhedged exposures.
8. Value of feasible current alternatives.
9. Uncertainty from small samples or changed economics.

Report net expected value **together with quantiles and tails**, and check calibration. Do not rely on a single conditional mean.

### 11.5 Time-to-exit is a distribution, not an average

For remaining holding time *T*, report `Pr(T > h | I_t)` at selected horizons, plus:

- median and upper quantiles;
- probability of reaching the expiry buffer;
- probability of no economic normalisation before forced exit;
- mark-to-market outcomes of positions still open at evaluation boundaries.

**Right-censored trades must not disappear from the sample.** If the evidence cannot estimate the tail reliably, retain the simpler fixed holding and expiry rules rather than fitting a fragile model.

### 11.6 Expiry and loss risk inside the continuation test

- Calendar spreads lose deterministic theta (cost of carry) every day regardless of signal, so IEV must subtract daily theta — for FX the RUSFAR day, for commodities the seasonal drift. **If theta turns adverse (curve inversion), IEV goes negative before any price move occurs.**
- For quarterly FX and index contracts, forced flattening at the expiry buffer bounds the loss but does not make it small: a 5–10 % adverse move on roughly RUB 12 k notional is RUB 600+. **Report the tail (e.g. 99th percentile loss), do not hide it behind an average.**
- Physical and perpetual funding carry overnight gap risk (observed on 2026-10-21: `CRZ6` close 12.404 versus settle 12.419, a 0.12 % overnight gap). Stress scenarios already embed an extra spread multiple for gap days.

### 11.7 Freed funds and the anti-oracle rule

Opportunity cost is real **only if** the alternative (a) exists now, (b) fits the RUB 30 k margin, and (c) was not cherry-picked ex post. The queue must therefore be evaluated on the **available-at-t** set, never on the full-sample optimum. An oracle that always reallocates to the ex-post best trade is a diagnostic ceiling, never a decision rule.

### 11.8 Illustrative arithmetic — not a forecast

Suppose a position is down RUB 2,000. From now: expected additional gross payoff RUB 900; remaining costs RUB 250; incremental `LQDT` return foregone RUB 200. Expected continuation excess is **RUB 450** before the uncertainty and risk penalty. **The sunk RUB 2,000 does not enter the arithmetic at all.** Continuation may still be prohibited by the loss rules, expiry risk, or insufficient confidence — but not because of the RUB 2,000.

If a currently feasible replacement has greater net value over the same horizon **after switching costs**, switching dominates. The replacement may not be chosen using its later realised return.

## 12. Sizing: fixed minimum, capped adaptive and preplanned scale-ins on identical splits

### 12.1 The fairness requirement

All three variants must use **exactly the same**: signal specifications and train/test boundaries; initial capital and maximum authority; execution-delay paths; cost scenarios and liquidity assumptions; loss rules; calendars and corporate-event information; and the signal-arrival ledger.

Use **paired comparisons on the same market path**. Do not give adaptive sizing better forecasts or additional capital. Do not allow the adaptive or scale-in variants to quietly receive better fills than the fixed-size baseline.

**Equal authority, not artificially identical utilisation.** The policies may use different amounts of capital at different times — that is part of the question. They must share the same **ceiling**, not be forced to hold equal exposure. Report both total net RUB excess **and** exposure, margin, funding and risk utilisation. Additional capital carries an `LQDT` counterfactual; it is not free merely because it was approved.

### 12.2 The three policies

| Policy | Definition | Main question | Cap | Emergency behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S0 — Fixed minimum** | One minimum acceptable integer basket per accepted opportunity. Forecast gates entry (yes/no) only; size never varies with \|z\| or volatility. | Does the economic hypothesis work **without** sizing sophistication? | 1× (or the minimum feasible basket, e.g. 5× gas micro) | No add. Emergency funds only to meet VM and flatten. |
| **S1 — Capped adaptive** | Size responds to a frozen forecast/risk rule, then maps to integers. | Does forecast-strength information improve net excess after granularity and turnover? | **2× at pilot scale** (≈4× on a scaled book) | No increase on loss. Adaptive size is an *a priori* function of signal strength, not of P&L. |
| **S2 — Preplanned scale-in** | Initial tranche plus **at most one** scheduled conditional addition. | Does staged exposure add value beyond equivalent authority and timing controls? | Same maximum as S1 — **no larger budget** | Any addition funded from **uncommitted pilot budget only**; never from the emergency reserve; never after a warning/action state; never after invalidation. |

**S0 in detail.** Use the smallest basket satisfying the hedge and risk constraints. **If it cannot fit, reject the signal** — it is not acceptable to take a badly hedged "one contract each" position merely because it fits the nominal allocation. Entry fails if required margin plus a conservative VM and fee reserve exceeds available allocated pilot funds. **No assumption that the core `LQDT` holding can be borrowed against.** No discretionary omission of eligible signals; rejected, stale and unfilled outcomes are recorded.

**S1 in detail.** A continuous target, then integer mapping:

```
k*_t = min( k_max ,  B_risk / L_stress,unit ,  B_funding / M_stress,unit ) × g(|z_t|)
```

where `g` is a preregistered capped monotone function, `B_risk` the risk budget, `L_stress,unit` the stressed per-unit loss, `B_funding` the funding budget and `M_stress,unit` the stressed per-unit margin. Equivalent Carver-style form:

```
units = (daily cash volatility target) / (instrument value volatility in RUB) × (forecast / 10)
```

with the forecast standardised to a long-run average absolute value of 10 and capped.

Use **at most two or three preregistered size multipliers** (e.g. 1× and a capped 2× risk-unit variant) rather than a continuous optimiser. Round **down** to feasible integer positions, preserving residual hedge-error constraints. Apply a **volatility floor and a stress-loss constraint** so that quiet historical periods cannot produce uncontrolled leverage. Reduce size for wider spreads, weak fill probability, high margin volatility, correlation with existing positions, or limited response capacity. **Keep rebalancing coarse:** a daily fractional target that repeatedly rounds between zero and one adds cost without useful risk control.

**The cap must be set by the test allocation and the broker margin requirement — never by the emergency reserve.** If the cap is set by the reserve, the reserve is quietly funding normal exposure, which the pilot rules prohibit.

**S2 in detail.** Predefine at entry: all allowed add levels, maximum total contracts, maximum total spread risk, and the adverse event that blocks further adds. Total maximum funding and stressed liquidity must be **reserved before entry**. Do not improvise adds after a loss.

**Comparison set for S2 (three arms, all with the same maximum authorised basket):**
1. One-shot full authorised basket.
2. Initial smaller basket, never add.
3. Initial smaller basket, add only when the preregistered condition remains valid.

### 12.3 Why S2 is not averaging down — the test that separates them

Averaging down is "I am losing, so I add to improve my breakeven." A legitimate scale-in is "my *ex-ante plan* said that if the spread widens another 0.7σ, the conditional edge *per unit* is higher, so a second unit has positive IEV **on its own**."

**Formal test:** compute the incremental expected value of the second tranche **ignoring the first position entirely**. If `IEV₂ < 0`, the second tranche never fires — even though the average entry price would look better. **An adverse price move alone is never sufficient justification to add.** If the rule deliberately adds on a larger residual, it must additionally require positive conditional marginal value, valid economics and adequate remaining loss capacity.

If adding requires emergency funding, the trade plan is inconsistent with the pilot rules and must be **rejected at the design stage**, not discovered later in simulation.

### 12.4 The null hypothesis the ablation must disprove

> **S1 and S2 do not improve net X versus S0 after costs, given the RUB 30,000 cap and manual latency.**

Adopt complexity only if the improvement is material — one source requires median ΔX to exceed the Conservative RTC **and** a Deflated Sharpe p < 0.05; another requires improvement in the holdout without worsening tail loss; a third requires a 1.5× multiple of the added transaction and slippage cost.

**Expected outcome at pilot scale, stated in advance:** S1 and S2 are **unlikely** to beat S0, because the cap of 2× limits leverage and the second tranche will often fail the independent `IEV₂ > 0` test against the queue. That expectation is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

**Critical identifiability caveat.** At minimum integer size, fractional staging may be impossible, in which case S0, S1 and S2 can collapse to the **same one-basket position**. If so, record them as **economically indistinguishable / unidentifiable** — **not** as three successful features. A variant that cannot be distinguished from integer-rounding or exposure effects is not evidence.

### 12.5 Margin discipline (applies to all three)

- **Pre-trade:** `required_IM_spread × buffer + k × daily high-percentile adverse move < C_avail`, else skip the trade even if the queue ranks it top. Buffers tested at 1.2×, 1.5× and 2.0× gross IM.
- **Intraday:** if the evening variation-margin close breaches a preregistered fraction of the VM buffer, flatten preemptively at the next open rather than waiting for an exchange margin call. Model this as a next-settlement exit with an extra tick, consistent with manual latency.
- **Human quota:** one new entry plus one exit per trading day maximum. An unconstrained portfolio is oracle-like and must not be reported as achievable.

## 13. Opportunity-arrival modelling without an oracle

### 13.1 The as-of opportunity ledger

Each signal produces a record containing:

- detection time and **information-availability** time;
- family, instruments and specification versions;
- forecast and uncertainty interval **as known then**;
- proposed integer basket;
- expected costs and capital requirement;
- signal expiry/staleness rule;
- contemporaneous rank;
- status: **accepted, stale, rejected, unfilled or partially filled**;
- reason for rejection;
- human actions required;
- executable outcome where observable.

**Include candidates rejected for insufficient capital, margin, stale data, expiry, human quota and execution constraints.** The realistic portfolio **processes every eligible signal**; finite capital and rules determine which orders are attempted. It must not retrospectively omit inconvenient opportunities.

A rejected idea may be followed as a clearly labelled **shadow observation** where data permit. **Its later outcome must not enter an earlier forecast or be mistaken for an executable fill.**

Retain days with no signal, no fill, partial fill, stale signal or insufficient capital. **Do not erase inconvenient observations or pretend an unavailable leg filled.** A no-signal / no-trade outcome is a normal state, not an operational failure.

### 13.2 Baseline: the ranked queue

1. Remove invalid, stale and unaffordable candidates.
2. Rank currently visible candidates by a **frozen conservative expected-excess score**.
3. Apply a **fixed tie-breaker** (e.g. lower IM first, then higher OI).
4. Allocate within available authority.
5. Retain cash / `LQDT` when no candidate clears the hurdle.

Candidate scoring rules proposed across the sources, all evaluated on the same ledger:

```
score_i(t) = ( |S_i(t) − fair_i(t)| − RTC_Cons_i ) / IM_i(t)          # excess per ruble of margin locked
score_i(t) = ( |z_i,t| × θ_i ) / ( Margin_i × σ_i,daily )             # strength × speed / (margin × vol)
score_i(t) = ( |z_i,t| × E[reversion] ) / ( Margin × Vol )            # generic form
score_i(t) = conservative expected RUB excess / committed funding-days
```

**A ratio of expected excess to expected holding time should be only a heuristic** — it ignores exit-time tails and the interaction between capital release and future arrivals. If the top-ranked opportunity cannot fit the available margin, skip to the next; the queue must **never fragment the book** into fractional positions.

### 13.3 Arrival model: add only if justified

Estimate arrivals from **prior ledger history only**. A first candidate is a rolling count model by family; test whether clustering and regime shifts make even that unreliable. Forecast, with uncertainty and shrinkage for sparse cohorts:

- the probability of at least one eligible opportunity in a fixed future window;
- its likely capital requirement;
- a conservative value distribution.

**Include stale and rejected opportunities when defining the observable opportunity process**, and separately distinguish four nested sets: signals detected → economically eligible opportunities → executable opportunities → opportunities affordable to this portfolio. **Do not collapse these into the ex-post number of profitable trades.**

### 13.4 Opportunity-aware exit and the anti-oracle test

For a current position *j*:

```
switch only if  V̂_t(feasible replacement) − V̂_t(continue j) > δ_switch
```

after all switching costs and uncertainty. Model the **full close-and-open friction** and the temporary unhedged-leg risk. A candidate switch is useful only if the alternative is not stale by execution time and there is capacity to close the current pair and open the new one after costs.

A forecast of future arrivals can justify a **value of waiting** — but **not an assumed future winner.** Test against three simpler benchmarks:

- no early reallocation (fixed holding/expiry rules);
- the current-opportunity ranked queue;
- **FIFO** by time (no ranking, first come first served).

**If the queue performs similarly to the complex arrival-aware allocator, keep the queue and reject the complexity.** Compare a more sophisticated ranking against FIFO to check whether ranking itself matters.

**The oracle ceiling.** An oracle that at time *t* knows each eligible spread's realised future P&L to its optimal exit is an **unachievable upper bound**, permitted only as a labelled diagnostic to measure the *regret gap* `X_oracle − X_queue` after identical costs. It may never select entries or exits, set parameters, or support a deployable result.

## 14. Validation discipline: walk-forward, purging, logs, hold-out

### 14.1 Register the universe before outcomes

Admission requires a **documented economic relationship**. Retain expired, inactive and failed candidates where data permit; **do not build the historical universe from current survivors alone.** Record all families and all rejected leads (the list in §7).

### 14.2 Preregistration

For every family, freeze before opening test returns: the rationale; candidate contracts/pairs; chosen normalisation; hedge direction; integer rule; parameter ranges; entry/exit/horizon ranges; structural-break rules; cost scenarios; fill policy; capital constraints; and the primary metric. **Log every family, pair, filter and parameter tried — including rejected candidates and failed leads.** Do not rename or re-tune a failed family without recording it as a new hypothesis.

### 14.3 Walk-forward procedure

1. Reserve a final untouched evaluation period.
2. Fit parameters, hedges, filters, cost calibration and queue rules on **training data only**.
3. Select among preregistered configurations within training / inner chronological validation.
4. **Freeze** the configuration for the next test interval.
5. **Purge** observations whose outcome or holding interval overlaps the next evaluation boundary. Size the purge to the **actual maximum signal lookback and holding overlap**, including label construction. One source specifies an embargo of at least the maximum registered holding horizon (candidate maximum 40 sessions) plus any longer expiry/roll overlap; others use 10, 15 or 20 sessions.
6. Advance chronologically and repeat.
7. Evaluate the final hold-out **once**, after design freeze.

**Sample-size discipline.** Train with at least ~756 daily observations where data allow; a proposed outer test block is ~126 sessions with a final hold-out target of 252–378 sessions. If the data cannot support meaningful independent folds **and** an untouched final period, report **insufficient evidence** — do not shrink the hold-out repeatedly until a favourable result appears.

**Boundary treatment.** Predefine whether positions may cross retraining boundaries. Simple first choice: retain the old frozen trade rule until exit; apply the new model only to new trades; **prohibit retroactive hedge changes.** At evaluation boundaries, mark all open positions and disclose censored outcomes; use a common terminal-liquidation convention.

**Historical hold-outs are imperfect** because the researcher already knows the period; an explicitly prospective final evaluation is preferable where practical. **The trial ledger remains necessary because a hold-out alone does not account for repeated configuration search** (Bailey et al., §3.7).

### 14.4 Statistical guards

- **Deflated Sharpe Ratio** (Bailey–López de Prado) across the ≈168 trials; require DSR p < 0.05 to claim an edge — not a raw Sharpe.
- **Benjamini–Hochberg** on per-hypothesis significance.
- **Block bootstrap** (block length ≈ half-life) for confidence intervals on X, respecting serial dependence; report sensitivity to block choice.
- **Multiplicity-aware uncertainty** (fold dispersion), **placebo direction** and **shifted-signal** checks.
- **Minimum effective sample and exposure-diversity gates.**
- **Do not make Sharpe ratio or trade win rate the selection criterion.**

**A confidence bound is not a guaranteed future outcome.**

### 14.5 Required reports

**Primary:**

- terminal and time-path **net RUB excess X** versus the matched `LQDT` counterfactual, with all external flows identical and all modeled costs paid;
- **excess-curve high-water drawdown**;
- **cumulative `LQDT` shortfall**.

**Supporting:**

- gross / fee / spread / slippage / funding / `LQDT` reconciliation;
- completed **and censored** trades;
- **time-to-exit distribution** with right-censored trades retained;
- maximum margin and cash demand;
- **emergency gross usage and incidents** (including unapproved broker use of core collateral);
- stale, rejected, partial and unfilled counts;
- human interventions and leg tickets;
- results by fold, family, expiry, regime and cost/delay scenario;
- contribution concentration (how much of the result comes from the top trade, pair or month);
- stress-liquidation outcomes.

### 14.6 Pass / reject / inconclusive

**Eligible for further research** (not live deployment) if: economically valid specifications; acceptable integer positions; positive net excess under Base **and** Conservative; no dependence on a single event; uncertainty analysis consistent with a genuine effect; no hidden core-collateral dependence; and acceptable manual and cash demands.

**Reject for this pilot** if: the minimum basket fails funding or risk constraints; the edge survives only at implausibly cheap costs or instantaneous fills; a missing cash flow is essential to the result; settlement economics invalidate the supposed relationship; complexity merely leverages one favourable episode; or ordinary execution would rely on emergency funding.

**Inconclusive** if sample size, rules, costs or permitted data are insufficient. **Do not relabel inconclusive as successful.**

## 15. Ablation plan: complexity must earn its place

**Purpose.** Prove that each complexity layer earns its keep against a trivial baseline, on the same splits, delays, costs and total authority. **Missing evidence must prevent complexity from being treated as necessary architecture.** If a component cannot be shown to improve the primary metric in the hold-out, it is removed from the candidate design.

### 15.1 The ablation grid

| Comparison | Held fixed | Isolated question | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 — Flexible size** (S0 vs S1) | Entry/exit model, authority, execution | Does adaptive sizing add net value? | Keep only if net excess improves **without** increasing the probability of breaching the RUB 10,000 drawdown trigger; median ΔX > Conservative RTC and DSR p < 0.05 |
| **A2 — Scale-ins** (one-shot vs staged) | Maximum authorised basket and reserved liquidity | Does staging improve timing rather than merely add exposure? | Keep only if the primary metric improves in the hold-out. **If it only reduces drawdown while lowering net excess, it is not justified as a return-enhancement feature.** |
| **A2b — Smaller initial basket with / without addition** | Same initial tranche | Is the addition itself valuable, or only the smaller initial size? | Keep the addition only if it adds value independently |
| **A3 — Opportunity-aware exit** (fixed vs forward continuation) | Entry and sizing | Does conditional continuation improve decisions? | Keep only if it beats the fixed exit rule in the hold-out; otherwise prefer the fixed rule for lower complexity and lower selection-bias risk. One source requires it to capture ≥ 30 % of the oracle regret gap without more than doubling turnover. |
| **A4 — Economic filter** (F9 vs F1–F8) | Data, splits, costs | Does the economic mechanism add value beyond statistical screening? | **If the economic filter cannot beat the pure statistical benchmark F9 on the same data and costs, the economic story adds nothing measurable and the hypotheses must be rejected or reformulated.** |
| **A5 — Hedge estimation** (full-sample vs rolling training-only) | Entry family and costs | How much of the apparent result is look-ahead in the hedge ratio? | The full-sample variant is a **diagnostic**, not a candidate. If the result survives only with the full-sample ratio, the hypothesis is invalid. |
| **A6 — Static vs refitted hedge** (identity/static vs Kalman or rolling) | Entry family and costs | Does hedge flexibility add value after turnover? | Reject flexibility if the gain disappears after turnover and costs |
| **A7 — No seasonal adjustment vs coarse seasonality** | Commodity family and folds | Does seasonal structure generalise? | Reject if the seasonal variant's advantage comes from one event or a foreign-market backtest |
| **A8 — Continuation vs current-opportunity switching** | Forecast model and sizing | Does a visible alternative justify switching costs? | Keep only if it clears the hysteresis buffer after full switching costs |
| **A9 — Ranked queue vs arrival-aware allocator** | Eligible ledger and total authority | Is future-arrival modelling necessary? | If the queue matches the complex allocator, **keep the queue** |
| **A10 — FIFO vs ranked queue** | Eligible ledger and authority | Does ranking itself matter? | If FIFO matches ranking, keep FIFO |
| **A11 — Combined configurations** (e.g. S1 + A3, S2 + A3) | — | Interaction effects | Only after the individual components pass independently |

**For the pilot specifically**, the labelled arms are:

| Arm | Configuration | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **A0** | F1 sizing + ranked queue + fixed target/time stop + no scale-in | Reference X |
| **A1** | A0 with capped adaptive sizing | Isolates flexible size |
| **S1** | A0 (or A1 if it passed) with a preplanned second tranche | Isolates scale-ins |

### 15.2 When complexity is rejected

Reject a complexity feature if it: adds no stable net excess after costs; worsens cash or shortfall tails; relies on a few observations; increases human actions beyond capacity; **cannot be distinguished from integer-rounding or exposure effects**; or requires emergency funding to function.

**Avoid launching a large factorial search.** Test the simplest viable baseline first, then a small number of one-factor changes. Any later combined configuration is a **new logged trial**.

## 16. Governance, risk and emergency-funding protocol

**These are provisional action rules supplied by the project, not externally validated and not guaranteed loss bounds.** Gaps, locked markets, margin changes, delayed human response and partial fills can carry losses beyond them. A backtest cannot demonstrate that they cap live losses.

### 16.1 The action ladder, computed from the excess ledger

Define the excess high-water mark and drawdown:

```
H_t = max(0, max_{u ≤ t} X_u)        DD_t = H_t − X_t
```

| Condition (excess-curve basis) | Action | Funding allowed? |
|---|---|---|
| `DD_t ≥ RUB 5,000` — **warning** | Pause new entries; review open positions' conditional value daily; log | No |
| `DD_t ≥ RUB 10,000` **or** `X_t ≤ −RUB 10,000` — **action** | Cancel new exposure; cancel unfilled additions; initiate orderly de-risking at market (stress slippage). **Any funding is solely to reach flat.** | Only to meet VM to reach flat; counts against the RUB 15,000 gross cap; requires explicit approval; incident-logged |
| `X_t ≤ −RUB 15,000` — **outer boundary** | Hard stop; immediate flat; 90-day review gate; no new risk until the review passes | Same as above, up to the cap |
| 90 calendar days elapsed | **Review gate** even with no breach. **No resetting** of losses, high-water marks or the lifetime emergency allowance | No auto-reset |

### 16.2 Funding and collateral rules the simulator must enforce

| Constraint | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Initial allocation RUB 30,000 | Includes initial margin, variation-margin liquidity and reserves |
| Emergency allowance RUB 15,000 **cumulative gross** | **No replenishment after repayment; no netting of earlier emergency uses against recoveries** |
| Maximum gross contributed funding RUB 45,000 | Not an entry budget. **Entry must pass with zero emergency funding.** |
| Emergency purpose | Only to avert disorderly liquidation or complete de-risking of **existing** exposure; explicit approval required |
| At RUB 10,000 cumulative shortfall | Any additional funding is **solely to reach flat** |
| Core collateral used by the broker | Counts toward the gross emergency allowance; unapproved use is an **incident** |
| Core asset sale proceeds | A funding/collateral event, **not automatically an economic loss in X** |
| Internal budgets | Do not ring-fence the account and do not prove broker collateral eligibility |

**Three quantities that must never be conflated:** economic excess, cash liquidity, and collateral/authority. They are not interchangeable.

**Record warnings, actions and forced de-risking as strategy outcomes, not as "operational noise" to be removed.**

### 16.3 Separate hard feasibility constraints still undefined

The pilot needs, and does not yet have, user-defined limits on: maximum active spread pairs; maximum daily new entries and interventions; maximum unanswered alert/reaction time; and emergency-response availability. No report can infer permission from "24/7 monitoring", and monitoring does not imply that each contract trades continuously.

### 16.4 Trigger detection must be simulated honestly

Simulate trigger detection **with** human delay (3–90 minutes, typical 5–15 but distribution unknown), market closures, weekend and holiday gaps, and gap/VM risk. One source flagged that MOEX weekend sessions create Sunday gap risk, so the backtest must include the Monday WAPRICE gap as a realised trade. **If a strategy survives only at zero delay, it is not pilot-viable.**

## 19. Falsification criteria, project gates and possible project changes

### 19.1 Per-hypothesis reject triggers

**Reject a family — do not retune it indefinitely — if any one of these occurs:**

1. **Cost fail.** Base-scenario median net X ≤ 0 on C1; or the Conservative 10th percentile < −RUB 5,000 (the warning level) on a rolling window; or the result is not robust to a plausible change in the cost assumption (e.g. doubling the spread assumption).
2. **Frequency fail.** Fewer than ~2 eligible trades per year at the median parameter after filters — the opportunity set is too sparse to justify a manual workflow.
3. **Stability fail.** Chow / Bai–Perron break p < 0.01 on training data; or the half-life confidence interval spans more than ~3× (e.g. 5–60 days); or the drawdown path breaches the RUB 10,000 action trigger before convergence in a material proportion of trades even where the terminal result is positive.
4. **Expiry trap.** More than ~40 % of trades are force-flattened at the expiry buffer at a loss — the edge is not realised within the expiry window.
5. **Tail fail.** The 99th-percentile loss per trade exceeds ~2.5× the median win — a left-tail-dominated "non-convergence" pathology.
6. **Look-ahead dependence.** The result depends on a full-sample hedge ratio, a revised dividend or rate series, or a survivor-only pair choice.
7. **Concentration.** The result depends materially on one event, one asset/pair, one data-source revision, or unrealistic same-close fills.
8. **Multiplicity.** The number of parameter combinations tried is large relative to the number of independent trades, and the hold-out result is not clearly separated from the distribution of random rules.
9. **Data/cost inadmissibility.** Required point-in-time data, specification or all-in costs cannot be obtained lawfully and reproducibly.
10. **Funding inadmissibility.** A required entry fails at zero emergency funding, or simulated margin/liquidity needs core collateral, an unapproved contribution, or excess gross pilot funding.
11. **Execution infeasibility.** Stale, rejected or partial outcomes, or the 90-minute response stress, make the trade infeasible; or tail/liquidation risk exceeds the defined pilot authority.

### 19.2 Portfolio-level gates

- **After the individual family experiments:** if **zero** families survive Conservative costs, **stop the hypothesis hunt.** Report "no pilot-viable spread at RUB 30,000 with manual execution" as the honest outcome. Broad hunting without the validation discipline increases selection bias rather than reducing it.
- **After the portfolio queue experiment:** if queue net X ≤ 0 after C0 accounting on the hold-out, or if the queue captures too little of the realistic opportunity set, **do not automate at BCS.** Either reduce to a single-calendar carry study or abandon the futures-spread pilot and keep the capital in `LQDT`.
- **After the ablations:** if adaptive sizing and scale-ins do not beat the fixed-minimum baseline, **ship fixed minimum sizing plus the ranked queue only** — no scale-ins, no adaptive sizing at pilot scale.

### 19.3 Scale gates

- A family surviving Conservative ⇒ paper-trade at 1× with a 1.5× buffer for 90 days, then hold the formal review.
- Surviving **Stress** as well would justify revisiting the VPS sizing and any data-resolution upgrade — but only then.
- **Scale to RUB 10–20 million only after KPUR-tier initial margin is confirmed in writing by the broker and the EDP haircut is quantified.** Internal budgets do not prove collateral eligibility. At that scale, fill capacity, market impact, collateral treatment and operational load must be re-estimated from scratch; **pilot fills do not extrapolate.**

### 19.4 Possible project changes if gates fail

| Finding | Appropriate change |
|---|---|
| No acceptable integer basket within RUB 30,000 | Retain a virtual study; narrow the universe. **Do not** automatically enlarge the capital. |
| Data-use terms do not cover the intended workflow | Obtain written clarification/agreement or a permitted alternative before implementing anything |

### Appendix C — Deterministic cost arithmetic (how the RTC table was computed)
