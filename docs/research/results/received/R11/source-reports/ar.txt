## 1. Executive Summary & Foundational Architectural Decision

### 1.1 The Operational Paradox and Architectural Bifurcation
The objective of this investigation is to dimension and safeguard the execution environment for a private Moscow Exchange (MOEX) derivatives (FORTS) futures-spread trading pilot. The operational context presents a strict set of constraints:
1. **Shared Low-Resource Host:** A single Virtual Private Server (VPS) with 1 vCPU, 1 GB RAM, 2 GB swap, and a 20 GB SSD running Ubuntu 24.04 LTS. This VPS currently hosts an active, business-critical VPN service (`x-ui` / Xray) and another scheduled workload.
2. **Strict Background Budget:** An unverified, shared RAM budget of 250–350 MB.
3. **Protected Production Services:** Network ports 22 (SSH), 443 (HTTPS/Xray Reality), and 20724 (3X-UI control panel), along with existing firewall and BBR kernel network tuning, must not be disrupted or modified.
4. **Capital & Collateral Asymmetry:** A modest pilot allocation of RUB 30,000 (covering margin, variation margin buffers, and reserves) coexists on the **same broker account** at VTB with a core holding exceeding RUB 40,000,000 in the LQDT money market fund. Core LQDT collateral must never be silently encumbered, drawn upon, or treated as test capital.
5. **Human Execution Dynamics:** Initial execution is manual via VTB (with reaction latencies spanning 3–90 minutes, typical median 5–15 minutes); potential future automation is targeted at BCS.
6. **Zero-Cost Preference & Geopolitical Reality:** No foreign payment cards or foreign phone numbers are available, eliminating Western commercial cloud infrastructure (AWS, GCP, Azure, Hetzner, DigitalOcean).

**The Core Decision:** To prevent catastrophic interference with the production VPN and guarantee deterministic operational reliability, the project architecture must be **strictly bifurcated into two distinct operational environments**:

### 1.2 Summary of Key Findings
- **Workload Feasibility on VPS:** A continuous MOEX FORTS collection workload covering an EOD baseline of ~100–300 contracts and an intraday watchlist of 5–30 pairs (10–60 contracts at 15-minute intervals) generates only **~4.7 MB/day of raw JSON** and **~350 KB/day of structured tabular data**. When stored in SQLite WAL mode with point-in-time raw JSON staging tables pruned after 30 days, 5 years of historical futures data consumes **under 150 MB of disk space**, well within the 20 GB VPS disk limit.
- **Memory Safety & Runtime Selection:** Running analytical dataframes (e.g. `pandas` or `polars`) on the VPS daemon is an anti-pattern; importing pandas alone consumes 45–65 MB RSS before allocating data. The continuous VPS daemon must be implemented in **vanilla Python 3.12 using standard library structures and SQLite** (steady-state RSS: 35–55 MB, peak RSS: ~85 MB) or a compiled Go binary (steady-state RSS: 15–25 MB). This leaves ample margin within the 250–350 MB shared budget.
- **VPN Protection Guarantee:** Systemd cgroups v2 (`MemoryMax=180M`, `CPUQuota=30%`, `Nice=15`) and process scoring (`OOMScoreAdjust=500` for the pilot daemon vs `OOMScoreAdjust=-900` for `x-ui` and `sshd`) guarantee that even in a catastrophic runaway condition, the Linux OOM-killer will terminate the pilot collector while preserving VPN routing, SSH access, and network performance.
- **Telegram Operational Bounds:** Telegram Bot API operates over outbound HTTPS long-polling (`getUpdates`), requiring **zero open incoming ports** and zero modification to firewall rules. Rate limits (1 msg/sec per private chat, 30 msg/sec global) comfortably exceed pilot requirements (<10 alerts/day). However, alert delivery does not equal trade execution; human reaction latency (median 5–15 min) necessitates signal expiration TTLs (15–30 min) and explicit price-band validation.
- **Capital Governance & LQDT Protection:** The virtual ledger enforces automated circuit breakers: a Telegram warning at RUB 5,000 excess-curve drawdown; an automated action trigger at RUB 10,000 drawdown from high-water mark or cumulative LQDT shortfall of RUB 10,000 (suspending new entries and recommending position flattening); and a hard stop at RUB 15,000 shortfall. Emergency additional funding is strictly capped at RUB 15,000 gross (RUB 45,000 gross lifetime contribution) and restricted solely to de-risking open positions to flat.

## 2. Embedded Workload Dimensioning & Sensitivity Analysis

### 2.1 Universe, Ingestion, and Sampling Boundaries
The workload model is dimensioned from the stated planning ranges:
- **Broad Futures Universe:** Tens to a few hundred active MOEX FORTS contracts across commodities (BR, NG, GOLD, SILV, PL, PA), equity indices (MIX, RTS), single stocks (SBER, GAZP, LKOH, GMKN), FX (Si, CNY), and interest rates (OFZ). For sizing, we evaluate $N \in [30, 100, 300]$ active contracts.
- **Continuous Watchlist:** 5 to 30 candidate pairs, representing $K \in [10, 60]$ underlying individual contracts.
- **Collection Intervals:**
  1. *Baseline EOD:* 1 daily pull per contract post-clearing (19:05 MSK), capturing Open, High, Low, Close, Volume, Open Interest (OI), Settlement Price, and Number of Trades.
  2. *Watchlist Intraday:* Snapshots taken during the active trading session (09:00–23:50 MSK = 14.83 trading hours). Evaluated at 60-minute intervals (15 bars/day) and 15-minute intervals (59 bars/day).
- **Trading Calendar:** Approximately 250 statutory trading days per calendar year.

### 2.2 Mathematical Sizing Model
We establish explicit mathematical formulas for network transfer, storage growth, memory consumption, and CPU processing time:

1. **Network Ingestion Volume per Trading Day ($V_{\text{net}}$):**
   $$V_{\text{net}} = \left( N \cdot S_{\text{eod\_raw}} \right) + \left( K \cdot B_{\text{intraday}} \cdot S_{\text{bar\_raw}} \right)$$
   Where:
   - $S_{\text{eod\_raw}} \approx 1.5\text{ KB}$ (MOEX ISS JSON response per contract with `iss.meta=off`).
   - $S_{\text{bar\_raw}} \approx 1.2\text{ KB}$ (15-min or hourly candle JSON snippet from MOEX ISS).
   - $B_{\text{intraday}} \in \{15, 59\}$ bars per contract per day.

2. **Storage Footprint Growth ($V_{\text{disk}}$):**
   $$V_{\text{disk}}(t) = V_{\text{structured}}(t) + V_{\text{staging}}(t)$$
   - *Structured Relational Store (SQLite):* Normalized record size $S_{\text{row}} \approx 80\text{ bytes}$ (timestamp, secid, open, high, low, close, vol, oi, settlement, flags) plus B-Tree indexing overhead (~30 bytes/row) $\approx 110\text{ bytes/row}$.
   - *Raw JSON Staging Store:* Raw JSON payload cached for reproducibility, bounded by a 30-day retention window ($T_{\text{ret}} = 30\text{ days}$).
   $$V_{\text{staging}} = 30 \times V_{\text{net\_day}}$$

3. **Memory Footprint (Resident Set Size, RSS):**
   $$\text{RSS}_{\text{total}} = \text{RSS}_{\text{runtime\_base}} + \text{RSS}_{\text{engine\_cache}} + \text{RSS}_{\text{batch\_buffer}}$$
   Where:
   - $\text{RSS}_{\text{runtime\_base}}$ is the base interpreter footprint (~30 MB for CPython 3.12, ~12 MB for Go).
   - $\text{RSS}_{\text{engine\_cache}}$ is the SQLite connection page cache (`PRAGMA cache_size = -2000` $\approx 2\text{ MB}$).
   - $\text{RSS}_{\text{batch\_buffer}}$ is the in-flight JSON decoding and validation buffer ($< 5\text{ MB}$ for bounded batches of 10 contracts).

### 2.4 Ingestion Architecture: Bounded Batches, Lineage, and Export
To guarantee that data collection never starves the CPU or triggers memory spikes:
1. **Polite Sequential Ingestion:** MOEX ISS does not require concurrent multi-threaded hammering. Requests are dispatched sequentially over a persistent HTTP/1.1 connection with an intentional inter-request sleep of $0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ second}$. A full batch of 60 watchlist contracts takes ~45 seconds, well within the 15-minute bar window, consuming $< 2\%$ average CPU.
2. **Point-in-Time Data Lineage:** Every ingested bar is stored with metadata:
   - `retrieved_at` (UTC timestamp of HTTP response).
   - `source_url` (MOEX ISS query path with parameters).
   - `http_etag` / `last_modified` (cache validation headers).
   - `payload_hash` (SHA-256 hash of the raw JSON snippet).
3. **Compaction and Checkpointing:** SQLite operates in WAL mode (`PRAGMA journal_mode = WAL`). A passive checkpoint is executed every 1,000 pages, with a mandatory scheduled full checkpoint (`PRAGMA wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE)`) executed during the overnight market closure at 03:00 MSK to keep the WAL file under 2 MB.
4. **Portable Research Outputs:** The SQLite database file (`/opt/moex-spread-pilot/data/market_data.db`) is fully self-contained. The local MacBook pulls periodic incremental copies via `rsync -avz -e ssh` or `scp` over port 22. Analytical pipelines on the MacBook convert tables into Apache Parquet format using DuckDB/Polars for intensive vectorized modeling.

### 3.1 Runtime Alternatives: Benchmarking Operational Trade-offs

**Analytical Conclusion on Runtime:**  
Ubuntu 24.04 LTS includes Python 3.12 as the system default. A dedicated virtual environment containing only `httpx` (or `aiohttp`) and using the built-in `sqlite3` module provides the optimal balance between rapid iteration, research code portability, and low resource overhead. Go is technically superior in memory footprint (18 MB vs 45 MB), but Python 3.12 operating under strict systemd memory caps is well within the 180 MB safety threshold. Dataframe libraries (`pandas`, `polars`) are **strictly banned** from the VPS collector.

### 3.2 Storage Alternatives: Durability, Consistency, and Migrations

| Engine | Storage Architecture | Memory Footprint | Durability & ACID | Schema Migration Tooling | VPS Viability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SQLite (WAL Mode)** | Single file on SSD; append-only WAL | 2–5 MB page cache | Full ACID; atomic commits; crash-safe | PRAGMA `user_version` or `yoyo-migrations` | **OPTIMAL (Zero daemon overhead)** |
| **DuckDB** | Single file; vectorized columnar | 35–80 MB minimum | ACID; optimized for bulk OLAP | Embedded schema evolution | Excellent for MacBook; heavy for VPS |

**SQLite WAL Configuration Best Practices:**
To ensure zero corruption and immediate concurrency between the collector thread, the virtual ledger, and read queries:
```sql
PRAGMA journal_mode = WAL;
PRAGMA synchronous = NORMAL;       -- Safe in WAL mode; eliminates fsync latency spikes
PRAGMA busy_timeout = 5000;         -- 5-second lock queue to prevent "database is locked"
PRAGMA cache_size = -2000;          -- 2 MB memory cache limit
PRAGMA temp_store = MEMORY;         -- Keep temporary indices off disk
PRAGMA foreign_keys = ON;           -- Enforce relational integrity
```
*Schema Migrations:* Managed via an integer version stored in `PRAGMA user_version`. On startup, the daemon checks `PRAGMA user_version;`, executing sequential SQL upgrade scripts in an atomic transaction if updates are required.

## 4. Telegram Bot API: Quotas, Delivery Reliability & Human Latency

### 4.1 Quotas, Limits, and Financial Costs (2026 Verification)
- **Cost:** Free. Bots creating private chats or small operational groups do not incur fees. (Telegram Paid Broadcasts introduced in API 7.11+ charge 0.1 Stars/message only for mass commercial broadcasts exceeding 30 msg/sec).
- **Core Documented Rate Limits:**
  - *Single Private Chat:* Approximately 1 message per second. Short bursts of 2–3 messages are tolerated; sustained transmission triggers HTTP 429 with a mandatory `retry_after` parameter.
  - *Group Chat:* 20 messages per minute.
  - *Global Broadcaster Ceiling:* 30 messages per second across all chats.
  - *Message Payload Limits:* 4,096 UTF-8 characters for text; 1,024 UTF-8 characters for media captions.
- **Payload Design:** Spread signals and risk summaries fit well within 600–1,200 characters, requiring zero multi-part message splitting.

### 4.2 Security, Access Control & Privacy
1. **Outbound-Only Communication (Long Polling):** The daemon uses Telegram's `getUpdates` method with a 30-second timeout. This is an outbound HTTPS request to `api.telegram.org:443`. No incoming ports are opened, ensuring **zero attack surface** on the VPS.
2. **Strict Identity Whitelisting:** Bots cannot initiate contact with users. The operator must initiate communication by sending `/start`. The daemon enforces strict authorization in software:
   ```python
   # Pseudocode: Mandatory Recipient Whitelist Guard
   AUTHORIZED_USER_ID = int(os.environ["TELEGRAM_AUTHORIZED_USER_ID"])
   
   def on_update_received(update):
       sender_id = update.message.from_user.id
       if sender_id != AUTHORIZED_USER_ID:
           logger.warning(f"Unauthorized access attempt from Telegram ID {sender_id}. Dropping.")
           return  # Silent drop; zero disclosure of bot existence or system state
   ```
3. **Privacy of Financial Metadata:**
   - Under no circumstances are real broker account numbers, full login credentials, or absolute core collateral values (> RUB 40,000,000) sent over Telegram.
   - All alert notifications report relative or pilot-bounded figures: e.g., *"Pilot Drawdown: -RUB 1,250 (-4.17% of RUB 30k test allocation) | Core Collateral: SECURE / UNTOUCHED"*.

### 4.3 Delivery Dynamics, Deduplication, and Network Drops
Telegram HTTP POST requests can fail due to transient network drops, TLS timeouts, or MOEX VPS routing hiccups. Naive retries risk spamming the operator with duplicate trade alerts:
- **Idempotency Keys & Deduplication Cache:** Every alert is assigned an internal UUID and state hash (e.g., `sig_SiM6_CNYM6_20261022_110000`). If a network timeout occurs during `sendMessage`, the daemon checks its local delivery journal.
- **Message Editing Pattern:** Instead of firing new messages for incremental quote updates, the daemon updates existing status cards using `editMessageText` (limited to 1 edit/sec per message). This keeps the chat history clean and eliminates noise.
- **Exponential Backoff:** On receiving an HTTP 429 response, the daemon parses the `parameters.retry_after` JSON field, sleeps for the designated interval $+ 0.5\text{ seconds}$, and resumes transmission without dropping the message.

### 4.4 The Human Execution Gap: Alert vs Action vs Market Fill
In algorithmic trading systems, signal generation is assumed to execute in milliseconds. In this project's manual execution phase at VTB, there is an unavoidable human latency distribution:
- **Observed Manual Latency:** 3 to 90 minutes; typical median is **5 to 15 minutes**.
- **Market Degradation Risk:** Futures spreads are dynamic. Over a 15-minute delay, the spread price can drift significantly, invalidating the economic rationale or worsening the entry basis.
- **Signal Specification & Expiration (TTL):** Every spread signal emitted by the sentry daemon must include an explicit validity envelope:
  - `Signal Timestamp`: 2026-10-22 11:15:00 MSK
  - `Recommended Entry Spread`: -135 to -125 pts (Current: -130 pts)
  - `Adverse Price Limit (Invalidation)`: -120 pts
  - `Time-to-Live (TTL)`: 20 minutes (Expires 11:35:00 MSK)
- **Legging Risk (Execution Risk on Leg 2):** When the operator manually places two separate limit orders in the VTB app or QUIK terminal, the risk exists that Leg 1 (e.g. Long Si) fills while Leg 2 (e.g. Short CNY) does not. The sentry bot provides a dedicated interactive check: `/legged <secid> <price>` to compute immediate re-balancing adjustments or emergency flattening instructions.

### 4.5 Alert Prioritization and Resource Starvation Defense
A common flaw in co-located monitoring scripts is that a heavy data ingestion batch or database lock freezes the alert loop.
- **Architectural Defense:** The risk monitoring and alert loop runs in an independent, asynchronous task with its own dedicated SQLite read connection (`PRAGMA busy_timeout = 5000`).
- If an ingestion pull from MOEX ISS hangs or takes 40 seconds to process, the risk sentry continues evaluating stop-loss rules and heartbeat timers every 5 seconds, completely unblocked.

```
   |
   +---> MEMORY PRESSURE / OOM THREAT

   |     * OOM Score: Sentry = +500 (first victim), x-ui/VPN = -900 (protected)
   |
   +---> CAPITAL DRAWDOWN & COLLATERAL PROTECTION

   |     * Action Trigger: RUB 10,000 drawdown or shortfall -> Halt & flatten

```

### 5.2 Exchange Session Awareness & Clearing Windows
MOEX FORTS operates on a strict schedule. Stale-data detection must not generate false alarms during expected market pauses:
- **Morning Session:** 09:00 – 10:00 MSK (Pre-market auction 08:50–09:00).
- **Day Main Session:** 10:00 – 14:00 MSK.
- **Intermediate Clearing Session (Quotes Frozen):** **14:00 – 14:05 MSK**. (No trading; mark-to-market variation margin calculated).
- **Day Main Session (Continued):** 14:05 – 18:50 MSK.
- **Evening Clearing Session (Quotes Frozen, Daily Settlement):** **18:50 – 19:05 MSK**. (Trading halts; full cash settlement, variation margin debited/credited, margin requirements updated).
- **Evening Additional Session:** 19:05 – 23:50 MSK.
- **Overnight Market Closure:** 23:50 – 08:50 MSK (plus weekends and Russian statutory public holidays).
*Heartbeat Rule:* Stale-quote watchdog triggers if no updates arrive for **> 25 minutes**, but is programmatically **suspended** between 14:00–14:06, 18:49–19:06, overnight (23:50–08:55 MSK), and on non-trading days defined by the MOEX trading calendar table.

### 5.3 Swap Pressure, Memory Capping & Kernel OOM Protection
Swapping on a 1 GB VPS degrades disk I/O and creates severe latency spikes for the Xray VPN proxy.
1. **Linux Memory Cgroup (cgroups v2):** Implemented in the systemd service file:
   - `MemoryHigh=140M`: When memory reaches 140 MB, the kernel aggressively throttles the process and reclaims page caches.
   - `MemoryMax=180M`: If memory breaches 180 MB, the kernel terminates the daemon immediately without affecting other services.
   - `MemorySwapMax=50M`: Binds the maximum allowable swap usage, preventing runaway swap thrashing.
2. **OOM Score Adjustment Hierarchy:**
   - Linux kernel assigns an Out-Of-Memory score from -1000 to +1000.
   - `sshd.service`: `OOMScoreAdjust=-1000` (Never kill SSH).
   - `x-ui.service` / Xray: `OOMScoreAdjust=-900` (Protect VPN at all costs).
   - `moex-sentry.service`: `OOMScoreAdjust=+500` (Sacrificial process; killed first if total system memory is exhausted).

### 5.4 Clock Drift and Session Synchronization
Futures spread calculations depend on accurate timestamps across individual legs.
- Ubuntu 24.04 uses `systemd-timesyncd` by default.
- If clock drift exceeds 1.0 second, bar aggregation may misalign. The preflight check verifies NTP synchronization via `timedatectl status`.

### 5.5 Virtual Ledger Integrity, Replay Safety & State Restoration
The virtual ledger records all virtual transactions, margin allocations, cash balances, and mark-to-market valuation events.
- **Append-Only Event Sourcing:** The ledger uses an append-only table `virtual_ledger_events`:
  - `event_id` (auto-incrementing integer).
  - `event_type` (`SIGNAL_FIRED`, `USER_ORDER_INTENT`, `FILL_SIMULATED`, `MTM_CLEARING`, `EMERGENCY_DEPOSIT`, `CIRCUIT_BREAKER_HALT`).
  - `event_payload` (JSON string detailing prices, volumes, slippage).
  - `cumulative_pnl` (running total in RUB).
  - `excess_over_lqdt` (performance relative to matched LQDT counterfactual).
  - `event_hash` (HMAC-SHA256 linking to the previous record hash, guaranteeing tamper-evident auditability).
- **Crash Recovery & Replay:** On daemon restart after an ungraceful crash, the engine reads the last confirmed checkpoint and replays all events from `virtual_ledger_events`, reconstructing the exact position and risk state in $< 200\text{ milliseconds}$.

## 11. Material Conflicts, Unknowns & Falsification Criteria

1. **Shared VPS Headroom Reality:** While specifications state 1 vCPU and 1 GB RAM, the actual unallocated memory headroom is unknown until Stage 3 preflight measurement. However, broker-specific margin calculation rules for calendar and cross-commodity spreads vary. ### 11.2 Falsification Criteria (Conditions Invalidating the Architecture)
- **Falsification Criterion 1 (Memory):** If Stage 3 measurement reveals steady-state available RAM (`MemAvailable`) is $< 180\text{ MB}$, the VPS-based sentry model is **invalidated**. The sentry must either be relocated to an upgraded server or converted into an on-demand collector running only on the MacBook during trading hours.
- **Falsification Criterion 2 (Network IP Block):** If MOEX ISS responds with HTTP 403 or persistent drops to requests originating from the VPS IP address, the free collection architecture is **invalidated**, requiring a local proxy or broker feed.
- **Falsification Criterion 3 (Execution Latency Slippage):** If historical tracking in the virtual ledger reveals that human execution latency (median 5–15 min) causes realized spread entry prices to degrade by $> 25\%$ of expected spread profit across 10 consecutive trades, the manual VTB model is **falsified**, mandating a transition to automated execution (e.g. BCS API) before scaling.
