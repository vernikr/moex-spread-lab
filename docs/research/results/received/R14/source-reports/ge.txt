# Evidence-Backed Research Report: Optional Text, News, and Channel Features for MOEX Futures-Spread Project

## Executive Summary

This report evaluates whether alternative text, news, and channel sources can add verifiable, net-positive value beyond existing price-based signals and formal corporate disclosures for a medium-term MOEX futures-spread project. Operating within a strict RUB 30,000 test allocation (matched against an LQDT counterfactual), a tight 1-vCPU / 1-GB RAM VPS footprint, and a free Gemini Flash quota, any text-derived feature must justify its compute, latency, and operational overhead.

Price action and scheduled corporate event flags serve as our core comparison baselines. Unverified sentiment, promotional Telegram channels, and retroactive data adjustments are systematically excluded to prevent look-ahead bias and structural leakage.

## 2. Bounded Export Brief for Channels and News Feeds

Because unstructured text streams (such as Telegram channels or social media feeds) suffer from post-publication edits, deletions, and unverified timestamps, raw scraping without strict provenance controls invalidates historical testing.

### Verified Identity and Scope

* **Target Channels:** Official MOEX announcement channels and primary regulatory press channels (CBR).
* **Data Fields to Extract:** Message ID, precise UTC/Moscow timestamp of initial publication, raw text body, edit flag status (if available via API/web metadata), and attached document hashes.
* **Exclusion Criteria:** Any channel relying on qualitative "market sentiment," anonymous trading tips, or paid subscriber-only content.

> **Assessment on Deeper Export:** For a medium-term futures spread strategy operating under a manual-to-semi-automated execution framework with a 3–90 minute reaction window, **deep historical text exports of high-frequency social channels are not worth the engineering overhead.** The risk of look-ahead leakage, survivorship bias from deleted posts, and high noise-to-signal ratio heavily outweighs potential alpha. Future text utilization should be strictly restricted to official, timestamped regulatory and exchange RSS feeds.

## 4. Leakage-Aware Prospective and Historical Ablation Protocol

To prove that any text-based feature genuinely improves net returns over the matched LQDT counterfactual, it must pass a strict ablation protocol.

### Evaluation Methodology

1. **Chronological Walk-Forward Splits:** Divide historical data into expanding training windows and out-of-sample testing periods. No random shuffling.
2. **Baseline Comparison:**
* *Baseline A:* Pure price-only technical features (moving averages, spread standard deviations).
* *Baseline B:* Deterministic event flags derived from dated formal disclosures (e.g., exact dividend record dates, CBR rate decision dates).


3. **Ablation Test:** Add text features (e.g., keyword indicators from CBR or MOEX notices) on top of Baselines A and B. The model must demonstrate statistically significant net RUB excess over the matched LQDT counterfactual *after* accounting for transaction costs, slippage, and execution delay.

### Operational Constraints & Rejection Criteria

* **Latency & Execution Window:** Signals must be parseable and actionable within the 5–15 minute median human reaction window (outer bound 90 minutes). If text parsing introduces delays that degrade execution prices beyond historical spread capture, the feature is rejected.
* **Quota & Compute Budget:** Processing must operate comfortably within free Gemini Flash API limits and the 1-vCPU / 1-GB RAM VPS background budget (allocating no more than 250–350 MB).
* **Rejection Thresholds:**
* Any text feature that relies on pretrained model general knowledge rather than explicit, timestamped input text snippets must be rejected.
* If the net return after fees fails to beat the simpler deterministic event flag baseline, text processing is dropped from the active strategy.
