# Title
Free Futures Data on MOEX for Medium‑Term Spread Strategies: Granularity, Gaps, and Practical Limits

## 1. Executive Answer: What Is the Smallest Practically Useful Free Granularity?

For MOEX futures, the smallest *systematically* available and legally straightforward free granularity for both history and monitoring is:

- **Historical research:**  
  - **Daily OHLCV candles (D1)** from MOEX ISS (`/iss/candles`) with 15‑minute delay, plus **end‑of‑day trading results and settlement prices** via ISS and public CSV/XML reports.  
  - **Some intraday candles (1‑ and 10‑minute, 1‑hour)** are available for part of the history, but coverage is incomplete by contract and has no formal completeness guarantees.

- **Ongoing monitoring for medium‑term spreads (holding days–weeks):**  
  - **Delayed best bid/offer and last price** (no depth) and **current day OHLCV** from ISS with ~15‑minute lag.  
  - For richer minute‑level monitoring and execution simulation, you need either a **broker API (BCS, Tinkoff, etc.) tied to your account** or your own recording from a terminal / datafeed.

Key implication: with purely free public data (ISS + open CSV/XML) you can do **coarse, research‑level backtests at daily resolution** and approximate medium‑term spreads, but **any serious point‑in‑time analysis of execution, intraday slippage, microstructure or margin/OI dynamics requires paid or self‑collected data.**

## 2. Landscape of Free Official Sources and Access Regime

**2.1. MOEX ISS (Informational & Statistical Server)**

MOEX’s ISS is the central free API for market data across engines (stock, futures, FX) and is explicitly described as a freemium service:

- Official programming interface docs state that real‑time data requires subscription, while delayed data (15 minutes) is free:  
  *“Access to real‑time data requires subscription, delayed data (15 min) is provided for free.”*[1][2].
- The same documentation explicitly restricts **orderbooks and historical data** to paid subscribers:  
  *“Orderbooks and historical data are only available to the paid subscribers.”*[3][4].
- ISS overall description:  
  *“Informational & Statistical Server (ISS) is the Web service that provides access to market data from all the Moscow Exchange markets over the Internet.”*[5].

The official Russian developer guide adds two important constraints:

- Market data via ISS *may* be provided free with delay or under subscription in real time[6].
- Free ISS information is **“for familiarization only” and not for commercial redistribution or derived products**[7]. This does not mechanically forbid using it to research your own trading, but it does limit redistribution, resale and some types of public derived data.

**2.2. MOEX Historical Data Service (paid) vs. public archives**

MOEX runs a distinct **Historical Data Service**:

- *“Moscow Exchange offers a full range of historical trading data used for optimizing Participants' trading algorithms, carrying out technical analysis, risk management and back‑testing.”*[8].
- Access is subscription‑based with limited access window:  
  *“The access period for downloading historical data from Moscow Exchange's archive database is one month from the date when access to the database begins.”*[9].

Parallel to that, there are **public EOD/aggregated archives** accessible without login:

- *“Archive the results of trades on the market. Trades: 1,924,892. Instruments: futures options. Download Data in CSV (delimiter – comma).”*[10].
- Another page on aggregated trading results lists downloadable archives for derivatives:  
  *“Derivatives (csv.7z, 447 Мб)… Shortened samples: Derivatives (csv.zip, 13 Мб)”*[11].

This creates an important nuance:

- **Free ISS data and some CSV archives**: delayed, coarse, and not guaranteed complete on intraday / microstructure level.
- **Paid Historical Data Service**: deep tick/order data, guaranteed completeness and point‑in‑time coverage, including full trades and orders logs[12].

**2.3. MOEX FTP**

MOEX operates ftp.moex.com hosting various reference and report files:

- A FORTS stats directory exists: *“/pub/info/stats/forts/ contains FORTS_LIST.TXT and FORTS_LIST_SPREAD.TXT (contract lists)”*[13].
- There are FORTS‑related report directories, but public paths with actual trades/quotes are not clearly documented:  
  *“Multiple FORTS‑related FTP directories found: /pub/FORTS/, /pub/Reports/FORTS/, /pub/FORTS/reports/, /pub/FORTS/volat_coeff/ … but unclear if historical price data is present.”*[14].  
  For example `/pub/FORTS/volat_coeff/` holds only historical volatility coefficients archives[15].

For your purposes, FTP adds **reference data (lists of contracts, series, some risk parameters)** but not free tick‑by‑tick or minute quotes.

**2.4. Rate limiting and operational aspects**

Third‑party ISS manuals recommend conservative rate limits:

- *“Не более 2 req/sec к MOEX ISS, корректный UA с контактами для связи.”*[16].

Combined with the ISS per‑request row limit (see below), this constrains how fast you can sweep large universes without tripping anti‑abuse measures.

## 3. What Exactly Can You Get for Free from ISS for Futures?

### 3.1. Available endpoints and key fields

Official ISS reference describes multiple futures‑related endpoints[17]. For your task the core ones are:

- `/iss/engines/futures/markets/forts/securities` – **instruments and specs**, including:
  - `LOTVOLUME`, `LOTSIZE`, `MINSTEP`, `DECIMALS`, `PREVSETTLEPRICE`, `INITIALMARGIN`, `LSTTRADE`, `LSTDELDATE`, `ASSETCODE` etc.[18][19].
- `/iss/candles` – **pre‑calculated candles**:  
  The Russian documentation (as surfaced via aiomoex) states:  
  *“MOEX ISS формирует свечки в формате HLOCV, при этом используются следующие условные числовые коды: 1 – 1 минута, 10 – 10 минут, 60 – 1 час, 24 – 1 день, 7 – 1 неделя, 31 – 1 месяц, 4 – 1 квартал.”*[20].
- `/iss/engines/[engine]/markets/[market]/history` – **daily trading results by security** (EOD; subject to historical‑data subscription for full depth according to docs[3][4]).

The **candles** endpoint returns, per official and community docs:

- Fields: `open`, `close`, `high`, `low`, `value`, `volume`, `begin`, `end`[21].
- `value` is widely used as turnover (RUB), with `value / volume` as VWAP in community examples[22].

For **quotes and best bid/ask**:

- Marketdata endpoints (e.g. `/iss/engines/futures/markets/forts/securities.json`) include `BID`, `BIDDEPTH`, `OFFER` and `OFFERDEPTH` fields in column metadata[23].
- In practice, direct orderbook endpoints like `/orderbook` for futures securities return an HTML “subscription required” page when called unauthenticated[24], confirming depth is not free.

For **margin, limits and risk parameters**:

- Security description includes `INITIALMARGIN`, `HIGHLIMIT`, `LOWLIMIT`, `PREVOPENPOSITION` and `OPENPOSITIONVALUE` among others[19][25].
- These are **current values only**; there is no free ISS endpoint documented for **historical margin schedules**.

### 3.2. Time resolution and interval codes (candles)

Per official ISS docs and aiomoex:

- Interval codes and corresponding TFs are:

  | interval | Meaning |
  |---------|---------|
  | 1       | 1 minute |
  | 10      | 10 minutes |
  | 60      | 1 hour |
  | 24      | 1 day |
  | 7       | 1 week |
  | 31      | 1 month |
  | 4       | 1 quarter |

  As summarized in aiomoex: *“1 – 1 минута, 10 – 10 минут, 60 – 1 час, 24 – 1 день, 7 – 1 неделя, 31 – 1 месяц, 4 – 1 квартал.”*[20].

Candles are **pre‑aggregated** by the exchange and cover “intervals for which candles are available” per `/candleborders` documentation[26].

### 3.3. Historical depth and pagination

ISS applies record limits and pagination:

- Official docs: *“The limit on the number of records per request is 1000.”*[27][28].  
  Community libraries note 100‑row paging defaults for many endpoints, requiring looped `start`/`limit` to fetch more[29].

Empirical and community evidence:

- *“By one request 1000 rows could be retrieved maximum, it is a 3 year of history (~ 760 rows) for a single equity.”*[28].  
  For futures, per‑contract listing periods are much shorter than for stocks, so 1000 rows can cover a full contract life at D1 resolution, but not entire multi‑year rolling series on 1‑minute candles.

MOEX provides **candle borders** endpoints to query available history intervals[26][30], but the practical pattern observed in community code and examples:

- Daily candles for liquid futures can extend back several years.
- 1‑minute and 10‑minute candles are usually limited (often 3–6 months) and can be missing entirely for delisted or illiquid contracts.
- Actual guarantees are **not stated** in public docs; historical depth is “best effort”.

For **historical `history` endpoint**:

- The developer guide designates `/iss/history` as historical data/trading results space[31].
- The same guide emphasizes: *“Orderbooks and historical data are only available to the paid subscribers.”*[3].  
  Yet, in practice, some `history` URLs for individual futures securities are widely used in examples and work without authentication[32].  
  Interpretation: **not all historical data is paywalled; but the officially supported “archive database” with full coverage and bulk files is subscription‑based.** The free `history` responses likely represent a limited slice (for example, recent days or reduced fields).

### 3.4. Delay and real‑time constraints

Several official texts converge on:

- *“It is possible to get delayed market data for free or subscribe for real-time. Orderbooks and historical data are only available to the paid subscribers.”*[1][3][4].
- *“Market data delayed by at least 15 minutes and results of trading will still be available for free on the Exchange's website.”*[33].
- ISS dev manual (Russian): *“данные (биржевая информация) могут предоставляться в режиме реального времени (по подписке) или с задержкой (без подписки)”*[6].

A manual timestamp comparison of ISS response `SYSTIME` and wall clock confirms ≈15–20 minute lag[34].

For your project this implies:

- Backtests using ISS historical candles and EOD results are unaffected by delay.
- Live monitoring and execution decisions based on ISS must tolerate **structural 15‑minute staleness**, which is acceptable for slow‑adjusting positional spread rules but not for intraday arbitrage.

### 3.5. What about expired contracts?

Official docs do not clearly state retention period for individual futures series in free ISS.

- Some community posts claim: *“Определение истекших фьючей скачать невозможно. ISS их не помнит”* (expired futures cannot be downloaded; ISS does not remember them)[35].
- Contradicting examples show `/iss/history/engines/futures/markets/forts/securities/SiU9.xml` and similar URLs being used for old contracts[32].

This suggests **heterogeneous retention**:

- Recent expired liquid contracts often remain available for some time.
- Deep history and thin instruments may disappear or become accessible only through paid archive products.

For a robust, multi‑year cross‑section of spreads, this is a structural risk: **you cannot assume that all expired legs are present in the free API**.

## 4. Non‑MOEX Free Sources: Brokers and Third Parties

### 4.1. Broker APIs (BCS, Tinkoff, etc.)

These are technically “free” in the sense of no per‑quote fee, but **conditioned on brokerage relationship and tokens**.

#### BCS Trade API

- Official description: a WebSocket/HTTP API that lets BCS customers build “профессиональную торговую инфраструктуру” (professional trading infrastructure), with access to market data (candles, quotes, trades, orderbook)[36].
- Authentication is mandatory: token is issued in client’s personal account and linked to brokerage account:  
  *“Откройте раздел «Профиль»… нажмите на брокерский счет, к которому вы хотите получить доступ с помощью API. Токен выпускается …”*[37].
- Market data endpoint for candles:  
  *“Максимальное количество баров в одном запросе — 1440.”*[38].  
  Official HTTP endpoint: `GET .../trade-api-market-data-connector/api/v1/candles-chart`[39].
- Supported intervals include minute/hour/day varieties (M1, M5, M15, M30, H1, H4, D, W, MN)[39].

Implications:

- For **intraday monitoring and 1‑minute backtests from the time you start recording**, BCS can be excellent — but:
  - Data is only legally accessible as a **BCS client**.
  - There is **no guarantee or documentation** that BCS exposes arbitrarily deep history – typical pattern is some months to a year per contract.
  - Usage terms may restrict systematic redistribution or public research output.

#### Tinkoff Invest API

- The current official API (investAPI) replaces the deprecated OpenAPI[40].
- MarketDataService provides historical candles:  
  *“GetCandles: Максимальное значение limit — 2400. CANDLE_INTERVAL_HOUR: От 1 часа до 3 месяцев. CANDLE_INTERVAL_DAY: От 1 дня до 6 лет.”*[41][42].
- Docs state it provides data for all instruments including futures; MarketDataService includes orderbooks (limited depth), trades and statuses[43].

For your use:

- Hourly candles with up to 3 months per request and daily with up to 6 years allow building **reasonably deep backtests at H1/D1 once you are a client**.
- Again, tokens and terms are tied to a **retail brokerage relationship**; these are not anonymous public feeds.

#### VTB

Search finds general “VTB API Platform” mostly for payment/banking integration, with **no clear futures market‑data API** exposed publicly[44]. For this project, VTB cannot be assumed to provide a usable free programmatic feed.

### 4.2. Third‑party “free” data (Finam, MT5 export, Stooq, etc.)

Evidence for non‑MOEX sources is mixed and generally weaker than for ISS:

- **Finam** historically offered free exports via web UI and libraries like `finam-export`[45][46], but smart‑lab posts report periodic breakage and instability of automated exports[47]. Coverage for modern MOEX futures is inconsistent and ToS may restrict systematic harvesting.
- **MT5 terminals** can export ticks for symbols supported by a given broker[48]; whether this covers the full MOEX futures universe with archival depth depends on the broker’s own data policies.
- **Stooq, Yahoo, Investing.com, Quandl**:
  - Stooq appears not to list MOEX futures like Si‑3.27; searches show other exchanges’ symbols instead[49][50].
  - Yahoo/Investing.com/Quandl focus on indices and equities; no evidence of complete MOEX futures coverage[51][52][53][54].

These alternatives are *at best* sources of partial or unofficial candles for a subset of instruments. For a systematic cross‑section of MOEX futures spreads with robust legal footing they are **insufficient as a primary source**.

## 6. Smallest Practically Useful Timeframe for Your Use Case

You target **medium‑term spread strategies on MOEX futures** with holding horizons of days to weeks, not HFT. Key decisions:

### 6.1. For research backtests

Given free data constraints, a sensible hierarchy is:

1. **Primary research timeframe: Daily (D1) candles and daily EOD fields.**

   - D1 candles from ISS (`/candles` with interval=24) are free delayed and relatively deep[20][28].
   - CSV/XML aggregated results provide official settlement prices, volumes and OI for many futures and options[10][11].
   - For spread strategies whose signal is based on slow‑moving relationships (term structure, cross‑asset spreads, basis vs. carry), daily data is **sufficient to establish statistical viability**, given your horizon and capital scale.

2. **Secondary refinement: Hourly candles (H1).**

   - H1 candles are available in ISS via `interval=60`[20], but with **shorter and irregular history**; you must query `/candleborders` for each contract to see what is actually present[26][30].
   - BCS/Tinkoff APIs provide hourly and finer candles with better controlled history windows (= client‑only, not pure public)[39][41][42].
   - For execution cost modelling (entering spreads within a day rather than at random daily close), H1 offers a good compromise between detail and data volume.

3. **Minute data (M1/M10) free usage is research‑only and shallow.**

   - ISS defines `interval=1` and `10`, but does not promise uniform coverage; many contracts will have short windows or gaps.
   - For a medium‑term project, using M1 only to **sanity‑check intraday volatility and volume patterns in recent history** is reasonable, but not as a backbone for long‑span backtests.

### 6.2. For live monitoring and manual execution

Considering your reaction latency (5–15 minutes typical; up to 90 minutes) and 15‑minute data delay:

- **ISS delayed quotes and candles** are aligned with your human timescale:
  - Price signals for multi‑day spreads will not be materially degraded by 15 minutes.
  - Execution: you will place orders in a separate trading terminal (VTB/BCS), seeing **real‑time** quotes there; ISS is then a monitoring / logging source, not a trading microstructure oracle.

- Adding a **broker API (BCS/Tinkoff)** later lets you:
  - Build **own minute‑level recorder** going forward.
  - Evaluate **intra‑day execution quality, slippage vs. mid‑price**, and refine entry timing.  
  This is distinct from historical reconstruction: you will have accurate data **only from the point when you start recording.**

Hence the **candidate “main research timeframe”** for now is:

- **Daily** for robust cross‑sectional model design and risk control logic.
- Optionally **hourly** from ISS/broker APIs where history exists, used conservatively.

## 7. What You Cannot Reliably Infer Without Paid or Self‑Collected Data

### 7.1. Execution quality, slippage and realistic fill modelling

Without historical bids/asks and Level‑2 orderbook you cannot:

- Reconstruct **true mid‑price path** at execution time; OHLCV only gives range and volume by bar.
- Model **limit order fill probability** conditioned on queue position and depth.
- Quantify **impact of your own order size vs. visible liquidity**.

ISS free data provides at best:

- Top‑of‑book at delayed timestamps (`BID`, `OFFER`, `BIDDEPTH`, `OFFERDEPTH`) in real time for subscribers; delayed for free users[23][1].
- No time‑stamped event stream of individual trades and orderbook updates without Historical Data Service or broker feeds.

Implication: backtests based solely on OHLCV must assume **simplified execution (e.g., fills at bar’s close or VWAP)**. For your moderate scale (10–20M RUB total, small per‑instrument clips in liquid futures), this may be tolerable, but it will **understate slippage and adverse selection**.

### 7.2. Point‑in‑time risk parameters and margin

You cannot reconstruct historical **margin requirement changes and risk parameters**:

- ISS exposes current `INITIALMARGIN`, `HIGHLIMIT`, `LOWLIMIT`, etc. for each contract[19][25].
- There is no public ISS or FTP endpoint documented for comprehensive **historical time series of those fields**; detailed OI and analytical statistics `/analyticalproducts/futoi` are explicitly subscription‑gated:  
  *“The data is available for viewing and downloading only after subscribing /analyticalproducts/futoi/securities/”*[26].

For spread strategies, missing margin history implies:

- Historical drawdowns in your backtest **do not account** for situations where margin jumped and would have forced a position reduction or margin call.
- Modelling **capital efficiency vs. LQDT baseline** over time becomes approximate – you can only use today’s margin multipliers as proxies.

### 7.3. Full survivorship‑bias‑free cross‑section

Using only free ISS endpoints:

- You can get **current and recent contract lists** from FORTS_LIST.TXT and securities endpoints[13][25].
- But for past years, **delisted, extremely illiquid or short‑lived contracts may be partially or fully missing.** Community comments that “ISS doesn’t remember expired futures”[35] may reflect such pruning.

Consequences:

- A naive universe constructed from “what is visible today” will **miss contracts that were tradable in historical windows but no longer exist**, biasing empirical distributions (e.g., underestimating tail risk).
- Even if contract codes are available from FTP lists, their **time series might be incomplete** in free ISS.

To achieve a clean survivorship‑free panel you need either:

- **Paid historical data** with formal guarantees of archive completeness, or
- Long‑term **own recording** started today (which does not fix the pre‑start history).

### 7.4. Microstructure‑level interval alignment between legs

Spreads between two futures legs need **synchronous pricing** for precise signals. With only OHLCV:

- Bar timestamps for each instrument may drift (e.g., missing bars, different volume patterns), especially for illiquid contracts.
- ISS returns candles per instrument, not multi‑asset aggregates; **stale quotes** or data glitches are hard to detect.

You cannot robustly:

- Perform **high‑frequency cointegration tests** or algo execution on sub‑hour timescales.
- Check for **lead‑lag relationships at the trade level**.

For medium‑term daily spreads, this is less critical, but it limits **refinement** of models at intraday granularity.

### 7.5. Robust stress testing on historical regime shifts

Historical archive services include structured data products such as:

- Type A: full trades and orders;  
- Other types: aggregated results, best ten levels, etc.[12].

Without access to these, you cannot:

- Replay stress periods (e.g., 2014, 2020, 2022 episodes) with **tick or orderbook granularity** to evaluate spread behaviour under liquidity shocks.
- Model **temporary dislocations** that last minutes but materially affect P&L even for day‑scale holds.

For your initial small capital this is not fatal, but for scaling to 10–20M RUB it becomes important.

## 8. Point‑in‑Time Data Sketch and Local Storage Sizing

### 8.1. What a point‑in‑time dataset would minimally contain

For each futures contract and date/time, to support credible medium‑term spread research you ideally want:

- **Prices:** OHLCV candles at D1 (mandatory) and H1/M10 (desirable for execution sensitivity).
- **Contract specs:** tick size, multiplier, margin, settlement type, last trading day, delivery date; all **versioned by date** (spec changes mid‑life are possible).
- **Risk parameters:** initial/variation margin levels over time; price limits (`HIGHLIMIT`, `LOWLIMIT`).
- **OI and volume:** daily and possibly intraday OI (per `/futoi` or similar products, paid[26]).
- **Calendar:** full MOEX derivatives trading calendar (trading/holiday days, session times) via dedicated calendar pages[55][56].

For a point‑in‑time architecture, you would maintain:

- A **contract dimension table** with version ranges (valid_from, valid_to).
- A **daily prices table** with contract, date, OHLCV, settlement price, OI.
- Optional **intraday table** with (contract, datetime, OHLCV).
- A **parameters change log** for margin, limits, etc. if you acquire such data via paid services.

### 8.2. Storage sizing (order‑of‑magnitude)

Assume:

- ~100 relevant futures contracts per year (major indices, FX, rates, commodities and their nearest 2–3 expiries).
- 252 trading days/year; 10‑year horizon.
- D1 candles: 100 * 252 * 10 ≈ 252k rows.
- With 64‑bit floats and integers plus overhead, ≈100 bytes/row → **~25 MB** for daily prices.

For intraday H1:

- Trading ~16 hours/day; 16 bars/day/contract.
- Rows: 100 * 16 * 252 * 10 ≈ 4.0M rows.
- At 100–150 bytes/row including indices → **0.4–0.6 GB**.

For intraday M10:

- 6 bars/hour * 16 h = 96 bars/day/contract.
- Rows: 100 * 96 * 252 * 10 ≈ 24.2M rows → **2–3 GB** at similar density.

For full M1:

- 60 bars/hour * 16 h = 960 bars/day/contract.
- Rows: 100 * 960 * 252 * 10 ≈ 242M rows → **20–30 GB**.

On your:

- **Laptop (16 GB RAM)**: storing D1+H1+meta (~1 GB) is trivial; even adding M10 remains easy.
- **VPS (1 vCPU, 1 GB RAM, 20 GB disk)**: practical upper limit is **D1 + a sliding window of intraday (~6–12 months)**; full multi‑year M1 across many contracts would saturate disk and RAM budgets.

Hence for phase 2:

- Target **D1 for full history** and, if desired, **H1 or M10 only for a rolling window** (e.g., last 1–2 years or only currently traded expiries).

## 9. Scenario Analysis: Data Strategy Paths

### Scenario A – “Purely free MOEX ISS + public CSV”

- **What you use:** ISS candles at D1 (plus partial intraday); ISS securities specs; public CSV/XML EOD results.
- **What you can do:**
  - Build **daily spread signals** and portfolio construction rules.
  - Approximate transaction costs with coarse heuristics (spread multiples, volatility).
  - Evaluate performance vs. LQDT baseline over several years, with daily P&L.
- **Main risks:**
  - Survivorship bias from missing contracts / incomplete history.
  - Underestimation of slippage and margin stress.
  - No reconstruction of point‑in‑time parameters (margin, limits).

Suitable as **Phase 1 research** under your small test capital.

### Scenario B – Add broker API recording (BCS or Tinkoff) from now on

- **What you add:** Real‑time or near‑real‑time candles, quotes and trades streamed to your own storage, starting today.
- **What you can do:**
  - Build accurate **intraday execution models** for the prospective period (post‑start).
  - Monitor **live spreads at minute‑scale** while still using ISS for historical D1.
- **Main risks:**
  - Historical backtests remain reliant on ISS OHLCV for the past; the richer data only applies going forward.
  - Engineering workload (feed handler, storage, monitoring) on constrained VPS.

This is a natural **Phase 2+** path as you approach larger capital deployment.

### Scenario C – Purchase MOEX Historical Data (or equivalent vendor)

- **What you add:** Full historical trades/orders, formal archive coverage, possibly historical risk parameters.
- **What you can do:**
  - Perform **production‑grade backtests** with realistic execution simulation.
  - Build point‑in‑time exposure, margin, and liquidity analyses across regimes.
- **Main risks:**
  - Direct monetary cost.
  - Need to handle large datasets (tens to hundreds of GB) with more robust infrastructure than current VPS.

This is the path to **industrial‑quality research**, but likely premature for a 30k RUB pilot.

## 10. Recommendations (Prioritized)

### 10.1. Immediate (Phase 1, free only)

1. **Standardize ISS D1 data as your core research dataset.**

   - Use `/iss/candles` with `interval=24` for each target futures contract; combine with aggregated CSV results where available.  
   - Query `/candleborders` (or equivalent) to log **first/last available dates** per contract and explicitly mark missing periods[26][30].
   - For each contract, also fetch securities metadata with `LOTVOLUME`, `MINSTEP`, `INITIALMARGIN`, `LASTTRADEDATE`, `LSTDELDATE`, `ASSETCODE`[18][19][25].

2. **Define an explicit futures universe with realistic survivorship notes.**

   - Build your universe per year using a combination of:
     - FORTS_LIST.TXT and FORTS_LIST_SPREAD.TXT from FTP (for “what existed”)[13].
     - ISS `securities` endpoint for current and recent listings[18][19][25].
   - Flag contracts whose D1 history cannot be fully pulled as **“incomplete”** and either drop or handle separately.

3. **Adopt D1 as the official backtest timeframe, with controlled intraday checks.**

   - Use **D1** for return, risk, and capital rules; only use **H1** or **M10** opportunistically where ISS provides enough depth to test whether intraday execution materially shifts performance.
   - Document that fills in backtests are approximated (e.g. at D1 close or midpoint of range) and that slippage vs. real fills is a key out‑of‑sample check.

4. **Respect ISS rate limits and usage terms.**

   - Throttle to ≤2 requests/sec and send a UA with contact info[16].
   - Avoid building a public service redistributing ISS data; keep usage for personal research/trading.

### 10.2. Short‑term (before auto‑trading, still mostly manual)

5. **Experiment with 1–10 minute candles for a small subset of contracts.**

   - For 5–10 key futures (e.g., RI, Si, BR, GD, key spreads), query `interval=1` and `10` and log how far history goes per instrument.  
   - If you consistently see, say, ≥6 months of M10 for core contracts, you can optionally:
     - Use M10 bars to refine entry timing in backtests for the **most recent period only**.
     - Compare realized reversion horizon (e.g., how often spreads revert intraday vs. over days).

6. **Design your data schema and storage with D1 full history + rolling intraday window.**

   - On the VPS, target:
     - **Full D1** history across your universe.
     - **Rolling H1 or M10** for last 1–2 years or only current front/next contracts.
   - Keep M1 experiments on the laptop if needed, not on the constrained VPS.

### 10.3. Medium‑term (if pilot succeeds; 10–20M RUB scale)

7. **Add a broker API recorder once you stabilize your ISS‑based research.**

   - Choose between BCS and Tinkoff depending on your planned operational account:
     - BCS: 1440 bars/request historical, M1–MN intervals[38][39].
     - Tinkoff: H1 up to 3 months and D1 up to 6 years per request; market‑wide coverage[41][42].
   - From day one of recorder:
     - Store at least **M1 or M5** candles and quotes for live‑traded instruments.
     - Tag all future trades with **concurrent best bid/offer** at decision and fill times (for ex‑post slippage analysis).

8. **Consider targeted paid data rather than full archive immediately.**

   - Instead of buying full Type A order logs, you might:
     - Subscribe only to **aggregated trading results** and **open interest analytics** relevant to your contracts (if MOEX or vendors offer such subsets).
     - Use them to augment ISS for margin/OI and stress scenarios without processing full tick feeds.

9. **Explicitly separate “research backtest” and “executable backtest” layers.**

   - **Research layer:** ISS D1 data, free candles, approximated execution; used to test many ideas cheaply.
   - **Executable layer:** limited set of candidate strategies re‑evaluated using broker‑recorded minute data (from the start date), with realistic slippage and order types.

   Clearly mark in your documentation which performance numbers come from which layer, and enforce that **production decisions** are tied to the executable layer wherever feasible.
