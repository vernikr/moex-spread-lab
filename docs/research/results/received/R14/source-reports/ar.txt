## 1. Executive answer

**One-sentence answer:** none of the sampled text streams is a plausible source of verifiable *return* value at this scale; the only testable use is structured extraction of official decisions and effective dates, benchmarked against a deterministic parser, and it should be rejected unless it changes a decision.

1. **No text source in scope passes a pre-test plausibility screen for return prediction at this pilot's scale.** The two structural facts are:
   - the official Russian streams that matter are either *post-hoc notifications of numeric changes already available in ISS* — measured median lag **0.5 min** after the stated event time (§6, M5) — or *scheduled releases* whose decision number is public at **13:30 MSK** and mirrored to Telegram **14 seconds later** (§6, M6);
   - at RUB 30,000 with integer contracts, the pilot trades **1–2 lots** (MXZ6 initial margin RUB 28,833.42 = 96.1% of the allocation; SiZ6 RUB 13,172.91), so a small text edge cannot be statistically distinguished from noise within the pilot (§2.3).
2. **The only defensible use of a small free Gemini allowance is structured extraction**, not opinion generation: turn official documents into an as-of table of decisions, effective dates, parameter names and old/new values, and benchmark it against a deterministic parser. Its success metric is extraction accuracy and decision-change rate, not profit prediction.
3. **The only feature family with a plausible (not demonstrated) path is pre-announced rule/parameter changes with effective dates** from NCC/MOEX documents, used as an *execution-risk* flag (margin, schedules, contract parameters). The only genuine lead-time stream in the sampled RSS feeds is the MOEX derivatives technical notice feed: a release effective **30 November 2026**, announced **22 October 2026** (cat=107), i.e. 39 days of lead time, but no price-relevant content.
4. **Historical channel testing is not defensible.** Public Telegram previews expose ~15 recent posts, no edit history and no prior text of edited messages; an export contains only the current version. ISS does expose a long archive (2013→2026), but its `modified_at` field cannot be used as an edit proxy: in a 300-item sample, 26.3% of records have `modified_at` *earlier* than `published_at`, 22.7% exactly three hours earlier (§6, M4).
5. **The pilot is underpowered for small effects.** A two-group comparison needs ≈32/(Δ/σ)² trades: ~126 trades for a 0.5σ effect (≈10–29 months at 1–3 trades/week) and ~502 for 0.25σ (§2.3). A correlation measured on many daily observations does not substitute for this.

**Default recommendation.** Do not add text to the trading pilot. Spend one bounded, zero-trading week on a *Gemini-versus-regex extraction benchmark* over CBR/MOEX/NCC documents. Reject the extension unless it changes at least one decision and passes every rejection criterion in §8.6.

## 2. Decision frame and arithmetic

### 2.1 What "value" has to mean here

The primary metric is **net RUB excess over the matched LQDT counterfactual with identical external flows**, after per-contract fee/spread/slippage under conservative/base/stress scenarios, with 3–90-minute signal-to-action delay. A text feature therefore has to change a *position decision* after costs; a correlation with future returns that never flips a position has zero value by construction.

**Illustrative hurdle.** At RUB 30,000 and a 14% money-market yield for 90 days: 30,000 × 0.14 × 90/365 = **RUB 1,035.62**. The 14% figure is the Bank of Russia key rate as of the 11 September 2026 decision ([CBR key-rate page](https://www.cbr.ru/press/keypr/), accessed 2026-10-22) [R]; the actual LQDT yield is not measured here and must be taken from the fund's own data. This hurdle is small relative to futures P&L noise; the binding constraint is signal-to-noise and statistical power, not the hurdle itself.

### 2.2 Integer-contract granularity

From the live ISS futures security records on 2026-10-22 [R]:

| Contract | Initial margin, RUB | Min step / step price | Lot | Previous settle | Margin ÷ RUB 30,000 |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MXZ6 (MIX‑12.26) | 28,833.42 | 25 / 25 | 1 | 246,425 | **96.1%** → 1 contract |
| SiZ6 (Si‑12.26) | 13,172.91 | 1 / 1 | 1,000 | 82,516 | **43.9%** → 2 contracts = 87.8% |

Sources: `https://iss.moex.com/iss/engines/futures/markets/forts/securities/MXZ6.json?iss.meta=off` and `.../SiZ6.json` [R]. A calendar spread may receive margin netting at the clearing house; the netted requirement and the broker's actual collateral rules were **not** verified and are flagged as an unknown. Even with netting, the position is quantised in single contracts: a text signal can at most flip one or two lots.

### 2.3 Power arithmetic

For a two-group comparison of per-trade P&L with equal variances, two-sided α = 0.05 and 80% power, total n ≈ 2·(1.96+0.84)²/(Δ/σ)² ≈ **32/(Δ/σ)²**. Pairing (same signal, with and without the text filter) can reduce the required n, but the reduction is unknown and cannot be assumed.

| Effect size Δ/σ per trade | Approx. total trades | Time at 1 trade/week | Time at 3 trades/week |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.0 | 32 | 7 months | 2.5 months |
| 0.5 | 126 | 29 months | 10 months |
| 0.25 | 502 | 9.7 years | 3.2 years |
| 0.10 | 3,140 | 60 years | 20 years |

Assumptions: i.i.d. normal trade outcomes, no regime clustering, no multiple testing. Clustering and regime dependence make this optimistic. **Implication:** only a large, decision-changing text effect is verifiable in a 90-day pilot; a genuine but small effect is indistinguishable from noise and should be rejected as *unverifiable*, not declared false.

### 2.4 What would make text worth testing anyway

Only two situations: (a) the text is the *only* way to learn an operational fact that affects execution (margin change, schedule change, contract parameter change), or (b) the text stream is genuinely pre-announced and slow-moving (consultation papers, rule drafts) so that the market has not already priced it. Neither is a return-prediction claim.

### 2.5 Why a spread is a harder text target than an outright

- A calendar spread's P&L is second-order in the common factor: news that moves the whole curve (a rate decision, a sanctions headline, a macro surprise) largely cancels between the legs.
- The remaining spread drivers are numeric and scheduled: carry, convenience yield, storage, financing, margin and roll dates. These are already in price and contract data, not in prose.
- A text feature would therefore have to predict a *relative* move — asset-specific supply/demand or a financing/margin change — and those are precisely the events that are published as numbers or effective dates (H2–H4), not as sentiment.

This is the main reason the ranking in §3–§4 puts operational extraction above language modelling.

**Scheduled decision time is explicit [R].** The key-rate page `https://www.cbr.ru/press/keypr/` (11 September 2026 release) states:
> "Время публикации пресс-релиза о решении Совета директоров Банка России и среднесрочного прогноза – **13:30 по московскому времени**."

with `<time datetime="2026-09-11T10:30:14+00:00">` = **13:30:14 MSK** [R]. The channel is linked from the CBR home page (`https://t.me/centralbank_russia`), and its title is "Банк России" [R].

**RSS.** `https://www.cbr.ru/rss/RssPress` returned **13 items** with `lastBuildDate Thu, 22 Oct 2026 04:51:00 +0300`; the description contains HTML and the link uses the legacy `?file=…` pattern [R]. This is a rolling feed, not an archive.

**Analytical bulletins.** "О чем говорят тренды" releases are dated 14.10.2026, 15.07.2026, 09.06.2026, 16.04.2026, 12.03.2026, 03.02.2026, 09.12.2025 and 14.10.2025 in the pages observed [S]. **A caution on page edits.** A guessed legacy deep link for the February 2022 decision (`https://www.cbr.ru/press/pr/?file=28022022_090000key.htm`) returned HTTP 200 but a generic "Пресс-релиз" page whose only visible stamp was `Последнее обновление страницы: 12.09.2025` [R]. The lesson is not that CBR's archive is broken — 2024 and 2026 `?file=…_133000…` pages resolve correctly [R] — but that **HTTP 200 plus a page shell is not evidence of content**, and a later page-update stamp does not prove what was visible historically.

**Implication.** The decision number is a scheduled, deterministic event at 13:30 MSK and is mirrored 14 seconds later. Any text model reading the release is racing a public number that is already in the price; the only potential incremental content is the guidance sentence, which occurs ~8 times a year.

**Quota architecture that follows.** A conservative design assumes **20 RPD**. The observed document flow is ~15–20 notices/day when all MOEX categories are included, which would sit at the limit. Therefore:

- use Gemini **only for rare, messy documents** (CBR releases, NCC methodology PDFs, MOEX notices whose tables a regex cannot parse), not for the high-frequency risk-corridor feed;

## 8. Leakage-aware protocol

### 8.1 Unit of analysis and timing

- **Unit:** one document (press release, notice, methodology version) with a verified `published_at`.
- **Decision time:** the first time the document could have been ingested by the project (publication time + poll interval), not the page's current "last update".
- **Outcome:** forward return of the target spread over horizons of 1 hour, 1 day and 5 days, net of the conservative/base/stress cost sets; and a binary "would the position change?" flag.
- **As-of bundle:** the document text as published, the prior version of the same document (if any), the decision calendar, and the price/margin state at the decision time. Nothing else.

### 8.2 Baselines (must be run first)

| ID | Baseline | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **B0** | Price-only technical reference | The existing spread signal without any text. |
| **B1** | Deterministic event flags | Dated formal events: CBR meeting dates, known release times, expiry dates, published parameter changes. |
| **B2** | Simple lexical features | Keyword counts, presence of a fixed phrase list, title length, document type; TF‑IDF + logistic/ridge if a learned baseline is needed. |

### 8.3 Treatments

| ID | Treatment | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | Gemini structured extraction | JSON schema: `doc_type, decision, effective_date, parameter, old_value, new_value, guidance_phrase_id, confidence`. No free-form prose; no web access; temperature 0. |
| **T2** | Deterministic parser for the same fields | Regex/HTML/table extraction; the benchmark T1 must beat. |
| **T3** | Gemini sentiment/opinion score | **Last**; only if T1 passes. Expected to fail; included so the log is complete. |

### 8.4 Historical ablation (extraction fidelity only)

Historical runs can test whether T1 reproduces fields that a human can also read, and whether T1 beats T2 on messy documents. They **cannot** establish predictive value, because:

- a 2013–2026 document processed by a 2026 model may be recalled from pretraining;
- a date-blind prompt does not remove the model's knowledge of the outcome;
- document versions have been edited since publication (M3, M4, M7);
- the current page may not be the as-published page.

Design:

1. Freeze a document inventory: CBR key-rate releases (all meetings, 2013–2026), MOEX cat=107/112/113/122 (24 months), NCC methodology versions (24 months).
2. Snapshot each document at retrieval, store raw bytes, SHA-256, URL, HTTP status, retrieval timestamp, and the verified publication time.
3. Hand-label a stratified sample of 100 documents (fields listed in T1).
4. Run T2 and T1 on identical text; compute field-level precision/recall/F1, exact-match on dates/values, and latency.
5. Run the T1 extraction a second time with entity/date scrambling; if the output changes materially, the model is using priors → flag as contaminated.
6. Only if T1 exceeds T2 on messy documents, proceed to a decision-impact test on B0/B1/B2 versus T1.

Splits: chronological 60/20/20 with an embargo of at least one trading day; purge overlapping labels; fit thresholds and dictionaries only in-fold; keep the final 20% untouched until the end; log every feature and threshold tried (deflated performance / trial count).

### 8.5 Prospective shadow test (the only clean test)

- **Duration:** 12 weeks **and** at least 3 CBR meetings.
- **Mode:** zero trading authority. Every document is processed as-of; every output is logged with the input hash and the model version.
- **Metrics:** extraction accuracy; share of documents where the text changes the model's *decision* relative to B0/B1/B2; latency from publication to log entry; quota failures; cost (zero cash, but engineering hours).
- **Gate:** if no decision changes in 12 weeks, stop. Do not run a trading backtest to "see if it works anyway".

### 8.6 Metrics and rejection criteria

**Primary (only at the paper-trading stage):** net RUB excess over the matched LQDT counterfactual, identical external flows, cost scenarios conservative/base/stress.

**Secondary:** incremental AUC/IC versus B1/B2 on identical folds; calibration; turnover; decision-change rate; extraction F1; latency; `429` rate.

**Rejection criteria (pre-registered):**

- **R1.** A deterministic parser reproduces ≥99% of target fields on 200 documents → reject Gemini for that task.
- **R2.** No trade decision changes in the prospective shadow → reject.
- **R3.** Incremental net RUB ≤ 0 after costs in the untouched holdout → reject.
- **R4.** Plausible effect <0.5σ per trade and the pilot cannot reach ~126 trades in 90 days → reject as unverifiable.
- **R5.** Any feature requires text that was not provably available before the decision time → reject.
- **R6.** The only evidence is a historical run with a model trained after the events → reject as contaminated.
- **R7.** The licence does not permit the use → stop for legal review.
- **R8.** Extraction accuracy <95% on a 100-field hand-labelled sample → reject.

### 8.7 Latency, quota and cost assessment

| Source | Publication time | Earliest ingest (poll) | Model time | Human reaction | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CBR key rate | 13:30:00 MSK | 13:30:00–13:35:00 (5-min poll) | < 5 s (assumed) | 3–90 min | Human and public availability |
| MOEX risk notice | e.g. 10:32:00 | 10:32:12 (RSS) | < 5 s | 3–90 min | Event has already happened |
| MOEX technical notice | 12:56:17 | 12:56–13:01 | < 5 s | Days (effective 30 Nov) | Calendar, not latency |
| NCC documents | Version date | On publication | Seconds | Days | Effective date |

**Cost.** Cash cost is zero on the free tier. The real costs are quota (unknown; the 20-RPD planning figure is a third-party placeholder, not a Google number), engineering time, and legal/licensing review. There is no paid upgrade in this design, and none is assumed.

### 8.8 Bias register

| Bias | Where it appears | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Promotional / conflict of interest | Broker channels (deposit at 14%; awards) | Exclude; never treat as independent |
| Editing / deletion | Telegram | No historical backtest from current text; snapshot prospectively |
| Survivorship | Channels that remain online | Pre-register the channel list before looking |
| Pretraining contamination | Gemini on 2013–2026 documents | Prospective shadow; entity-scramble test; dictionary comparison |
| Reverse causality | News written after price moves | Use only pre-announced/effective-date documents for causal claims |
| Timezone / clock | ISS `modified_at` 3-hour anomalies | Corroborate two channels; never use `modified_at` as event time |
| Duplicate / syndication | MOEX categories; CBR → Telegram | Deduplicate by ID; count each event once |
| Language / tokenisation | Russian morphology, decimal commas, NBSP | Hand-check a sample; do not use an English dictionary |
| Multiple testing | Many features, few trades | Log all trials; deflated metrics; untouched holdout |

## 11. Conflicts, unknowns, falsification criteria and project changes

### 11.1 Material conflicts

1. **MOEX is both the venue and the publisher.** Its notices are authoritative but not independent; its user agreement restricts profit-related and technological use. Free ISS access does not automatically equal permission for automated trading research.
2. **CBR is the regulator and the source.** Its releases mix facts (the decision) with guidance (a forecast/opinion). Treating the whole release as "fact" is a category error.
3. **Broker channels sell competing products.** The VTB channel advertises a 14% deposit while the pilot's counterfactual is a money-market fund; this is a direct conflict of interest for any "broker view" feature.
4. **Telegram is editable and not archived for edits.** Any historical claim based on current channel text is unverifiable.
5. **Gemini's free tier may be human-reviewed and is used to improve Google's products.** Uploading anything beyond public documents is inappropriate.

### 11.2 Unknowns (explicitly not filled with assumptions)

- The actual Gemini free-tier quota, model version, latency and determinism for this user/project.
- Whether the free tier is accessible from Russia (Russia is not in the available-regions list; Poland is).
- The MOEX ISS/market-data contract terms for profit-oriented automated use; the website agreement is not the ISS contract.
- The exact broker margin/netting rules at VTB for calendar spreads, and whether the core LQDT collateral is eligible.
- The actual LQDT yield and the true matched counterfactual.
- Whether NCC publishes a machine-readable event feed; none was observed.
- Whether any Telegram edit history can be obtained lawfully; none is exposed publicly.
- Whether a Russian finance-specific sentiment lexicon exists with a suitable licence.

### 11.3 Minimal inputs that would resolve the remaining unknowns

Only four items are needed, and none requires credentials or private data:

1. **The project's own AI Studio rate-limit view** (numbers for RPM/TPM/RPD), as a screenshot or copied text — replaces the 20-RPD placeholder and determines whether a daily batch is feasible.
2. **VTB's (and later BCS's) contract specification and margin/netting rules for the intended calendar spread** — resolves whether a two-leg spread consumes one or two initial-margin blocks and whether LQDT is eligible collateral.
3. **LQDT fund documents** (yield/NAV history, distribution policy) — fixes the exact matched counterfactual instead of the 14% illustration.
4. **A written statement of the MOEX ISS/market-data terms for personal, non-commercial research vs profit-oriented use** — resolves the licensing gate in §3 before any automated archive build.

### 11.4 Falsification criteria (what would change the conclusion)

- **Against "no text value":** a prospective shadow in which a text feature changes at least one decision per month, and a paper-trading holdout in which net RUB excess over the matched LQDT is positive after the stress cost set, with ≥126 trades or an effect ≥0.5σ.
- **Against "no channel archive":** a lawful, timestamped export with pre-edit snapshots and proof of first-observable time; without that, the claim stands.
- **Against "ISS is redundant":** evidence that a text notice precedes the corresponding ISS numeric change by a tradeable interval (≥3 minutes) on a representative sample. The measured median is 0.5 minutes in the opposite direction.
- **Against "Gemini adds nothing":** T1 beats T2 by a material margin on messy documents (≥100 hand-labelled fields, ≥95% accuracy), and the extracted fields change decisions.
- **Against "underpowered":** a larger pilot (RUB 10–20 million, ≥3 trades/week) or a longer history with a licensed archive that raises the effective sample to the required n.

### 11.5 Possible project changes

1. **Default:** drop text from the pilot; keep the RUB 30,000 test focused on price/event strategies and the LQDT counterfactual.
2. **Narrow alternative:** keep a **deterministic operational monitor** (MOEX cat=107/112/113/122 + NCC version pages) for execution safety — margin changes, schedules, settlements — with no Gemini and no trading authority.
3. **Research alternative:** if text is pursued, restrict to **one** hypothesis (H1 guidance tone) and test it only prospectively over 8 meetings, with a pre-registered small phrase list and T1 extraction as a benchmark. Accept in advance that the result will likely be inconclusive.
4. **Data-quality alternative:** reallocate the text effort to fixing timestamp handling (M3/M4) and building an as-of event calendar from official sources. This is likely to improve every later backtest more than any text feature.
5. **Scale gate:** revisit text only when the pilot can support ≥126 trades per test or when a licensed, timestamped archive is available.

## Appendix B — Reproduction notes

The measurements in §6 were produced with plain HTTP GETs and standard-library parsing; no third-party package is required except `pypdf` for the CBR working paper (used only to read its title page). The key reproducible steps:

1. RSS: fetch `https://www.moex.com/export/news.aspx?cat={c}&lang=ru`, parse `<item>` elements, read `<pubDate>`.
2. ISS: paginate `https://iss.moex.com/iss/sitenews.json?iss.meta=off&start={0,50,…}`; stop when the page is empty.
3. Edit statistics: for each row, parse `published_at` and `modified_at` as naive datetimes; count `modified != published`, the distribution of deltas, and negative deltas.
4. Notice lag: extract the first `DD.MM.YYYY, HH-MM` from the title or description; compare with `pubDate` converted to MSK.
5. Telegram: fetch `https://t.me/s/{channel}`; parse `data-post` and `<time datetime>`; page with `?before={first_id}`.
6. CBR: fetch the key-rate page and the release URL; verify the headline, not the status code.
7. All counts above are snapshots and will drift; re-run them before relying on the numbers.
