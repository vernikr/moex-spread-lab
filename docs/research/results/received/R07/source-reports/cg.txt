# 1. Executive decision

Answer to the question: the defaults are mostly internally coherent, but live feasibility for even one minimum-lot spread is unproven and must be treated as a no-trade state until the missing inputs are resolved. The critical failure mode is joint: spread P&L deteriorates while initial margin rises or offset relief disappears, then a leg is left unmatched or the trader cannot react quickly enough. These are different budgets and must be tested separately.

| **Decision item**                                                             | **Finding**                                                                                                                   | **Disposition**                                                                                 |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| RUB 30,000 pilot envelope                                                     | Can be a strict all-in sleeve budget; it is not an equity-loss limit or evidence that one lot fits.                           | Retain as a ceiling; require lot-level price, margin and liquidity pass. \[1–4\]                |
| RUB 5,000 warning / RUB 10,000 action                                         | Coherent as internal escalation states. A trigger is not a liquidation price, and D can breach while X is positive.           | Retain provisionally; block scale-ins at warning until review, block all new risk at action.    |
| RUB 15,000 outer LQDT shortfall boundary                                      | A policy breach condition, not a guaranteed maximum. Execution gaps, fees, variation margin and forced selling can overshoot. | Retain as a hard governance boundary; do not call it a loss cap. Stress post-trigger exit path. |
| RUB 15,000 lifetime gross extra funding; RUB 45,000 total gross contributions | Defines authority, not net risk capital, recourse or maximum loss. Emergency cash may arrive too late or be unavailable.      | Retain as a lifetime cumulative cap; entry must pass with zero rescue cash.                     |
| 50% starting-margin ceiling / RUB 5,000 stress buffer                         | Useful hypotheses, unsupported as universal rules. Starting margin ignores margin expansion, VM and partial-fill loss.        | Test, do not approve as sufficient. Compare 25%/35%/50% margin-use sensitivity and joint paths. |
| 3–90 minute manual reaction                                                   | Too wide to assume a stop can be executed at the observed signal price. The tail distribution is unknown.                     | Model 3, 5–15, 30, 60 and 90 minute reaction/exit paths plus missed/partial fills.              |
| Future RUB 10–20m scale                                                       | A small pilot cannot establish capital capacity, market impact or stability at scale.                                         | Separate promotion gate; require capacity and stress evidence at proposed size.                 |

## Three conditions that dominate the decision

1.  A feasible trade must pass both the relative economic-loss test and the cash/margin-path test under the same joint scenario. Passing only one is not enough.

2.  When X reaches −RUB 10,000, the remaining loss to get flat plus a positive uncertainty buffer must fit within the remaining RUB 5,000 outer-boundary distance (or less if X is already below −10,000). The adequacy of that distance is unknown.

3.  If actual broker/account terms allow pilot-related exposure to consume the core LQDT holding or other account assets without a fresh user decision, the policy cannot guarantee containment. Treat an unapproved spillover as an incident, not as available liquidity.

MOEX describes futures collateral as portfolio margining: risks may net where known price relationships exist. NCC describes margin benefits for eligible inter-contract/inter-month spreads, and publishes lists of eligible groups. That is a modelled collateral offset, not an assurance that the spread remains hedged in all execution states. \[1, 3\]

# 4. What public rules establish—and do not establish

## 4.1 MOEX / NCC

- The current MOEX futures GO page had a dated snapshot labelled 20 October 2026. Actual rates are instrument-specific and change; no value has been imported into this report because R06 supplied no specific contract family and broker mode. The currently published list is the proper starting point when the universe is known. \[2\]

- NCC says dynamic parameters (including settlement prices, theoretical option prices and other risk parameters) are defined at each clearing, including interim clearing, and also during the calculation period when price bands or risk-assessment ranges change. The static-parameters page is therefore not proof that current-day requirements will be unchanged. \[3\]

- NCC states that listed inter-contract and inter-month spread groups receive GO benefits. The list matters: correlation observed by the researcher alone does not create exchange margin relief. \[3\]

- MOEX’s current derivatives FAQ states that GO is based on historical and stress volatility, may increase when volatility rises, and that futures losses may exceed initial GO. It also says MOEX/ISS data may be real-time with the relevant subscription or delayed without it. Therefore, a free feed is not assumed to be live enough for the strategy: R04 must verify timestamps, delay and interval. The exact size of a margin scenario must come from instrument-specific data and account/broker implementation, not an invented blanket multiplier. \[4\]

## 4.2 VTB / BCS and the meaning of KPUR

- The public VTB KDS page states broker-specific KDS threshold KDS-X=0 and target KDS-Y=0.2 for its published futures-account sufficiency framework (these are VTB symbols, unrelated to the project economic X=V−B): when the minimum KDS threshold is reached, the bank closes positions to the sufficient value KDS-Y. The page also describes reserving exchange/bank guarantees and opening/closing commissions. The page did not expose a publication/effective date in the result reviewed; the figures must be reconfirmed against the live client interface and current contract terms before use. \[5\]

- The Bank of Russia’s Instruction No. 6681-U entered into force on 1 April 2025. NCC says the regime brings futures into client risk categorisation, and NCC has a service for transmission of customer risk categories and corresponding risk rates. “Qualified investor” status and a broker-recorded risk category such as КПУР are different facts; neither should be inferred from the other. Confirm the category actually applied to the specific VTB/BCS account and the associated rates. \[6\]

- The NCC Clearing Rules approved 26 February 2026 state that on the futures market a clearing-member margin requirement arises when free collateral SZSC is negative, must be met within the time set by the applicable temporary regulations, and non-compliance may lead to settlement-regime/asset-transfer/closing actions. These are clearing-member rules, not the retail customer’s full contract; they reinforce that the user cannot assume a discretionary 90-minute rescue window. \[7\]

- BCS’s accessible Futures Service Agreement was approved by order dated 17 July 2023 (document marked 1 August 2023). Its section 7 permits closing positions without client instruction in specified cases including a negative NПР2. It is useful evidence that broker action can supersede the user’s plan, but this dated copy does not prove the exact conditions currently applicable to the user or a later BCS account. Obtain the current effective appendix and linked agreement. \[8\]

**Implication:** a risk monitor that only checks X/D or “initial GO \< RUB 15k” is incomplete. It must read actual broker indicators and model the earliest possible broker action. If the exact account terms/indicator behaviour cannot be obtained, do not live-trade on the assumption that the emergency policy controls the account.

# 5. Entry and continuation inequalities

Define the stressed horizon from a reliable current mark through the first feasible fully-flat state. It must include the assumed 3–90 minute reaction, time to submit/confirm orders, any clearing/market closure exposure, rejected/partial fills, price gaps, spread dislocation and exit fees/slippage. Only include costs once: if the marked X already contains a given fee or close-cost estimate, do not subtract it again.

Relative-survival test: X_now − L_X,stress(n, scenario) − U_X(n, scenario) ≥ −RUB 15,000

L_X,stress is the adverse change in X from the current synchronized mark to fully flat, after incremental trading/funding costs not already in X. U_X is a strictly positive uncertainty buffer for residual modelling, timestamp, fill and shock uncertainty. Do not set the buffer to zero because a backtest lacks tail data.

Action-trigger test: L_exit,stress + U_remaining ≤ X_at_action + RUB 15,000

At the X=−RUB 10,000 trigger, the right-hand side is RUB 5,000. If X has already reached −RUB 11,000, only RUB 4,000 remains; at −RUB 14,000, only RUB 1,000 remains. Therefore the familiar “RUB 5,000 gap” is a path-dependent budget, not a proven safety margin. If exit stress plus uncertainty exceeds it, either earlier/tighter entry and de-risking conditions are needed or the strategy is infeasible at that size.

Cash/margin test at every stress timestamp t: E_stress(t) ≥ M_stress(t) + R_oper(t)

E_stress is eligible account equity/cash after the scenario’s adverse VM and costs, excluding core assets unless their use is expressly authorized and controlled. M_stress is the broker-relevant GO / margin requirement in that same state (including house add-ons and the single-leg state). R_oper is remaining free liquidity for further VM/fees and operational uncertainty. Equivalently, the test must ensure the RUB 30,000 sleeve can cover stressed blocked GO, paid/adverse VM, fees and reserve concurrently. Avoid adding current GO and the entire stressed GO together: the blocked margin is a resource requirement, not itself a P&L loss.

Integer sizing: n\* = max n ∈ {0,1,2,…} for which every relative, cash, margin, gross-exposure and leg-risk test passes

Use \`n=0\` as a valid answer. For a two-leg ratio a:b, the execution plan must preserve the required whole-lot ratio; the temporary states between individual fills must each pass the single-leg test. A fractional solution of 0.4 lot is not rounded up to one lot. The maximum size is the minimum allowed by all constraints, then rounded down to the feasible whole-lot ratio.

Tax overlay (deferred, not zero): for each taxpayer/account scenario use X_after_tax = X_before_tax − (tax on V − tax on B), with the same genuine external flows on both paths. Exclude only the loss of the LQDT long-term holding deduction, as specified in the brief; other tax/account treatment remains unknown. Keep the report’s numeric defaults pre-tax, then present tax sensitivities separately once R05 and investor-specific inputs exist.

## Ledger E — unhedged fill state can dominate the nominal spread

## 7.1 Per-idea and portfolio caps

- Per idea: calculate the full-hedged spread P&L stress; standalone leg P&L stress; marginal and portfolio stressed GO; maximum cash need during the response/exit path; exit costs; and gross/delta-adjusted exposure. Use whichever is most restrictive.

- Portfolio: test every concurrently open spread under common market shocks and pair-specific basis dislocations. No diversification credit just because past correlations are low. Use CCP offsets only if eligible, then run the offset-removal case. For common scenarios, aggregate the resulting instrument-level P&L and cash obligations at the same time steps.

- Concentration: cap aggregate risk by shared underlying/factor, maturity, broker risk category and liquidity regime; a set of “different” pairs linked to the same underlying is one joint exposure for stress purposes.

- Rounding: compute the largest feasible integer contract package after applying leg ratios. Never round up; if the first lot violates a cap or would require emergency funding at entry, use zero.

- Scale-in: each new tranche must pass the whole portfolio checks against the \*remaining\* budget and all prior realized/embedded losses. Profit, returned cash, emergency funding and withdrawals do not reset X, H or the gross-funding allowance.

## 8.1 Partial-fill and stop policy

4.  Use a spread order if the actual platform supports it with acceptable mechanics; test whether it is truly a single spread execution or still creates leg risk. Do not assume an atomic hedge without evidence.

5.  If orders can execute as separate legs, predefine which leg may be entered first, max time unhedged, ratio repair/rejection handling and one-leg stop/de-risk actions. The allowed time is the tested operational limit, not the 90-minute maximum the person may sometimes need.

6.  A stop instruction is an action rule, not a guarantee of a fill or price. Model market gaps, price-band interactions, circuit/market closure, rejected/partial fills, wide bid-ask and failed cancellation. Do not represent stop-losses as loss caps.

7.  Record actual signal time, data timestamp/age, order send/ack/fill/cancel timestamps, each leg’s price/quantity, fees, broker indicator, GO and VM before/after; count every eligible signal and every unfilled/rejected one in the virtual portfolio.

8.  Emergency cash is a de-risking contingency, never an entry prerequisite. If time-to-credit and approval are not guaranteed by evidence, model it as unavailable in the base stress path. If core resources are required to pass the test, mark no-trade under the current delegated policy.

# 9. Exact 90-day review, high-water giveback and promotion

Planning convention: define T0 as the timestamp at which the pilot is formally declared started. The formal review is due at T0 + 90 calendar days (not 90 trading days), using a stated time zone and a saved snapshot of X, H, D, realized/unrealized P&L, all cash flows, commissions, GO/VM, margin calls, actual/virtual fill discrepancies, funding used and incidents. The review does not force every position to expire on day 90; each position still needs its ordinary expiry/delivery and risk management, and every continuation must pass current and stressed constraints.

No reset: at day 90 and every other review, carry forward the original X/H history, funding already consumed and any policy breaches. If records or reliable marks are missing, the portfolio is not demonstrably compliant; no added exposure until reconciled. The time review itself does not erase losses or transform an open loss into a new pilot.

Peak giveback opportunity cost: D≥RUB 10,000 can cause de-risking when X\>0. This can prevent the strategy from giving back more excess profit, but creates exits/re-entries, fees, slippage and missed-recovery costs. Pre-register a comparison of the delegated D-trigger rule against alternative drawdown/action thresholds, using matched LQDT flows, all eligible signals, and identical execution assumptions. Report trigger frequency, opportunity missed, after-cost X distribution, worst post-trigger overshoot and funding path. A profitable small sample is not evidence that a threshold controls tails.

## 9.1 Stable promotion gates for RUB 10–20 million

- Evidence and reproducibility: specified universe/parameters before evaluation, frozen data lineage, point-in-time availability, realistic fills, all eligible signals, leakage audit and multiple-testing correction under R09.

- Execution stability: sampled or replayed 3–90 minute latency distribution; partial/rejected fill and single-leg state counts; stressed close cost and liquidity by contract and time of day, including weekends/holidays only where that contract actually trades.

- Economics: positive after-cost X versus matched LQDT with external flows matched; tax sensitivity under R05; stable by regime and not concentrated in one event/pair. Report uncertainty; no pilot profit target at the cost of validating the defaults.

- Capacity: recalculate the whole strategy at RUB 10m and RUB 20m order sizes, not linear extrapolation from tiny lots. Examine displayed depth, fill ratio, market impact, order-book replenishment, simultaneous orders, and correlated liquidation. Existing CCP margin relief does not prove market depth.

- Controls: current broker/client category and margin settings verified; actual usable funds/recourse known; alert and execution process tested; user availability and intervention quota specified; no single stressed path requires unapproved core use. Fail any gate → do not promote.

# 11. No-trade criteria, falsification and project changes

## 11.1 No-trade criteria

- No feasible whole-lot package passes the relative-survival and cash/margin tests simultaneously under the same joint path.

- Actual contract multiplier/tick value/settlement/expiry, current broker GO or broker-specific category treatment is unknown, stale or not independently reproducible.

- The only way to avoid a margin shortfall or orderly exit deficit is an unapproved core-collateral transfer, hoped-for rescue funding, asset sale without permission, or assumed access to funds inside the 3–90 minute window.

- Spread offset is required for viability but the one-leg fill state, margin-reset state or offset-removal scenario fails.

- After-cost fees and likely spread/exit cost are not available; or free data is delayed/irregular at a level that invalidates the intended signal execution window.

- The virtual portfolio selectively skips eligible signals, omits partial fills, assumes guaranteed stop fills, looks ahead to settlement/revision data, or cannot reproduce the matched LQDT counterfactual.

- Account-wide recourse is not bounded/documented, actual KPUR/risk category is unverified, or the broker’s early action conditions can occur before the operational response path.

- At action trigger, stressed remaining loss to flat plus positive uncertainty exceeds X+RUB 15,000, or current risk violates the D action state.

- No stable free-cash path exists across price shock, VM, increased GO, exit costs and uncertainty—even if current initial GO is low.

## 11.2 Falsification criteria

- Any admissible minimum-lot stress path that breaches the outer X boundary after the prescribed reaction/exit disproves that the candidate size is protected by the proposed rule; it does not justify widening the boundary after the fact.

- Any sample where broker collateral rules/house add-ons materially differ from the model, or forced liquidation occurs sooner than the simulated action schedule, falsifies the assumed broker-response model until revalidated.

- Any unpaired position lasting longer or losing more than the validated leg limit falsifies the partial-fill control and forces rework or no-trade.

- A positive pilot result that disappears under matched LQDT flows, all fees, conservative fills or multiple-testing controls does not pass an economics gate; a small profitable pilot alone does not establish tail safety or capacity.

- Frequent alert non-response, stale data, capital constraints, or omitted signals in the manual portfolio falsify execution feasibility even if the theoretical spread is profitable.

## 11.3 Possible project changes if gates fail

Options to test—not decisions taken here: reduce to a smaller / different eligible pair only if a mini contract materially reduces per-lot stressed risk; use a truly executable spread order instead of independently legged orders if verified; narrow the universe to contracts with more depth and stable timing; increase the starting sleeve only through a new explicit authorization and only if the relative-loss policy also changes appropriately; select a ring-fenced account/sleeve arrangement if feasible under actual broker terms; or stop the pilot. A separate account may reduce operational spillover but is not automatically a legal or collateral guarantee. The present shared-account choice remains conditional on proof that core use is controlled.

# 12. Targeted library lead: Robert Carver

Robert Carver, \*Systematic Trading: A unique new method for designing trading and investing systems\*, Harriman House, published 14 September 2015, 325 pages, ISBN 9780857194459. The publisher’s table of contents identifies Chapter 9 “Volatility targeting,” Chapter 10 “Position sizing,” Chapter 11 “Portfolios,” and Chapter 12 “Speed and Size.” \[9\]

Applicable idea for R07: position size should connect forecast/strategy risk, instrument volatility, portfolio weights/diversification and trading cost; overall size needs to reflect the cost of trading and capacity. The first implementation question is “can the desired risk be expressed in the instrument’s executable unit?” not “what percentage of the equity would a continuous optimizer allocate?”

Small-lot limitation for this project: continuous portfolio weights can be fractional while exchange futures are integer contracts and a spread can have a fixed leg ratio. Rounding risk is discontinuous: a target allocation below one lot means the feasible size is zero, not one. Portfolio diversification assumptions must also be haircut in joint stress and overridden by any exchange/broker collateral rules. Forecast probabilities and covariance estimates are not enough to budget a rare single-leg failure or cash call.

# 13. Final answer and next evidence package

The provisional policy can be used as an initial governance skeleton, but it cannot yet be called feasible. Three revisions are warranted now: (1) define the remaining-loss-to-flat inequality relative to the RUB 15,000 boundary at every action point; (2) make joint cash/margin and temporary single-leg tests pass separately from X; and (3) treat emergency funding as unavailable unless explicitly approved and its arrival time is supportable. The 50% initial-GO ceiling and RUB 5,000 stress buffer remain sensitivity hypotheses, not sufficient safeguards.

The decisive next evidence is not a wider generic risk survey. It is the smallest package needed to resolve feasibility for a specific candidate: R06 pair/specification rows, exact current VTB rules/account category/collateral treatment (R01), full round-trip costs (R02), usable data timestamps/history (R04), and realistic fill/latency assumptions (R08), tested under R09 and measured against R05’s matched LQDT path. Until those inputs establish at least one integer package that passes every constraint without emergency money, no-trade is the supported result.
