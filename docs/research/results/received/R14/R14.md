## 1. Scope, inputs, and evidence status

### 1.1 Question being answered

The original prompt asks which text sources or features, if any, could add **verifiable** value beyond (1) price-only technical references and (2) simple deterministic event flags from dated formal disclosures, with (3) simple lexical features as the next comparator. The context is a private, medium-term MOEX futures-spread project, manual initial execution, a small RUB 30,000 pilot, and a limited free Gemini Flash allowance. Formal corporate and financial events—such as dividends and corporate actions—are a separate factual input stream, not the optional text subject of this investigation.

The relevant question is not whether an official text is authoritative, interesting, or correlated with a future price. It is whether a specific, lawfully reusable, historically observable text feature changes an eligible spread decision and improves **net RUB excess over a matched LQDT counterfactual**, after realistic manual delay, costs, integer-contract sizing, and finite liquidity.

### 1.2 What the supplied materials establish—and do not establish

Across the reports, the authors describe bounded public-source checks on MOEX, the Bank of Russia (CBR), NCC, Minfin, Rosstat, Google/Gemini documentation, and selected public channel pages. Some cited items were direct page/API reads; others were search snippets or bibliographic leads. The reports do not share a uniform evidence-grade convention, so this synthesis uses the following grades. Some retrieval attempts are reported as blocked (including e-disclosure/BCS, a Rosstat release page, and one CBR announcement); no authentication, paywall, or bot-protection bypass is assumed or proposed here.

- **Reportedly observed:** a report says it fetched/read the primary page or API response. This is still not an independent recheck by this merger.
- **Snippet/secondary:** the report says a claim came from a search result, secondary source, or bibliographic listing; it is not elevated to a fully verified fact here.
- **Unknown/proposed:** an account-level check, legal review, historical replay, export, API call, benchmark, or test was proposed but not performed.
- **Failed/blocked:** the report records a retrieval failure; no bypass or authentication workaround is assumed.

**No report supplies measured trading value.** The reports state that no backtest, live trade, paper trade, account/broker check, Gemini API call or quota measurement was performed. No transcript/channel export, private repository, broker statement, or personal account material was supplied. Some reports inspected primary-source pages; no source library or book was read in full. Those limits remain in force in this merged document.

## 2. Consolidated decision and executive findings

### 2.1 Decision

**Do not add a text-model feature to the trading pilot now.** The defensible near-term options are narrower:

1. **Operational control, not alpha:** if reuse rights are confirmed, maintain a small deterministic monitor for official MOEX/NCC changes to contract terms, risk parameters, trading schedules, suspensions, settlement mechanics, and production-system status. This may help prevent an execution or eligibility mistake; the reports do not demonstrate a return-predictive edge or quantify risk reduction.
2. **One conditional semantic hypothesis:** a tightly defined change in CBR forward-guidance wording or discussion of policy alternatives could contain information about the medium-term rate path for an instrument whose spread is explicitly mapped to rates/carry. It is a hypothesis only. The numeric decision, published forecast, price response, and a small phrase list are the mandatory baselines; first-observed time and historical version are not established for old documents. The number of independent meetings is small.
3. **Gemini only as an optional extractor:** use it, if legally and regionally available, to extract structured fields from an occasional ambiguous official document or table. First compare it with a deterministic parser and human labels. Do not ask it for a trade opinion, price target, or free-form “market impact.”
4. **No broad social/news ingestion:** do not build a whole-channel Telegram, YouTube, broker-commentary, analyst, or general-news corpus. Such streams add provenance, rights, edit-history, latency, and incentive problems while mostly duplicating official releases or prices.

This reconciles the reports’ different recommendations: a “conditional extraction pilot” is acceptable only as a **zero-trading data-quality/shadow exercise** after rights and access gates pass; it is not a conditional go for a live or predictive text strategy. If those gates fail, the fallback is price data, dated event flags, and, at most, local deterministic lexical rules.

### 2.2 What might plausibly matter

- **MOEX/NCC:** directly relevant to the mechanics and operational risk of a futures spread. The useful content is usually a fact, parameter, effective time, or production/test distinction that can be encoded as an event field. This is primarily an eligibility/risk-control use.
- **CBR:** the only repeated report-level semantic hypothesis with an explicit macro mechanism: guidance, risk balance, conditionality, and alternatives discussed may affect expected future rates and curve/carry. Even here, most information is already in the actual decision, forecast table, event calendar, simple phrase features, or market prices.
- **Minfin/Rosstat and foreign macro sources:** mostly numbers, calendars, or estimates. They belong in a structured event/data baseline, if linked to the selected contract, rather than an LLM-sentiment layer.
- **Issuer-specific text:** potentially useful only after the candidate contract universe and its economic exposure are named. No broad issuer export is currently justified.

### 2.3 What is not supported

There is no measured evidence that text improves futures-spread returns, exceeds LQDT, beats any baseline, or can be used at the user’s actual broker/account. A cited or suggested result from another market or task—such as recession classification, inflation-expectation correlation, generic sentiment research, or an external sanctions example—does not establish MOEX futures-spread P&L. In particular, an `cr.txt` claim of an ECB news-sentiment ROC AUC of 0.93 is not used as evidence: the report does not provide a sufficiently identifiable citation, and recession classification is not the project’s outcome metric.

## 3. Project decision frame, baselines, and economic scale

### 3.1 Meaning of “value” and fixed comparators

All arms must use identical eligible opportunities, contract pairs, timing rules, capital, and cost assumptions. The required comparison order is:

| Arm | Comparator |
|---|---|
| **A — price-only** | Existing spread/price technical reference; no text. |
| **B — deterministic events** | A plus simple dated formal-event flags available at the decision time. Formal corporate/financial events remain their own stream. |
| **C — simple lexical** | B plus fixed, auditable keyword/phrase/numeric features. Russian vocabulary, negation, inflections, and decimal formatting must be tested; an English dictionary cannot simply be transplanted. Any TF–IDF/PCA or learned lexical model is an additional preregistered arm, not a substitute for the prompt’s simple lexical baseline. |
| **D — constrained model extraction** | C plus only structured, source-grounded Gemini fields. |
| **E — falsification/placebo checks** | Delayed, shuffled, anonymized, or post-edit text tests, preregistered and used to detect timing/model contamination. |

The primary endpoint is net RUB excess versus the **matched LQDT counterfactual with identical true external flows**. The counterfactual keeps the pilot-diverted core LQDT invested; eligible collateral return is counted once, not deducted twice. The only tax effect stipulated for exclusion is loss of the LQDT long-term holding deduction; other tax residence/account treatment is unknown. Accuracy, AUC, sentiment direction, or a correlation with returns is not a substitute for incremental net RUB value.

### 3.2 Capital, execution, and risk constraints that the study must preserve

These are supplied project assumptions, not findings from the source reports or broker-approved permissions:

- Initial pilot authority: **RUB 30,000 all-in**, including margin, variation-margin liquidity, and reserves, on the same account as a core LQDT holding exceeding RUB 40 million. The account is not ring-fenced; internal budgets do not prove collateral eligibility. Future candidate scale is RUB 10–20 million, not tested here.
- Manual reaction is **3–90 minutes**, with a reported typical median around **5–15 minutes**; the latency distribution is unknown. Monitoring 24/7 does not mean every futures contract trades 24/7.
- A single realistic virtual portfolio must attempt every eligible signal without discretionary omission, while respecting finite capital and recording stale, rejected, unfilled, and partially filled orders. Human intervention capacity and emergency response coverage still need definition.
- Provisional project controls: RUB 5,000 excess-curve-drawdown warning; RUB 10,000 action trigger (drawdown from excess-result high-water mark or cumulative LQDT shortfall); RUB 15,000 outer shortfall boundary; formal review at 90 calendar days without resetting losses. These are unvalidated action rules, not guaranteed loss bounds.
- Emergency funding is capped at RUB 15,000 cumulative **gross** for the whole pilot, making total gross contributed funding at most RUB 45,000. Any use requires explicit approval and can only avert disorderly liquidation/de-risk existing positions—never fund new exposure or averaging down. At the RUB 10,000 cumulative-shortfall trigger, it may only take the portfolio to flat. Entry must pass without emergency funding. Broker use of core collateral counts toward the gross allowance and is an incident if unapproved; sale proceeds are not automatically economic loss in the project’s X measure.
- Fees, spread crossing, slippage, quote depth, failed fills, financing, and 3–90-minute signal-to-action delays must be parameterized in conservative/base/stress scenarios. The signal cannot assume that a stale quote or an unfilled order was executable.

A practical accounting expression is:

> **Excess result** = test-portfolio marked net liquidation value, adjusted for true external withdrawals and deposits, **minus** the matched LQDT counterfactual on those same flows.

The comparison must not treat the core LQDT collateral as silently available test capital or double-count its retained return.

### 3.3 Scale and statistical power

One report’s 22 October 2026 MOEX futures snapshot gives the following **illustrative** initial-margin figures, not stable requirements or permission to trade:

| Contract in the cited snapshot | Initial margin | Share of RUB 30,000 | Implication in that snapshot |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| MXZ6 (MIX-12.26) | RUB 28,833.42 | 96.1% | One lot consumes almost the entire nominal pilot allocation. |
| SiZ6 (Si-12.26) | RUB 13,172.91 | 43.9% | Two lots represent 87.8% of the allocation before reserves. |

Source records cited by the report: [MXZ6 ISS record](https://iss.moex.com/iss/engines/futures/markets/forts/securities/MXZ6.json?iss.meta=off) and [SiZ6 ISS record](https://iss.moex.com/iss/engines/futures/markets/forts/securities/SiZ6.json?iss.meta=off). Margin can change; broker/clearing spread netting and the eligibility of the core LQDT collateral were not checked. Integer sizing therefore sharply limits the number of independent position changes.

A simple two-group power calculation in `ar.txt` assumes two-sided α=0.05, 80% power, equal variances, and independent, approximately normal trade outcomes. It gives total sample size about `32 / (Δ/σ)^2`:

| Per-trade effect | Approximate total trade outcomes | Approx. duration at 1–3 trades/week |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.0σ | 32 | 2.5–7 months |
| 0.5σ | 126 | 10–29 months |
| 0.25σ | 502 | 3.2–9.7 years |
| 0.10σ | 3,140 | 20–60 years |

This is optimistic: it ignores clustering/regime dependence and multiple testing. A 90-day window at 1–3 trades per week yields only roughly 13–39 opportunities, before zero-trade days, rejected fills, and source attrition. The reports therefore treat a small effect as **unverifiable** at pilot scale, not as disproved. The CBR guidance hypothesis is more constrained still: roughly eight meetings per year, with fewer independent policy-summary observations.

A separate report gives an illustrative opportunity-cost calculation: RUB 30,000 × 14% × 90/365 ≈ **RUB 1,035.62**. The 14% is the key rate reported for the 11 September 2026 decision, not an observed LQDT yield; it must not replace the fund’s actual matched return. See the [CBR decision archive](https://www.cbr.ru/press/keypr/) and use the fund’s own records for any real counterfactual.

### 3.4 Why a spread is a difficult text target

A calendar spread tends to cancel much of a broad common-factor move between its legs. The residual drivers are often relative supply/demand, carry, financing, storage, margin, contract mechanics, and roll dates. Many of these are already numeric or formally dated. A text feature would need to predict a relative move, not merely restate a macro headline or directionally describe an outright market. This strengthens the case for contract mapping and structured-event controls before sentiment analysis.

## 5. Timestamp, revision, and historical-leakage controls

### 5.1 Keep distinct clocks

For every item, record these separately; do not collapse them into one `date`:

1. **Event time:** when the meeting, operational change, production stoppage, or other real-world event occurred.
2. **Publisher-stated publication time:** exact displayed time and original timezone/precision; distinguish planned from actual.
3. **First-observed time:** first time the study’s own capture process or defensible contemporaneous archive proves that exact content was retrievable. This is the safest historical availability time.
4. **Ingestion/parse time:** when the project actually received and parsed the bytes/text.
5. **Edit/revision lineage:** observed edit marker, previous snapshots/hashes, correction, deletion, republication, and attachment changes. Never overwrite a prior version.
6. **Effective time:** when a rule/parameter/session change becomes binding. This is not the same as when its announcement becomes known: a future-effective notice can be informative before its effective date; an already-effective notice cannot be backdated.
7. **Media/transcript time:** video/stream publication and start, upload/reupload, caption track, transcript creation/update, and the time that version became available.

A minimum event record should include: stable source and native item ID; publisher; canonical URL; title; source type (fact, forecast, analysis/opinion, marketing, technical notice); all clocks above with timezone and precision; capture method; raw permitted text/attachment or a permitted excerpt; SHA-256; version and duplicate/syndication group; original language; transcript/caption provenance; exact evidence spans/offsets; model/prompt/version if processed; output JSON; and latency to the simulated action.

For an as-of simulation, define **information eligibility** from the earliest defensible observable/ingested version—not the event date. One source report used an overloaded timestamp name for availability; in this merged document, “effective time” is reserved for when a rule/parameter actually binds, and it is not folded into publication/ingestion time. Simulate action only after the selected source is available and the sampled human reaction delay has elapsed, with the market session and fill constraints applied. Keep the rule’s effective time as a separate operational field. If first-observed time/version is missing, exclude that item from a fast historical test rather than treating it as zero-latency or assuming the present-day text was the original.

### 5.2 Concrete timing examples and conflicts

- **CBR meeting versus later summary:** the reports cite the 11 September 2026 decision at 13:30 MSK and a separate discussion summary dated 23 September 2026 (one report gives 17:00 MSK). The summary is information available on its own publication/first-seen date—not on 11 September. The CBR calendar uses “expected” publication/conference times and warns that calendars can change; a schedule is not a capture log. See [CBR decision archive](https://www.cbr.ru/dkp/mp_dec/decision_key_rate/), [calendar](https://www.cbr.ru/dkp/cal_mp/), and [publication calendar](https://www.cbr.ru/calendar/).
- **Summary cadence disagreement:** one cited CBR process page is described as saying “approximately one week” (or the sixth working day); the actual calendar dates in another report are about 12–13 calendar days after meetings. Use the date/time on the actual document and prospective first-seen log; do not hard-code either cadence. The [CBR archive](https://www.cbr.ru/dkp/mp_dec/decision_key_rate/) lists the 11 September statement and the later 23 September summary separately.
- **CBR quiet period:** one report cites the Bank’s information policy as a quiet period from 13:30 MSK seven days before a rate decision until 13:30 on decision day ([CBR information policy](https://www.cbr.ru/eng/dkp/information_policy/)). Verify the current rule and its scope before using it; it is context for source classification, not a feature or proof that third-party commentary is reliable.
- **CBR RSS/page time:** one report observed a rolling `RssPress` feed with 13 items at the time of its 22 October check, and argued that feed `pubDate`/`lastBuildDate` can reflect feed regeneration rather than original first availability. A page-level timestamp or dated URL may help, but does not prove the exact time a historical consumer could fetch the particular version. A guessed legacy CBR URL reportedly returned HTTP 200 but only a generic page shell with a later “last updated” stamp; verify the actual headline/body, not just status code. CBR’s RSS directory is [here](https://www.cbr.ru/development/RSS/); the Russian press feed cited is [`RssPress`](https://www.cbr.ru/rss/RssPress).
- **MOEX `sitenews`:** reported fields include `id`, `tag`, `title`, `published_at`, and `modified_at`; the item body is returned separately as HTML. The list/body join is necessary ([ISS list](https://iss.moex.com/iss/sitenews.json?iss.meta=off), [example item 105342](https://iss.moex.com/iss/sitenews/105342.json?iss.meta=off)).
- **Reported bounded `modified_at` checks:** `ar.txt` describes 401/600 sampled records with `modified_at != published_at` (66.8%); 15/600 over one hour, 3/600 over a day, maximum about 19.9 days. In a separate 300-record sample, 79/300 (26.3%) had a negative difference and 68/300 (22.7%) were exactly three hours earlier. `gs.txt` separately reports older records modified years later and examples with three-hour discrepancies. These are bounded sample observations, not a claim that all rows are edited. They are enough to reject `modified_at` as a safe historical version or event-time proxy without further validation.
- **Reported near-synchronous risk notices:** in the `ar.txt` sample, parsed MOEX categories `cat=113` (13/20) and `cat=122` (217/281) had a median lag of about 0.5 minutes from the stated change time to `pubDate`, with no negative lags in the parsed sample. This indicates those sampled notices were generally post-change or near-synchronous and duplicated numeric fields; it does not prove every MOEX notice is post-change.
- **Settlement notices and volume/novelty:** the same report found 53 settlement-price items in one 51-day category sample and judged them post-expiry/duplicative of ISS `LASTSETTLEPRICE`; it proposed only a cheap, low-priority count/novelty test as a possible volatility proxy. Both are secondary to the event/price baselines and neither is a demonstrated signal.
- **A different kind of notice can lead its effective date:** the reports cite MOEX notice [n92848](https://www.moex.com/n92848), published 14 August 2025 at 18:53 and describing risk parameters for calendar spreads effective 16 August. This is not inconsistent with the 2026 near-synchronous sample: publication time and future effective time are different, and notice classes vary. Similarly, [n104052](https://www.moex.com/n104052?nt=107) was reported on 9 September 2026 as a planned system release for 14 September; [n104190](https://www.moex.com/n104190) was explicitly about a **test** futures polygon, not a production outage.
- **Long lead but operational, not directional:** one reported ISS item, [n105342](https://www.moex.com/n105342), was published 22 October 2026 at 12:56 and announced a planned 30 November system change. This is post-pack-date evidence and is not a historical input for a 7 October cutoff. It illustrates that a technical notice can have long lead time without containing a price signal.
- **Telegram:** one bounded report sample observed the 11 September CBR rate post `@centralbank_russia/3803` at 13:30:14 MSK, about 14 seconds after the scheduled 13:30 release. This is one mirror observation, not a general latency estimate. Public previews showed only a recent slice (roughly 15 posts/page in one sample) and no prior edited versions. Telegram’s own [channel description](https://telegram.org/tour/channels) says messages can be edited and deleted; the API message object exposes `edit_date` (last edit only) and `edit_hide` ([Telegram message constructor](https://core.telegram.org/constructor/message)).
- **Transcripts:** a current transcript/caption does not establish when that wording became available. The cited [YouTube transcript help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15930243) describes how to view transcripts for captioned videos; it is not a versioned historical transcript archive.

### 5.3 Category IDs, archives, duplicates, and data joins

Reports use MOEX identifiers from different interfaces: RSS `cat=` categories, HTML-news `ncat=` categories, and ISS `tag`/item IDs. Reported examples include RSS `cat=100` (all news), `107`, `112`, `113`, and `122`; HTML listings use values such as `ncat=112` or `ncat=201` for futures/derivatives in different reports. These identifiers are **not interchangeable**. Before an export, verify the category definition on the exact endpoint and confirm it against sample item content; do not combine category labels from different APIs by number alone.

Reported archive measurements also differ: one report estimates roughly 62,000–65,000 retrievable ISS items with a 2013 pagination observation; another reports 63,837 items and also cites older item IDs dated 2001. RSS `cat=100` was reported to contain 1,197 items spanning about 51 days. Treat those as dated snapshots and query-dependent archive observations, not a stable completeness guarantee. The RSS is a rolling feed, not a proven full historical archive.

### 5.4 Historical eligibility rule

A historical item is eligible only when the study can establish the exact text/version and a defensible availability time before the simulated decision. In particular:

- Do not backdate a summary, later transcript, current edited page, or search-result date to the event it describes.
- A current channel export is a discovery lead, not a historical tape.
- A correction/edit creates a new observation from its own observable time; it must not rewrite the earlier record.
- Deduplicate syndicated copies and cross-posts; one underlying fact is not multiple independent observations.
- Preserve time zones in original form and normalize separately to UTC. Do not assume that an unlabelled timestamp is Moscow time.
- If only date-level precision is evidenced, record day-level precision and exclude the record from a 3–90-minute event test.

## 7. Rights, Gemini access, quota, privacy, and compute constraints

### 7.1 Source rights are a gate, not a footnote

- **MOEX:** reports conflict on whether private/internal parsing is permitted. `ar.txt` quotes the MOEX User Agreement clauses 3.2.2, 3.2.4, 3.2.5, and 4.1: in substance, information may be used only for personal/official non-commercial purposes not connected with profit; technological extraction/copying is restricted; profit-related use requires the applicable contract; and reproduction/redistribution may require prior written permission. Other reports characterize private internal analysis as potentially permitted. The separate ISS/market-data and non-display/derived-use terms were not resolved. Therefore, **do not treat free/public access or an RSS link as permission for automated profit-oriented use**. Obtain the applicable terms or written clarification before building a parser/archive for trading research. See the [MOEX User Agreement](https://fs.moex.com/f/3499/agreement.pdf), [MOEX data policy](https://www.moex.com/ru/datapolicy/), and [ISS reference](https://iss.moex.com/iss/reference/).
- **CBR:** official source status does not settle reuse rights. Reports cite a user agreement and a requirement to link/attribute the Bank’s Press Service when quoting. Check the applicable terms; do not republish extensive text without permission. See [CBR user agreement](https://www.cbr.ru/user_agreement/) and the press-material example cited in [CBR’s press archive](https://www.cbr.ru/eng/press/pr/?file=639118653205990149ENG_PNP.htm).
- **Minfin:** reports cite an explicit CC BY 4.0 notice for material on the official site, with attribution. This is the clearest reuse lead in the set, but verify the specific page/item and preserve attribution. See [Minfin legal information](https://minfin.gov.ru/ru/about/).
- **Rosstat/NCC/issuer material:** open web access was not treated as a settled licence; review applicable terms before bulk storage, reuse, or model submission.
- **Telegram:** reports cite Telegram’s [Content Licensing Terms](https://telegram.org/tos/content-licensing?setln=en) and [API Terms](https://core.telegram.org/api/terms) as restricting platform-derived data for AI development/benchmarking/deployment absent the specified permission/consent. Until applicability and permission are resolved, the safe default is **no platform-derived AI training, benchmarking, or deployment and no channel export for modeling**. Channel identity being official does not grant reuse rights.
- **Books, papers, transcripts, and paid/private data:** none was supplied as a library. Do not upload proprietary libraries, paid material, private/account-only channel content, or personal documents without both appropriate rights and a deliberate privacy review.

These are research gates, not legal conclusions. Public availability, an RSS feed, a source’s official status, or an item being readable in a browser does not by itself establish unrestricted AI or commercial reuse.

### 7.2 Gemini access and terms

The reports’ checks of Google’s public documentation describe model/project-specific limits visible in AI Studio, access only from supported regions, and free-tier data-use terms that may allow product improvement and human review. One report’s observed region list included Poland and did not include Russia; another states Russian access is not listed. The user’s actual API project, account region, billing state, model, and permissions were not inspected. Do not use a VPN or other workaround to evade a region restriction; check the applicable current terms and account conditions before any call.

On the unpaid tier, the cited documentation says submitted input/output may be used to improve products and may be read/processed by human reviewers, and warns against submitting sensitive, confidential, or personal information. EEA/UK/Switzerland data terms may differ from other regions; verify the terms that actually apply. Even if only public text is contemplated, its own source licence still matters. Do not submit account statements, positions, private broker materials, credentials, or non-public project files. See [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms), [available regions](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/available-regions), and [pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing).

### 7.3 Quotas, versions, determinism, and architecture

- The official [rate-limit page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits) describes limits by project and model (e.g. RPM, TPM, RPD) and directs users to inspect the active project’s limits. The reports cite conflicting secondary figures—20, 500, or 1,500 requests/day depending on model/report/date; `gr.txt` also gives 15 requests/minute and 1,000,000 tokens/minute. None is a verified quota for this user. **Do not plan on any single published or user-reported number.** One report uses 20 requests/day as an intentionally conservative stress case, not as a Google guarantee.
- One report says the observed free-tier documentation did not offer batch processing/grounding; other reports propose batching. Do not make the design depend on batching or a `latest` alias. Verify current model, feature, and quota availability in the actual account. If free-tier processing is permitted, reserve it for rare ambiguous documents; parse routine RSS/HTML/number fields deterministically.
- Model names and pricing pages change. Pin the exact permitted model identifier and version, prompt/schema/settings, temperature, call time, raw input/output, response ID, usage/retry/error data, and input/output hashes. A model change creates a new series; do not silently overwrite prior outputs. A zero-temperature setting or structured JSON schema does not guarantee determinism or semantic correctness; repeat a small fixed sample and validate field values against the source.
- The prompt’s operating environment is a MacBook Pro (Intel 6-core i7, 16 GB RAM) plus a 1-vCPU/1-GB RAM VPS with 2 GB swap and 20 GB disk; the shared background budget is only about 250–350 MB and is unverified. Remote Gemini calls do not remove the need to budget local ingestion, logging, retries, and storage. The original prompt prefers free resources, assumes no foreign cards/phones, permits only small justified one-off spending, and does not assume a paid Gemini upgrade. No production probing/deployment is authorized or performed in this study.
- The reports include current/free-tier model names and model-cutoff dates that differ. One reports a March 2026 cutoff with some domains limited to January 2025; another cites different Flash generations. One report cites Russian-language support for a Flash model, but that does not establish extraction accuracy on this Russian financial/market corpus. Treat model names, language support, and cutoffs as version-specific reported facts to recheck—not proof that a later model can produce a historically valid predictor. The cited API terms also include account/use conditions (such as age and permitted product use) that must be checked for the actual project.

### 7.4 Safe extraction pattern

If all gates pass, use a frozen document-to-JSON task—not a trading prompt. Example fields:

- immutable `source_id`, URL, document type (`fact`, `forecast`, `analysis/opinion`, `marketing`, `technical_notice`, `unknown`);
- topic and explicit entity/contract family only when stated in the document;
- event/publication/effective times with timezone and precision, kept separate;
- old/new parameter, unit, affected session/market, and production/test/unknown status;
- change versus the last document actually available then; fact-versus-estimate/opinion; horizon/conditionality only when explicit;
- verbatim evidence quote and source offset for every nontrivial field; `unknown`/`abstain` if unsupported; flag unsupported inference.

Disable web search/grounding in the prompt, provide only the captured as-of text and allowed preceding version, and prohibit buy/sell, price-target, or expected-return generation. Validate JSON **and** its semantics; unsupported quotes/entities/values or facts outside the supplied text are errors, not “model judgment.”

## 8. Leakage-aware validation and ablation protocol

### Stage 0 — source, rights, and contract gate

1. Name the exact spread/contract pairs, economic mechanism, horizon, and feature hypothesis before searching further. A broad universe is not a reason to scrape every source.
2. Confirm exact source identity, applicable terms, native IDs, timestamps, edit/version behavior, duplicates, and whether an official structured alternative already contains the same values.
3. If any essential historical text lacks an as-published version or defensible first-observed time, exclude it from historical signal testing. Do not treat missing time, license, cost, or access as zero.
4. Confirm the relevant live data, executable quote, trading-session, and human-intervention assumptions separately; no broker/account check is included here.

### Stage 1 — deterministic and extraction-quality benchmark

- Build A and B first; add a fixed Russian lexical C only after source and timestamp controls pass.
- Hand-label a small, independent sample of roughly **30–50 documents** for feasibility, as proposed in `cg.txt`; include facts, forecasts/opinions, corrections, duplicates, negation, and production-versus-test examples, but report each class separately. `ar.txt` also proposes a bounded, zero-trading week for a Gemini-versus-regex extraction benchmark; this is an extraction/decision-change check only, not a performance sample.
- If a model remains worth evaluating, preregister a larger blind audit—one report proposes 100 documents/fields and another proposes 200 parser cases. A defensible merged engineering gate is **at least 100 manually adjudicated fields**, with at least 95% field-level accuracy for the model on decision-critical fields, exact checking for dates/values, and every label supported by a quote. These are proposed QA thresholds, not published standards or measured outcomes.
- If a deterministic parser already reproduces at least 99% of the target fields in a representative 200-field audit, the reports’ proposed rule is to reject Gemini for that task. The exact thresholds and the sample must be fixed before testing; the differing 90%, 95%, and 99% figures in the reports are proposals, not test results.

### Stage 2 — historical ablation, only for eligible vintages

Use chronological walk-forward splits, not random shuffling. At each simulated decision time, fit preprocessing, dictionaries, labels, thresholds, scaling, source selection, and prompt/schema choices only from information available up to that time. Group duplicates/syndicated reports and adjacent legs/events; purge overlapping forward outcomes and use an embargo appropriate to the holding period. Keep a final untouched chronological holdout. Log every source, prompt, model, feature, threshold, split, failed attempt, and model change. One report suggests triple-barrier labeling; treat it only as an optional, separately preregistered outcome-label design if it suits the holding horizon, not as a replacement for the project’s primary matched-LQDT net-RUB metric or for time/version controls.

- Historical **deterministic/lexical** features may be evaluated only where the underlying document version and publication/first-visible time are defensible.
- Historical **Gemini** runs on old text can test extraction fidelity against humans/regex on the same supplied document; they cannot show that a later model’s feature existed for a historical trader. A date-blind prompt or “use only this text” instruction does not remove pretraining knowledge, later edits, or model priors. Do not report this as historical predictive performance.
- Useful contamination/placebo checks include anonymizing entities/dates, asking whether the model can recover later outcomes, comparing with the same text delayed to the next day, and testing post-edited versions only as post-edit observations. A purported edge that survives timing shifts or depends on later text should be rejected.

### Stage 3 — prospective zero-trading shadow

After the protocol is frozen, capture only the predeclared official source items prospectively. A report proposes **12 weeks and at least three CBR meetings** as a shadow interval. Because scheduled meetings may not fit that event count inside 12 weeks or the project’s 90-calendar-day review, do not promise that the interval will be sufficient; preserve the project review date and never reset losses or controls to await text observations. This stage measures capture quality, extraction accuracy, latency, quota failures, and whether a feature changes a decision—not profitability. One report proposes stopping if no decision changes during its proposed 12-week shadow; if adopted, make that stop rule explicit before collection rather than continuing to search the same data for a favorable signal.

Every item must be processed after first observation, with immutable version/hash and pinned model/prompt. Record source-poll, ingest, parse/model, queue, and human reaction time separately. No trading authority, production deployment, or trading feasibility is implied.

### Stage 4 — virtual portfolio test only if a feature survives

Replay one realistic virtual portfolio attempting every eligible signal. Respect finite capital/integer contracts and record stale/rejected/unfilled/partial fills and capital competition. Apply identical conservative/base/stress per-contract fees, spread, slippage, liquidity, and the same delay grid to every arm. The reports propose grids including 3/5/15/30/45/90 minutes or 3/15/45/90 minutes; choose and preregister one grid that covers the user’s 3–90-minute response range, rather than selecting the most favorable delay after seeing results.

Report the primary net RUB difference versus matched LQDT and paired uncertainty, not model accuracy alone. Secondary metrics include field precision/recall/exact-match, abstention and unsupported-claim rates, decision-change rate, latency quantiles, quota/API errors, false alerts, source attrition, attempted/filled trades, slippage, cost, and source/regime ablations. A genuine extraction-quality improvement that never changes a position or operational decision has no trading value by construction.

### 8.1 Preregistered rejection criteria

Reject or suspend a source/model feature if any one of the following occurs:

1. Source identity, lawful access, contract mapping, first-observed time, or exact text version is unprovable; a transcript is available only after the action window; or a rights gate fails.
2. The content is already captured by a formal event flag, numeric table, lexical baseline, or price response, with no residual decision change.
3. The signal requires event-date backdating, a later edit/transcript, or a modern model’s unverified historical knowledge.
4. The model invents unsupported facts, confuses fact with forecast/opinion, fails Russian negation/conditionality, or varies materially across repeat runs/model versions.
5. The incremental effect disappears on identical walk-forward splits, the untouched holdout, realistic 15–90-minute delays, or base/stress costs; it depends on one event/contract; or the trial log/multiple-testing adjustment was not preserved.
6. Net RUB excess over matched LQDT fails to improve against the best simpler baseline after the same cost/account-flow treatment. A label/AUC/IC improvement alone is insufficient.
7. A simple parser performs equivalently or better on the required fields, or an extraction QA gate is missed.
8. The permitted free-tier access, privacy terms, source rights, reproducibility, or alert workload does not fit the project constraints.

A small/noisy sample that cannot reject the null is **inconclusive**, not proof that a small edge is absent. Conversely, an attractive single event or retrospective plot is not evidence of value.

## 10. Conflicts, unknowns, falsification criteria, and project changes

### 10.1 Conflicts reconciled in this synthesis

1. **“Conditional go” versus “drop text”:** reconciled as no text-driven trading feature now, with a zero-trading extraction/operational test only if rights and data quality pass.
2. **CBR vs MOEX priority:** MOEX/NCC are first for operational controls; CBR is first/only for a semantic return hypothesis. Neither is shown profitable.
3. **Official-source “unbiased” language:** official sources are authoritative about their own actions/rules, but forecasts, policy communication, and venue/broker material have incentives and should not be treated as neutral predictions. Separate factual fields from commentary.
4. **Historical archive fidelity:** some short reports call official archives immutable/high fidelity. Detailed reports show changed pages, unreliable modification fields, missing old versions, rolling feeds, and channel edits/deletions. The merged rule is to use an immutable prospective capture and admit old items historically only when their exact vintage and availability are evidenced.
5. **MOEX notice timing:** one sample shows near-synchronous/post-change risk notices; another example announces a future effective date. Preserve both; classify per item and keep publication, first-observed, and effective time separate.
6. **MOEX identifiers:** RSS category numbers and HTML `ncat` numbers differ across reports/endpoints. Resolve against the actual endpoint before export; do not assume the numerals mean the same category.
7. **MOEX licence:** reports’ “internal analysis permitted” conclusion conflicts with quoted User Agreement wording restricting profit use/technological extraction. Treat permission as unresolved until the applicable agreement is checked.
8. **Gemini quota:** report estimates of 20, 500, or 1,500 RPD and model names conflict or are secondary/version-specific. Only the active project’s current limits are usable; none were checked.
9. **Gemini region/privacy:** public docs were reportedly checked, but the actual API account, region and data-use terms were not. The region list observed in a report included Poland and omitted Russia; it does not certify the user’s project eligibility or provide legal advice.
10. **CBR summary cadence:** “about a week/sixth working day” and calendar-based 12–13 calendar-day gaps differ. Use the actual dated document and first-observed log.
11. **Source coverage and archive depth:** reported ISS totals/date ranges differ; RSS is a rolling window in the sample. No exhaustive or complete archive is claimed.
12. **Third-party evidence:** `cr.txt`’s ECB sentiment/AUC claim, claims of state/media bias, and broad foreign-sanctions examples are not accepted as validated evidence of this task’s outcome. `ds.txt`/`ex.txt` claims of high historical fidelity are not accepted without vintage/availability proof. `gr.txt`’s arbitrary >5% edit and >80% promotional-volume cutoffs are proposals, not standards; the merged protocol rejects any item whose version/rights cannot be established, and requires thresholds to be preregistered rather than adopted post hoc.

### 10.2 Unresolved facts and dependencies

- Candidate contract universe, spread definitions, and causal mapping to policy, issuer, commodity, or foreign events.
- Exact MOEX ISS/non-display/derived-use permissions for automated or profit-related use; CBR, NCC, Rosstat and issuer-specific reuse terms; Telegram rights for any proposed access; transcript/caption rights.
- Actual Gemini project/model, supported region, account eligibility, live RPM/TPM/RPD, data-use terms, latency, determinism, and model version. No API call was made.
- First-visible timestamps and historical content versions for old CBR, MOEX, Telegram, video, and transcript items; no as-of archive was supplied.
- Exact VTB/BCS fees, collateral eligibility, calendar-spread margin/netting, execution permissions, KPUR recognition, and emergency response procedure. No account or broker check was performed.
- Actual LQDT return/yield/NAV and the project’s true matched-flow counterfactual; investor tax residence/account treatment.
- The free, finest defensible market-data timeframe and data entitlements; one report cites an ISS manual with a 15-minute unauthenticated delay/historical-access restriction, which remains a separate data-access question.
- A licensed, fit-for-purpose Russian financial lexicon; no verified one was identified in the reports.
- Human workload/alert quota and emergency response availability.

### 10.3 Falsifiers and conditions to reopen the decision

Reconsider only if the following are demonstrated prospectively or with a lawfully licensed point-in-time archive:

- A predeclared source item is captured in its exact as-of version and **precedes** the corresponding structured value/decision by a tradeable interval; for MOEX risk notices, one report proposes evidence of a representative lead of at least three minutes as a threshold worth investigating. This would reopen an operational or execution hypothesis, not automatically prove alpha.
- A constrained model beats a deterministic parser on a blind, representative sample of messy decision-critical fields (with the QA threshold fixed before testing), produces evidence-grounded outputs, and changes at least one decision that a baseline would not change.
- A CBR forward-guidance feature mapped to a named eligible spread survives the same chronological splits, realistic manual delays, costs and untouched holdout, with a predeclared economically meaningful net-RUB threshold and sufficient independent observations.
- A later-day or post-edit placebo does **not** reproduce the apparent live advantage; if it does, treat the result as leakage, hindsight, or a weak timing claim and reject it.
- The free-tier/region/source terms permit the intended use and the pipeline is reproducible within the project’s verified quota and workload budget.

A larger future allocation may change integer granularity but does not by itself repair timestamp leakage, multiple testing, rights, or weak source-to-contract mapping. The reports suggest revisiting the statistical test only when trade/event counts are adequate (one estimate is ≥126 trade outcomes for a 0.5σ effect under idealized assumptions) or a licensed, timestamped archive exists. Do not interpret the larger RUB 10–20 million candidate scale as tested performance.

### 10.4 Project changes recommended by the merged evidence

1. Keep the current pilot centered on price and the specified formal-event baseline; do not add an LLM trade signal.
2. If permitted, add only a deterministic MOEX/NCC operational status/risk monitor, with no trading authority and no claim of return improvement.
3. If a semantic study is still wanted, preregister one CBR guidance hypothesis after the contract map exists; start with official web text, not Telegram, and capture prospectively.
4. Resolve source and model rights/access before collecting a large archive or making any API call. If Gemini fails a gate, stop or use a local deterministic/lexical approach as a separate future decision.
5. Keep all provisional loss, emergency-funding, matched-LQDT, and review controls unchanged; the text layer cannot loosen or reset them.
6. Do not start production probing, deployment, or real trading from this synthesis. Later technology and execution choices remain separate gates.
