# R11 — Operational constraints for later environment/stack choices

**Scope and status.** This is desk research and a proposed measurement plan only. I did not connect to the VPS, scan ports, install tools, deploy anything, or execute any benchmark. No repository, server attachment, prior report or credential was available. All resource figures below are **planning estimates derived from the embedded assumption ranges**, not measurements. Current-facts cut-off is **2026-10-22**; all sources were accessed on that date unless stated otherwise.

---

## 1. Bottom line

The later environment should be shaped by six constraints, in this order:

1. **The VPS is a shared 1 GB appliance, not a research server.** Ubuntu 24.04 cloud images have a 1 GB minimum and a 3 GB suggested minimum, so the VPS is already at the low end before Xray/3X-UI and the other scheduled workload are counted. The project must be subordinate to VPN health, not compete with it.
2. **The 250–350 MB background allowance is a shared ceiling, not a per-process allowance.** A monitor plus a collector plus a daily ETL job can easily exceed it if each is given its own generous limit. The project should have a parent cgroup/slice cap, not just per-service caps.
3. **Heavy research and 15-minute history belong on the laptop.** Daily collection, monitoring, virtual-ledger updates and Telegram alerts can be made small enough for the VPS. Parsing multi-year 15-minute history is not a 1 GB VPS workload.
4. **SQLite is sufficient for the ledger and reference data; containers, microservices, message queues and a public dashboard are premature.** SQLite is a single-file, in-process, ACID database; WAL mode gives concurrent readers with one writer. It is the right default for a low-volume event ledger and alert state.
5. **Telegram is free and adequate for low-volume alerts, but it is not an execution channel.** Bots cannot start conversations with users; the user must add the bot to a group or send it a message first. The Bot API is free for developers, but there are broadcast and per-chat limits, and the token grants full control of the bot.
6. **Free Gemini and Hugging Face resources are optional research conveniences, not production 24/7 infrastructure.** Gemini’s free tier exists but its rate limits are not guaranteed and vary by model and region. Free Hugging Face Spaces sleep after inactivity and have non-persistent disk.

**Recommended default for stages 5–6:** laptop for research/backtests; VPS for a thin always-on monitor, virtual ledger and Telegram relay only; a conditional VPS collector only after a read-only benchmark shows adequate headroom; no public dashboard; no container orchestration; no paid dependency unless measurement proves the VPS cannot do even the thin job.

## 3. Workload sizing from the embedded assumptions

### 3.1 Planning formulas and base case

These are **estimates**, not measurements. I use:

- Universe `N` = 100 / 200 / 300 contracts.
- Watchlist `W` = 5 / 20 / 30 pairs.
- Trading days `D` = 250 per year.
- History `H` = 3 / 5 years.
- Session length `S` = 10 hours/day for the base case; sensitivity to 14 hours is shown.
- Raw record size `R` = 2 KB (JSON/CSV row plus metadata); sensitivity 1–5 KB.
- Compression ratio 8× for retained raw snapshots; sensitivity 5–10×.

**Daily baseline, all contracts:**
- Rows = `N × D × H`.
- Base `N=200, H=3`: 200 × 250 × 3 = **150,000 rows**.
- Raw ≈ 150,000 × 2 KB = **0.3 GB**; compressed ≈ **0.04 GB**.
- High case `N=300, H=5`: 375,000 rows; raw ≈ **0.75 GB**; compressed ≈ **0.09 GB**.

**Hourly watchlist:**
- Snapshots = `W × S × D × H`.
- Base `W=20, S=10, H=3`: 20 × 10 × 250 × 3 = **150,000 snapshots**; raw ≈ **0.3 GB**; compressed ≈ **0.04 GB**.
- High case `W=30, S=14, H=5`: 30 × 14 × 250 × 5 = **525,000 snapshots**; raw ≈ **1.05 GB**; compressed ≈ **0.13 GB**.

**15-minute watchlist:**
- Snapshots = `W × S × 4 × D × H`.
- Base `W=20, S=10, H=3`: 20 × 40 × 250 × 3 = **600,000 snapshots**; raw ≈ **1.2 GB**; compressed ≈ **0.15 GB**.
- High case `W=30, S=14, H=5`: 30 × 56 × 250 × 5 = **2,100,000 snapshots**; raw ≈ **4.2 GB**; compressed ≈ **0.53 GB**.

### 3.2 Sensitivity table

| Workload | Universe / watchlist | History | Raw storage (estimate) | Compressed retained (estimate) | Main operational concern |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Daily all contracts | 200 | 3 y | 0.30 GB | 0.04 GB | Negligible; can run on VPS or laptop |
| Daily all contracts | 300 | 5 y | 0.75 GB | 0.09 GB | Still small; batching and retries matter more than size |
| Hourly watchlist | 20 | 3 y | 0.30 GB | 0.04 GB | VPS-feasible if collection is lightweight |
| Hourly watchlist | 30 | 5 y | 1.05 GB | 0.13 GB | VPS-feasible, but avoid loading all history in memory |
| 15-min watchlist | 20 | 3 y | 1.20 GB | 0.15 GB | Do ETL on laptop or in bounded chunks; not a pandas-on-VPS job |
| 15-min watchlist | 30 | 5 y | 4.20 GB | 0.53 GB | Raw + indexes + logs can approach the 20 GB disk budget; retention and compression essential |

**Key reading:** the *storage* is not the problem. Even the high 15-minute case is under 1 GB compressed. The **CPU and peak memory** of parsing and transforming millions of rows is the problem on a 1 vCPU/1 GB VPS.

### 3.3 CPU, memory and network estimates

| Workload | Estimated CPU | Estimated peak RSS | Network estimate | Comment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Daily ETL, 150k–375k rows | 10–60 s of one core with pandas; longer with Python loops | 200–500 MB with pandas; 100–200 MB with DuckDB memory_limit=150 MB and threads=1 | <1–5 MB/day raw, plus retries | Fits laptop easily; risky on VPS unless chunked and capped |
| Hourly watchlist ETL, 150k–525k snapshots | 5–30 s of one core | 100–300 MB with careful chunking | <5 MB/day raw, plus overhead | VPS-conditional |
| 15-min watchlist ETL, 600k–2.1M snapshots | 30 s–5 min of one core, depending on tooling | 0.5–2 GB with pandas; 150–300 MB with DuckDB memory_limit and chunking | <20 MB/day raw, plus overhead; <7 GB/year | Do on laptop; if on VPS, must be a separate bounded batch, not concurrent with monitoring |
| Always-on monitor / ledger loop | <5% of one core average | 50–120 MB with stdlib/sqlite3/requests; 150–250 MB if pandas is imported | negligible | Keep this process free of heavy imports |
| Telegram alert send | negligible | negligible | negligible | Far below 1 message/second in normal use |

The memory issue is **total**, not per process. If the OS uses 200–300 MB, Xray/3X-UI uses 100–250 MB (unverified), and the project monitor uses 100 MB, the project is already at or near the 250–350 MB shared allowance before any collector or ETL runs. A 15-minute ETL with a 500 MB pandas peak would either swap heavily or be OOM-killed. `MemoryHigh` would slow it down before `MemoryMax` kills it, but the VPN may still suffer from swap and I/O contention.

### 3.4 What this means for placement

| Component | Recommended placement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Research/backtests over multi-year 15-min data | Laptop | 16 GB RAM and 6-core CPU; no VPN co-tenancy; offline scheduling acceptable |
| Point-in-time raw daily snapshots | VPS or laptop | Tiny; VPS gives 24/7 capture |
| Hourly watchlist snapshots | VPS conditional | Feasible if one-shot batch, bounded memory, retention capped |
| 15-minute watchlist snapshots | Laptop preferred; VPS only after benchmark | CPU/RSS risk; disk growth manageable only with retention and compression |
| Virtual ledger and alert state | VPS | Small SQLite DB; must survive laptop sleep |
| Telegram relay | VPS | Needs to work when laptop is asleep; low resource |
| Dark-only UI | Laptop or localhost-only on VPS | No public dashboard exposure by default; avoid adding a web server to the 1 GB VPS |
| Broker API automation | Separate later gate | Read-only token first; do not co-locate order credentials with research scripts |

## 4. Runtime, storage and UI alternatives

### 4.1 Embedded database choice

| Option | Strengths | Weaknesses | Best use here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SQLite** | Single file, ACID, 750 KB compiled, WAL concurrency, mature backup API, `integrity_check` | One writer at a time; not columnar; analytical scans slower on large data | Virtual ledger, alert state, reference data, small daily tables |
| **DuckDB** | Columnar OLAP, ~20 MB binary, fast on 5M–50M rows, configurable `memory_limit` and `threads` | Less proven as a long-running transactional ledger; single-file concurrency model differs; analytical engine can still use significant memory unless capped | Offline research on laptop; bounded analytical batch on VPS only with `memory_limit` and `threads=1` |
| **Parquet/CSV + manifest** | Portable, compressible, reproducible, no server | No transactions; schema drift; must manage checksums and partitions | Immutable raw snapshots and research outputs |
| **PostgreSQL/ClickHouse** | Strong concurrency and analytics | Another daemon, more RAM/disk, more attack surface, more backup work | Premature on a 1 GB shared VPS |
| **SQLite + Parquet** | Best of both: transactional state plus portable research data | Two formats to manage | **Recommended default** |

Durability and consistency: SQLite with WAL, `busy_timeout`, `synchronous=FULL` or `NORMAL` depending on acceptable loss window, and `PRAGMA integrity_check` on backups. Reproducibility: raw snapshots immutable, timestamped, checksummed, and stored with source/URL/retrieval time. Schema migration: a simple `schema_version` table and forward-only migration scripts is enough; no ORM/migration framework is required at this scale. Backup: SQLite online backup API or `VACUUM INTO` for a consistent copy; retain daily/weekly/monthly; verify with `PRAGMA integrity_check` on the backup, not just the source.

### 4.2 Local research vs VPS monitoring

| Aspect | Laptop research | VPS monitoring |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy search/backtest | Yes; offline scheduling acceptable | No; would contend with VPN |
| 15-min history retention | Yes | Only compressed/summarised, or not at all |
| Always-on alerting | No; laptop sleeps | Yes; small process |
| Public exposure | None | None by default; SSH tunnel/localhost only |
| Failure blast radius | Local only | Shared with VPN; must be contained by cgroup and no-touch rules |
| Backup | Time Machine/external + encrypted copy | SQLite backup + raw snapshot sync to laptop |

### 4.3 UI and service shape

- **Default:** command-line/scripted monitor, SQLite ledger, Telegram alerts, static HTML reports generated on the laptop. No web dashboard on the VPS.
- **If a UI is later needed:** bind to `127.0.0.1`, access via SSH tunnel; no `0.0.0.0`, no public port, no authentication bypass. Dark-only is a front-end concern, not a reason to add a public server.
- **Avoid:** Docker/Compose, Kubernetes, Redis, RabbitMQ, microservices, a separate API gateway, a public Grafana. They add memory, disk, update surface and attack surface without solving a demonstrated problem at this scale.
- **One process, one systemd service, one timer** is the appropriate architecture until measurements show otherwise.

## 5. Telegram: costs, limits, recipients, delivery and privacy

### 5.1 Costs and limits

- The Bot API is free for developers; bot accounts do not require an additional phone number. The user still needs a Telegram account to create the bot and receive alerts.
- Default broadcast limits: about **30 messages/second across all chats**, about **20 messages/minute in a group**, and avoid more than **one message/second in a single chat**.
- Paid Broadcasts can raise the limit to **1,000 messages/second** for **0.1 Telegram Stars per message** above the free 30/second; the bot must hold at least 10,000 Stars. This project’s alert volume is far below the free limit, so paid broadcast is not justified.
- Exceeding limits returns **429**; clients must honour retry behaviour rather than hammering the API.

### 5.2 Authorized recipients and privacy

- A bot **cannot initiate a conversation**. The user must either add it to a group or send it a message first. For private alerts, the user must `/start` the bot and the bot must store the resulting `chat_id`.
- Group privacy mode is enabled by default; a bot sees only commands/replies relevant to it unless made an admin or privacy mode is disabled. This is a feature, not a bug: the bot should not be given broad group visibility.
- The bot token is **full control** of the bot. It must be stored in a secrets file with restrictive permissions, never in code or a repository, and rotated via BotFather if exposed.
- The bot should maintain an explicit **allowlist of chat IDs** for commands and never treat an arbitrary incoming message as authorization. Telegram does not provide a built-in user allowlist; this must be enforced in the application.

### 5.3 Delivery, retry, deduplication and persistence

- Use `getUpdates` with an `offset` of `update_id + 1` to confirm processed updates and avoid replaying the same update. If webhooks are used later, the supported ports are 443, 80, 88 and 8443, and a secret URL path is recommended.
- Outbound alerts should have an **idempotency key** (for example, `event_type + contract + signal_timestamp + state_version`) persisted before send. On retry, the same key should not produce a duplicate user-visible alert.
- On 429, honour `retry_after`; cap retries and move exhausted messages to a dead-letter table. Do not let a Telegram outage block the monitor loop.
- **Notification success ≠ user action ≠ trade fill.** A delivered Telegram message proves only that Telegram accepted the API call. It does not prove the user saw it, acted on it, or that a broker order was filled. The virtual ledger must record `alert_sent`, `alert_delivered` (if obtainable), `user_ack` (if implemented) and `trade_outcome` as separate states.
- Risk and outage alerts must not silently stop during research jobs. The monitor process must be independent of any research/ETL process, with its own systemd service and resource cap. Research jobs must never share a process with the alert loop.

## 6. Operational failure budget and safe-stop design

### 6.1 Failure-mode table

| Failure | Detection | Safe stop / containment | Recovery | Backup / replay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monitor process crash | systemd service state; missing heartbeat | systemd `Restart=on-failure` with `StartLimitBurst`; alert after repeated failures | Restart; resume from last ledger checkpoint | Ledger is transactional; replay last event batch |
| Collector process crash | systemd state; missing snapshot | Restart bounded; if repeated, stop collector and keep monitor alive | Re-fetch missing interval; idempotent snapshot keys | Raw snapshots are immutable; missing interval is visibly marked |
| Stale market data | `max_staleness` per feed; last-update timestamp | Mark feed `STALE`; suppress signals; send one outage alert | Resume only after fresh data and explicit state transition | Do not backfill signals on stale data; record the gap |
| Disk full | `df` threshold at 80/90/95%; inode threshold | At 90% stop writes to project DB and collector; at 95% stop all project writes; keep monitor read-only if possible | Delete expired raw/logs; compact; restore from backup if DB corrupted | Journald `SystemMaxUse`, logrotate, retention policy; never fill root |
| Swap pressure | `vmstat`/`pgmajfault`; `MemoryCurrent` near `MemoryHigh` | `MemoryHigh` throttles; if sustained, stop collector/ETL; keep monitor | Reduce batch size/frequency; move ETL to laptop | Do not rely on swap for correctness; swap is a symptom, not a solution |
| External MOEX/Telegram outage | HTTP errors/timeouts; 429; missing responses | Exponential backoff with jitter; circuit breaker; mark data stale; alert once | Resume when healthy; do not replay stale signals | Raw requests and responses retained for post-mortem |
| Session/day boundary | Exchange calendar and session clock | No signals outside session; no orders outside session | Next session resumes; previous session closed | Calendar/spec lineage stored with each record |
| Clock drift | `timedatectl`; NTP offset | Suspend signal generation if offset exceeds threshold; alert | NTP correction; manual review of affected timestamps | UTC internally; exchange timezone for display; log clock offset |
| Job overlap | systemd active state; `flock` | systemd timers do not restart an active service; use `flock` for scripts | Next scheduled run; no concurrent ETL | Checkpoint table records last complete batch |
| Corrupt state | `PRAGMA integrity_check`; failed transaction | Stop writes; switch to read-only; alert | Restore last verified backup; replay raw snapshots | Daily backup + integrity check; retain 7 daily/4 weekly/6 monthly |
| VPN degradation | VPN latency/handshake probe; system load | Stop project workloads; do not touch VPN config; alert user | User-approved investigation; project remains stopped | Separate cgroup; no auto-reboot; no firewall changes |
| Telegram duplicate/loss | Idempotency key; `update_id`; dead-letter count | Do not resend blindly; mark dead-letter | Manual review or safe retry with same key | Alert log and dead-letter table |

### 6.2 VPN protection rules

- Put the project in a **separate systemd slice/cgroup** with `MemoryHigh`, `MemoryMax`, `MemorySwapMax`, `CPUQuota` and `TasksMax`. `MemoryHigh` should be the primary throttle and `MemoryMax` the last line of defence. `CPUQuota=20–50%` leaves headroom for Xray and the other workload.
- **Never** change ports 22/443/20724, the x-ui service, its data directory, Xray configuration, nftables/iptables rules, network interfaces, kernel network tuning, or VPN certificates.
- Do not install a public dashboard, reverse proxy, or anything that binds a new public port. If a UI is needed, bind to `127.0.0.1` and use SSH tunneling.
- Monitor VPN latency and handshake success from the project. If the VPN degrades, the project stops itself, not the VPN.
- No reboot, no `apt upgrade`, no kernel change, no firewall reload without explicit user approval and a maintenance window.

### 6.3 Backup and replay design

| Asset | Backup method | Frequency | Retention | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Raw market snapshots | Compressed immutable files + SHA256 manifest | Per collection batch | Daily 5 y; 15-min 1–2 y | Checksum on write and restore |
| SQLite ledger/state | SQLite online backup or `VACUUM INTO` | Daily + before migration | 7 daily, 4 weekly, 6 monthly | `PRAGMA integrity_check` on backup |
| Reference data/calendars | Versioned JSON/CSV with source timestamp | On change | All versions | Diff and manual review |
| Config/secrets | Encrypted archive, offline/laptop | On change | Current + previous | Restore test; token rotation drill |
| Research outputs | Parquet/CSV + manifest + notebook/script hash | Per experiment | Per project policy | Reproduce from raw + code hash |

Replay safety: the ledger should be **event-sourced with deterministic event IDs** and an append-only table. Replaying a batch should be idempotent: `INSERT OR IGNORE` on the event key, then recompute derived state in a temp table, compare hashes with the production state, and only then swap. Do not “replay” by re-sending Telegram alerts; alerts are a side effect, not the source of truth.

Do not design a critical path around Gemini until that is confirmed.

| SQLite write/read | Writes/second, read latency, WAL growth | `sqlite3 db "PRAGMA journal_mode;"`; `sqlite3 db "PRAGMA wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE);"`; small benchmark insert/select | Writes far below 1,000/s; WAL checkpoint <1 s; no `SQLITE_BUSY` storms |
| Telegram delivery | 429 frequency, retry_after, end-to-end latency | A test bot and test chat; send a controlled burst; log `429` and `retry_after` | No 429 at normal volume; if 429, `retry_after` handled and no duplicate |
| Disk full behaviour | Simulated threshold in a test directory, not root | `df` monitoring script against a test directory; no real disk fill | Project stops writes at 90%; monitor remains alive; alert sent |
| Restore drill | Restore SQLite backup and raw snapshot | Copy backup to temp; `sqlite3 backup "PRAGMA integrity_check;"`; replay a small batch | `ok` from integrity check; replay produces identical derived state |

**Go/no-go rule:** if the measured project cgroup cannot stay under the negotiated shared allowance while the VPN remains healthy, do not deploy the collector on the VPS. Move collection to the laptop, reduce frequency, or use a separate paid VPS. Do not “solve” the problem by removing the memory cap or by letting the project swap heavily.

## 9. Stage-3 read-only preflight and least-privilege deployment prerequisites

### 9.1 Read-only preflight (suggested, not executed)

These are inspection commands only. Do not change state during preflight.

- OS and kernel: `uname -a`, `lsb_release -a`, `cat /etc/os-release`.
- Memory/swap: `free -m`, `swapon --show`, `cat /proc/meminfo`, `vmstat 1 5`.
- Disk/inodes: `df -h`, `df -i`, `du -sh /var/log /var/lib/x-ui 2>/dev/null`.
- VPN service: `systemctl status x-ui --no-pager`, `systemctl cat x-ui`, `journalctl -u x-ui --since -1h --no-pager`.
- Existing workloads: `systemctl list-timers --all`, `crontab -l` (current user), `ps aux --sort=-%mem | head -20`.
- Ports and firewall: `ss -lntup`, `nft list ruleset` or `iptables -S` (read-only), without modifying.
- Time: `timedatectl`, `chronyc tracking` if installed.
- Connectivity: `curl -I` to `iss.moex.com` with a short timeout; no high-frequency probing.
- Tool inventory: `python3 --version`, `sqlite3 --version`, `duckdb --version` if installed; these are not assumed.

### 9.2 Least-privilege deployment prerequisites

| Prerequisite | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Dedicated OS user | `futures` user, no sudo, no shell login beyond deployment; owns project directory |
| Project directory | `/srv/futures` or `/opt/futures`, not inside `/root`, `/etc`, `/usr/local/x-ui`, or the x-ui data directory |
| Python environment | Project-local venv; pinned dependencies; no global `pip install` |
| Secrets | `EnvironmentFile` or secrets file mode `600`, owned by `futures`; bot token and broker tokens never in code/repo |
| systemd unit | `User=futures`, `Group=futures`, `NoNewPrivileges=yes`, `PrivateTmp=yes`, `ProtectSystem=strict`, `ProtectHome=yes`, `ReadWritePaths=/srv/futures`, `Restart=on-failure`, `StartLimitBurst`, `StartLimitIntervalSec` |
| Resource caps | `MemoryHigh`, `MemoryMax`, `MemorySwapMax`, `CPUQuota`, `TasksMax`; preferably a parent slice for the whole project |
| Logging | journald `SystemMaxUse` and/or logrotate; no unbounded project log files |
| Network | Outbound only to MOEX/Telegram/broker endpoints; no inbound public port; no port binding to `0.0.0.0` |
| Backups | Backup directory outside the project data directory; verified restore path; encrypted off-VPS copy |
| Approval | SSH method, sudo rules, package installation, firewall changes and any reboot are later user-approved configuration, not part of research |

### 9.3 RAM/disk budget negotiation

The VPS has 1 GB RAM and 20 GB disk. A defensible negotiation is:

- **RAM:** OS baseline + VPN/3X-UI + other workload + project ≤ 1 GB with at least 100–150 MB headroom. If the project is allowed 250–350 MB, that is a **shared** project budget, not per service. Put the project in a parent slice with `MemoryMax` at or below the negotiated figure. A monitor-only process should fit in 100–120 MB; a collector plus monitor should be treated as one budget, not two.
- **Disk:** OS + packages + swap + VPN logs/data + other workload + project ≤ 20 GB with at least 2–3 GB free. Project steady-state target ≤3 GB, peak ≤5 GB. If free disk after the existing workloads is below 5 GB, do not retain 15-minute raw data on the VPS.
- **Swap:** 2 GB swap is a safety buffer, not capacity. `MemorySwapMax` should cap project swap so the VPN is not forced to swap. Sustained swap-in/out is a no-go signal, not a normal operating mode.
- **CPU:** 1 vCPU is shared. `CPUQuota=20–50%` and `Nice=10` reduce the chance that a batch starves Xray. Do not run CPU-heavy ETL while the VPN is under peak load.

### 9.4 No-touch protection list

Do not modify, restart, reconfigure, or probe aggressively:

- Ports **22/443/20724**.
- The **x-ui** service, its systemd unit, its data directory, its database, and its panel credentials.
- Xray configuration, certificates, keys, and inbound/outbound settings.
- nftables/iptables rules, network interfaces, routing, DNS, and kernel network sysctl.
- The other scheduled workload, its timers, and its data.
- System-wide Python, systemd, OpenSSL, or kernel packages.
- Any file under `/etc`, `/usr/local/x-ui`, `/var/lib/x-ui`, or the VPN certificate paths.
- Do not run `apt upgrade`, `do-release-upgrade`, `reboot`, `systemctl daemon-reload` outside the project unit, or firewall reload without explicit approval.

## 12. Falsification criteria and possible project changes

The environment choice should be falsified by measurements, not defended.

**Reject or change the VPS collector if:**

- Project cgroup `MemoryCurrent` cannot stay below the negotiated `MemoryHigh` without sustained swap or OOM kills.
- VPN latency/handshake success degrades materially while the project runs.
- Free disk after existing workloads is below 5 GB, or project disk growth exceeds the negotiated quota.
- Daily ETL peak RSS exceeds 250 MB on the VPS, or 15-minute ETL peak RSS exceeds 250 MB even with DuckDB `memory_limit` and `threads=1`.
- MOEX ISS error rate exceeds roughly 1% at the planned request rate, or 429/connection resets become frequent.
- Telegram 429 `retry_after` is routinely long or duplicate alerts cannot be prevented.
- Gemini is unavailable in the user’s region, or its free-tier limits are insufficient for the intended use.
- Hugging Face free Spaces sleep during an alert test.

**Possible project changes if criteria fail:**

- Move all collection to the laptop and keep only a monitor/alert relay on the VPS.
- Reduce the universe to 5–10 pairs and use daily-only collection on the VPS.
- Keep 15-minute data on the laptop only; sync daily summaries to the VPS.
- Use a separate small paid VPS if the user accepts the cost and the shared VPS cannot meet the thin-job budget.
- Drop the LLM dependency entirely; use deterministic rules and Telegram templates.
- Delay broker automation until a read-only BCS token is tested against the actual futures/spread data and limits.
- Replace the public dashboard idea with static HTML reports generated on the laptop.

## 13. Material unknowns and conflicts

1. **Actual VPN/Xray/3X-UI resource use is unknown.** The 3X-UI ecosystem reports vary; some guides mention 512 MB/1 GB minimums, but these are not measurements of this VPS. The VPN’s actual RSS/CPU must be measured read-only before any deployment.
2. **The 250–350 MB background allowance is unverified and shared.** It is not clear whether it includes page cache, journald, or the other workload. Treat it as a total project ceiling until measured.
3. **Free disk after OS, swap, VPN logs and the other workload is unknown.** A 20 GB disk can be consumed by logs and snapshots faster than the raw data estimate suggests. Measure `df` and `du` before choosing retention.
4. **MOEX ISS rate limits are not publicly documented.** Secondary sources mention ~20 req/s or ~100k/day, but these are not vendor commitments. Use caching, backoff and conservative cadence; do not design to a rumoured limit.
5. **Telegram limits are guidance, not a hard contract.** The FAQ says “about 30 messages per second” and warns of 429s. The design must handle 429 even at low volume.
6. **Gemini free-tier limits and regional availability change.** The official page says limits are not guaranteed and vary by model/tier. The available-regions list did not show Russia in the rendered excerpt, but this must be verified against the current page and the actual account region.
7. **Hugging Face free compute policy is ambiguous.** The documentation contains both “Static Spaces are free” and “CPU Basic… FREE”, but also “Gradio and Docker Spaces run on compute and require a paid plan”. Free Spaces sleep and have non-persistent disk. Do not depend on them for 24/7 work.
8. **BCS API capabilities for futures spreads are not verified.** The public page documents token auth and read-only vs trading scopes, but not the full rate limits, streaming behaviour, or spread-order support. Treat automation as a later gate.
9. **Ubuntu 24.04 at 1 GB is at the minimum, not the suggested level.** Canonical suggests 3 GB or more for broader scenarios. This is a structural constraint, not a tuning problem.
10. **No installed-tool inventory is verified.** Python, SQLite, DuckDB, curl and monitoring tools may or may not be installed. Versions must be read on the VPS later; do not assume features.
