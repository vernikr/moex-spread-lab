**Decision brief | Prepared to the requested information cutoff: 2026-10-07**

**Language:** English report; source text is predominantly Russian.  
**Scope:** Independent public-source research and study design only. No strategy backtest, live trade, quota check inside the user's account, or trading-feasibility test was performed.

## 1. Decision

**Conditional go for a small extraction/recordkeeping pilot; no claim that text predicts returns.** Prioritize a few high-provenance sources with a clearly named economic mechanism. Test whether text-derived labels add information to (a) price-only references, (b) deterministic event flags from dated formal disclosures, and (c) simple Russian lexical features. Do not start with generic news sentiment, broad Telegram scraping, or model-generated trade opinions.

The most defensible first semantic hypothesis is that **changes in official forward guidance / risk balance** may contain information beyond the numeric policy decision for interest-rate-sensitive spreads, and may matter to FX/index futures through linked macro channels. The caveat is decisive: the Central Bank of Russia's meeting summary can be published days after the meeting (its 11 September 2026 summary was dated 23 September), too late to serve as a 3–90-minute reaction signal for the original meeting. Only source text actually observable at the decision time belongs in that fast window. Later summaries may be tested only as later information, with their actual later timestamp.

The second conditional hypothesis is **issuer/sector operational guidance** (production, exports, outages, logistics, input costs, supply constraints, management forecast changes) for a contract whose economic exposure is explicitly mapped to that issuer/sector. No contract universe was provided, so there is not enough evidence to justify a broad issuer-channel export now.

MOEX technical notices should initially be treated as **execution/eligibility inputs**, not alpha: scheduled system releases, trading calendars, funding calculation changes, risk-parameter changes, and outages are directly structured as event flags. Broker commentary has commercial incentives and should be excluded from the first pass except for a narrowly defined operational-status hypothesis.

No text value or profitability is measured in this report. A release's truth, authority, or publication date does not prove incremental predictive value, historical observability, lawful bulk reuse, or actionable latency.

## 3. Timestamping and historical leakage controls

Treat the following as separate fields; never collapse them to a single `date`:

1. **Event time** — when the real-world meeting, production stoppage or policy event occurred.
2. **Publisher-stated publication time** — as printed on the site/channel, preserving its original timezone and precision.
3. **First observable time** — first time the study's own capture process or defensible archive proves that exact content was retrievable. This is the earliest safe time for a simulated decision unless stronger evidence exists.
4. **Ingestion time** — time the study collected it; it can be later than first observation.
5. **Edit/revision time and lineage** — observed edit marker, revision history, previous snapshot/hash, deletion/republication and any correction notice.
6. **Effective time** — when a disclosed rule, contract change or operating change takes effect.
7. **Transcript/media time** — video publication time, video creation/stream time, caption-generation time if known, and time each transcript version became available.

**Minimum event record:** stable source ID; publisher; canonical URL; native post/article/video ID; title; source class (fact, forecast, analysis/opinion, marketing, technical notice); event/publication/first-seen/ingestion/effective timestamps; timezone; capture method; raw text or permitted excerpt; SHA-256 of captured bytes/text; revision and duplicate-group IDs; original language; transcript/caption provenance; model/prompt/version if processed; output JSON; exact supporting spans; latency from first observation to simulated action.

**Historical eligibility rule:**

* A visible article date or a current post timestamp alone is not adequate proof for a 3–90 minute backtest when actual first availability or the version then shown is unknown.
* Do not backdate text to the date of the event it describes. The CBR summary posted 23 Sep about the 11 Sep meeting is a new 23 Sep information event, not an 11 Sep signal.
* The current transcript of a video does not prove those words/captions were available during the live conference. YouTube's help page says a transcript is viewable for videos that have captions, but that is a user feature description, not a historical-caption archive or proof of availability time. [YouTube: View video transcripts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15930243).
* A public channel export is a **lead**, not a time machine. Telegram permits edits and deletion. A contemporaneous collector should save immutable snapshots, native post IDs and observed timestamps. If the only source is a present-day edited page, label it “retrospective text only” and exclude it from the historical 3–90 minute signal test.
* Preserve source timestamps in original timezone and normalize separately to UTC; Russian source times often display Moscow time. Do not infer timezone from the machine locale. Record whether a listed schedule is planned, announced, actual or collector-observed.
* Deduplicate cross-posts and wire copies. If an official site, Telegram post and video repeat the same statement, count the information as first available only once and store later copies as syndication. Track corrections as new versions rather than overwriting the original captured bytes.

## 5. Feature extraction: bounded, auditable use of Gemini Flash

Use the model as a **constrained document-to-structured-fields extractor**, not as a market forecaster. The model must receive the exact text known at the simulated decision time, source metadata and only preceding documents that were available then. Disable web/search/grounding in the extraction task. Never ask “what will the market do?” or permit external claims unsupported by the supplied text.

### Suggested output schema

One JSON object per document (or one array with stable source IDs), with strict allowed values and `unknown` where evidence is absent:

```json
{
  "source_id": "immutable-id",
  "document_type": "fact|forecast|analysis_opinion|marketing|technical_notice|unknown",
  "topics": ["rates", "inflation", "supply", "costs", "logistics", "operations", "market_mechanics", "other"],
  "stance_change_vs_prior_available_text": "more_tightening_risk|less_tightening_risk|unchanged|mixed|not_comparable|unknown",
  "risk_balance": "inflationary|disinflationary|balanced|mixed|not_stated|unknown",
  "conditionality_change": "more_conditional|less_conditional|unchanged|not_stated|unknown",
  "horizon": "intraday|days|weeks_months|longer|not_stated|unknown",
  "novelty_vs_prior_available_text": "new|repeated|corrected|unclear|unknown",
  "affected_entity_or_contract_family": ["only if explicit"],
  "operational_scope": "production|test|unknown|not_applicable",
  "evidence_spans": [{"exact_quote": "verbatim source text", "supports": "field_name"}],
  "unsupported_inference": false
}
```

For non-CBR sources, fields that do not fit the document should be `not_applicable`/`unknown`; do not force a directional sentiment label onto an operational bulletin. The exact schema and allowed labels must be frozen before validation. Compare change versus the **last document known then**, not a later “cleaned” summary.

### Baselines, latency and quotas

1. **Baseline A — price-only technical reference** exactly as independently preregistered.
2. **Baseline B — deterministic event flags** derived from dated formal disclosures and source timestamps, kept separate from text. Corporate/financial events remain their own factual input stream.
3. **Baseline C — simple Russian lexical feature set**: fixed/manual term lists and counts/ratios for predeclared concepts such as tightening/easing, inflation/supply/demand, outage/production/export, uncertainty/conditionality and corrections. Fit any learned vocabulary only on the training window. Record negation, inflection and ambiguous-context errors. Do not port an English sentiment dictionary and assume it works in Russian.
4. **Treatment D — Gemini structured output** from the same documents, same as-of text and same folds. Compare each model-only feature and then the combination; hold the feature set and tuning rules fixed.

**Time/cost mapping:** every candidate feature must be available before the simulated action time. Evaluate 3, 15, 45 and 90-minute decision delays separately, using the project’s 3–90-minute manual action distribution as unknown, not as a guaranteed 5–15-minute fill. A document arriving later than the signal is ineligible for earlier decisions. Use identical fee/spread/slippage sets (conservative/base/stress), integer contracts and finite RUB 30,000 all-in test capital for each arm; store stale/rejected/unfilled/partial-fill cases. Apply the matched LQDT counterfactual with identical external flows and do not double-deduct retained-collateral LQDT return, as specified in the project assumptions. The primary metric is **net RUB excess over matched LQDT**, not sentiment accuracy.

**Quota/prompt discipline:** request limits vary by model and active project; Google's official rate-limit page directs users to view active limits in AI Studio and describes RPM, TPM and RPD dimensions. Do not assume the user's reported “free allowance” is fixed, available in the intended region, or unchanged. Reduce cost and variability by batching only small, homogeneous sets, limiting input to one source plus at most one as-of predecessor, limiting output to compact JSON, and caching exact input/output pairs. Record model identifier/version, date, prompt hash, system settings, temperature, response, errors, retries, latency and token counts if shown. A zero-temperature setting does not prove deterministic/reproducible behavior.

**Critical Gemini data-use condition:** Google’s Gemini API Additional Terms effective 23 March 2026 say that on unpaid services (including Google AI Studio and unpaid Gemini API quota) submitted content and generated responses may be used to provide, improve and develop products and machine-learning technologies; human reviewers may read/process API input and output, and Google says not to submit sensitive, confidential or personal information. See [Gemini API Additional Terms, “How Google Uses Your Data”](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms?hl=en), lines/section “How Google Uses Your Data”. Free pricing tables also indicate use to improve products. Submit only small, public, permitted excerpts after checking source terms; do not upload private account files, non-public Telegram exports, proprietary source libraries, credentials or confidential notes. Free access is not equivalent to confidential processing. The exact account quota, supported model, regional availability and current terms were not verified inside the user's account; check them at the study start. No API calls were made for this report.

### Pretrained-knowledge contamination and reproducibility

A prompt instructing Gemini to rely only on supplied text does **not** erase pretrained knowledge. A 2026 model processing a 2020 announcement may have seen later coverage, a later version of the release, commentary or the event outcome during training. This makes retrospective generated labels vulnerable to future-knowledge contamination even when the input text is truncated correctly. Therefore:

* Historical model extraction is a **diagnostic of extraction feasibility**, not evidence that the same feature existed in a real-time system in 2020.
* Stronger evidence must be prospective: capture source text and timestamp first; then run the frozen model/prompt on the saved item after the known observation time, with immutable logs. Report that it is a live-prospective pipeline only after its model version and data-access rules are actually fixed.
* Never rerun old data and silently replace outputs. Preserve first output, later retries and version changes separately. Re-run only as an explicitly named robustness analysis.
* If provider/model ID changes, break the series; do not combine model generations without a preregistered compatibility experiment.
* Require each nontrivial label to cite literal source spans. Human audit a blind sample for label accuracy, evidence support, Russian negation/conditionality and duplicate sensitivity. Model claims not entailed by the text must be marked unsupported and rejected.

## 6. Leakage-aware validation and ablation protocol

### Stage 0 — source and timestamp gate (no trading inference)

1. Freeze a contract-universe map, per-contract causal hypotheses and the eligible source families. Each source family must have a named, falsifiable hypothesis; do not invent a foreign-language expansion unless a selected contract has a clear causal link to a specific foreign issuer/event. No Chinese-source expansion is justified on the current inputs.
2. For each selected source, test a small handful of pages/posts for exact identity, accessibility, timestamp fields, edit lineage, duplicate rate and terms of use. Do not use a specialist or bypass tool absent a documented failure to retrieve an essential **lawfully public** primary page with a normal fetch or official mirror.
3. Make an immutable snapshot log from day one. If the historical content version and first-visible time cannot be proven, exclude it from fast historical testing; do not set missing time or cost to zero.
4. Obtain the precise allowed excerpt and reuse status for any text not clearly reusable. Public accessibility alone does not automatically establish unrestricted bulk-reuse rights.

### Stage 1 — extraction reliability pilot

Use approximately 30–50 manually selected **independent** documents as a practical small pilot (this is a design target, not a power calculation): mix factual releases, forecasts/opinions and technical notices, but keep the classes separate. Include hard cases with copied headlines, changes/corrections, negation, ambiguous wording and test-vs-production scope. Two human passes or one human pass plus a blind second review should determine whether outputs are correct, evidence-grounded, stable and auditable. Repeated copies of the same underlying event do not count as independent documents.

Pass only if: (a) at least 95% of required output records parse under the fixed schema; (b) all feature values in a manually checked sample are supported by quoted source spans or explicitly unknown; (c) no future or unsupplied facts are relied on; (d) edit/duplicate and timezone handling is reproducible; and (e) measured API usage fits a tested allowance with retries and batch failures included. The numerical acceptance bars here are proposed engineering gates, not sourced standards. If they fail, simplify to deterministic parsing or lexical features.

### Stage 2 — historical ablation, only for eligible archived text

* Chronological walk-forward splits; preprocessing, dictionaries, cutoffs, labels and thresholds fitted only with information available by each decision date.
* At each timestamp, the only permissible inputs are the captured version available by then, previous versions already seen, prices that had already arrived, and event flags already public. A subsequent source edit may generate a *new* observation from edit time onward, never rewrite the earlier observation.
* Compare A, B, C and D on identical time ranges, eligible opportunities, contract sets, delays, capital constraints and cost scenarios. Report source attrition due to timestamp/version uncertainty and number of independent events, not just trade count.
* Log every tried source, feature, prompt, model, threshold, split and failure. Retain the final chronological holdout untouched until schema, hypotheses and transformations are frozen. Use robustness tests that account for multiple model/feature tries; White's Reality Check addresses data-snooping in model selection, and backtest-overfitting research cautions that a simple holdout alone may be unreliable after extensive repeated strategy selection.
* If archived content lacks a defensible as-of version or precise availability time, historical treatment D is omitted for that source; do not compensate with a modern model guess.

### Stage 3 — prospective shadow evaluation

After the protocol and feature schema are frozen, collect only predeclared sources and score them after first observation. No production probing/deployment is part of this task. Every eligible signal is logged in a single realistic virtual portfolio without discretionary omission; record stale/rejected/unfilled/partial fills and capital competition. Establish actual human intervention capacity and emergency response availability before interpreting a trade-simulation result. Do not allow the optional text layer to loosen any funding or risk gate.

Use the requested primary performance measure: net RUB excess relative to matched LQDT under identical external flows. Use the same conservative/base/stress cost scenarios and action delays as other arms. The user’s RUB 5,000 warning, RUB 10,000 action trigger, RUB 15,000 outer shortfall boundary and 90-calendar-day review rule remain provisional controls, not guarantees; text features cannot modify/reset them.

## 7. Falsification and project-change rules

Reject or defer a source/model feature if any of the following holds:

1. No provable exact source identity, native ID/URL, permitted access route or first-observed time; post edits/deletions/revisions cannot be resolved; transcript appears only later than the action window.
2. After the event is deduplicated, most extracted features are duplicates of the formal event flag, title keywords, numbers already released or price reaction.
3. The feature only “works” when applied before publication, using a later transcript/revision, using text posted on event date rather than first visibility, or using contemporary Gemini to label historical text.
4. Extracted labels drift materially across repeated runs/model versions, lack literal evidence, misclassify forecast as fact, or fail Russian negation/conditionality checks.
5. Incremental effect disappears on walk-forward/untouched holdout, at 15–90-minute delays, or under base/stress execution costs; it relies on one event or one contract; or result selection ignores prior tested sources/features.
6. Net RUB excess versus matched LQDT does not improve, after the same cost and account-flow accounting, relative to the best simpler baseline. Statistical or direction-label improvement without economic incremental value is not a pass.
7. The free tier/terms are incompatible with the project's privacy/reuse requirements or not stable enough to reproduce, and there is no approved alternative.

Possible project change after rejection: keep source monitoring as **human-readable situational context or operational veto only**, not a trade trigger. If text has no trustworthy fast timestamps, restrict it to a slower, explicitly lagged medium-term regime feature, or do not model it at all. If MOEX structured notices are machine-readable, handle them via simple deterministic rules. If the contract universe remains broad and causal mappings cannot be written in advance, stop issuer-specific research rather than searching more channels.
