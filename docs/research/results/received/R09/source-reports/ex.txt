**Conclusions**

- Evaluate the entire predeclared selection-and-execution policy, not the prettiest pair backtest. Use nested chronological evaluation, an immutable experiment history, dependence-aware uncertainty and a genuinely untouched final period. Selection correction cannot repair unavailable information or unrealistic execution. E1, E2, E5, E8.
- Sparse medium-term trades, shared legs and overlapping holding periods make the number of bars a poor description of evidence. Report calendar span, trades, concurrent exposure and economic episodes separately. No universal 30-trade rule, automatic annualized Sharpe, DSR cutoff or PBO cutoff establishes profitability. E1, E2, E13–E15.
- The primary economic outcome should be RUB excess wealth versus a flow-matched LQDT counterfactual. Test whether its uncertainty clears a preregistered economically meaningful hurdle; separately test whether the account can survive the path. Statistical confidence is not funding protection.
- Use expanding or rolling walk-forward to address deployment into later Russian market conditions. Purged/combinatorial cross-validation can diagnose sensitivity, but training on later regimes is not a simulation of an earlier deployable decision. Official 2022 notices demonstrate why a single stationary-market assumption needs scrutiny. E2, E6, E7, E22–E24.
- Default to not displaying numerical win probabilities until horizon-specific, time-separated calibration is decision-useful. High classification accuracy, a reassuring reliability plot or a high win rate is not evidence of positive net LQDT excess. E16–E19.
- A 90-calendar-day review can establish some operational facts without establishing profitability or capacity. With no dataset or backtest supplied, none of the five gates—data, statistics, operations, small live test and scaling—can be declared passed; this is unassessed evidence, not an empirical finding of failure.

**Timeline**

- Development fold k: [older inner TRAIN → label-maturity/purge boundary → later inner VALIDATION] repeated entirely before outer origin τ_k.
- Optional probability layer inside development: [model TRAIN → completed-label CALIBRATION → later VALIDATION]. Choose the calibrator without using the outer test.
- At τ_k: refit the selected policy using only permitted, completed information → OUTER TEST [τ_k, τ_(k+1)). Record outcomes until their registered horizon/exit, even if this extends beyond the test decision window.
- Repeat later origins using the fixed refit/selection rule → freeze research version at τ_freeze → FINAL HOLDOUT decision window → label-maturity tail → one registered efficacy review.
- After final evaluation: PROSPECTIVE SHADOW. A consumed historical final block remains in the audit trail and is never relabelled unseen.

**Embargo rule:** An embargo addresses residual information/dependence overlap, not a magic percentage of bars. In a design that trains after a test block, exclude later training examples until their outcome and feature-information support no longer consumes the held-out information. In strictly forward-only evaluation there is no later training set inside that fold, so a post-test embargo may add nothing. Specify any extra separation from actual label/holding overlap, lookback support, release latency and training-only dependence diagnostics; document sensitivity.

**Purging rule:** At origin τ, training labels require a_i < τ, or the explicitly registered same-timestamp event ordering. In blocked designs, remove training labels whose outcome-information intervals intersect held-out outcome intervals; inspect feature dependencies too. For strict forward deployment, maturity already removes many boundary leaks. Fit imputation, scaling, volatility thresholds, hedge ratios, pair ranking, PCA, cointegration tests, regime models and selection rules inside the permitted training set. Feature windows, missing-value handling and normalization/update rules must already be fixed at each origin. If a calibrator is used, reserve a pretest calibration segment not used to fit the corresponding base-model predictions; any later base-model refit must follow the validated calibration protocol rather than silently making those predictions in-sample.

**Observation record:** For observation i store decision time d_i, feature-information availability, label interval [d_i,e_i], and time a_i when the completed label is actually available. Store the raw information dependencies of every transform. Actual holding endpoints may vary; an endpoint realized later cannot be used to fit a model earlier.

**Important exception:** Using already-known past prices to initialize a test-period rolling feature is legitimate. Shared historical lookback alone is not proof of future leakage. Conversely, purging cannot make a model trained on a later structural regime historically deployable. Avoid discarding valid past data merely to obtain a cosmetically independent split.

**Hypothetical boundary:** Illustration only: a signal starts on research day 40 and its label ends on day 55; the next test begins on day 50. That label is unavailable at the day-50 fit and must be removed from training. Prices known by day 49 may still initialize day-50 features. If a fitted regime label uses day-60 information, renaming it day 40 does not make it permissible.

**Matched lqdt accounting**

**Counterfactual:** The counterfactual retains core LQDT diverted to the pilot and places genuinely available incremental cash according to a fixed LQDT purchase/cash-residual convention. It must reflect executable fund transactions, whole-unit constraints where applicable and matched external-flow timing—not simply a risk-free-rate line. Unaffected core holdings cancel in the paired difference; do not divide by the RUB 40m-plus core to disguise pilot losses. Freeze when new external cash buys whole fund units, residual-cash remuneration, transaction charges and how closed sessions delay purchase. Cash raised by selling existing core LQDT is internal; the counterfactual keeps those units. Borrowing, financing charges and changes to collateral remuneration must be recorded explicitly. Core wealth cancels only to the extent its economics really remain identical.

**Funding ledger:** A transfer or broker use of existing core assets is internal to the account, not an external inflow into X. Record it separately against the pilot’s gross funding allowance. A genuinely new external deposit enters both wealth ledgers. Neither kind of funding resets X or its high-water mark. Match each true flow’s timestamp, RUB value and transaction convention, so it causes no mechanical jump in X or its high-water mark; subsequent strategy-versus-LQDT returns on it remain in X. Record flow-related fees separately. The future claim is conditional on the registered funding/flow rule.

**Historical scope:** The manager documents a 2019 fund-rules registration and current unit/NAV data, not an all-history investable benchmark or historical broker eligibility. Earlier history, missing dated LQDT observations and retrospective fee assumptions require an explicitly labelled proxy/exploratory analysis, not a claimed matched LQDT backtest. E25.

**Valuation and tax:** Predeclare mark-to-market and conservative flattening-value conventions; reconcile their difference. Use actual dated LQDT unit/market-price information where available; NAV is not necessarily an executable quote. Returns embedded in observed fund values already reflect fund-level expenses, so do not subtract those expenses again. Report pre-investor-tax X and explicit tax-treatment sensitivities until residence/account treatment is known; exclude only the requested loss of the long-term holding deduction, not all investor taxes. The primary reporting layer is pre-investor-tax but after execution/financing/fund costs; after-tax superiority is conditional until applicable taxes are specified. Historical benchmark observations need a dated source and publication-lag manifest, not just a current download.

**Primary definition:** Proposed estimand: X(t) = [W_actual(t) − W_actual(0)] − [W_LQDT(t) − W_LQDT(0)], in RUB. Start the two ledgers from the same wealth and apply identical true external deposits/withdrawals at identical times. W is consistently valued net account wealth, including cash, LQDT, futures economic P&L, liabilities and recognized costs. Identical external flows cancel; their different subsequent earnings do not. Use one common valuation timestamp and a frozen price hierarchy for executable quotes, marks, cash, liabilities and unsettled transactions. Record asynchronous or missing marks instead of concealing them in X.

**Avoid double counting:** If retained LQDT earns returns in the actual account, include those returns there and in the counterfactual. Deduct only their difference. Do not subtract a blanket LQDT opportunity charge again. Margin posted is not itself a loss, and variation-margin cash movements must reconcile with futures P&L rather than be counted twice. Core sale proceeds are principal converted to cash, not automatically a loss in X.

**Uncertainty methodology**

**Count information:** Report raw observations, decision opportunities, completed round trips, concurrent positions, unique economic episodes and calendar span. Define episode grouping before seeing profits using overlapping risk, common legs and common shocks; examine alternative reasonable groupings. Non-overlap alone does not prove independence. A large cluster of overlapping trades may provide little independent information. Few-cluster results from regression are a warning, not a transferable minimum trade count. E13–E15. Cancellations, unfilled opportunities, partial and still-open positions remain in the opportunity/portfolio record; completed round trips are a descriptive count, not the inferential denominator.

**Coherent path risk:** Resampled daily P&L blocks may inform mean statistics but can cut through positions and destroy state. Do not use their stitched prices as a realistic liquidation or margin path. Funding/drawdown claims require contiguous actual paths or explicit scenario replays preserving contracts, open positions, treasury state, warm-up and trading interruptions.

**Sequential reviews:** Freeze efficacy review dates and the horizon; repeated peeking at ordinary fixed-sample intervals is not the same procedure. Risk stops may operate continuously but their stopped paths belong in the evaluated policy. Time-uniform inference is an alternative only after its IID/martingale and other conditions are defensible; available confidence-sequence results do not automatically apply to adaptive dependent spreads. E20, E21.

**Precision and power:** Before gathering a final period, define the economically meaningful excess δ_H and useful interval width over horizon H. An episode-based power/sensitivity calculation may show what evidence could distinguish that hurdle, but label its assumed effect sizes, dependence and regimes. More resamples or bars do not create more independent episodes. Do not annualize a short sparse record by default. E13–E15.

**Selection adjustment:** Fixed-matrix joint resampling addresses the registered candidate family under its assumptions. It does not reproduce hidden feature invention or unlimited adaptive search. Use a genuinely future outer path for the fixed selection procedure; if resampling an entire selection pipeline, rerun every fitted selection step within valid temporal samples. Do not double-count the same outer observations as independent validations. E5, E8–E10.

**Separate uncertainties:** Report three separate objects: realized X conditional on the recorded ledger; uncertainty/bounds from missing or ambiguous quotes, costs, taxes and benchmark construction; and sampling/predictive uncertainty about a repeatable future policy. A resampling interval for an expected mean is not a guaranteed range for realized pilot losses.

**Primary inference target:** Preregister θ_H, expected net RUB excess over H calendar days for the frozen complete policy, starting capital and funding rule. The observed statistic is terminal X(H), not the number of profitable labels. When a within-regime stationary additive-increment approximation is defensible, estimate μ = E[ΔX_t] on common-calendar increments and translate to Hμ, using joint block/HAC uncertainty and selection adjustment. If changing capital, state or regimes make that approximation untenable, do not mechanically multiply a daily estimate: report observed horizon/window paths and sensitivity bounds, and mark future θ_H unidentified/inconclusive. Drawdown and funding path functionals require separate coherent-path analysis.

**Block choice and stationarity:** Set plausible block scales from holding/label overlap, autocorrelation and event clustering using development data; show sensitivity rather than optimize the block length for significance. Stationary bootstrap assumes weak dependence and stationarity; it is not a remedy for a Russian market break. Report chronological subperiods, leave-one-economic-episode-out sensitivity and transition failures. If conclusions rest on a few crises or blocks, label them inconclusive or episode-specific. E7, E14, E22–E24.

**Mean and effective sample size:** As a diagnostic under suitable stationarity, long-run variance Ω = γ₀ + 2Σ(k≥1)γ_k gives Var(sample mean) approximately Ω/T; an estimated n_eff = Tγ₀/Ω illustrates information loss or gain from dependence. Requires a stable positive long-run variance estimate and justified lag treatment. It is statistic-specific, can exceed T under negative dependence, and is not a universal replacement for T in DSR, probability calibration or a small-sample theorem. With sparse data, report inability to estimate it reliably. E13. Here γ_k and Ω are population autocovariance/long-run-variance quantities; their plug-in estimates need not be reliable in this setting.

**Paired dependence aware analysis:** Retain a common-calendar vector of incremental net excess for every eligible full-portfolio candidate and the matched benchmark, including inactive days. A no-entry day can still have cash drag or open-position P&L. Resample the same contiguous/moving or stationary-bootstrap blocks jointly across candidates; never resample legs, candidates or overlapping trades independently. An observed true no-trade value is not the same as an unavailable result. E8, E14. If inactive-day cash/collateral/benchmark values are unobservable, preserve the missingness and bound it; never zero-fill it as no-trade performance.

- A frozen simple spread rule motivated before inspecting the test—for example a fixed past-only hedge-estimation window with one fixed entry/exit rule—and an untuned version of the proposed model. Both must use the same universe-information constraints, funding, delays, costs and LQDT ledger.
- For probabilities, a training-only prevalence forecast with the identical label/horizon; never the final test’s realized prevalence presented as an available live forecast.

**Scenario design**

- Freeze a primary delay convention and illustrative 3, 5, 15, 30 and 90-minute scenarios. These are hypothetical stress points anchored to the supplied manual-response range, not an estimated probability distribution. Include no-response and next-permitted-session cases where relevant.

**Probability calibration guide**

**Default:** Do not display a numerical win probability merely because a model produces a score. With sparse dependent outcomes, a score/rank plus an explicit uncalibrated label—or no probability—is more honest than unsupported precision.

**Evaluation:** Use proper scores such as mean Brier score and log score, with a prespecified rule for extreme probabilities, against the training-only baseline. Inspect calibration-in-the-large, reliability by forecast level and uncertainty with counts, but do not create many nearly empty bins. Resample dependent episodes/time blocks jointly for score differences when justified. AUC/ranking and average win rate are not calibration. CORP improves reliability-diagram construction, but its published uncertainty bands require independent or at least exchangeable cases; do not transplant them unchanged to overlapping trades. E16–E18.

**When to abstain:** Suppress numerical probabilities when the event or quote/fill mechanism is unidentified, effective information is too small to distinguish useful thresholds, results depend on one episode, the calibrator changes materially across reasonable windows, or new regimes/costs fall outside validation. Show the specific reason and collect future labelled cases. Prior calibration does not guarantee calibration after dataset shift. E19.

**Event and horizon:** Before fitting, define Y = 1 if the specified executable policy’s net matched-LQDT excess at horizon H is positive, otherwise 0. State when H starts, its calendar/trading-time convention, treatment of open positions, stale/rejected/unfilled/partial outcomes and zero/tied outcomes. A probability conditional on a completed fill must be labelled conditional; it is not the probability that an eligible signal generates excess. Prefer policy-level or clearly defined episode outcomes; do not sum overlapping marginal probabilities or assume independent wins. Freeze whether the claim is conditional on an eligible signal or unconditional for a policy horizon. Do not discard censored labels at evaluation end: wait for the registered maturity tail or report the unresolved outcomes/bounds.

**Calibration design:** Generate genuinely out-of-time predictions; fit a simple calibrator on later-but-still-pretest completed labels, and evaluate on untouched later outcomes. Calibrator choice, window and regularization belong inside nested selection. Never calibrate on the final evaluation labels. With very little data, adding an isotonic mapping can overfit; simplicity does not itself establish calibration. E5, E17.

**Decision threshold:** Hypothetical two-outcome illustration only: if gross gain is G > 0, gross loss magnitude L > 0, and incremental execution cost c plus foregone benchmark return b are fixed, expected excess is pG − (1−p)L − c − b; break-even p* = (L+c+b)/(G+L). Here b is only the incremental matched-benchmark drag, not a second charge on retained LQDT. If G and L are already net excess, do not subtract c or b again. This algebra supplies no probability estimate or trading recommendation. In this illustration c ≥ 0 is an incremental charge and b is a signed opportunity cost, positive when the benchmark advantage is foregone; if p* is outside [0,1], no interior probability cutoff exists under those assumptions.

**Uncertain payoffs and costs:** Real payoff magnitudes, fill chances, holding times, costs and benchmark returns may depend on the outcome and regime; then use their joint net-excess expectation rather than the two-outcome shortcut. A high p can coexist with negative expected excess. Compare a dependence/selection-aware lower estimate of expected net excess with the preregistered economic hurdle across the required scenarios, and separately assess path funding. If the relevant probability/expectation range straddles the threshold, classify the decision as inconclusive; do not choose the cheapest scenario to force a pass.

**Preregistration and holdout rules**

**After consumption:** Keep the original result, freeze a new version and reserve genuinely later data for the revised claim. Do not silently extend a disappointing window until significance, reset pilot losses or rename a previously viewed period final. Specialized reusable-holdout theory does not grant unrestricted reuse of dependent market data. E20.

**Prospective shadow:** Use one timestamped virtual portfolio that attempts every eligible signal under finite capital, intervention quota and availability. Record alert, human acknowledgement, intended order, quote age, permissible session, fill disposition, rejection reason, open exposure and subsequent outcome before retrospective judgment. Shadow results may establish workflow and reveal model failures; realistic live fill/impact uncertainty can remain. Predeclared risk actions are part of the policy. An unplanned human override is a protocol deviation: report both the as-operated path and any clearly labelled counterfactual, do not censor the intervened loss, and version any permanent change.

**Consumed definition:** A final holdout is consumed when its economic outcomes are first used for confirmation or any outcome-sensitive research decision—including a summary, chart, ranking, calibration plot or failed result. Changing a model after looking does not refresh it. Testing several candidates on it is a declared multiple test, not several independent final confirmations. E20.

If a repair requires viewing performance or changing a material assumption, record the access and treat that period as consumed/development evidence.

| Choosing the best delay scenario | Preregister the primary executable-delay rule and full scenario grid. If the real delay distribution is unknown, report scenario results without invented probabilities. Choosing a delay after observing profit is another selection step and consumes validation. | The 15-minute simulation loses but the 3-minute simulation wins, so only the latter is reported as the manual strategy. |
| Asynchronous quotes, stale fills and partial legs | Require source times, bid/ask side, quantity and permissible quote age; order decisions after information availability. Record rejected, unfilled and partial orders and the intervening unhedged exposure. With coarse data, use defensible bounds or declare execution unidentified, not a precise invented fill. | The backtest combines a fresh bid in one leg with an old ask in the other, or assumes both legs fill at a closing midpoint after the close signal. |
| Finite-capital and discretionary-omission leakage | Replay one chronological portfolio with shared capital, lot constraints, margin liquidity and a frozen simultaneous-signal priority. Every eligible signal must have a disposition and reason code. Test reconciliation of the signal ledger against the portfolio ledger. | Each pair is backtested with the full RUB 30,000; their profitable results are then added. Overlapping losing signals are omitted as ones a human supposedly would have rejected. |

**Minimal reproducible validation protocol**

| Step | Requirement | Specification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Freeze the research question and decision rule | Define economically defensible spread families, point-in-time eligibility, forecast/holding horizon H, portfolio-level allocation, entry/exit/roll rules, refit schedule, costs, matched LQDT ledger and the minimum worthwhile excess δ_H in RUB over H. The latter is not the tolerated loss budget. A proposed reporting convention is 95% intervals and familywise α = 0.05; these are governance choices, not validated thresholds. Fix starting capital/funding and H with δ_H; do not transfer the same hurdle to another size or horizon. Familywise control applies to the primary superiority family; secondary metrics and scenario diagnostics are descriptive unless separately designated confirmatory. |
| 2 | Create an as-of data manifest | Record original observation, exchange event, first publication/availability, receipt and revision times; contract identity/specification, sessions, rolls, quotes and quality flags. Freeze snapshots and checksums. If past availability cannot be established, exclude the information from causal signals or mark that historical analysis exploratory. |
| 3 | Register the complete search | Append every family, pair screen, parameter configuration, feature/label variant, benchmark, filter, cost/delay alternative, failed experiment and decision. Distinguish computational failures from completed poor results. Preserve all predecessor records; corrections create new versions. E1, E2, E8. |
| 4 | Build one feasible portfolio simulation specification | Every eligible signal receives a deterministic disposition under finite capital and simultaneous-signal priority. Include stale, rejected, unfilled and partial outcomes. Specify contract-lot rounding, shared-leg netting, legging exposure, treasury treatment and manual availability. Never sum individually funded pair results as if each had the full account. |
| 5 | Run nested chronological development | Inside each outer training interval, use earlier inner training and later validation intervals to select every learned transform, hedge ratio, universe screen, regime rule and hyperparameter. Calibration, if used, also precedes the inner/outer test. Fit only labels available at that origin. Evaluate the frozen selection procedure on the next outer block. E5–E7. |
| 6 | Keep testing and research decisions distinct | Concatenate outer test results in calendar order once per timestamp. Follow the registered boundary policy for open positions rather than silently closing/reopening them. A later scheduled refit may use earlier outcomes after they mature; it cannot change the already-recorded forecasts or selection algorithm. If outer results cause redesign, those results are development evidence, not final confirmation. Book each cash/P&L increment once in the continuing portfolio; retain positions across refits under their registered exit/control rule unless the policy explicitly changes management. Assign forecasts to their decision origin, retain their maturity tails and do not count overlapping labels as extra wealth observations. |
| 7 | Apply selection-adjusted and dependence-aware inference | Choose the error target in advance: superiority of the searched family, specific discoveries, or exploratory FDR. Preserve joint cross-strategy dependence; test block-length and subperiod sensitivity. Do not compute a precise p-value when stationarity, enough independent information or complete search history is not defensible. E8–E15. |
| 8 | Freeze and evaluate the final chronological holdout | Lock the registry version, data rules, costs, scenarios, benchmark, primary statistic, horizon and acceptance rules before any performance-sensitive holdout access. Evaluate one frozen candidate or a declared family with the required multiplicity adjustment. Report all registered metrics and scenarios, including failures. |
| 9 | Start prospective shadow evaluation | Timestamp signals, quotes, manual response, hypothetical orders and capital dispositions before outcomes occur. Reconcile all attempted signals, retain zero-signal days, and compare executable outcomes with forecast assumptions. No retrospective discretionary omission. Freeze efficacy assessment dates; risk intervention is allowed but recorded as part of the policy. |
| 10 | Issue an outcome and change log | Classify each evidence gate as accepted for its narrow claim, rejected/invalid or inconclusive. Publish window-level X, uncertainty assumptions, missing-data sensitivity, funding stress and negative findings. Every material redesign becomes a new version requiring fresh future evidence. |

**Acceptance rejection inconclusive outcomes**

| Outcome | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| Invalid for the stated claim | Future information, unresolved historical availability, leaked labels, unregistered final-period redesign, materially incomplete search history, infeasible capital aggregation or favorable invented fills invalidate confirmatory interpretation. Preserve results as exploratory if informative; correction requires a new version and fresh evidence. |
| Statistical acceptance for a narrowly specified claim | The frozen, executable policy beats the preregistered economically meaningful matched-LQDT hurdle with the chosen selection-adjusted uncertainty criterion under defensible dependence assumptions, survives the required cost/benchmark sensitivity and is not driven solely by an unrepeatable episode. A proposed criterion is a one-sided 95% lower confidence bound above δ_H at a prespecified review, with familywise control where applicable. This is a proposed governance rule, not an observed pass or probability of future success. The bound must be valid for the selected claim: genuinely untouched evaluation of a prechosen policy, or a justified simultaneous/post-selection method for the declared family, with review multiplicity handled. A nominal winner-only interval or passing calibration scores is insufficient. Passing probabilities is not required for a deterministic policy that makes no probability claim. |
| Reject the economic claim or project version | A defensible upper uncertainty bound falls below the declared hurdle, or the rule fails a mandatory data/operational/funding constraint. Report whether the rejection concerns statistical usefulness, implementation feasibility or risk; a funding failure does not prove the predictive signal false. |
| Inconclusive | The interval spans the hurdle; selection/dependence corrections are too unstable; few episodes dominate; cost/quote/tax ambiguity changes the sign; or prospective exposure is too short. Failure to reject zero is not proof of no effect, and a positive point estimate is not a pass. The honest next step may be simpler hypotheses, more prospective observation or ending the research—not necessarily more parameter search. |

**Falsification criteria and possible project changes**

| Possible change | Falsification or failure |
| --- | --- |
| Reject causal interpretation; rebuild vintage-aware inputs, simpler contract-level features or later decision times before new validation. | Appending future observations changes earlier decisions; reconstructed signal uses future dividend/roll/revision information. |
| Reject the currently executable claim; consider a lower-turnover/slower or simpler hypothesis as a new research version, not a repaired old pass. | Net matched-LQDT advantage disappears under a plausible predeclared delay/cost/quote treatment or one feasible portfolio. |
| Reduce the hypothesis family, use an explicit economic prior, extend prospective observation or report episode-specific evidence; do not manufacture sample size from more bars or resamples. | Selection-adjusted uncertainty is uninformative or results depend on one economic episode. |
| Remove probabilities or use an explicitly uncalibrated score; reassess labels/horizon and obtain fresh calibration evidence. | Out-of-time calibration fails, score skill vanishes against the training-only baseline, or cost uncertainty reverses the probability threshold. |
| Do not infer operational feasibility from statistical results. Restrict admissible opportunities, simplify response requirements or stop at shadow research; any new policy needs its own validation. | Manual availability, capital conflicts, margin/VM or plausible liquidation paths violate mandatory constraints. |
| Mark the old claim conditional/expired for that regime, retain its history and restart versioned prospective assessment rather than pooling away the failure. | A new structural regime breaks the validated information, execution or payoff relationship. |
