# Economically Justified Futures Spread Universe on MOEX: Families, Pairs, and Historical Scope

## 1. Framing: What Counts as an “Economically Justified” Futures Pair on MOEX

For MOEX derivatives, economically justified medium‑term spreads are those where both legs are tied by a clear structural pricing relation rather than by historical correlation alone. In this setting, five broad families matter:

- **Calendar spreads within the same futures family**  
  – Index, single‑stock, FX, commodity, and interest‑rate futures, including those with official MOEX calendar spread instruments and margin discounts.

- **Inter‑commodity (product) spreads**  
  – Primarily refinery and energy chains (Brent vs gasoline/diesel) and some metal spreads where both legs trade on MOEX.

- **Cross‑term fixed‑income / rate spreads**  
  – OFZ basket futures by duration and (historically) RUONIA / RUSFAR / RGBI structures.

- **Equity structures (index vs sector / single stock)**  
  – For MOEX: structurally justified mostly as index vs sector index, or index vs perpetual on same index, not arbitrary stock pairs.

- **FX structures (spot‑like vs funding‑heavy)**  
  – Standard quarterly currency futures vs daily perpetual futures on the same currency pair.

All other “pairs” (e.g. SBER vs GAZP, random equity duos, crypto vs equity) should be treated as **off‑universe** for this project until and unless a specific economic mechanism (not correlation) is proven.

The core actionable takeaway is: start from the exchange’s own spread infrastructure (calendar spreads, inter‑commodity spread groups, margin discounts) and from cost‑of‑carry/production relations, and explicitly exclude anything that is just “two correlated prices”.

## 2. MOEX Futures Families and Contract Types Relevant for Spreads

**MOEX officially classifies futures into index, stock, FX, interest‑rate and commodity futures; since 2023–2026 a separate layer of daily “perpetual” futures with automatic prolongation appeared.** The “Options and Futures Contracts Information” page lists categories “Index futures | Single stock futures | FX futures | Fixed income futures | Commodity futures”[1]. General characteristics include underlying asset, contract code, lot size, minimum tick and its ruble value[2][3]. Key information documents (KID) exist for each asset class[4].

### 2.1 Index futures

MOEX trades:

- RTS Index futures (RTS‑… standard, RTSM mini)[5][6].  
  Specification: multiplier 100 × index, tick 10 points, tick value USD 0.2 converted into RUB for the standard contract[7][8].
- MOEX Russia Index futures: MIX (standard) and MXI (mini)[9][10].
- Sector index futures (Oil & Gas, Financials, Metals & Mining, Consumer)[11][12], and historical contracts like OGI oil & gas index futures with documented contract codes and expiry windows[13].
- RGBI government bond index futures (2022–2025)[14][15].
- Perpetual daily futures on MOEX Russia Index and RGBI: IMOEXF and RGBIF, cash‑settled and automatically prolonged each day[16][17][18].

Index futures are explicitly described as **cash‑settled**, without physical delivery[19][20].

### 2.2 Single‑stock futures

MOEX trades physically settled futures on Russian shares (SBRF, GAZP, LKOH, ALRS, etc.)[21][22][23]. Generic spec for stock futures includes:

- Physical delivery via automatic T+2 trades in the equity market on expiry[24][25].
- Contract months March, June, September, December[26].

There are also perpetual daily futures on foreign shares (AMDF, AMZNF, etc.) with automatic prolongation and cash settlement[27][28].

### 2.3 FX futures

Two distinct FX futures families:

- **Standard quarterly FX futures** on USD/RUB (Si), EUR/RUB (Eu), CNY/RUB (CNY‑…)[29][30]. These are cash‑settled in RUB, quoted in RUB per unit of foreign currency[31].
- **Daily perpetual FX futures** with automatic prolongation (USDRUBF, EURRUBF, CNYRUBF) where the settlement price references the latest Central Bank fixing[32][33]. MOEX explicitly describes them as “daily futures contracts on RUB quoted currency pairs with automatic extension” and “cash‑settled”[34].

Deliverable FX futures exist under the separate **currency engine** (“Поставочные фьючерсы” market with boards FUTS/FUTN)[35][36], but these are structurally different (spot‑style with optional settlement dates[37]) and not part of the standard FORTS RFUD list.

### 2.4 Interest‑rate and fixed‑income futures

Historically and partially currently:

- **OFZ basket futures** (OFZ2, OFZ4, OFZ6, OFZ10, OFZ15) by maturity bucket, documented on a dedicated MOEX “Фьючерсы ОФЗ” page and in the FUTOFZ FTP directory[38][39][40]. Tick data CSV explicitly lists multiple duration buckets in 2022 (e.g. OFZ2‑3.22, OFZ4‑3.22, etc.)[41].
- **RGBI index futures** (RB… codes, RGBI‑9.27 / RBU7 etc.), launched 28.02.2022 and ceased 21.04.2025[14][15].
- **Short‑term rate futures**: RUONIA futures are documented as interest‑rate futures on the overnight index with 1‑month term and quote “100 minus expected RUONIA”[42]; RUSFAR futures existed 2020–2022 in a market‑making program[43][44].

Most fixed‑income futures beyond perpetual RGBIF are currently **historical** and must be treated as such for universe reconstruction.

### 2.5 Commodity futures

Official MOEX commodity derivatives segments include[45][46]:

- **Energy**: Brent (BR), Light Sweet Crude Oil (CL, discontinued), WTI (relaunched 2026), natural gas (NG)[47][48][49][50].
- **Refined products**: regular gasoline AI92, premium gasoline AI95, diesel (DTL), launched as ruble‑quoted cash‑settled futures[51].
- **Precious metals**: gold (GOLD / GL‑…, GLDRUBF perpetual, GOLDM mini), silver (SILV), platinum (PLT), palladium (PLD, historical)[52][53][54][55][56][57].
- **Base metals**: aluminum (ALUM, ALMN), copper (COPPER / Co), nickel, zinc[58][59][60][61].
- **Agricultural commodities**: Russian Wheat Index futures (WHEAT), raw sugar (SUGR), Russian sugar[62][63][64][65].

Most commodity futures are cash‑settled, but some precious metals originally were deliverable on MOEX’s precious metals spot market[55][66].

### 2.6 Perpetual (“вечные”) futures as a distinct type

MOEX characterizes perpetuals as **one‑day futures with automatic prolongation**, not expiry‑less swaps. Official FAQ (Aug 2025) describes them as contracts “без даты исполнения” in the trading sense but technically rolled daily, with a funding mechanism governed by parameters K1 and K2[67][68]. Settlement into quarterly futures is possible once per quarter at the initiative of one side[69]. Families exist on indices (IMOEXF, RGBIF), FX (USDRUBF, EURRUBF, CNYRUBF), gold (GLDRUBF), U.S. equities and ETFs (AMDF, AMZNF, SP500F, QQQF, etc.)[16][27][54][70].

These have their own economics (funding vs spot index), so any spread involving a perpetual leg must model the funding explicitly and cannot be treated simply as another maturity in a calendar strip.

## 3. MOEX Spread Infrastructure and Where the Exchange Itself Sees Economic Links

**MOEX explicitly implements and supports several spread types: calendar spreads, inter‑commodity spreads (МКС) and margin‑discounted spread groups. These are the first candidates for an “economically justified” universe.**

### 3.1 Calendar spreads

MOEX’s calendar spread service (“межконтрактные спреды”, synthetic matching) allows simultaneous buy/sell of two futures on the same underlying with different expiries[71][72][73]. The calendar spread instruments have their own tickers (e.g. GOLD‑6.23‑9.23, BR‑2.27‑3.27, MIX‑9.23‑12.23)[74][75][76]; MOEX publishes parameters and historical trade archives for these spread contracts[71][74].

Economic basis:

- For indices and equities: standard cost‑of‑carry relation \( F = S e^{(r - q)T} \), with funding rate \(r\), dividend yield \(q\).
- For commodities: storage, convenience yield, seasonal demand.
- For FX: interest‑rate parity between currencies.
- For rates/bonds: forward rates implied by the yield curve (OFZ, RUONIA, RGBI).

MOEX’s documentation of costs of carry and inter‑month spreads (including for gold, silver, oil, copper, OFZ) plus margin discounts for MKS groups[77][78][79][80] confirms that the exchange views these as structurally linked.

### 3.2 Inter‑commodity spreads (МКС) and margin discounts

The “Parameters of Derivatives market” and dedicated news releases describe **inter‑contract spreads (МКС)** across futures and options with explicit margin discounts[77][78]. Examples:

- Groups with **50% margin discount** for spreads (window_size = 0.5) between certain contracts[78].
- Precious metals MKS groups where PLTM and PLDM are added with **100% discount** (full offset) for opposite positions[80].
- Inclusion of perpetuals (e.g. GLDRUBF, IMOEXF) in MKS groups with spread discounts[79].

The presence of these groups is a strong signal that MOEX’s risk department recognizes stable co‑movement rooted in economics (common underlying or production chain), not just empirical correlation.

### 3.3 Product and crack spreads in energy

MOEX’s commodity derivatives materials explicitly refer to **Brent/WTI spreads** as a product spread, defining it as “the difference in prices of the contracts with different oil grades which have an expiration in the same month”[41]. A second layer follows from the launch of gasoline and diesel futures: MOEX’s press release emphasises hedging of refining margins using crude and product futures[51], mirroring classical crack spread logic (refinery margin = refined product value – crude input cost)[81].

These MOEX‑documented relations justify:

- Brent vs WTI (cross‑grade) spreads.
- Brent vs AI92 / AI95 / DTL (crack‑style) spreads, same delivery month.

### 3.4 Precious metals ratio and spreads

MOEX trades gold and silver futures, publishes metals indices (e.g. RUSILVER)[60] and supports calendar spreads on metals[47][82]. External futures literature and MOEX references suggest **gold–silver ratio** trading as a standard metals spread where both legs share macro drivers (inflation, monetary regime) but differ in industrial demand[61]. For MOEX this is economically grounded as both legs are precious metals denominated in RUB and linked to international USD benchmarks.

### 3.5 Fixed‑income spread structures

OFZ basket futures and RGBI futures were designed specifically to replicate portions of the Russian government yield curve[38][43]. The FUTOFZ directory contains methodology on duration buckets (OFZ2,4,6,10,15) and CTD (“cheapest‑to‑deliver”) mechanics[40]. Calendar spreads between OFZ futures of different maturities and between OFZ and RGBI, or RGBI vs perpetual RGBIF, have clear interest‑rate and term structure interpretations (steepener/flatteners, basis between basket and index).

### 3.6 FX term structure spreads

Comparing standard quarterly FX futures (e.g. Si‑12.26) with perpetual USDRUBF on the same pair creates a term structure spread combining:

- Interest‑rate differential (implied in the quarterly futures price).
- Funding spread and index methodology (in the perpetual’s funding mechanism and CBR fixing linkage)[32][33][69].

MOEX includes perpetual FX futures in MKS groups with margin discounts[79], indicating it expects consistent economic relations with their quarterly counterparts.

## 4. Contract Families and Spread Hypotheses: What Is In and What Is Out

Below is a compact universe‑level view by family. “Admit / Exclude pre‑stat” refers to whether the family should be pre‑admitted to the candidate universe before any statistical testing.

### 4.1 Index and equity structures

**Admit:**

- **MIX / MXI calendar spreads (standard vs mini inter‑month).** Same underlying index, different contract size and tick; MOEX lists calendar spreads and margin discounts[71][76].
- **RTS / RTSM calendar spreads.** Same logic as above; mini vs standard have proportional exposure[5][7][83].
- **Perpetual IMOEXF vs nearest MIX future.** Economic basis: both track MOEX Russia Index; difference reflects funding, dividend accrual, and technical differences in settlement; MOEX includes perpetuals in spread groups[16][79].
- **RGBI vs OFZ baskets (historical).** Term structure and basis between index and CTD‑style baskets, as per OFZ duration methodology[40][43].

**Exclude pre‑stat:**

- Arbitrary **single‑stock vs single‑stock** futures (e.g. SBER vs GAZP). Co‑movement is driven by sector and macro but lacks a strict pricing relation; equity pairs trading literature exists but requires stock‑level microstructure and cointegration tests for each pair.
- **Index vs single stock** spreads unless the stock is structurally part of the index with stable weight and the strategy is defined as “beta‑hedged stock vs index”. That becomes more a **stock‑beta** strategy than a symmetric spread.

Condition for possible later inclusion: documented stable cointegration plus robust fundamental argument (e.g. dual‑listed pairs, holding company vs main asset).

### 4.2 FX structures

**Admit:**

- **Calendar spreads on standard FX futures** (Si, Eu, CNY‑…), as provided by MOEX FX futures spread groups[58].
- **Standard quarterly vs perpetual FX futures** on the same pair (Si‑… vs USDRUBF, Eu‑… vs EURRUBF, CNY‑… vs CNYRUBF). Economic basis is FX forward vs funding‑indexed perpetual tied to CBR fixing; MOEX recognises them in MKS with margin discounts[32][33][79].
- **Cross‑maturity spreads within perpetual FX** are **not** meaningful (they are single‑maturity daily contracts), but **time‑series mean‑reversion of the basis (quarterly – perpetual)** is a viable hypothesis.

**Exclude pre‑stat:**

- **Cross‑currency FX spreads** (e.g. USD/RUB vs CNY/RUB) as generic statistical pairs; without a strategy explicitly based on uncovered interest parity or triangular arbitrage with a third leg (e.g. USD/CNY), the economic link is weak on MOEX where only some crosses trade.

### 4.3 Commodity structures

**Admit:**

- **Calendar spreads** within each commodity family:  
  – Brent (BR‑m.y calendars), WTI (history with a gap and relaunch), natural gas (NG‑…), gasoline AI92/AI95, diesel DTL, base metals, precious metals (GOLD, SILV, PLT)[47][48][49][50][58][82].  
  MOEX publishes calendar spread instruments and archives for many of these[71][74][75].
- **Brent vs WTI** (same month). MOEX materials explicitly describe Brent/WTI spread as a product spread, with defined economic meaning (two grades with common global demand but idiosyncratic logistics)[41][47].
- **Crack‑style spreads**:  
  – Brent vs AI92 / AI95 / DTL for same expiry month, mirroring refinery margins. MOEX’s gasoline and diesel launch press release frames use cases in terms of hedging refining profit[51], and back‑to‑back listing within the same energy complex enables such spreads.
- **Gold vs gold perpetual (GOLD‑… vs GLDRUBF vs GOLDM)**. Same underlying spot gold price; differences capture funding, roll and contract microstructure. MOEX adds GLDRUBF and GOLDM to MKS groups with discounts[58][61][54].
- **Gold vs silver** ratio spreads (GOLD vs SILV). Both are RUB‑settled, linked to international benchmarks, with a long‑used ratio as macro indicator[60][61][62].

**Exclude pre‑stat:**

- **Inter‑metal spreads without clear chain** (e.g. copper vs wheat, platinum vs aluminum). These might have macro correlation but lack direct economic identity linkage.
- **Spreads involving commodities with structural breaks or long trading gaps**, e.g. Light Sweet Crude Oil CL futures, halted in 2020 and officially ceased 2022[52][54][55]. They may be usable for historical research but not for forward‑looking strategies unless/until re‑listed with stable history.

### 4.4 Fixed‑income and rate structures

**Admit (historical universe):**

- **OFZ duration bucket spreads** (e.g. OFZ2 vs OFZ10, OFZ4 vs OFZ6) and their calendar spreads. MOEX’s duration methodology and CTD info explicitly define these baskets and list multiple buckets[40][44][75]. These spreads are pure curve trades (steepeners/flatteners).
- **RUONIA / RUSFAR calendar spreads, basis vs OFZ or RGBI** while they were listed[42][43]. Economic basis is forward rate expectation vs bond‑implied rates.

**Current constraints:**

- As of 2026, **many bond and rate futures programs ended** (RGBI futures ceased April 2025[15]; RUSFAR program ended 2022[43]). For live trading they are either unavailable or very illiquid. They still matter for backtests up to their deactivation dates.

**Exclude pre‑stat:**

- Any fixed‑income pair without both legs simultaneously listed and with adequate joint liquidity; structural gaps (e.g. RUONIA > 2023 vs absent OFZ futures) break the economic hedge.

### 4.5 Perpetual vs non‑perpetual futures beyond FX and gold

Perpetual futures exist on indices, FX, gold and foreign stocks/ETFs[16][27][54][70], but **not every possible pairing with standard futures is economically symmetric**:

- **Admit:** same‑underlying pairs (IMOEXF vs MIX, RGBIF vs historical RGBI, USDRUBF vs Si, GLDRUBF vs GOLD).  
- **Exclude pre‑stat:** cross‑asset pairs (e.g. SP500F vs MIX, AMZNF vs SBERF), despite possible correlation; their economics involve different underlyings and currency/settlement structures.

## 5. Example Pairs with Explicit Exposure, Tick and P&L Mechanics

Below are **illustrative, not prescriptive** examples showing how exposure, hedge ratios and P&L per tick are computed. Margin figures are deliberately omitted because they are volatile and broker‑specific.

### 5.1 Example 1 (current): Calendar spread on Brent oil futures BR‑10.26 vs BR‑11.26

From MOEX BR‑10.26 contract page[56][39]:

- Lot: 10 barrels.  
- Price tick: 0.01 USD.  
- Tick value: ~8.44 RUB per tick (for given RUB/USD rate)[56][39].

Assume:

- Long 1 BR‑11.26, short 1 BR‑10.26 (classic long deferred / short near).

Then:

- **Hedged exposure in barrels:** 10 long and 10 short → net 0 barrels.  
- **Spread quote:** \( Spread = F_{\text{11.26}} - F_{\text{10.26}} \) in USD per barrel.  
- **One‑tick move in the spread:** If far contract rises by one tick and near stays flat, spread increases by 0.01 USD per barrel; P&L = +0.01 × 10 barrels = 0.1 USD ≈ stepprice (converted to RUB).

So **per tick of the spread**, P&L per 1:1 calendar position is approximately one contract tick value (in RUB), assuming identical tick sizes and FX rate.

If BR calendar spread BR‑10.26‑11.26 is traded via MOEX’s spread instrument (as for other months[70]), the exchange quotes and margins the spread directly, but the fundamental exposures are identical.

### 5.2 Example 2 (current): Gold futures GOLD‑6.27 vs perpetual GLDRUBF

From MOEX gold futures and perpetual futures materials[24][54][66]:

- Standard GOLD‑6.27: cash‑settled Ruble‑denominated futures on 1 gram (lot and tick specification available on contract page; one MOEX document cites lot 1 gram and tick 0.1 RUB, tick value 0.1 RUB[43]).  
- GLDRUBF: perpetual settlement futures on gold, tied to MOEX gold fixing in RUB, cash‑settled with funding mechanism[54][66].

Suppose:

- Long 10 GOLD‑6.27, short 10 GLDRUBF.

Then:

- **Nominal exposure:** 10 g long and 10 g short → net 0 grams.  
- **P&L mechanics:**  
  – Any change in spot gold price impacts both legs; the spread reflects differences in implied funding, carry, and index methodology.  
  – One tick up in GOLD with GLDRUBF unchanged yields +10 × tickvalue(GOLD) RUB.  
  – For GLDRUBF, funding charges/credits accumulate daily; their difference vs quarterly contract’s carry should drive medium‑term mean‑reversion.

This pair leaves **no open metal or FX exposure**, but **does leave exposure to MOEX funding formula parameters K1/K2 and possible regulatory changes**.

### 5.3 Example 3 (historical): OFZ2 vs OFZ10 duration‑bucket spread

Based on MOEX OFZ futures methodology and futpricesgeneric.csv examples[40][44][75]:

- One OFZ2 contract represents a basket of 1–3‑year OFZ, duration ~2 years.  
- One OFZ10 contract represents a basket of 8–12‑year OFZ, duration ~10 years.

If contract multipliers are identical (one notional unit per RUB of bond price), then:

- **DV01 (per 1% rate move) is much larger for OFZ10** than for OFZ2; a duration‑neutral steepener with unit rates exposure needs approx. 5× OFZ2 vs 1× OFZ10 (10/2 ratio).
- Thus, a **duration‑hedged 5 OFZ2 long vs 1 OFZ10 short** creates a curve steepening position with minimal parallel‑shift risk.

Exact hedge ratios require reading the “OFZ Futures Duration Methodology.pdf”[40] to retrieve official durations and conversion factors on the date of interest.

## 6. Historical Universe Reconstruction: Families, Start–End Windows, and Structural Breaks

For a point‑in‑time universe you need **family‑level windows** where: (1) contracts actually existed, (2) there was a plausible minimum liquidity for both legs, and (3) no structural break made the economics non‑stationary.

Below are families where MOEX documents at least parts of that timeline.

### 6.1 Index futures

- RTS index futures: launched 2005, still active. MOEX news repeatedly states “The RTS Index futures contract was launched in 2005”[51][52][83]. Mini RTSM launched later (2021)[84].
- MOEX Russia Index (MICEX/MOEX): futures validated from at least 2017 via MOEX Russian Index Futures docs whose validity spans 2017–2018 and then 2018–2022, ceasing 03.04.2022[49][29]. Current MIX/MXI contracts continue this line with modern specs.
- Sector index futures (OGI Oil & Gas, others): documentation and contract examples around 2018–2022[12][32][33]. Some may be discontinued or illiquid today; each sector contract’s validity period must be taken from its specification document.

For **historical calendars and index–perpetual spreads**, avoid mixing periods before/after major regulatory shifts (e.g. dividend taxation changes, sanctions), as these alter cost‑of‑carry.

### 6.2 FX futures

- Standard USD/RUB, EUR/RUB futures (Si, Eu): present on MOEX for many years; calendar spread examples exist for at least 2016 (Si‑3.16‑6.16)[13].  
- Perpetual FX futures USDRUBF, EURRUBF, CNYRUBF: documented as “daily futures contracts with automatic prolongation” in MOEX perpetual futures materials and FX derivatives pages[12][25][32].  
- Deliverable FX futures (currency engine “futures” market): have their own timeline and are structurally distinct; unless you explicitly use them, they can be left out of the core spread universe.

Structural breaks: February–March 2022 sanctions, change in CBR FX market operations, and MOEX halts in certain FX pairs (e.g. JPY suspension 2022[54]) likely alter cost‑of‑carry and liquidity.

### 6.3 Commodity futures

- Brent crude oil (BR): futures have been available at least since 2017–2018; MOEX news mentions adding BR‑1.18 etc. and commodity derivatives overview confirms ongoing listing[45][32][40].
- Light Sweet Crude Oil (CL): launched 2018; trading suspended in April 2020 due to negative prices and officially ceased as of a 25.04.2022 specification marked “Ceased to be in force”[52][53][54][55][56].  
  → For backtests, CL spreads only make sense 2018–2020, carefully excluding the negative price shock regime unless explicitly modeled.
- WTI futures (relabeled or newly launched) were **relaunched in 2026** according to Kommersant, after the CL program ended[42][55][56].  
  → Pre‑2020 WTI/CL is a different universe from post‑2026 WTI/BR.
- Natural gas futures (NG): launched 03.02.2020[43][50], still active.
- Gold: deliverable futures launched 11.10.2018[44]; perpetual GLDRUBF launched 11.07.2023[66]; mini GOLDM launched 18.02.2026[64].
- Silver: MOEX resumed silver trading 11.10.2019[45][46].
- Aluminum: trading resumed 22.11.2018 after prior suspension[49][43].
- OFZ, wheat, sugar: each has its own start dates (e.g. Russian Wheat Index futures described as unique instrument, active before 2022[48][49][62][65]).

### 6.4 Fixed‑income / rates

- OFZ futures: clearly active at least 2014–2022, from the OFZ4‑6.14, OFZ4‑6.21, etc. examples and FUTOFZ CSVs[72][75].  
  Programs may have been reconfigured; liquidity likely concentrated around 2018–2021.
- RGBI index futures: active 28.02.2022–21.04.2025[56][59].
- RUSFAR rate futures program: 01.09.2020–01.06.2022[12][21].
- RUONIA futures: introduced earlier; remain listed according to MOEX derivatives pages[36]. Liquidity and usable history must be checked via ISS for each contract.

### 6.5 Crypto perpetual futures

MOEX launched perpetual futures on BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, TRX on 22.09.2026 for qualified investors[24][25]. They are cash‑settled in RUB and form a new asset class. For this project:

- **Economically justified intra‑MOEX spreads** (e.g. BTC vs ETH) lack cost‑of‑carry structure and rely on macro‑crypto relationships and cross‑exchange arbitrage.
- These are best **excluded pre‑stat** from a conservative universe focused on MOEX‑native structural spreads.

## 7. Scenario Analysis: Where Economic Links Can Break

Even structurally grounded spreads can fail if a background assumption changes.

### 7.1 Funding and regulatory regime shifts (indices, FX, perpetuals)

- **Scenario A: Stable funding environment.** Cost‑of‑carry models hold; perpetual funding parameters K1/K2 and index dividends stay relatively stable; spreads like IMOEXF–MIX or USDRUBF–Si mean‑revert around predictable levels.
- **Scenario B: Shock regime.** CBR policy shifts, capital controls, or MOEX methodology changes (e.g. index composition, fixing rules) cause structural breaks. Then past spread behaviour is a poor guide; strategies relying on historical mean reversion may suffer extended drifts.

Indicators of regime change: amendments to specification PDFs (new “validity period” versions), MOEX news on methodology updates, or abnormal sustained basis.

### 7.2 Underlying market microstructure changes (commodities & OFZ)

- **Scenario A: Continuous, liquid market.** Brent, gasoline, OFZ trade with tight spreads; calendar MKS discounts reflect low basis volatility. Spreads behave as cost‑of‑carry models predict.
- **Scenario B: Liquidity collapse or trading suspensions.** Events like April 2020 oil negative pricing or trading halts in wheat futures for risk reasons[22] render prior spread statistics unreliable; order book fragility and clearing actions dominate.

For medium‑term strategies, any historical segment containing suspensions, negative pricing or limit‑down cascades should either be excluded from calibration or modeled as a distinct crisis regime.

### 7.3 Exchange product lifecycle (start/end of families)

Trading strategies that span the **start or end** of a contract family (e.g. RGBI futures 2022–2025, CL futures ending 2022) are exposed to:

- Illiquidity near program termination.
- Changed margin rules or spread discounts as the exchange unwinds interest.

For universe definition, treat these windows as bounded; don’t extrapolate beyond the documented “validity period: from… to… ceased to be in force” in MOEX specification docs[42][52][56].

## 8. Practical Filters: What to Exclude Before Statistical Search

Given the project’s small initial capital (≈30k RUB) and target to avoid avoidable drawdowns, you should **reduce the combinatorial universe before any statistical screening**:

1. **Exclude non‑MOEX‑hedgable residual exposures.**  
   – Any two futures whose combination leaves a **third unhedged dimension** (e.g. RUB credit risk, foreign equity index risk) that is not explicitly desired. Example: SP500F vs MIX involves both U.S. equity beta and FX risk which can’t be neutralized with MOEX instruments at the same granularity.

2. **Exclude pairs where legs differ in settlement type or corporate actions.**  
   – Physical single‑stock futures vs cash‑settled index: dividends, buybacks, and corporate actions introduce asymmetric jumps; these are **complex basis trades**, not clean spreads.  
   – Stock perpetuals vs quarterly stock futures, if the perpetual uses a different fixing or omits dividends, require detailed modeling before inclusion.

3. **Exclude cross‑asset correlations without chain logic.**  
   – E.g. SBRF vs BR (oil), GOLD vs RTS, BTC vs gold. These are thematic trades, not neutral spreads; any “mean reversion” is incidental.

4. **Exclude families with poor or discontinuous joint liquidity.**  
   – OFZ futures with very low volumes after 2022; CL futures after their crisis; thin agricultural contracts where one leg trades sporadically.  
   – This is especially important given manual execution with 5–15 minute reaction times.

5. **Treat crypto, foreign ETF and exotic perpetuals as out‑of‑scope for baseline universe.**  
   – They may become interesting later, but initial work should focus on MOEX‑centric structures with long histories and robust documentation.

## 9. Preliminary Suitability: 30k RUB Test vs Future 10–20m RUB Scale

Without broker‑specific initial margin data, suitability must stay **conditional**:

- **Index mini futures (MXI, RTSM)**: designed for smaller lot sizes and lower initial margin[76][45]. One calendar spread (near vs next) is plausibly within a 30k budget if broker margin offsets approximate MOEX’s MKS discounts (50–100% for some groups[51][80]). For 10–20m, standard MIX/RTS size is natural.
- **Perpetual index futures (IMOEXF)**: very low notional per contract (lot size 10 index points[3][38]). Spreads like IMOEXF vs MIX or pure IMOEXF mean‑reversion could be implemented with multiple contracts even at 30k, though funding‑rate risk must be monitored.
- **Brent and gold**: one BR or GOLD contract’s margin is substantial but single‑contract calendar spreads may fit 30k if the broker recognises MKS discounts; otherwise they are probably too large for the initial budget. For 10–20m, multi‑leg energy and metals strips become feasible.
- **Gasoline AI92/AI95, diesel, base metals**: notional and margin may be too high for the test budget, especially with less stable liquidity; they look more like **medium‑term targets** for the scaled phase.

Without R01/R04 data, these assessments remain **conditional**; broker margin schedules, MOEX SPAN parameters and actual best‑bid/offer spreads must be checked before concluding that “it fits”.

## 10. Recommendations and Next Steps

### 10.1 Immediate: Define a Narrow, Economically Clean Candidate Universe

1. **Calendar spreads within families with proven MOEX support and history**  
   – Index: MIX/MXI, RTS/RTSM (near vs next or seasonal quarter pairs).  
   – FX: Si, Eu, CNY‑… (quarterly calendars).  
   – Commodities: Brent, GOLD, SILV, NG, AI92/AI95, where MOEX lists calendar spread instruments.  
   – Historical research: OFZ2/4/6/10/15 and RGBI within their active windows.

2. **Perpetual vs quarterly on same underlying**  
   – IMOEXF vs MIX; USDRUBF vs Si; GLDRUBF vs GOLD; RGBIF vs RGBI (historical).  
   – Treat these as **basis spreads** with explicit modeling of funding and dividend adjustments.

3. **Selected inter‑commodity spreads**  
   – Brent vs WTI; Brent vs AI92/AI95/DTL; GOLD vs SILV.  
   – Only same‑month pairs where MOEX explicitly lists both futures and, ideally, recognises MKS offset.

All other combinations should start **out of universe**, only added after strong theoretical and empirical justification.

### 10.2 Short‑Term: Data and Specification Infrastructure

1. **Use ISS API and FTP to build a point‑in‑time contract catalogue**  
   – ISS `/engines/futures/markets/forts/securities.json` for current futures, with fields SECID, SHORTNAME, LOTVOLUME, MINSTEP, STEPPRICE, is_traded[19][30][22].  
   – FTP `FORTS_LIST.TXT` and `FUTOFZ` directory for historical contracts and OFZ duration details[16][24][73][74][40].  
   – Parse specification PDFs (index, FX, commodities, OFZ) to attach validity windows and settlement types[4][36][24][52][56].

2. **Overlay MOEX spread instruments and MKS groups**  
   – Scrape calendar spread parameters pages (e.g. s3579, archive‑spreads.aspx) and MKS news (window_size, discount level)[71][74][77][78][51][80].  
   – Tag pairs where MOEX grants 50–100% margin offset as high‑priority.

3. **For each candidate pair, compute deterministic hedge ratios**  
   – Same‑multiplier calendars: 1:1.  
   – Mixed mini/standard (MIX vs MXI, RTS vs RTSM): ratio of multipliers (e.g. 1 standard = N minis).  
   – Duration‑neutral OFZ spreads using OFZ duration methodology[40].

### 10.3 Medium‑Term: Bring in Vidyamurthy’s Methodology Selectively

If you have access to Ganapathy Vidyamurthy’s “Pairs Trading: Quantitative Methods and Analysis”[20], the most relevant sections for this futures project are:

- **Cost‑of‑carry and cointegration for forwards/futures:** adapt to MOEX index, FX and commodity futures to distinguish true pricing relations from incidental correlations.
- **Spread construction and z‑score entry/exit rules:** can be reused in principle, but with careful **expiration handling** for non‑perpetual contracts and roll logic aligned with MOEX calendars and MKS transfer rules[50].

Transfer with caution:

- Equity pairs trading assumes continuous underlying and no expiry; MOEX futures require **explicit roll and basis modeling**.
- Many textbook examples ignore funding and funding regime shifts that are crucial for perpetual vs quarterly MOEX instruments.

### 10.4 Conditions for Revising the Universe

The proposed universe and exclusions should be revisited if:

- R01/R04 show broker margin recognises MKS discounts very differently from MOEX assumptions, changing feasibility.
- MOEX launches or discontinues key futures families (e.g. reintroduces OFZ, modifies perpetual funding formulas).
- Structural changes in Russian financial regulation or sanctions significantly alter cost‑of‑carry or liquidity.
