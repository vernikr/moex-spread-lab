**Entry and continuation tests**

- At each reliable timestamp and candidate integer vector n (including current open orders), value both portfolios at synchronized executable/defensible marks and conservative one-time exit charges: X=V−B, H=max(0,max prior X), D=H−X. Estimate stress S(n,ω,τ) for *joint* prices, spread basis, FX, failed hedge, depth, half-/no-offset margin, liquidation and costs over at least the 90-minute reaction plus closure/settlement window; U(n)>0 explicitly covers missing-data, tails and modeling error. Require X−maxω S−U≥−15,000 and analogous forward-looking D limit/review feasibility. If costs are already accrued in X, S counts only additional exit costs. Reject all n if one-lot S+U exceeds 15,000+X; it is not permissible to round up.
- Cash test for every intermediate event t≤τ: free pilot cash already credited at t + contractually verified liquid *pilot* assets realizable by t − pending debits/locked orders − adverse VM/debit(t) − stressed broker/exchange margin increase(t) − stress exit/hedge cash needs(t) ≥ broker-specific maintenance/settlement cushion(t)>0. Recompute account-level VTB KDS, maintenance, core haircuts and market-specific recourse *separately*; never insert unapproved core, hoped-for collateral sales or unarrived 15,000. Margin rise can force liquidation despite X above −15,000; benchmark accrual can worsen X without a futures cash debit; VM can drain cash despite viable economic hedge.
- Feasible gross exposure and temporary naked-leg size are independent constraints: compute n=(integer legs), gross notional Σ|nj|×contract value, minimum tick and multiplier, per-leg liquidity and one-leg worst fill before offset. At entry choose largest nonnegative integer n satisfying all single idea, correlated aggregate and funding inequalities, and maximum human-intervention quota; do not treat net delta or margin offsets as gross risk bounds. At continuation, if any inequality fails: stop additions, cancel live orders, attempt orderly reduction, record expected/actual breach and escalation rather than claim guaranteed halt.
