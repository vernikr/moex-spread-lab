# Honest Evidence Gates for Sparse MOEX Futures-Spread Research

## Executive Summary

- **Search Inflation**: Selecting the best of many specifications can make chance look like skill; White's Reality Check addresses the best model *among the models considered*, not an unrecorded wider search [11]. -> Register every attempted family, pair and selection decision before claiming an adjusted result.
- **Temporal Integrity**: An outcome that finishes after a proposed test decision was not yet a known training label. Reusing it contaminates that test, even if the signal itself uses old prices. -> Split by decision and information-availability times, and remove overlapping labels [9][6].
- **Executable, Not Synthetic, Prices**: MOEX distinguishes archived order-book, best-quote, trade-log and end-of-day products [8]; its futures settlement algorithm is not a fill rule [10]. -> Replay named contracts and realistic orders, or label execution feasibility unresolved.
- **Nonstationarity Is a Limit, Not a Parameter**: The authors of PBO warn that a structural break outside the historical sample can escape their assessment [6]. -> Require chronological and prospective evidence; neither PBO nor a deflated Sharpe ratio establishes future profitability.
- **Dependent Episodes**: Serial correlation can invalidate square-root-of-time Sharpe scaling [20], while a stationary bootstrap assumes weakly dependent, stationary observations [27]. -> Report calendar paths, overlapping exposures and distinct economic episodes before offering uncertainty intervals.
- **Benchmark Discipline**: LQDT is an exchange-listed fund unit, not a generic cash interest rate [7]. -> Compare the *whole relevant account* against continued LQDT ownership with identical external flows; charge only opportunity costs actually incurred.
- **Probability Restraint**: Proper scoring rules evaluate probabilities against outcomes [5], but common calibration methods can misstate error with finite bins [2]. -> Publish a horizon-specific win probability only if independently calibrated and useful beyond a base rate; otherwise abstain.
- **Separate Gates**: A 90-calendar-day pilot can reveal missed alerts, fills, funding incidents and compliance with operating rules. -> It is not, by its duration alone, evidence of a positive expected spread return. The RUB 5,000/10,000/15,000 rules below remain project-supplied, provisional controls, not statistical guarantees.

## 1. Evidence Scope: What Would Count as an Observation?

**Research cutoff: 2026-10-07.** I read the task context and public methodological papers, publisher abstracts/front matter, and MOEX pages identified below. I did **not** read a project dataset, repository, broker account, backtest, past investigation report, private contract, or tax determination. There were no attachments to audit. All project-specific performance, collateral eligibility, tax effects other than the expressly excluded LQDT long-term holding deduction, historical availability of free intraday data, and VTB/BCS treatment of the reported qualified-investor/KPUR status remain unknown. Proposed checks below have not been run.

The *observation unit* depends on the claim. A **bar** is a recorded market interval, not a new independent bet. A **leg fill** is an execution event, not an independent strategy outcome. A **trade** may overlap other positions, share financing and respond to the same shock. An **economic episode** groups linked entries, overlapping legs, rolls and exits driven by one opportunity or risk event. A **calendar account path** captures simultaneous exposures and the no-trade days. Report counts of each, the overlap graph, concentration by date and regime, and a defensible estimate or range for effective independent information. Never equate the number of bars or partial fills to sample size; do not invoke a universal "30 trades" rule. The 2018 Wiley contents explicitly distinguish "Overlapping Outcomes" from "Average Uniqueness of a Label"; those are reading locations, not evidence that this project's episodes have been measured [9].

**Decision rule:** No performance claim follows from this report. If vintage data, executable prices or a defensible count of economic episodes cannot be established, the correct result is **inconclusive**, even if a chart looks attractive.

## 2. Point-in-Time Leakage: Two Worked Cases and an Audit Matrix

**Hypothetical roll case.** Suppose a researcher forms a continuous futures series by changing earlier prices with a roll adjustment calculated when the next contract later trades. A rule evaluated at an earlier date then sees a level or spread defined using a future roll. Replacing that series with a present-day contract master compounds the error if contract availability, lot size or eligible pair list also changed. This is a hypothetical mechanism, **not** a finding that a particular MOEX feed does this. MOEX publishes distinct historical market-data products, while its current reference-data description says the instrument list and trading/risk parameters can change; neither statement establishes a complete historical as-of revision archive [8][14].

The prevention test is to replay each decision from archived contract identifiers, specifications and releases available by its timestamp. Calculate features from then-known inputs, keep a separately documented continuous series for research visualization, and book P&L on the actual contracts with explicit roll orders and costs. Recompute an old decision after adding future observations: its old signal **must not change**. If only the final, revised series exists, mark the affected historical result unusable for a point-in-time claim rather than guessing an adjustment.

**Documented market-state case.** A MOEX notice published **11 September 2025 at 15:29 Moscow time** said derivatives trading had been suspended **from 15:23**; a second notice published at **15:32** said orders could be entered or removed from **15:40** and trading would resume **at 15:55** [18][21]. A hypothetical simulator filling a 15:30 signal at an end-of-day settlement price invents an executable opportunity. A contemporaneous decision before 15:29 also cannot use the *notice* as an already published explanatory feature. Separate event-effective time, public-notice time, feed receipt time and permitted order/fill state. An end-of-day dataset or an undated, later-edited web page cannot establish what was known intraday. This dated episode shows why state transitions belong in both leakage and funding-stress tests; it is **not** evidence that this strategy traded that day.

| Pathway | Hypothetical contamination | Prevention or falsification test |
|---|---|---|
| Survivorship and universe selection | Only today's still-traded or attractive pairs appear in early years. | Reconstruct eligible instruments and exclusions *as of each date*, including expiries and failures; compare results before and after restoring omitted candidates. |
| Roll-adjusted contracts | Future adjustment changes a historical spread or hedge estimate. | Vintage replay, named legs and fixed ex-ante roll rules; test that earlier signals remain invariant as future rolls are appended. |
| Future parameters, events and dividends | A later-announced dividend, exchange specification, risk parameter or hindsight regime label enters an earlier signal. | Archive publication/receipt and effective times; perturb or hide later records and require unchanged prior signals. |
| Late revisions and stale prices | Today's corrected candle or official closing price is treated as the contemporaneous quote. | Retain source vintage and ingestion time; compare first-seen with final values. If vintages are unavailable, bound or exclude the affected decisions. |
| Overlapping labels | A training episode opened before the test date closes after its first decision. | Purge that training label until its outcome is knowable; audit every label's start, end and release times. |
| Reused final holdout | A disappointing result leads to revised thresholds on the same holdout. | Record access and decisions; retire that window for the *revised* hypothesis and wait for new prospective data. |
| Post-hoc costs and delays | Fees, failed fills or the worst delay are omitted; the best delay scenario is called "base case." | Freeze the primary cost/fill/delay rule first; report all prespecified stresses and failed attempts. |
| Settlement instead of execution | An official settlement mark is booked as two simultaneous executable leg fills. | Check market state, contemporaneous bid/ask, available size and order timing; otherwise report marks separately from fills. |

MOEX describes futures settlement prices as computed using filtered bid, ask and last-trade inputs; it does **not** describe that computation as the price at which the portfolio could trade [10]. **Decision insight:** a failure in vintage reconstruction or execution evidence is a *data-feasibility failure*, not a low p-value to repair later.

## 3. Chronological Selection: Purge Outcomes, Nest Choices, Freeze the End

A forward test must reconstruct the information set at each decision. For each proposed entry at time `d`, store the last market observation, each feature's lookback and release/ingestion time, signal time, possible fill time, the episode's label maturity or exit time `e`, and any revised-data vintage. A training example qualifies for a subsequent test decision only if its required outcome and other training inputs were knowable before that decision. Purge a boundary-crossing label according to its **actual** `e`, rather than dropping an arbitrary number of bars. A causal lookback that shares *past* observations with the test does not by itself introduce future information; an unreleased label, hindsight transform or feature does. For a scheme allowing post-test records in training, also exclude records whose information windows overlap the test and apply an embargo sufficient for actual holding and release delays. In a strictly forward split, future records never enter training, so a generic post-test embargo is not a substitute for boundary purging [9][6].

| Relative timeline | Permitted action | Unavailable to the selector |
|---|---|---|
| `t0 ... t1`: discovery | Specify economic pair families, features, costs and alternatives using records available then. | Later contract changes and all subsequent labels. |
| `t1 ... t2`: inner chronological validation | Fit transforms, regime definitions, hedge ratios and parameters on earlier admissible data; choose the rule using only inner forward tests. | Outer outcomes and anything released after each inner decision. |
| `t2 ... t3`: outer forward test | Refit the *entire* frozen inner selection procedure on eligible earlier data, then make decisions prospectively within this fold. Repeat across forward origins. | Using that fold's outcome to select its own candidate. |
| `t3`: freeze | Record one candidate, score, primary delay/cost assumptions, benchmark, risk limits and decision threshold. | Subsequent tuning based on final-window results. |
| `t3 ... t4`: reserved holdout | Evaluate the frozen procedure once, including no-trades and attempted but unfilled signals. | Reusing this same window as an independent test of a changed procedure. |
| After `t4`: shadow operation | Prequentially record every eligible alert and outcome as it becomes known. | Retroactively deleting alerts or changing the prediction horizon. |

This is **nested chronological walk-forward**: inner folds select and outer folds assess the complete selector. Varma and Simon show in a different application that choosing parameters with the same cross-validation score subsequently reported as performance biases error estimation; their remedy repeats selection inside an outer loop [26]. Their classifier experiment is not a proof about Russian spreads, but the selection/assessment separation applies directly. Ordinary shuffled folds or symmetric purged cross-validation can explore dependence and ranking *within* a historical dataset; they are not a faithful simulation of moving forward through an unseen market. Bergmeir, Hyndman and Koo's narrower result permits ordinary cross-validation for **purely autoregressive models with uncorrelated errors**, not a general license for this event-driven, capital-constrained portfolio [16].

**Registry before scoring:** give every attempt an immutable ID and timestamp; economic rationale and eligible universe; every considered pair, family, parameter grid and manual idea; rejected and failed runs; code/data versions or hashes and first-seen vintages; signal/episode and split boundaries; transformations, regime and hedge fitting scopes; initial and changed cost, delay, fill and quote-quality assumptions; benchmark-flow and collateral rules; selection metric and error-control family; all inspection of validation/holdout results; and reasons for stopping or amending. Unrecorded discarded attempts prevent a credible selection-adjusted claim. **Decision insight:** if a used holdout prompted a redesign, the redesign needs *new* prospective evidence, not a different label for the old window.

## 4. Many Tests, Sparse Outcomes: What Each Correction Can and Cannot Say

A prospective single portfolio decision calls for care about **any** false winner, whereas exploratory reporting across many pair families may ask for a controlled *proportion* of false leads. Those are different error targets. Harvey, Liu and Zhu observe that failed factor tests may never be visible; their factor-discovery t-statistic discussion therefore warns against treating a conventional single-test cutoff as adequate after broad search. Their proposed factor hurdle is **not** a portable t-statistic threshold for a few dependent spread episodes [15].

| Approach | Useful question | Essential assumption or limitation here |
|---|---|---|
| Nested chronological walk-forward | Does the **selection procedure** generalize to later windows? | Outer folds must remain untouched during inner search; few distinct regimes leave wide uncertainty [26]. |
| White's Reality Check | Is the best of the **recorded candidates** better than the fixed benchmark after data snooping? | Joint resampling must preserve dependence and include the actual attempted family; absent experiments invalidate the claimed search adjustment [11]. |
| Hansen's SPA | Could a more powerful benchmark-superiority test reduce the influence of obviously poor alternatives? | The published abstract says its studentized statistic and sample-dependent null improve on the Reality Check; neither fixes missing candidates, costs or structural breaks [25]. |
| Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) | Is an observed Sharpe unusual given non-normality, series length, cross-trial dispersion and the number of effectively independent trials? | The authors warn that estimating trial correlations in short samples can be ill-conditioned; it is not a probability of a profitable next trade or a corrected RUB excess [4]. |
| PBO via CSCV | How often does the **in-sample winner** rank below the out-of-sample median across symmetric partitions? | Requires synchronous candidate performance series; autocorrelation and unseen regimes are limitations, and the authors say it "does nothing to evaluate the correctness of a backtest" [6]. |
| BH / BY false-discovery control | Which exploratory hypotheses to flag while controlling the expected fraction of false rejections? | Original BH proof concerns independent test statistics; Benjamini-Yekutieli address specified positive dependence and give a conservative modification for other dependence. FDR is not a guarantee that the *one deployed winner* is genuine [22][23]. |

**Hypothetical search case.** Imagine selecting one spread from a family after trying many pairs, windows and three manual-delay settings. A naive trade-level test uses only the chosen filled trades and ignores rejected signals, correlated variants and days when capital was unavailable. A DSR computed from just the reported parameter variants would also misstate the search if entire failed pair families were omitted. Conversely, applying every correction simultaneously without defining the hypothesis and candidate family can yield opaque numbers rather than a defensible decision.

Prefer a preregistered primary portfolio-vs-LQDT hypothesis and a selection-aware procedure on synchronized **calendar differential paths**. Center the null, jointly resample dated candidate vectors or whole permissible historical blocks, and rerun the full selection and capital-allocation logic where possible. Compare White/SPA-style adjustments with untouched forward evaluation, rather than choosing whichever produces the best p-value. The stationary bootstrap's original guarantee concerns *weakly dependent stationary observations*, not arbitrary Russian regime breaks [27]; sensitivity to block length and regime boundaries is obligatory. If too few blocks or economic episodes exist, show scenario ranges and mark formal significance **unresolved**. **Decision insight:** a correction addresses a specified selection/dependence problem; none repairs wrong prices or creates missing independent episodes.

## 5. A RUB Account Ledger Beats Trade Sharpe

Use two dated ledgers with **identical true external deposits and withdrawals**: `A_t`, the account following the frozen spread/holding policy, and `B_t`, the account that instead keeps the diverted core LQDT invested and otherwise follows the agreed no-spread LQDT policy. Define **excess wealth** `X_t = A_t - B_t` in RUB after actual fees, realized/unrealized variation margin, financing and executable liquidation marks where available. Record both the end-of-day path and intraday funding peaks. Define shortfall `-X_t` and excess-curve drawdown `max(0, max_{s<=t} X_s - X_t)` separately: one measures distance below the starting benchmark, the other loss from an earlier excess high. Do not deduct the LQDT accrual on **retained, eligible collateral** a second time: it already appears in `A_t`. Charge foregone LQDT return only on holdings/cash actually displaced, and reconcile sale proceeds separately from economic loss. MOEX identifies LQDT as a listed fund unit; this does not establish account-level collateral eligibility or historical executable LQDT prices [7].

| Measure | Minimum reporting and stress question |
|---|---|
| RUB excess | `X_t`, terminal level, cumulative shortfall and drawdown; matched flows and LQDT price/valuation convention. |
| Capital-time | Time-integral of margin, liquidity reserve and unavailable cash; distinguish funds that still earn LQDT return. |
| Funding path | Worst variation-margin call, time to meet it, gross emergency contributions and any involuntary core-collateral use. |
| Exposure and duration | Concurrent positions, net/gross leg sizes, average and tail episode length, overnight/event exposure and zero-trade days. |
| Execution and turnover | Attempts, rejected/stale/unfilled/partial fills, leg asymmetry, roll trades, fees, spread/slippage and unwind cost. |
| Capacity | Available quoted size by time, participation/impact assumptions and stress at pilot size versus proposed RUB 10-20M size; do not linearly extrapolate. |
| Uncertainty | Calendar/episode concentration, forward-fold dispersion, joint block-resampling sensitivity and stress on events absent from the sample. |

**Hypothetical ledger case.** The RUB 30,000 pilot allocation includes margin **and** variation-margin liquidity/reserves, although it shares an account with over RUB 40M of core LQDT. A simulated spread might appear profitable as two filled trades but fail the actual ledger when one leg is unavailable, a margin call arrives before a human response, or liquidation would consume core collateral. Conversely, merely selling collateral to obtain cash is not itself the same as losing its sale proceeds: the account counterfactual decides the economic difference. MOEX's distinct book, trade and end-of-day data descriptions explain why a closing mark alone cannot settle these questions [8].

For this project, report the **provisional** RUB 5,000 excess-drawdown warning; action at RUB 10,000 drawdown **or** cumulative LQDT shortfall of RUB 10,000; RUB 15,000 outer shortfall boundary; and review at 90 calendar days **without resetting losses**. These are project-supplied operational rules, not a guaranteed maximum loss. Entry must work with **zero** emergency funds. At most RUB 15,000 *cumulative gross* emergency funding is approval-dependent for disorderly-liquidation avoidance or complete de-risking, never new exposure or averaging down; thus gross contributed funding can be no more than RUB 45,000. At the RUB 10,000 cumulative-shortfall action point, additional funding may only get positions flat. Count unapproved broker use of core collateral toward the gross allowance **and** as an incident. Stress a 3-90 minute manual response range; the stated approximate 5-15 minute median is not an estimated distribution. **Decision insight:** crossing a cash or availability constraint rejects operational feasibility even if mean backtest excess looks positive.

## 6. Uncertainty and the Choice Not to Show a Win Probability

For a portfolio claim, estimate uncertainty on **matched dated `X_t` increments**, not by independently shuffling winning and losing trades. Keep simultaneous legs, common event dates, no-trade intervals and the LQDT path together. Where the record plausibly supports weak stationarity *within* a regime, vary calendar-block lengths and rerun fills, finite-capital decisions and selection; show fold-by-fold results and concentration after removing the largest economic episode. For distinct regimes, report them separately instead of asserting that a bootstrap of one regime predicts another. Lo derives serial-correlation-dependent Sharpe scaling and warns that Sharpe is not a complete multiperiod risk measure [20]. Dietterich likewise describes repeated train/test overlap as a reason an ordinary paired resampled t-test can become unsafe [28]. No annualized Sharpe, precise effective sample size or narrow confidence band should be published merely because there are many bars.

If a probability is requested, first freeze its **event and horizon**: for example, `Y=1` if the fully specified policy's *attempted signal* produces positive **net incremental LQDT excess** by a fixed horizon, with an explicit rule for still-open, unfilled, partially filled and risk-stopped cases. A probability conditional on *successfully filled trades* answers a different, selected question; report fill probability separately. Natural exit can replace a fixed horizon only if the exit rule was fixed before the outcome. Include regime, size and delay scenario in the stated population. A per-trade "win rate" is neither a probability of positive portfolio RUB excess nor a funding-risk probability.

Fit any probability mapping and base rate inside the admissible **training** window; evaluate on untouched later predictions. Compare Brier score or logarithmic score with a prespecified constant-base-rate forecast, show reliability by coarse prespecified groups with episode counts and uncertainty, and check discrimination and net decision value separately. Gneiting and Raftery describe calibration as consistency of forecasts with observed outcomes and proper scoring as rewarding honest probabilities [5]. Kumar, Liang and Ma caution that finite-bin calibration error for continuous probabilities is not automatically measurable and binning can be sample inefficient [2]. These papers do **not** validate calibration on this small, shifting spread sample.

For intuition only, if a genuinely comparable, mutually exclusive outcome has gross upside `g`, gross downside `l`, unavoidable cost `c` and **actually foregone** LQDT value `b`, expected incremental value is positive only if `p > (l+c+b)/(g+l)`. This is a *definition-based illustrative threshold*, not a measured probability or permission to trade; do not charge `b` again when the actual account already retains that LQDT return. If costs, fills, `g/l`, benchmark treatment or model calibration are uncertain, require the conclusion to survive conservative bounds on each, as well as separate cash-path limits. Otherwise show **no numerical win probability** and state that outcomes are not yet calibratable. **Decision insight:** an accurate-looking point probability with an untested horizon or selected-fill population is less informative than an explicit abstention.

## 7. Minimal Reproducible Protocol and Five Decision Gates

1. **Preregister before reading the final window.** Define the economic universe and allowed exclusions; as-of source/availability policy; event and horizon labels; episode clustering; LQDT ledger and external-flow treatment; finite-capital virtual portfolio; primary hypothesis and practical RUB materiality; candidate family; cost, quote, fill and 3-90 minute delay scenarios; human quota and emergency availability; error-control method, uncertainty sensitivity, failure conditions and holdout date. Freeze a versioned manifest and append every attempted/failing experiment. Predefine a deterministic baseline such as a fixed, economically justified pair/hedge/roll rule as well as **no trade/continued LQDT**.
2. **Audit and replay.** Produce a timestamped feature/contract provenance sample, split-boundary label audit and old-signal invariance test. One realistic virtual portfolio attempts **every** eligible signal in timestamp order, without discretionary deletion, but must record stale, rejected, unfilled and partial results and respect finite capital and market hours. Use a conservative quote-quality stress when available quotes cannot substantiate fills; do not assume free minute bars or quote depth. MOEX ISS describes candles, anonymous trades, quotes and metadata, but says access can be subscription real time or freely accessible **with latency**; its historical-data page separately describes fee-liable subscriptions [1][3]. Neither establishes a free historical executable dataset for this project.
3. **Evaluate without peeking.** Run inner selection inside each forward outer origin, keep the entire candidate-vector and loss history, and compare the selected portfolio with both baselines on the matched LQDT excess path. State whether selection-adjusted inference is possible, show individual forward folds and stress all prespecified costs, delays, sparse quotes, outages, rolls, margin changes and liquidation. Reserve a final chronological window for **one frozen procedure**. Safety monitoring may always halt risk, but using a viewed holdout to redesign the candidate *consumes it* as independent evidence for that redesign.
4. **Prospective shadow, then narrowly defined pilot.** Timestamp signals, alerts, human arrival and intended orders; attempt the same feasible opportunities under a realistic quota. Shadow fills are modeled, not broker-confirmed fills. Subsequent small live observations, if separately authorized, establish actual operation and account treatment, not statistical validity by fiat.
5. **Reassess only with new evidence.** Revisions after a failure are new hypotheses. Preserve the old failed version, restart prospective evaluation for the changed rule, and do not reset the lifetime funding or loss records.

| Gate | Advance only if | Reject or hold as inconclusive when |
|---|---|---|
| **Data feasibility** | Contract, information-vintage, benchmark and sufficiently executable quote/market-state history support the chosen timeframe. | A required as-of field or quote is missing: reject that timeframe or narrow the universe; do not fill the gap with later values. |
| **Statistical evidence** | Frozen selector has a meaningful positive LQDT excess after costs across later windows, with credible selection/dependence uncertainty and tolerable downside. | Reliable adverse excess or fragile stress result: reject; too few episodes/regimes or wide bounds: inconclusive. |
| **Operational shadow** | Every feasible alert, latency, market closure and funding need is recorded; staff quota and emergency response are workable. | Silent skipped signals, unreconciled funding or impossible response: reject operational feasibility. |
| **Small live pilot** | Separately approved account-specific rules permit it; actual ledger stays within provisional actions and gross funding allowance. | Threshold incident, unauthorized collateral draw or unmanageable liquidation: de-risk and review; no statistical "profitability pass" merely at day 90. |
| **Scale to RUB 10-20M** | New size-specific depth, impact, margin, funding, broker and fresh forward evidence meet a separately frozen gate. | Pilot-size fills or 90 days alone: insufficient for scale. |

A **reject** outcome can arise from falsified point-in-time data, inability to execute, broken risk funding or convincingly adverse excess. An **accept** outcome is limited to the *named gate and version*, never blanket strategy validity. Most sparse histories should be allowed an explicit **inconclusive** statistical outcome. **Decision insight:** improving a failed pipeline may justify a new research cycle, not retroactive acceptance of the failed cycle.

## 8. Synthesis: Why No Single Backtest Statistic Settles This Decision

| Evidence framework | What it protects against | Time direction and dependence | Remaining decision risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Point-in-time replay and market-state audit | Future specifications, roll revisions and invented fills | Reconstructs information available at each decision | Does not demonstrate any positive economic edge [8][18]. |
| Nested forward selection and a frozen holdout | Choosing and assessing parameters on the same outcomes | Preserves deployment chronology; actual label overlap is purged | Short histories still miss new regimes [26][6]. |
| White/SPA and FDR-style search adjustment | False winners from a declared candidate family | Dependence assumptions and the tested family must be explicit | Unlogged searches, bad costs and nonstationarity persist [11][25][23]. |
| DSR/PBO diagnostics | Inflated Sharpe or unstable in-sample winner rankings | Need sufficient returns, well-defined trials and credible partitions | Neither is a fill audit or a future-profit guarantee [4][6]. |
| Shadow/live account evidence | Human delay, unavailable liquidity and collateral spillover | Observes a later operational process at a particular size | A short operating window does not identify a stable return distribution. |

The central tension is that **broad search requires more severe selection adjustment precisely when sparse independent episodes make every adjustment less stable**. Symmetric repartitioning makes use of limited history, yet moving forward through a Russian market transition is inherently asymmetric. A sharp statistical-looking result can also disagree with the economically decisive result: acceptable trade P&L but negative matched LQDT excess, or positive terminal excess but an intolerable interim cash demand. The practical resolution is not one universal threshold; it is a versioned sequence of independently testable claims, each permitted to end inconclusively. Only after point-in-time and execution feasibility survive should search-adjusted forward evidence, prospective operations, a separately authorized small pilot, and a **new** size-specific scale test be considered in that order.
