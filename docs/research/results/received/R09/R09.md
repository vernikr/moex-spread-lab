## 1. Executive conclusions

1. **The object of validation is the entire pre-declared research-and-selection procedure, not the best backtest found at the end.** The recommended evidence chain is: point-in-time data → chronological nested walk-forward selection → purging/embargo sized to actual overlap → one frozen outer confirmation block → search/multiple-testing adjustment → dependence-aware uncertainty → matched-LQDT RUB comparison → prospective shadow evaluation → operational pilot. Selection correction cannot repair unavailable information or unrealistic execution. Anything less than this chain yields, at best, "inconclusive".

2. **The honest question is not "is my Sharpe significant?" but "how many independent economic episodes have I observed, and how much apparent edge survives every conservative adjustment I pre-committed to?"** Bars, leg fills, trades, positions and episodes are different denominators; only episodes (clusters of trades driven by one dislocation or shock) are a defensible unit of evidence. "Many trades" is not "many independent observations". No universal "30 trades", automatic annualised Sharpe, DSR cutoff or PBO cutoff establishes profitability.

3. **The practical ranking of what will mislead this project** is: (1) a contaminated dataset (survivorship, roll adjustment, dividend/event timing, last-trading-day prints, revised settlement data, fabricated fills); (2) an unaccounted search across pairs, parameters, universes, cost and delay scenarios; (3) cost and delay assumptions chosen after seeing results; (4) non-stationarity that makes any long Russian history only partially relevant; (5) the correction formulas themselves — the smallest problem, but the easiest to compute and therefore the most tempting to over-trust.

4. **DSR and PBO are ceilings on how impressed one is allowed to be, not proofs of validity.** The authors say so: the PBO paper warns that CSCV "does nothing to evaluate the correctness of a backtest" and that "any counter-overfitting technique used to select an optimal strategy will result in overfitting"; the DSR paper states that a backtest whose author "has not controlled for the extent of the search … is worthless, regardless of how excellent the reported performance might be." Neither addresses leakage, cost realism, serial dependence or regime change.

5. **The primary economic outcome is RUB excess wealth versus a flow-matched LQDT counterfactual on the same account**, with identical true external flows, core LQDT diverted to the pilot kept invested in the counterfactual, no double deduction of LQDT yield on retained eligible collateral, and only the loss of the long-term-holding deduction excluded. Test whether its uncertainty clears a pre-registered, economically meaningful hurdle; test separately whether the account can survive the path. Statistical confidence is not funding protection.

6. **Non-stationarity in Russia is documented infrastructure change inside the recent sample, not a modelling nuisance**: the Feb–Mar 2022 closure with clearing still running, the staged lifting of the short-sale ban, discrete auctions for all shares from June 2023, the June 2024 delisting of on-exchange USD/EUR trading, intraday derivatives suspensions as recent as 11 September 2025, and a policy rate that moved 20% → 21% → 14% between 2022 and 2026. Regime conditioning must be declared ex ante from the documented event calendar, never discovered from one's own P&L.

7. **Most sparse histories should be allowed to end "inconclusive".** Inconclusive is a legitimate — and the most likely — methodological outcome; it is a success of the method when the data cannot support a stronger claim. "Accept" is only ever for a named gate and version.

8. **A 90-calendar-day pilot cannot establish profitability** at plausible effect sizes (illustrative: with annualised excess Sharpe 1 and one quarter of exposure, t ≈ 0.5 and two-sided 5% power ≈ 7%). Its legitimate purpose is operational validation — signal arrival, both-leg execution, margin/collateral behaviour, reaction-time distribution, incident handling — plus pre-registered incident logging. The delegated RUB 5,000 / 10,000 / 15,000 rules are operational action rules, not statistical bounds and not guaranteed maximum losses.

9. **Recommended posture in one line:** few economically motivated hypotheses; the wide search logged in full; causal-only data construction; purge/embargo sized to actual overlap; one sealed confirmation block used once; cost- and delay-stressed decision gates; prospective shadow trading as the only pristine sample; a pilot judged on operations rather than profit.

10. **Present verdict:** methodology specified; data feasibility, statistical edge, operations, account/broker treatment and scale all unverified. The first falsifiable deliverable is a vintage-aware, registered, LQDT-matched forward evaluation — not a selected backtest score.

11. **What is allowed and what is not.** Allowed: a broad search, zero-trade days, a sparse strategy, few opportunities. Not allowed: converting scarcity into certainty.

## 2. The statistical problem, stated precisely

### 2.1 What counts as an observation
| Unit | What it is | Why counting it inflates evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Bar / day** | a recorded market interval | overlap: one 20-day episode contributes ~20 correlated marks; "250,000 one-minute observations, therefore a tiny standard error" is exactly the fallacy the controls exist to prevent |
| **Leg fill** | an execution event | a calendar spread with a roll is 2–4 fills per episode; fills on the same day across roots are correlated |
| **Trade / round trip** | an entry–exit pair | trades may overlap, share financing, and respond to the same shock; completed round trips are a *descriptive* count, not the inferential denominator |
| **Position / trade chain** | all fills belonging to one economic bet, open-through-final-close, with one P&L and one duration | the right unit for duration, turnover and purge widths |
| **Decision opportunity** | each eligible signal, including stale, rejected, unfilled, partial and zero-trade days | the population a win-probability or fill-probability refers to |
| **Economic episode** | a cluster of linked entries, overlapping legs, rolls and exits driven by one opportunity or risk event (same pair within one holding period; different pairs driven by the same macro shock such as a RUB depreciation; shared legs or a common FX/oil factor) | the only frame in which "independent-ish" is defensible; ten entries during one volatility shock are one episode, not ten |
| **Calendar account path** | the daily marked portfolio with simultaneous exposures and no-trade days | the only object on which path drawdown, funding stress and the RUB thresholds can be measured |

Report counts of every frame, the overlap graph, concentration by date and regime, and a range (not a point) for effective independent information. Define the episode grouping **before** seeing profits, using overlapping risk, common legs and common shocks; examine alternative reasonable groupings. Non-overlap alone does not prove independence; a large cluster of overlapping trades may contain little independent information. Where a figure depends on the frame (a hit rate, a Sharpe), show it in at least two frames. AFML's chapter 4 titles ("Overlapping Outcomes", "Number of Concurrent Labels", "Average Uniqueness of a Label", pp. 59–61) name the formal treatment; they are reading locations, not evidence that this project's episodes have been measured.

### 2.2 Why "30 trades" is not a rule
For a per-episode Sharpe `SR_ep` and `n_eff` effectively independent episodes, `t ≈ SR_ep · √n_eff`. Required `n_eff` for t = 2 (illustrative arithmetic):

| SR per episode | 0.2 | 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `n_eff` for t = 2 | 100 | 44 | 16 | 8 |

The threshold depends on effect size, dependence structure, payoff variance, costs, the fixed test plan, and how many trials were run to find the candidate being tested. Thirty independent episodes with per-episode SR 0.5 are decent evidence; thirty overlapping fills of four trades, or thirty high-frequency trades inside one volatility spike, are almost none. Popular "100-trade" or "200-trade" rules are equally arbitrary. Lo (2002) makes the standard-error side concrete: larger Sharpe ratios have larger standard errors (SE 0.188 at SR 1.50 versus 0.303 at SR 3.00 in a 60-observation sample), serial correlation invalidates square-root-of-time scaling, and the minimum track-record length needed for confidence grows with non-normality (Bailey & López de Prado 2012, MinTRL). Few-cluster results in the clustered-regression literature (Cameron, Gelbach & Miller) are a warning about inference with few clusters, not a transferable minimum trade count. With 5–10 years of MOEX data, monthly decisions give at most 60–120 independent decisions per decade, far fewer if months are skipped; it is entirely plausible that, after dependence and multiplicity are respected, the data support only "inconclusive" even when raw metrics look decent. ("No 30-trade rule" is a statistical statement, not a regulatory one.)

### 2.3 Overlapping positions change the evaluation object
If several spreads can be open simultaneously, the correct evaluation object is the **daily marked portfolio ledger**, not the average of per-trade outcomes. Per-trade attribution with overlapping holds mixes episodes, double-counts common shocks and hides the path. Two filled trades can look profitable while the actual ledger fails because one leg was unavailable, a margin call arrived before a human could respond, or liquidation would have consumed core collateral. Never sum individually funded pair backtests (each run with the full RUB 30,000) as if each had the whole account. The delegated RUB thresholds are path-based and require the portfolio-level excess curve in any case.

### 2.4 Non-stationarity changes what "history" means
If the data-generating process changed at documented dates (Section 3), pre-break data are not an unbiased sample from the same population, yet this does not license cherry-picking a start date. Regime conditioning must be declared from documented events before performance is seen and applied identically to every strategy including the rejected ones. A defensible structure: the full history for mechanism plausibility and structural-break diagnostics; a *declared* estimation window for anything microstructure-, fee- or rule-sensitive; stress windows (Feb–Mar 2022; June 2024) used only to test robustness, never to fit. Structural-break methods (Andrews/Quandt–Andrews, Bai–Perron, CUSUM families) are diagnostics; discovering a break date and redesigning around it makes the break detector part of the model and therefore part of the search. Purging cannot make a model trained on a later regime historically deployable; training on later regimes is cross-period generalisation, not a simulation of an earlier deployable decision.

### 3.2 Market mechanics relevant to leakage and cost modelling
- **Settlement is not execution.** MOEX computes futures settlement prices from filtered best bids, asks and last trades; the page does not describe that computation as a price at which a portfolio could trade — the settlement algorithm is not a fill rule. Daily settlement is determined by clearing algorithms after the continuous session; the final row of an expired contract's daily history can be a settlement-only print (one probe of SBRF-6.24 / `SRM4` on 2024-06-21 showed OPEN/HIGH/LOW/CLOSE null, zero trades, `SETTLEPRICE` only).
- **Two clearing sessions.** The 28 Feb 2022 press release confirms an intermediate and an evening clearing on the derivatives market; variation margin is realised in RUB against account equity daily. One report states the windows as 14:00–14:05 and 18:45–19:00 MSK without a source (unverified). Close-to-close backtests that ignore VM cash flows mis-time funding needs.
- **Contract terms can change mid-life.** MOEX's corporate-actions page for derivatives states that the exchange may change the last trading day, execution date, lot, tick value or underlying, terminate delivery obligations, or change the settlement-price and variation-margin procedure. Contract descriptions in ISS carry `FRSTTRADE`, `LSTTRADE`, `LSTDELDATE`, `LOTSIZE`, `EXECTYPE`, `ASSETCODE` (example read: SRZ6 — first trade 2025-12-04, last trade 2026-12-17, delivery 2026-12-18, lot 100, deliverable, asset SBRF).
- **Dividends.** Standard quarterly single-stock futures follow the underlying through the ex-date (price gap, spread repricing) with no P&L adjustment; perpetual single-stock futures explicitly credit a dividend adjustment. Perpetual futures (e.g. IMOEXF; USD/RUB perpetual) are one-day contracts with automatic daily extension whose variation-margin formula includes revaluation, funding and dividend adjustment; quarterly index futures expire on the third Thursday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec. Mixing perpetual and quarterly legs in one continuous series without modelling these mechanics misstates carry.
- **Tariffs (futures market, read 2026-10-08).** Maker pays zero; only the taker pays. Anonymous taker base rates: stock contracts 0.01980%, index 0.00660%, commodity 0.01320%, FX 0.00462%, perpetual stock futures 0.03000%; addressed orders: stock 0.00660%, index 0.00220%; minimum 1 kopeck. Clearing/execution commission is a separate schedule (stock 0.00660%, index 0.00220%). "The commission for a calendar spread equals the sum of the commissions of each future" — the spread order type carries no fee discount. (A second report quotes ~0.00759% exchange commission plus clearing for commodity contracts from a tariff file; see Section 15 for this discrepancy.) The tariff page also lists a fee for erroneous transactions — a reminder that trades can be cancelled and data restated. Broker per-contract fees must be confirmed with VTB/BCS (a 2024 third-party survey listed ~1 RUB/contract at VTB and tiered 0.10–1 RUB at BCS; do not rely on it). *Illustrative arithmetic:* a two-leg stock-futures calendar spread with ~30,000 RUB notional per leg, taker on both legs, costs ≈ 11.9 RUB on entry and ≈ 23.8 RUB per round trip in exchange fees alone, before broker fees, spread and delay.
- **Reference data.** The current reference-data description says instrument lists and trading/risk parameters can change and are subscription-related; neither it nor the historical-data product page establishes a complete historical as-of revision archive. MOEX distinguishes archived full-order-book (millisecond), best-quote, trade-log (second) and end-of-day products; the ISS page describes candles, trades, quotes and metadata with real-time subscriptions or free access "with latency"; the historical-data page describes fee-liable subscriptions with a minimum period of one calendar month. None of this establishes a free historical executable dataset.

## 4. Leakage: pathways, worked examples, prevention tests

### 4.1 Principle
Point-in-time (PIT) means every number entering a decision at time *t* was **actually available** at *t* — not merely dated before *t*. Each observation therefore needs several timestamps: event-effective time, first-publication/announcement time, feed-receipt/ingestion time, revision vintage, and permitted order/fill state. A causal lookback that shares *past* observations with the test period is not leakage; a transform fitted with later information, an unreleased label, or a hindsight regime tag is. Sanctions-era regime labels defined with future knowledge ("the 2022 regime") are a leak. A vintage or execution failure is a **data-feasibility failure**, not a p-value to be repaired by a correction formula; where only a revised series exists, mark the data unusable rather than guess. Trade cancellations and restatements exist (MOEX even lists a fee for erroneous transactions), so a dataset must carry a retrieval timestamp and be re-pulled and diffed.

### 4.2 Pathway inventory
| # | Pathway | Mechanism / concrete MOEX form | Prevention test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Survivorship & retrospective universe** | Universe built from today's liquid contracts (e.g. 40 active contracts, with the 2020 top pair missing) omits delisted/halted/stranded names; liquidity on MOEX has shifted over time across single-stock futures (SBER, GAZP, LKOH), currency contracts (Si, CNY) and index contracts (MIX, RTS) and families (USD/EUR currency pairs after Jun 2024; names unshortable in 2022; perpetuals that did not exist earlier; MOEX launched and discontinued single-stock and index contracts). A full-sample cointegration screen presents survivors as a pre-existing universe. Post-hoc dropping of "weak pairs" after seeing history is the same error. Mutual-fund/CTA studies show survivorship and backfill add several percentage points; for futures the universe-construction effect dominates outright delisting. Stranded capital on halted names is also a path risk | Reconstruct the eligible universe at three past dates from PIT listing data and open-interest files (e.g. top-N by trailing 63-day volume re-evaluated every X days; trailing liquidity window `W_liq`) and diff against the present-survivor universe; the gap is the bias. Both legs must show non-zero open interest and a two-sided quote at the entry date, otherwise the trade is conditional. Registry proves no rule used current liquidity or post-2022 listings for pre-2022 decisions |
| 2 | **Continuous-contract roll adjustment** | A back-adjusted series is a "future-computed representation of the past": the adjustment at roll *k* uses the price of the next contract, which was not known when earlier bars were formed. Difference ("Panama") adjustment preserves point P&L but distorts levels (series can go negative; breaks ratios, % volatility, relative z-scores, %-based sizing); ratio adjustment preserves relative returns but distorts absolute RUB distances (fixed-RUB stops, targets, ATR in RUB become non-stationary). For spreads, back-adjusting each leg can destroy carry/roll-yield economics (roll yield = futures return − spot return); naive splicing creates artificial jumps ("the resulting backtest is wrong"); difference adjustment injects roll yield into a basis level so a basis "mean-reverts" by construction. Replacing history with a present-day contract master compounds the error (availability, lot size, eligible-pair list all changed) | Separate the research series from trade accounting. Never store one continuous series as the research object: keep per-contract prices, a dated contract map and an explicit roll rule (calendar / OI crossover / liquidity threshold); derive any continuous series on the fly inside the training window; book P&L on actual contracts with explicit roll orders and costs; use continuous series for visualisation only; build calendar spreads from two explicit contracts and use MOEX's spread-quote archive only as a validation check. **Construction-sensitivity test:** rebuild with difference / ratio / unadjusted / explicit-roll series; a sign flip or "profitable only when back-adjusted" is an artefact. Omit the roll-gap bar from feature updates; compute features on raw contract series. Do not claim every adjusted series necessarily leaks — test the dependence on construction. The same invariance test applies to vendor revisions, roll-rule changes and corrected metadata |
| 3 | **Roll-date pseudo-signal** | Liquidity migrates between contracts near expiry; a fitted entry rule can learn the migration rather than an economic dislocation. Last-trading-day rows can be settlement-only prints | Drop entries within ±n days of `LSTTRADE`; if the edge lives entirely inside roll ±3 days, it is a roll artefact. Truncate each contract at `LSTTRADE − k`; count null-OHLC rows; test sensitivity to a 3-day truncation shift |
| 4 | **Mixing perpetual and quarterly legs** | Perpetuals (IMOEXF, USD/RUB perpetual, perpetual single-stock futures) carry funding and dividend adjustments inside variation margin; quarterly futures do not; a universe built from current perpetuals excludes the period when only regular futures existed | Separate experiment IDs for perpetual and quarterly families; model perpetual settlement/roll rules explicitly or exclude; reconstruct the spread from individual contract settlement prices at entry and exit; a discrepancy larger than the bid-ask at the roll is a flag |
| 5 | **Future parameters & exchange specifications** | Lot size, tick value, initial margin, price limits, last-trading/execution dates, underlying, settlement/VM procedure, trading hours, index compositions, settlement/fixing methodology — all are "periodically redefined"; the modern parameter page is not the historical one; a 2024 methodology PDF applied to a 2010 series contaminates it unless reconstructable. A regime threshold set on the full sample, a hedge ratio on the full overlap, a vol-filter lookback extending into the test, or a vol threshold/normaliser fitted on all data are the same pathway. Hedge ratios are especially sensitive | Time-versioned parameter source keyed by effective timestamp; methodology-date check (did this version exist at the time?); fit every transform (hedge ratio, z-score mean/sd, imputation, scaling, vol thresholds, pair ranking, PCA, cointegration tests, regime models, selection rules) only on the permitted information set; **truncation / old-signal invariance test:** recompute each indicator at *t* on data ending at *t* and require identity (to the 10th decimal) with the value used by the backtest; perturb or hide later records and require unchanged prior signals |
| 6 | **Future corporate events & dividends** | Announcement, approval, record and effective dates differ; realised dividends are not historical dividend expectations; futures fair value subtracts the PV of discrete dividends; quarterly stock futures gap through the ex-date while perpetual stock futures credit an adjustment; MOEX may alter contract terms around corporate actions. A "signal" that keys off a dividend gap is dividend harvesting with different risks (announcement, cancellation, tax) | Event table with announcement timestamps; use the earliest actually available information only; corporate-event embargo (treat the observed futures curve as primitive, accept not decomposing carry vs expectation); separate event-modulated from event-free episodes; exclude each event-flag group in turn; remove ±2-day ex-date windows and see whether profit survives; segment series at contract-change dates |
| 7 | **Late data, revisions, timestamps** | MOEX distinguishes "reliable" streams from streams whose final values may differ from on-line values; settlement revisions alter VM; an end-of-bar feature treated as known at bar start; page-edit dates mistaken for availability; data corrections/restated fundamentals | Retain source vintage and ingestion time (every backtest silently assumes that the inputs it uses were available at the time — a point the PIT-database vendors themselves make); apply conservative release-latency assumptions; compare first-seen vs final values and record revision direction and magnitude; bound or exclude; re-pull from two independent copies (exchange archive vs broker) — large differences in Feb–Mar 2022 are model risk; conservative publication lags; if final settlement data are not freely available that is a feasibility limitation, not something to approximate |
| 8 | **Settlement mark used as execution** | The official settlement price (determined by clearing algorithms hours after the close from filtered bids/asks/last trades) booked as two simultaneous executable fills; an 18:45 "last price" treated as the final settlement | Check market state, bid/ask, size and order timing at the supposed fill; report marks separately from fills; settlement prints are not actionable triggers |
| 9 | **Market state** (halts, suspensions, closed or shortened sessions, discrete auctions, short-sale bans) | A simulator fills a 15:30 signal on 11 Sep 2025 at end-of-day settlement although trading was suspended 15:23–15:55; a 2022 backtest shorts an unshortable leg; the notice itself used as a feature before it was published | Market-state calendar with four fields (event-effective, public-notice, feed-receipt, permitted order/fill state); state transitions enter both the leakage audit and the funding-stress tests; Feb–Mar 2022 as a mandatory stress report |
| 10 | **Labels overlapping the split boundary** | Triple-barrier/fixed-horizon labels spanning *t…t+H*; a training label whose outcome is realised inside the test window; information interval `I_i = [t_decision, t_outcome]` | Purge every training observation whose interval intersects the test interval (`Φ_train ∩ Φ_test = ∅`); label availability `a_i < τ`; audit the boundary per fold in code |
| 11 | **Features reaching backward across the split** | Training rows *after* the test block whose lookback windows reach into it; persistent state variables; delayed execution/outcome | Embargo sized to the actual mechanism (holding horizon, longest feature window, documentation lag, autocorrelation horizon), asserted in the splitter and logged. Do not equate embargo with the entire feature lookback: a causal feature with a long window is not a leak |
| 12 | **Cross-pair / shared-leg / common-factor dependence** | Several pairs share a leg (SBER in two spreads) or a common FX/oil/rate factor; one macro shock drives many entries | Cluster trades into episodes by shared legs and calendar overlap; split by cluster; deflate trade counts by average cluster size |
| 13 | **Post-hoc hedge ratio / full-sample normalisation** | Static OLS β over five years "knows the cointegration vector"; a z-score normalised with the full three-year mean/sd "knows the 2025 volatility regime in 2023" | Rolling β_t on [t−W, t], residual `S_{t+1} = Si_{t+1} − β_t·CNY_{t+1}`; degradation from static to rolling measures the leak; truncation test as in row 5 |
| 14 | **Regime labels fitted on the full sample** | A break detector (Bai–Perron, Quandt–Andrews, full-sample CUSUM) or an 80th-percentile volatility threshold computed on all data; a regime tag renamed into the past | Causal detectors only (CUSUM variants; SADF) with labels identical for all dates ≤ t regardless of later data; declared regime calendar from documented events |
| 15 | **Reused final holdout** | Variant A fails, B is tried on the same block; "one more parameter"; adaptive queries on a fixed holdout (Dwork et al. 2015) | Access-log every economic output from the block (charts, summaries, rankings, failed results); audit the registry for repeated evaluations on identical out-of-sample ranges; `holdout_uses` counter; one opening per family version; a new holdout can only be prospective |
| 16 | **Post-hoc costs, fees, benchmark conventions** | Fee or slippage model, LQDT opportunity-cost convention or valuation rule changed after results; "unknown fee" or "missing quote" booked as zero; a post-hoc cost haircut cannot recreate the alternate universe of trades that would have been taken under the correct costs (post-hoc cost transformations may preserve some cross-sectional properties of results but can bias them) | Pre-specify versioned cost/delay scenarios; primary = most conservative plausible; report all; breakeven cost per episode — if it lies inside the measurement range of real costs the result is inconclusive |
| 17 | **Best-delay selection** | Delays 0/5/15/30/60 (or 3 vs 15) tested, the winner reported; "the garden of forking paths". Actual median human latency 5–15 min, range 3–90 | Delay is a searched dimension: register the grid, decide at the pre-registered conservative delay (e.g. the 90th percentile of the measured reaction distribution); report the whole grid without invented probabilities |
| 18 | **Asynchronous quotes, stale fills, partial legs** | Fresh bid on one leg against an old ask on the other; both legs at closing mid for a signal after the close; quote age unrecorded | Require source times, side, quantity and permissible quote age for every fill; record unhedged (legged) exposure; with coarse data report bounds or "execution unidentified" |
| 19 | **Finite-capital aggregation & discretionary omission** | Each pair simulated with the full RUB 30,000 and results summed; overlapping losing signals omitted because "a human would have rejected them"; failed or delayed fills removed in hindsight (operational hindsight) | One chronological portfolio with shared capital, lot constraints, netting of shared legs and a frozen signal-priority rule; a disposition and reason code for every signal; reconcile the signal ledger against the portfolio ledger |
| 20 | **Settlement & VM cash-flow timing** | Close-to-close P&L ignoring that VM is debited in RUB at intermediate and evening clearing; margin calls, overnight liquidity and VM financing are mis-timed | Replicate the cash balance daily including both clearings, margin eligibility and overnight reserves; model the evening VM in the ledger |
| 21 | **Historical-knowledge leakage into design** | A later-known crisis used to design a signal; "works except 2022" rescued by excluding 2022 after seeing results | Later-known stress is legitimate only as a labelled robustness test; exclusion rules come from the documented calendar, are declared before results, and are applied identically to rejected strategies; Feb–Mar 2022 is reported as a mandatory stress case, not silently dropped |

### 4.3 Worked examples
**WE1 — Full-sample normalisation.** A spread z-score uses the mean and standard deviation of the whole 3-year sample. The 2023 signal "knows" 2025 volatility. *Test:* recompute the indicator at each t using data ending at t and require identity with the stored value; any difference is leakage. The same test catches hedge ratios, vol filters and regime thresholds.

**WE2 — Static hedge ratio (Si–CNY).** One OLS β over five years. *Prevention:* rolling β_t on [t−W, t], residual S_{t+1} = Si_{t+1} − β_t·CNY_{t+1}. Compare the number of contracts implied by rolling vs full-sample β at each entry; performance degradation from static to rolling is the measured size of the leak.

**WE3 — Panama-adjusted MIX with a fixed z-threshold.** A 20-day z-score on a difference-adjusted index-futures series with |z| ≥ 2.0 ≈ 1,500 index points is a very different percentage move in year 1 than in year 5; difference adjustment also injects roll yield into the basis level so "basis mean reversion" appears by construction. *Tests:* rebuild on ratio-adjusted and on raw contracts; remove the roll-bar delta from feature updates; measure the fraction of signals inside roll ±3 days; check the sign of the basis-z vs 10-day-forward-return relation across constructions.

**WE4 — Roll via a later-known contract mapping.** Earlier prices adjusted using a roll computed when the next contract later traded; present-day contract master substituted for the historical one. *Prevention:* replay from archived contract identifiers, specifications and releases by timestamp; keep the continuous series for charts only; run the old-signal invariance test.

**WE5 — Regime threshold at the 80th percentile.** The volatility percentile is computed on the full sample, so the model knows which future days are "high-vol". *Prevention:* trailing percentile computed on data ≤ t; fit on training window only.

**WE6 — Late settlement.** An 18:45 last price is used as the fill while the final settlement published later differs. *Test:* compare decision-time price with final settlement for every signal; record revision direction/magnitude; if the final settlement is not freely available, state the limitation.

**WE7 — Overlapping label.** Training trade entered 10 Jan with outcome on 10 Feb; test window 20–31 Jan: the label contains test-period information. Equivalent day-count version: a day-40 signal whose label ends on day 55 is unavailable to a fit made at day 50 and must be purged; day-49 prices may legitimately initialise a test-period rolling feature; a regime label that uses day-60 information but is stamped "day 40" remains forbidden.

**WE8 — Best delay.** Delays 0/5/15/30/60 min tested, 15 min "looks best" (or 3 min wins while 15 min loses, and only 3 min is reported). *Rule:* the delay grid is part of the search; the decision cell is the pre-registered conservative one; report the surface. A next-day-open fill is easy to code and is the conservative default until the reaction-time distribution has been measured in the pilot — "delay doesn't matter" is a claim to be tested, not assumed.

**WE9 — Reused holdout.** Variant A is scored on the sealed block and fails; variant B is derived and scored on the same block; "it is just one more parameter". *Rule:* the first scored look consumed the block; the grid must be frozen before unsealing; B needs a later block or prospective evidence. The `holdout_uses` counter makes this auditable.

**WE10 — "Works except 2022".** Separate the legitimate component (a documented regime in which the strategy was not executable at all — short-sale ban, closed market) from the illegitimate one (excluding a losing stretch after seeing it). Only a calendar-based exclusion rule declared ex ante and applied to every candidate, including the rejected ones, is admissible; Feb–Mar 2022 is then reported as a stress case regardless.

**WE11 — Dividend harvesting.** Q2 profits concentrate around ex-dates: the "spread signal" is really a dividend bet with announcement, cancellation and tax risk. *Test:* remove ±2-day ex-date windows; separate event-modulated and event-free episodes.

**WE12 — Market state on 11 Sep 2025.** Suspension 15:23, notice 15:29, orders from 15:40, trading 15:55. A simulator that fills a 15:30 signal at EOD settlement invents an opportunity; a model that uses the suspension before 15:29 is clairvoyant.

**WE13 — Finite-capital summation.** Five pairs each simulated with RUB 30,000 and summed; in reality capital, lots and human attention were shared. *Prevention:* one chronological portfolio with a frozen simultaneous-signal priority.

**WE14 — Asynchronous quotes.** Entry priced from a fresh bid on leg 1 and a 40-minute-old ask on leg 2. *Prevention:* permissible quote age per leg; record legging exposure; otherwise bound the fill.

**WE15 — Two profitable trades, failing ledger.** Both fills look profitable per trade, but one leg was unavailable at the time, a margin call arrived before a human could respond, and liquidation consumed core collateral. Crossing a cash/availability constraint rejects operational feasibility even when mean excess is positive; selling collateral is not the same as losing the proceeds.

**WE16 — Hypothetical search case.** One spread chosen from a family after many pairs, windows and three delay settings. A naive trade-level test ignores rejected signals, correlated variants and capital-unavailable days; a DSR computed from the reported variants only misstates the search when failed families are omitted; applying every correction at once without defining the hypothesis yields opaque numbers. The remedy is a preregistered primary portfolio-vs-LQDT hypothesis with selection-aware inference on synchronized calendar differential paths (Section 7).

**WE17 — Null test (the single highest-value test).** Run the full pipeline on shuffled labels within regime and on a synthetic random walk with matched volatility and roll schedule, including the selection step; run it with the sign of the signal flipped. Any apparent edge on null data is pipeline leakage. It costs an afternoon and is rerun whenever the pipeline changes. A positive IS→OOS rank relation across candidates is a companion check: if it is not positive, stop searching.

## 5. Validation architecture: splits, purging, embargo, walk-forward

### 5.1 The observation record
For every candidate decision *i* store: decision origin `d_i`; the last market observation used and the feature lookback it depends on, with release/ingestion times; signal time; fill time (or disposition); label interval `[d_i, e_i]` with `e_i` the exit/maturity; label availability time `a_i` (an outcome that finishes after the test decision is a not-yet-known label); the raw information dependencies of every transform applied (hedge ratio, scaler, regime model); data vintage. An endpoint realised later cannot be used to fit anything earlier. The record also carries the time zone and session calendar, and for every eligible signal — including stale, rejected, unfilled, partial and zero-trade days — a disposition and reason code.

### 5.2 Timeline (consolidated)
```
t0 hypothesis &        t1 DEVELOPMENT WINDOW                 t2 OUTER FORWARD TEST            t3 FREEZE   t3–t4 SEALED CONFIRMATION      after t4 SHADOW        PILOT
   preregistration     (discovery; inner chronological       (entire frozen inner procedure   (candidate, BLOCK (+ maturity tail):      (prospective,          (operational
   (families, grid,     selection per origin; all fits        refit at every origin and        score,      one registered efficacy       prequential; the       validation,
    scenarios, rule,    in-window; purge/embargo by actual    applied forward; never           scenario,   review; opened once;           only pristine          Section 12)
    gates, δ_H)         overlap; everything logged)           influences selection)            limits)     never relabelled "unseen")     sample; never re-run)
```
Within each development fold *k* (origin τ_k): `inner TRAIN → maturity/purge boundary → inner VALIDATION`, all before τ_k; an optional probability layer `TRAIN → completed-label CALIBRATION → VALIDATION`; at τ_k the *entire frozen inner procedure* is refit with permitted completed information only and applied to the outer test `[τ_k, τ_{k+1})`; outcomes are recorded to the registered horizon even if they mature beyond the window; repeat through time so that **the deployment procedure is the evaluated procedure**. The outer test never influences selection; if outer results cause a redesign they become development evidence and the redesigned version needs a new outer period. Between adjacent training and test segments sit the purge (width = actual label/holding overlap) and, where dependence requires it, the embargo. The schematic `Train₁ | purge (h = max holding) | Test₁ | embargo (e) | Train₂ …` is the same structure seen fold by fold.

At `t3` freeze: candidate (or declared family with its multiplicity), scoring statistic, primary delay/cost scenario, benchmark rule, risk limits, decision threshold. The `t3–t4` block is scored once, including no-trade and unfilled periods; opening it is a registry event. After `t4` the only pristine sample is prospective shadow operation with timestamped signals, book snapshots, decision times and hypothetical fills; no retroactive deletion or horizon change is allowed. A consumed block is never relabelled "unseen"; do not retro-modify historical validation and report it together with prospective data as one out-of-sample test.

### 5.3 Purge and embargo — rules, not percentages
- **Purge (label overlap).** With trade decision `t0`, exit `t1`, feature lookback `L` and estimator window `W`: drop a training observation if `[t0 − max(L, W), t1]` intersects the test interval; equivalently keep only training `i` with `max(t_{i,1}, t_test_start) > min(t_{i,2}, t_test_end)`, and require label availability `a_i < τ` (or a registered same-timestamp ordering). Blocked designs remove intersecting intervals on both sides. The purge width is the **actual** overlap: average hold 8 days / maximum 20 days → purge 20 days; a 15-day maximum hold → 15 days; an average horizon H = 5 days → at least 5 days. If the holding period is itself optimised, purge by the maximum holding evaluated **anywhere in the search**. Split at chain level — no trade chain crosses a boundary; position chains that can last 30 days imply purge + embargo ≥ 30 days. A 60-day volatility lookback means the feature window must not overlap the test block either.
- **Embargo (residual dependence).** Training observations after the test block (`t_test_end < t_{i,1} ≤ t_test_end + h_embargo`) are removed because their features, persistent state variables, delayed outcomes or documentation lags reach back into the test window. Size it from the dependence mechanism: `h_embargo ≥ max(holding horizon, longest feature window w_f, documentation/release lag w_doc, observed autocorrelation horizon)` — e.g. a 20-day hold with a 60-day lookback implies ≥ 60 days, *not* "1% of T" and not "10 bars because 10 sounds conservative". Document the sensitivity of results to the embargo length. In a strictly forward-only split the future never enters training, so a generic post-test embargo may add nothing and is no substitute for boundary purging. The embargo is not the entire feature lookback: a causal feature with a long window is not leakage. A sparse programme "can afford generous gaps" — but do not discard valid data for cosmetic independence.
- **Implementation.** The splitter asserts both conditions per fold, logs the purged observation IDs with reasons, and the embargo support and duration. The same parameters (maximum holding, lookback) define the capital-time terms later.
- **Legitimate exception.** Known past prices may initialise a test-period rolling feature. Purging cannot make a later-regime model historically deployable (Section 2.4).

### 5.4 What must be fitted inside the permitted information set
Imputation, scaling/normalisation, volatility thresholds and percentiles, hedge ratios (OLS/Ridge β), pair ranking and screening, PCA, cointegration tests, regime models, selection rules, calibrators (which additionally need a *pre-test* completed-label calibration segment), and the window length of a rolling scheme (a tuned parameter inside selection, never a post-hoc rescue). The in-sample pipeline is therefore: partition → β_train → μ_train, σ_train → z_t on the test block with training parameters only → execution with delayed quotes under the registered rule (e.g. |z| > 2.0). Fitted objects carry a `fit_end` timestamp ≤ decision time; a unit test shifts the dataset end date and checks that nothing before it changes.

### 5.5 Method comparison
| Scheme | Verdict for this project | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Expanding-window walk-forward (rolling origin)** | **Primary decision object** | Decisions use only observations predating the decision; one ordering, so report each origin-to-origin transition, not a single pooled number; stale regimes accumulate — freeze the update policy. AFML (ch. 12) stresses that a "flawless" WF demands attention to data, microstructure, risk, performance measurement and multiple testing; Tashman's rolling-origin literature is the general reference |
| **Rolling-window walk-forward** | Pre-declared branch | Expanding vs rolling is an economic assumption about regime persistence; the window length is a tuned parameter inside selection, not a late optimisation |
| **Nested chronological walk-forward** | **Adopt** | Inner layer selects pairs/hyper-parameters within each origin from a pre-registered grid; outer layer evaluates the entire frozen procedure (Varma & Simon 2006: selecting on the same CV score biases the error estimate). "Best of 50 pairs" remains biased unless the outer layer sees the selection rule. With little data for the inner layer, simplify the family or declare inconclusive; cap the configuration count rather than hope for power; do not collapse the layers while retaining the same evidential claim |
| **Purged K-fold** | Inside the training window only | Explores dependence within the dataset; later blocks training a model tested on earlier blocks is cross-period generalisation, not deployment simulation |
| **CPCV** (combinatorial purged CV; `C(N,k)` splits, `φ = (k/N)·C(N,k) = C(N−1,k−1)` paths) | Diagnostic only | Paths are recombinations of the same few episodes: path diversity is not sample diversity, path dispersion is not a confidence interval for true performance, and CPCV cannot manufacture independent economic histories. It needs the whole dataset at once (conflicts with vintage data) and on sparse data its training sets lack density (under-fitting). Arian, Norouzi Mobarekeh & Seco (2024) report CPCV superiority and WF's "notable shortcomings in false discovery prevention … increased temporal variability and weaker stationarity" — but on synthetic Heston/Merton/regime-switching data with dense-bar ML classifiers, and the WF tested was not necessarily purged. One report rates CPCV "high suitability" for sparse spreads; the others disagree (Section 15). Open-source implementations exist (e.g. `timeseriescv.CombPurgedKFoldCV`) |
| **CSCV / PBO** | Report as a diagnostic, never an objective | Relative in-sample/out-of-sample ranks across symmetric half-combinations; the authors flag autocorrelation (§5.1) and that a structural break outside the sample escapes assessment; requires synchronous series (Section 7.5) |
| **Ordinary random K-fold** | Not admissible | Exchangeability is violated. Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo (2018) justify K-fold only for purely autoregressive models with uncorrelated errors under stationarity/ergodicity, consistent estimation and martingale-difference/moment conditions on the errors (their A1–A3) — not established here, and even then the trading policy's information set, overlap, execution and selection need their own checks |
| **Shuffled / symmetric purged CV, blocked CV (Roberts et al. 2017)** | Exploratory only | Useful to study dependence structure; "different modelling objectives … different blocking approaches"; not a faithful forward simulation |

### 5.6 Windows and regimes
Three complementary views are reported for every candidate: full history (mechanism plausibility, break diagnostics), era/stress windows, and the recent regime — the last may be the production rule only if declared ex ante. Two admissible designs: **regime-aware windows** (documented breakpoints delimit stable sub-regimes; less data per segment) or **calendar walk-forward** (e.g. 18-month train / 6-month test) with break diagnostics as an explanatory covariate, never optimised. One report proposes a declared default estimation window from January 2023 with 2022 as a mandatory stress window and 2019–2021 as orientation only; another proposes a primary backtest after March 2022; another starts pre-2022 with regime-aware windows (Section 15 (l)). Whichever is chosen must be written into the preregistration before results and applied identically to rejected candidates; documented regime breaks (the 2022 halt and reopening, contract-specification and trading-hour changes, dividend treatment in share futures, FX-benchmark changes after sanctions) must not sit inside a single training window; and inconvenient history is never discarded automatically. Concept-drift detection inside walk-forward frameworks is a research topic, not a licence to re-tune.

### 5.7 Practical constraints
A 90-day pilot yields one or two outer blocks — it is not a walk-forward; historical walk-forward must be completed before the pilot and the pilot treated as prospective shadow/operational data. Monthly decisions over 5–10 years give at most 60–120 independent decisions per decade. Record every signal in timestamp order in a virtual portfolio that attempts every signal under the finite-capital rule; shadow fills are modelled, not broker-confirmed.

### 5.8 Boundary policies to register
Concatenate outer results once per timestamp; a registered policy for positions open at a refit boundary (retain positions across refits); a later refit may use matured outcomes but may not change recorded forecasts; assign each forecast to its decision origin and retain maturity tails; book each wealth increment exactly once — overlapping labels are not extra wealth observations.

## 6. Accounting for the search: trials, registry, preregistration, holdout

### 6.1 What counts as a trial
A trial is an immutable **fingerprint** of everything that could change a result: universe definition; pair-selection rule; strategy family; signal formula; transforms; hedge method; regime method; parameter values; entry rule; exit rule; sizing; capital/concurrency rule; delay; execution model; cost model; data snapshot; selection objective; train/validation scheme. Re-running the same fingerprint is a repeat; changing anything creates a new branch with a parent link. Everything the researcher was exposed to counts: every pair, family, grid point, manual idea, universe variant, cost/delay/quote scenario and benchmark convention that was *evaluated*, whether it finished, crashed, or was abandoned ("17 configurations in the report versus 4,000 tried"). Computational failures are distinguished from completed poor results, but both are recorded. Experiments that predate the registry are an **unresolved exposure**: a retrospective reconstruction is not a new test, and there is no defensible universal correlation haircut for an unrecorded adaptive search. "Failed experiments are data."

Two hard rules: (1) failed, bugged and rejected runs are entries — they must stay; (2) the registry is append-only; corrections are new entries or new versions. A lightweight tool is acceptable (a spreadsheet or a version-controlled text file), provided timestamps are immutable.

### 6.2 Registry schema (consolidated)
**Identity & lineage:** `experiment_id` / `run_id` (immutable), `parent_ids`, `family_id`, `hypothesis_id`, researcher, `created_at` / `modified_at` (UTC), `decision_at`, status, hypothesis statement and economic rationale, exploratory-or-confirmatory flag, `change_reason`, the question "was this specification recorded before the result was inspected?"

**Data & availability:** sources and versions with hash/checksum, `retrieved_at`, endpoint, observation time, `first_available_at`, revision vintage, time zone and session calendar, contract-specification version / contract-map version, roll rule, universe rule and universe-membership history, excluded dates (e.g. MOEX suspensions Feb–Mar 2022), missingness and quote-quality policy, feature-set version.

**Complete search specification:** all pairs and families screened; every parameter value or the search algorithm and its budget; features with their PIT constraints; label definition and horizon; hedge-ratio fit; regime definition; normalisation and transform fit windows; seed; prior information used; signal definition verbatim; full parameter grid and `n_configurations_evaluated`; failed runs with error reasons; rejected candidates with reasons.

**Selection history:** candidate set at each step, information visible at selection, selection statistic and threshold (verbatim selection rule), selected and discarded IDs, selection rank, manual overrides, trial-family definition, guided-search endpoints, multiplicity method, effective-trial estimate and its sensitivity, `trials_count_at_selection`.

**Temporal validation:** train origins and endpoints, label-availability cutoffs, inner and outer splits, split scheme and CV type, purged observation IDs with reasons, embargo support and duration, maximum holding used, calibration splits, `final_holdout_id`, holdout access log, `holdout_uses` counter, `consumed_at`, refit and open-position boundary rules.

**Executable portfolio & benchmark:** initial capital, external-flow ledger, internal core-transfer ledger, matched-LQDT rule (flow timing, valuation convention), signal priority, contract lots, position limits, cash and margin rules, transaction-cost model version and cost scenarios (fee-schedule version), delay rule and scenarios, fill and quote-quality rules, tax scenarios, benchmark-flow and collateral rules.

**Risk & human process:** human availability and intervention quota, manual response timestamps, dispositions of stale/rejected/unfilled/partial signals, emergency-funding approvals, cumulative gross emergency usage, core-collateral incidents, warning and action events, de-risking completion time.

**Results & uncertainty:** all registered metrics by window and scenario, paired excess path, drawdowns and funding path, counts of bars/trades/episodes/chains/decisions and calendar span, standard errors and intervals, dependence diagnostics, resampling unit and block lengths, adjusted and unadjusted inference (DSR/PSR, PBO, FDR-adjusted p where used), capital-time, turnover, missing results and reasons, calibration diagnostics if applicable, outcome (accept / reject / inconclusive; or rejected / promising / baseline / bugged), reasons including protocol violations found later, deviations from preregistration.

**Reproducibility & audit:** specification version, implementation version, code commit, environment and random state, input/output checksums, run start/finish, warnings, reviewer, `inspected_by` ("who looked at which partition when"), reasons for stopping or amending, immutable original result, amendment log, negative-result retention, link to the preregistration reference and the pre-registered decision gate.

*Illustrative row:* `R-0042 | 2026-03-14T09:12Z | family: calendar-spread z-score | pairs: SRM6/SRU6 | params: L=20, z_in=2.0, z_out=0.5 | selection: max net excess (stress scenario) | train 2023-01…2025-06 | purge 20d, embargo 60d | test 2025-07…2025-12 | result: +1,840 RUB, SE 2,900 | episodes 6 | holdout consumed: no | notes: 3rd of 48 grid points`.

### 6.3 Preregistration
**What is frozen before any confirmatory data are touched:** hypothesis and universe rule; the entire selection algorithm and its search budget (grid, selection rule, stopping rule); information/label availability conventions and split design; horizon H, LQDT accounting convention and the hurdle δ_H; one feasible portfolio simulation (finite capital, lot rounding, shared-leg netting, legging exposure, treasury/cash rules, manual-availability quota and simultaneous-signal priority); cost, delay, quote-quality, stress and missing-data scenarios; multiplicity family, error target, uncertainty method and sensitivity grid; holdout dates, maturity tail, access permissions and review times; risk/funding actions and the outcome rules (accept/reject/inconclusive criteria), exclusion rules and permitted holdout uses; a deterministic baseline (fixed pair / hedge / roll rule) and the no-trade baseline. **How:** a timestamped file under version control, or a public/third-party registry (OSF, AsPredicted-style); the CASRAI/research-community definition — a time-stamped specification of hypotheses, methods and analyses prior to observing outcomes — is the standard.

### 6.4 Holdout lifecycle
- **Creation.** Only after data and contract construction are frozen, the family defined, cost/delay frozen, the selection algorithm frozen and the outer validation complete. Suggested span: the most recent 12–18 months including the current regime, encrypted or blinded; one unblinding per family version.
- **Consumption** happens at the first outcome-sensitive use by anyone who has seen results: any performance computation, summary, ranking, chart or calibration plot, including a failed result; running the fixed strategy, computing primary metrics, or deciding to proceed. Any model, cost or delay change informed by the block also consumes it. Several candidates scored on it constitute a declared multiple test. Changing the model after looking does not refresh it.
- **Allowed without consumption:** outcome-blind quality control (schema, timestamps, corruption) under a logged rule; a repair that requires viewing performance consumes the block. Safety monitoring may halt risk during live operation, but a holdout viewed for redesign is consumed.
- **After consumption:** keep the original result; create a new version; evidence must come from later data or prospective shadow; never extend the window until significance appears, reset losses, or rename a viewed period "final". A bug found later leaves the period usable operationally but statistically compromised. The DSR paper notes that "if we apply the holdout method enough times (say 20 times for 95%) … false positives … [are] expected"; Dwork et al. (2015) show that "reusing a holdout set adaptively multiple times can easily lead to overfitting to the holdout set itself" — and their reusable-holdout mechanism is not a licence for unrestricted reuse of dependent market data. A fixed "k-peek budget" is a weaker alternative to single use and harder to defend. One secondary source (unverified) reports ~36.9% false acceptance under the null when "best-of-K on a reused holdout with t > 2" is practised.

## 7. Multiple testing and selection-adjusted inference

### 7.1 Choose the error target before choosing a formula
Data snooping, in White's sense, is the repeated reuse of the same data for inference and model selection; a time series offers one path, so every reuse is a trial.
- **One prospective deployment decision** (will this one candidate be funded?) → any false winner matters → family-wise error (FWER) on the declared family, or a superiority test of the family against the benchmark.
- **Exploratory screening across many families** → a controlled *proportion* of false discoveries (FDR) is appropriate — strict FWER control raises Type II errors in an exploratory pair search — but an FDR guarantee says nothing about the one winner actually deployed.
- **Layering.** Reality Check / SPA apply to the pre-specified family; nested walk-forward covers the adaptive procedure; both are reported.
- **Define the family first.** Trials must be registered; failed factor tests are invisible in published literature (HLZ) and equally invisible in an unlogged private search, which is why no published hurdle is portable to this project.

### 7.2 Methods
| Method | What it controls / assumes | Use here |
|---|---|---|
| Bonferroni `α/m`; Holm step-down (`p_(i) ≤ α/(m−i+1)`) | FWER with valid individual p-values; no dependence assumption | Valid when p-values come from an episode-aware block bootstrap/permutation; with ≥ 20 trials they "kill almost everything" — acceptable for a confirmatory family of 2–4 themes |
| Benjamini–Hochberg (`p_(k) ≤ kα/m`); Benjamini–Yekutieli (divide by `c(m) = Σ_{j≤m} 1/j ≈ ln m + γ`) | FDR; BH proven under independence and positive regression dependence (PRDS); BY under arbitrary dependence, conservative | Shared-leg spreads do not automatically satisfy PRDS; not valid after outcome-dependent filtering; test unit = family/parameterisation with p-values from HAC t-statistics on walk-forward excess; with dozens of families run FDR directly and expect very few survivors |
| Storey q-values | Estimates the proportion of true nulls | Requires a scale of tests this project does not have |
| White (2000) Reality Check | H₀: `max_j E[d_{j,t}] ≤ 0` where `d_{j,t}` is the costed, common-calendar net excess increment of candidate *j* over the benchmark; stationary-bootstrap distribution of the maximum | Benchmark = matched LQDT, not zero; joint resampling of the dated candidate vectors must preserve dependence and include the **actual** family (it addresses the best among models *considered*, not an unrecorded wider search); missing outcomes are not zero; infeasible policies are reported infeasible; an economic-hurdle test shifts the null; "a block length of one gives the standard bootstrap" |
| Hansen (2005) SPA | Studentised, sample-dependent null; more powerful and less sensitive to the inclusion of poor alternatives | Relevant when a large universe contains many obviously poor candidates; does not fix missing candidates, costs or breaks |
| Romano & Wolf (2005) StepM | Step-down FWER with joint resampling | Suitable for a short list; "the stepdown label alone is not a small-sample guarantee" |
| Model Confidence Set (Hansen, Lunde & Nason 2011) | Set of models not significantly dominated | Alternative framing for a shortlist |
| Stepwise Cauchy Combination Test (StepC) | FWER under dependence for sparse, contaminated signals without an independent-trial estimate | Requires valid p-values from an episode-aware block bootstrap/permutation |
| HLZ (2016) hurdle `t > 3.0` (≈ annualised SR > 1.0 over 10 years); Harvey–Liu haircut Sharpe | Derived from the cross-section of published factors | Context, not a threshold: rules of thumb "like halving the Sharpe can be too strict or too lax" |
| DSR / PSR (Bailey & López de Prado 2014) | Adjusted PSR threshold for the expected maximum of N trials | Diagnostic ceiling (7.3) |
| PBO / CSCV (Bailey et al. 2015/2017) | Relative in-sample/out-of-sample rank degradation | Diagnostic (7.5) |

Centre the bootstrap statistics under the null; compare the White/SPA result with the untouched forward result; do not pick the better p-value. Block-length and regime sensitivity are obligatory. With too few blocks, report scenario ranges and mark significance "unresolved". Selection-adjusted inference always means: account for the number of alternatives, the error rate chosen, and shrinkage.

### 7.3 The Deflated Sharpe Ratio
**Formulas (as recalled/read; verify against the equation version before use).**
`PSR(SR*) = Φ[ (SR̂ − SR*) · √(T−1) / √(1 − γ₃·SR̂ + ((γ₄−1)/4)·SR̂²) ]`, where γ₃, γ₄ are skewness and kurtosis of the returns and T the number of return observations (`σ̂(SR) = √((1 − γ₃SR + ((γ₄−1)/4)SR²)/(T−1))`).
`SR₀ = √V[SR_n] · ( (1−γ)·Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/N) + γ·Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/(N·e)) )`, with N the number of independent trials, `V[SR_n]` the variance of trial Sharpe ratios, γ ≈ 0.5772 (Euler–Mascheroni). `DSR = PSR(SR₀)`. The authors' own description: "DSR is a PSR where the rejection threshold is adjusted to reflect the multiplicity of trials." Eq. 1 gives the expected maximum Sharpe under N trials; Eqs. 7–9 give the implied number of independent trials; Appendix 3 treats dependent trials and notes that "correlation is a limited notion of linear dependence".

**Paper's numerical example:** SR̂ = 2.5, T = 1,250, N = 100, V[SR_n] = ½, skew −3, kurtosis 10 → DSR ≈ 0.9004; with N = 46 → 0.9505; with normal returns, N = 88 → 0.9505. Lessons: the search penalty is large, and non-normality is roughly equivalent to doubling the trial count.

**Related formulas.** `MinTRL = 1 + [1 − γ₃·SR + ((γ₄−1)/4)·SR²] · (Z_α/(SR − SR*))²` (minimum track-record length; a deck example: 2.73 years for SR 2 vs benchmark 1 at 95%). `MinBTL < 2·ln(N) / E[max SR]²` years (Pseudo-Mathematics, 2014) — the minimum backtest length below which the expected maximum in-sample Sharpe of N random trials exceeds a target. Stopping rule from the paper: after 1/e ≈ 37% of the planned trials, stop at the first candidate exceeding the best so far (secretary problem) — a discipline against open-ended search. The paper also quotes the ASA guideline on reporting the number of tests, warns that memory effects can turn selection into "loss maximization", and states DSR "could also be computed on" Harvey–Liu's threshold — the two are complementary.

**What the DSR assumes and where it fails here (consolidated critique).**
1. It measures the *search*, not truth: passing DSR is not proof; failing (e.g. DSR < 0.95) means "not proven", not "disproven".
2. N and V[SR_n] are the weakest inputs; the authors warn that estimating trial correlations in short samples is ill-conditioned. Nominal N over-corrects, underestimated effective N under-corrects. "1,000 correlated variants ≠ 1,000 trials"; "30 pairs ≠ 30 opportunities" when many transforms were tried per pair; three variants with z = 1.80/1.85/1.90 are ≈ one trial, while N = 1 ignores the search altogether.
3. The expected-maximum formula is asymptotic in N (extreme-value); with N = 5–20 it is crude.
4. It assumes i.i.d. normal-ish returns under the null — violated by volatility clustering, jumps and sanctions-driven tails — and makes no serial-correlation adjustment (Lo 2002, "time aggregation"; AFML §14.7.3), which overlapping positions and mostly-flat bars violate; V[SR_n] is non-stationary across breaks.
5. Skewness and kurtosis are unreliable with few episodes (a medium-term spread programme may produce only 15–40 independent trades in three years, so the DSR output is itself unstable); a mostly-zero daily series distorts them; trade-level returns break the identical-distribution assumption.
6. It cannot distinguish "no edge" from "no power"; it is not the probability of a profitable next trade or of RUB excess; it does not correct the *timing* of trials, and the null itself may be misspecified. Several sources observe that DSR rejects sparse strategies more often and "will almost always reject unless the raw Sharpe is exceptionally high", demanding hundreds of episodes.
7. What T is: bars, trades or episodes? Resolutions in use: a capital-time-weighted daily series (allocate each trade's return across its holding days) with episodes as the effective count; or T = number of walk-forward test windows or independent chains, never all decision points. "A daily series padded by inactivity must not be sold as hundreds of independent outcomes." The effective number of *trials* and the effective number of *time observations* are different objects.
8. Before computing, fix: the equation/version (one report cites a secondary claim that Eq. 2 on p. 8 of the original contains an error — unverified; see Section 15 (b)), return frequency and denominator, skew/kurtosis convention, trial-dispersion estimate and effective-trial treatment. If these are unidentified, omit the DSR rather than report a number.

**How to use it.** DSR penalises three axes — non-normality, track length and the number of independent trials. Take N from the registry (families × grid points × scenario variants) and report the total attempts, the effective multiplicity and the method used to derive it; report DSR at nominal N and at an effective N (bracket); report it alongside episode count, overlap structure, regime composition and number of variants; treat it as a filter, not a badge, and never as the objective of search. A replacement of the asymptotic threshold by a bootstrap of the maximum (RC/SPA) is preferable when feasible. Numeric cut-offs proposed by individual reports (0.95 "flag"; ≥ 0.95 accept / < 0.90 reject at N_eff; < 0.8 "too weak to distinguish from chance given the search") are governance choices, not statistical facts; one report adopts no numerical threshold at all.

### 7.4 Effective number of trials
Build the trial-return matrix (one column per fingerprint on a common calendar); compute the correlation matrix; take the number of principal components explaining 90–95% of variance, or `N_eff ≈ N / (1 + (N−1)·ρ̄)`, or a clustering of trials (López de Prado & Lewis 2018) and eigenvalue-based counts; report a **range**, not a point, and the DSR at both ends. Record the method as `effective_trial_estimate_and_sensitivity`.

### 7.5 PBO / CSCV
**Definition.** Split the matrix of M candidate return series into S (even) sub-periods; for every combination of S/2 sub-periods as "in-sample" (`C(S, S/2)` combinations), pick the best in-sample candidate j*, compute its out-of-sample rank percentile ω among all candidates; PBO = fraction of combinations in which the IS winner ranks in the bottom half OOS (`logit(ω) ≤ 0`). Four complementary analyses in the paper: PBO itself, performance degradation (IS vs OOS regression), probability of loss, stochastic dominance.
**Limitations.** Data-hungry; combinatorial recombinations of the same episodes are not independent histories; non-stationarity violates the identical-distribution assumption and a break outside the sample escapes assessment; a sparse strategy leaves missing values in many sub-periods and imputation changes ranks — compute only on sub-periods where all candidates traded and report the excluded fraction; it requires synchronous series and the whole dataset at once; it is relative-rank only (all candidates may be poor); it is informative only when the candidate matrix and the block granularity are meaningful; §5.2 covers incomplete records and "doomed" trials; the authors warn against using CSCV to guide the search and that it "does nothing to evaluate the correctness of a backtest". A high PBO is strong evidence of noise-fitting; a low PBO is not sufficient. Preregister the matrix columns, scoring statistic, block partition, complementary-half rule, tie handling and overlap treatment; label the output "PBO under the specified resampling model", never "probability the strategy fails live"; report it with episode count, overlap structure, regime composition and number of variants. Cut-offs proposed by one report (≥ 0.5 reject; 0.25–0.5 inconclusive; < 0.25 accept) are governance choices.

### 7.6 Empirical precedents and the literature debate
Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999): calendar effects lose significance once the full universe of rules examined is accounted for. Bajgrowicz & Scaillet (2012): technical rules' apparent performance is "completely offset by low transaction costs" and "an investor would never have been able to select ex ante" the rules that later performed. HLZ (2014/2016): most published factor findings are likely false at conventional thresholds. Counterweights: Harvey & Liu (2020) — hurdles that are too strict cause missed discoveries ("lack power to detect outperforming managers"; double-bootstrap approach); Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023) — "the majority of factors can be replicated" and the literature is "multiple versions of a smaller number of themes". Giglio, Liao & Xiu (2021) and Harvey & Liu (2015) treat the same problem with FDR and shrinkage. Practical reading for this project: bet on 2–4 economic *themes* rather than many parameterisations, and pre-declare the cost asymmetry (a pilot capped at RUB 10–15k of loss versus the opportunity cost of a missed edge) so that the FWER/FDR choice is deliberate. Scope limit: these are cross-sectional equity-factor studies (153 characteristics × 93 countries), not a 3–6-pair MOEX futures book; the mechanisms transfer, the thresholds do not.

### 7.7 Central tension
A broad search demands more severe adjustment exactly when sparse episodes make every adjustment less stable. Symmetric re-partitioning schemes (K-fold, CPCV) and asymmetric forward evaluation answer different questions, and only the latter mimics deployment. The way out is not a better formula but a smaller, economically motivated hypothesis set declared before the data, parameter plateaus preferred to optima, and a willingness to end at "inconclusive".

## 8. Uncertainty: what to resample, what to report

### 8.1 Three separate objects
1. **Realised excess** X(t) on the actual path — a fact, no interval.
2. **Bounds from unidentified inputs** — missing quotes, unknown fees, tax treatment, benchmark convention; reported as ranges, never collapsed into one number ("a missing quote or unknown fee is not zero cost").
3. **Sampling / predictive uncertainty** about the expected excess and about future paths.
A resampling interval for the expected mean is not a guaranteed range for realised pilot losses.

### 8.2 Primary inference target
`θ_H = E[net RUB excess over horizon H]`; the observed statistic is the terminal X(H). Only under a within-regime, stationary-additive-increment approximation does `θ_H = H·μ` with `μ = E[ΔX_t]`; otherwise report paths and bounds and state that θ_H is unidentified. Declare the hurdle δ_H (an economically meaningful required gain — *not* the tolerated loss budget: "a 10–15k tolerated shortfall does not specify the required expected gain") and a useful interval width before the final period. Drawdown and funding are a separate coherent-path analysis. Do not annualise by default, and never produce an annualised Sharpe, a precise ESS or a narrow band merely because there are many bars.

### 8.3 Dependence-aware resampling (procedure)
1. Work on the **matched, dated differential increments** `ΔX_t` on a common calendar that includes inactive days (a no-entry day has cash drag and open P&L); never shuffle trades, legs or candidates independently; keep legs, event dates, no-trade intervals and the LQDT counterfactual together.
2. Use moving-block or stationary bootstrap (Künsch 1989; Politis & Romano 1994; Politis & White 2004 for block length) with block length tied to the dependence structure: holding overlap, autocorrelation and event clustering measured on development data (e.g. block = maximum holding period). Try several block lengths — block length is a sensitivity, not an optimisation target; report the range, not the favourable one. Permutation/sign-flip tests against baselines are the companion. The stationary bootstrap assumes weakly dependent stationary observations — it is no remedy for a structural break.
3. HAC/Newey–West standard errors for return-series means; `Ω = γ₀ + 2Σγ_k`, `Var(mean) ≈ Ω/T`, `n_eff = T·γ₀/Ω` — statistic-specific, can exceed T under negative dependence, and not a universal replacement for N in DSR or calibration; when the series is too sparse to estimate it, say so rather than report a number. Simpler proxies: `N_eff = N(1−ρ₁)/(1+ρ₁)` for AR(1) dependence; Kish `ESS = (Σw)²/Σw²` for weighted observations. Illustrative: 100 raw fills across five macro events → N_eff ≈ 8–12. Cluster at episode level (Cameron, Gelbach & Miller: few-cluster inference is itself fragile).
4. **Never let the uncertainty step erase the selection step:** either re-run the full selection and capital allocation inside each resample, or use RC/SPA on the fixed registered matrix. "A winner from 5,000 candidates bootstrapped alone understates uncertainty." Do not double-count outer observations.
5. Re-run fills, finite-capital decisions and lot rounding inside each resample where they depend on the path.
6. Report fold-by-fold (forward-fold dispersion), by regime (stress regimes separately, transition failures), leave-one-episode-out, and concentration after removing the largest episode (top-2 episode share). Dietterich (1998): overlapping samples make the paired resampled t-test unsafe.
7. Missing inactive-day values are bounded, never zero-filled; "observed no-trade" differs from "unavailable".

### 8.4 Coherent-path risk
Resampled blocks cut positions, so block bootstraps are not valid for liquidation, margin or drawdown paths. Use contiguous actual paths and scenario replays (halts, gaps, delayed liquidation) for funding stress.

### 8.5 Sequential looks and power
Risk stops are continuous, but efficacy reviews happen only at pre-declared dates (day 90, quarterly); stopped paths remain in the record. Interim statistical looks require alpha spending (Lan & DeMets 1983) or time-uniform confidence sequences (Waudby-Smith et al. 2024) whose conditions must be defensible. Power is computed in episodes with labelled assumptions: with annualised excess SR of 1, 2 or 4 and a quarter of exposure, `t ≈ 0.5 / 1.0 / 2.0` and two-sided 5% power ≈ 7% / 17% / 52% (i.i.d. daily marks). Lo's standard error `SE(SR) ≈ √((1 + SR²/2)/T)` for i.i.d. returns gives, with T ≈ 10 completed trades and SR 1.5, an interval of roughly ±0.9 in SR units — i.e. uninformative. Few trades in a pilot is the expected state, not a defect.

## 9. Metrics and their definitions

### 9.1 Matched-LQDT excess (primary)
**Estimand.** `X(t) = [W_actual(t) − W_actual(0)] − [W_LQDT(t) − W_LQDT(0)]`, where W is net account wealth (cash, LQDT units, futures P&L, liabilities, costs) valued at one timestamp with a frozen price hierarchy; asynchronous or missing marks are recorded. Both accounts start with identical wealth and receive identical true external flows at identical times. Only the *whole account* is compared, because LQDT is an exchange-listed fund unit, not a cash rate.

**Counterfactual conventions (freeze before results).** Core LQDT diverted to the pilot stays invested in the counterfactual; incremental cash in the counterfactual follows a fixed LQDT-purchase/cash-residual convention with executable fund transactions, whole-unit constraints and closed sessions that delay purchases; cash raised by selling core LQDT is an internal transfer (the counterfactual keeps the units); borrowing, financing and collateral remuneration are explicit. Flow timing: at the day's LQDT close (second-order at ≈ 0.038%/day unless a flow coincides with a rate change). Do not divide by the RUB 40M core.

**Recipes.** Unit-matched shadow: `shadow_units_t = shadow_units_{t−1} + F_t/P_t`; `excess_rub_t = (NAV_t − NAV_{t−1} − F_t) − shadow_units_{t−1}·(P_t − P_{t−1})`; `cum_excess_rub = Σ excess_rub`. Return form: `R_X(t)` = daily P&L plus cash/margin interest over allocated capital including margin; `R_L(t)` = LQDT return; `e(t) = R_X − R_L`; average, HAC SE, interval for the cumulative. Two-ledger form: `A_t` (policy account), `B_t` (keeps LQDT), `X_t = A_t − B_t` after fees, VM, financing and executable liquidation marks, end-of-day path plus intraday funding peaks. Report both the time-weighted and the RUB series; **thresholds are evaluated in cumulative RUB**, and the headline is the terminal RUB difference `E_T − E_0`, not "Sharpe = X".

**Double counting and collateral.** LQDT yield on *retained* eligible collateral already appears in A_t — deduct only the difference, i.e. charge foregone LQDT only on displaced holdings: `P&L_excess = gross − execution costs − Σ_τ C_diverted(τ)·R_LQDT(τ)`. Margin posted is not a loss; VM cash must reconcile with futures P&L; core-sale proceeds are principal converted, not economic loss. Whether LQDT units are accepted as collateral (GO) at VTB/BCS is **open**: if accepted, nothing is charged; if only cash is accepted, the foregone yield is a strategy cost; if not eligible, the "counterfactual keeps the units" convention is not achievable in the account and must be redesigned. Decide this before the pilot.

**Funding ledger.** Internal transfers and broker use of core are not external inflows but are recorded against the gross emergency allowance; a genuine new deposit enters both ledgers; neither resets X or the high-water mark; each true flow is matched by timestamp, RUB value and transaction convention so that it causes no mechanical jump in X or its high-water mark, while the subsequent strategy-versus-LQDT returns on it remain in X; flow-related fees are separate; the future claim is conditional on the registered funding/flow rule. Core wealth cancels in the paired difference only to the extent that its economics really remain identical in both ledgers.

**Valuation, tax, history.** Pre-declare mark-to-market and conservative flattening-value conventions; dated LQDT unit/market price (NAV is not an executable quote; fund expenses are already embedded); primary layer pre-investor-tax with tax sensitivities; exclude *only* the loss of the long-term-holding deduction, not all taxes. The benchmark's uncertainty is low, so it is no reason to soften the hurdle. The hurdle is regime-dependent: 30,000 × 14% × 90/365 ≈ 1,036 RUB per quarter; at 21% ≈ 1,553 RUB (illustrative). An all-history investable LQDT series needs a dated source with a publication-lag manifest; the fund rules were registered 2019-11-28 and the unit was issued 2020-01-20, so earlier history is a labelled proxy and exploratory only.

### 9.2 Drawdown versus shortfall
`M(t) = max(0, max_{s≤t} X(s))` (high-water mark of the excess curve); **excess-curve drawdown** `D(t) = M(t) − X(t)`; **cumulative shortfall** `S(t) = max(0, −X(t))` (net inception-to-date, not a sum of losing trades). D and S are different triggers: D can fire while X is positive. Report also the maximum absolute strategy drawdown, time under water from HWM to recovery (censored if unrecovered; AFML §14.5.2), and keep the trigger log after recovery.

### 9.3 Capital-time
RUB-days of exposure, separating margin posted, VM liquidity, cash reserve and LQDT diversion; distinguish funds still earning LQDT; peak and average; gross leg notionals. Utilisation `CTU = ∫ Margin_utilised dt / (allocation × T) ∈ [0,1]`. Excess per RUB-day is the only scale-free comparison but must be labelled "RUB excess per RUB-day", not an annual return ("broker margin is not economic risk capital"). Capital-time-adjusted Sharpe and return on capital-time are secondary.

### 9.4 Stress funding (path)
Maximum cash/VM need, `max_t(IM_required + cumulative VM deficit)`, margin escalation, liquidity gap before flattening, time to meet a call, cumulative **gross** emergency usage (do not net repayments), an incident log of emergency funding events (date, amount, reason, whether the account was flat or adding), involuntary core-collateral incidents with timing; fraction of the 30k/45k envelope stressed historically; revaluation is not a fresh contribution; eligible core ≠ actually drawn core. Stress funding is not mark-to-market loss.

### 9.5 Exposure, duration, activity, turnover
Eligible signals and their dispositions; completed vs open; calendar vs tradable duration; average and tail chain/episode length; concurrency and peak concurrent exposure; net/gross leg sizes; overnight/event exposure; zero-trade days; unwind cost; turnover in contracts and gross notional (annualised turnover = traded notional per year / average notional capital; or notional / average deployed capital); roll frequency; censoring rules.

### 9.6 Costs
Full implementation shortfall (Perold 1988; AFML §14.6): commissions on all legs and rolls, exchange/clearing fees, half-spread × 2 × legs, delay slippage, impact, partial-fill/legging costs, cash/benchmark opportunity cost, tax sensitivities — separated into measured / dated / assumed, with unknowns **not** set to zero. Model costs as a band (± 1 tick or ± X% notional per leg; spread floor ≥ 1 tick per leg) and report edge erosion within the band. **Hurdle waterfall:** commissions + half-spread×2×legs + delay slippage + LQDT hurdle ((RUSFAR − TER) × days/365 as proxy) + margin/funding wedge + model-uncertainty haircut + operational reserve. Cost as a fraction of gross edge and the breakeven cost per episode are reported alongside.

### 9.7 Capacity
Executable depth where observed; participation/impact (pilot size "well below 1 bp of ADV"; participation cap ≤ x% ADV); order/leg size; lot rounding; available capital; simultaneous-signal conflicts; liquidation time; the maximum notional that does not move the spread by more than a fraction of the bid-ask (from order-book depth or trade-size-vs-slippage). State the tested size and resolution; **no linear extrapolation** from the pilot to 10–20M (still far below index-futures volumes, but leg/spread dislocations and crowding must be checked).

### 9.8 Operational metrics
Stale-signal count, rejected/unfilled/partial fraction and leg asymmetry, decision-to-fill latency and the reaction-time distribution, slippage vs model, incidents, manual overrides and missed signals.

### 9.9 Probability quality (only if probabilities are shown)
Horizon/event definition; Brier and log score vs a training-only base-rate forecast; calibration diagnostics with counts; discrimination secondary; abstention coverage; "accuracy is not utility".

### 9.10 Reporting frames
Every headline figure is shown with its estimator named and in at least two frames (bars / legs / trades / episodes / n_eff), together with calendar and episode concentration, forward-fold dispersion, block-resampling sensitivity and the stress scenarios for events absent from the sample.

## 10. Baselines, stress scenarios and falsification tests

### 10.1 Baselines (all run under the identical walk-forward protocol, costs and constraints)
| Code | Baseline | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| B0 | Flow-matched all-LQDT / no-trade (`X ≡ 0` by construction; **not** zero return) | Defines the hurdle |
| B1 | Hold the underlying exposure of the legs (or a fixed LQDT-beta futures overlay) | Separates spread edge from directional exposure |
| B2 | Deterministic spread rule with no tuning: always-long (or perpetually-short) calendar spread rebalanced on roll dates only; naive mechanical roll; fixed pair/hedge/roll rule with one entry/exit; fixed-window z-score (e.g. 20-day, ±2) with no extra tuning; calendar-only (monthly) entries; seasonality-only spread; **and** an untuned version of the proposed model under the same constraints | Measures the value added by the selection/tuning |
| B3 | Random entry with matched trade frequency and holding-period distribution | Null distribution for path statistics |
| B4 | Training-only prevalence forecast (for probabilities) | Never the test period's realised prevalence |

### 10.2 Falsification tests on the candidate
T1 sign flip of the signal; T2 entry delayed by one day and by the 90th-percentile reaction time; T3 null data (shuffled labels within regime; synthetic random walk with matched volatility and roll schedule) run through the whole pipeline including selection; T4 construction variants (difference / ratio / unadjusted / explicit roll; roll-method sensitivity; universe reconstruction; truncation of each series end); T5 cost stress (×2 spread, +1 tick per leg, both legs taker, ×1.5 fees); T6 regime split (pre-2023 orientation; 2023–2024; post-June-2024) and leave-one-regime-out; plus removal of ±2-day ex-date windows and of roll ±3-day windows.

### 10.3 Scenario design
Three categories that must never be conflated: the **primary scenario** (pre-registered, conservative, carries the decision), **stress scenarios** (reported for fragility), and **selection alternatives** (searched dimensions that enter the multiplicity count).

**Delay axis** (hypothetical stress points, not a probability distribution — the real distribution is measured in shadow/pilot): 0 (decision bar close), 3, 5, 15, 30, 60, 90 min, +1 bar, next permitted session, **no response**; the decision cell is the pre-registered conservative delay (e.g. the 90th percentile of measured reaction time, or the 15-minute cell). The project brief's "5–15 min median, 3–90 range" is not a distribution.

**Quote-quality axis:** mid / bid-ask / worst price in [t+3, t+90] min; adverse quote age; absent depth; wider spreads (median / 90th percentile / maximum; ×1.5, ×2); partial or one-leg fills; cancellation/rejection; gaps; price limits; closed sessions; delayed liquidation; time-of-day windows (avoid the illiquid lunch hour and the last minutes before an auction; best/typical/worst windows; some practitioners use 10–15 min before close).

**Cost axis:** base = the official dated fee schedule (exchange + clearing + broker) ± a one-tick slippage band with a fixed decision-to-fill delay; stresses with ×1.5 / ×2 multipliers, +1 tick, 50% extra slippage, impact; spread floor ≥ 1 tick per leg.

**Funding/benchmark axis:** margin increases, VM timing, cash shortfall, collateral haircuts/eligibility, LQDT transaction timing, tax scenarios.

**Example grids proposed by the reports** (harmonise into one registered grid): *Base* = historical commission + median spread, 5 min, mid; *Stress* = ×1.5 fees + 90th-percentile spread, 15 min, bid/ask; *Severe* = ×2 fees + maximum spread, 60 min, bid/ask + 50% extra slippage; alternatively *A* = 3 min + spread, *B* = 15 min + 1.5× spread, *C* = 90 min worst price in [t+3m, t+90m] + doubled fees. Reports differ on which cell decides (Section 15 (n)); the consistent core is: decision on a pre-declared conservative cell (Stress/B-type), Severe/C reported, and "profitable only under the cheapest delay and best quotes" = execution-sensitive, inconclusive unless demonstrated prospectively. A pass in the best case combined with a fail in a plausible case is fragility. Do not combine impossible assumptions into a probability-weighted forecast. The sensitivity of a *given* strategy to a scenario is not the same object as a strategy *newly selected* under that scenario. Report per-scenario P&L and slippage distributions, the full delay × quote-quality surface, the breakeven cost per episode, and the point where costs consume the alpha.

### 10.4 Shadow-trading conditions
A shadow period counts as prospective evidence only if: the specification is frozen; every signal is recorded without discretion (alert time, acknowledgement, intended order, quote age, session, fill disposition, rejection reason, open exposure, outcome); the LQDT counterfactual runs on the same dates and flows; nothing is modified during the period; workflow and model failures are logged; an unplanned override is a deviation (report as-operated plus a labelled counterfactual; do not censor the intervened loss; version permanent changes). Otherwise it is an "operational rehearsal". Shadow fills are modelled, so live fill/impact uncertainty remains. Typical duration before a pilot: 1–3 months clean; 6–12 months if it must carry statistical weight. Monitor slippage vs model, rejected/partial fraction, decision-to-fill latency, roll logic, feeds and costs.

## 11. Probability calibration guide

### 11.1 Default: do not display numerical probabilities
Show a number only when (a) the event and horizon are frozen, (b) a prequential, out-of-time calibration record exists on untouched later data, (c) the interval around the probability does not span the decision range, and (d) the regime and cost environment lie inside the validation range. The default output text is: *"Probability not shown: insufficient independent out-of-sample evidence for reliable calibration."* That is better than "62% ± very wide". Until hundreds of resolved, independent observations exist, show counts, percentiles of episode P&L, the excess curve and intervals; if a UI insists on a number, attach the sample size and the label "model-implied, uncalibrated". Directional signals with conservative sizing are the fallback; miscalibrated probabilities lead to oversized positions. Machine-learning outputs are notoriously poorly calibrated.

### 11.2 "Probability of what?"
Candidates: P(trade P&L > 0); P(trade beats LQDT over its holding period); P(portfolio beats LQDT after 90 days); P(positive excess without breaching the drawdown/funding boundary). The last two are closest to the decision. Recommended definition: **Y = 1 if the attempted signal (or the policy over horizon H) produces positive net incremental matched-LQDT excess by the fixed horizon**, with frozen rules for H's start, calendar convention, open/stale/rejected/unfilled/partial/zero/tied cases and risk-stopped positions. Conditioning on filled trades answers a different, selected question — report fill probability separately and label conditional estimates. A "natural exit" horizon is acceptable only if the exit rule is fixed ex ante. Include regime, size and delay in the population definition. Per-trade win rate ≠ portfolio excess probability ≠ funding-risk probability; do not sum overlapping marginal probabilities or assume independent wins; freeze conditional vs unconditional; censored labels wait for the maturity tail.

### 11.3 Design
Out-of-time predictions only; fit the mapping `p_i = P(Y_i = 1 | I_i)` and the base rate in the training window; fit any calibrator on later-but-pre-test completed labels; evaluate on untouched later data; calibrator choice, window and regularisation are selected inside the nested scheme, never on final labels. Platt scaling or a beta calibrator for small samples; isotonic regression needs more data and "is more prone to overfitting" (Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005); simplicity does not guarantee calibration; time-decay or recent-window recalibration is fragile under breaks.

### 11.4 Evaluation
Proper scoring rules (Gneiting & Raftery 2007; skill scores §2.3): Brier (with its decomposition) and log score with a rule for extreme probabilities, always against the **training-only constant base-rate** forecast; calibration-in-the-large; reliability diagram by level with counts and uncertainty (a reliability diagram alone is weak evidence for a tiny sample — report the score with its uncertainty, e.g. a 90% interval from bootstrap or a Bayesian posterior; Wilson or Clopper–Pearson per bin, not Wald; decile or coarser prespecified bins; no many-empty-bin diagrams; finite-bin estimates can themselves misstate error — Kumar, Liang & Ma 2019 — and CORP bands (Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan 2021) assume independent/exchangeable cases); PIT histogram (Dawid 1982; calibration vs sharpness, Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery 2007); joint episode-level resampling for score differences; binomial intervals are not justified for dependent trades — cluster at episode level; AUC/win rate measure discrimination, not calibration; report discrimination and net decision value separately. With fewer than ~50 episodes report the raw hit rate with an interval and nothing else. Reference points offered by one report (Brier > 0.25 ≈ coin flip, unusable for sizing; interval spanning 0.5 = "indistinguishable from random") are heuristics; the defensible comparison is against the base-rate forecast (Section 15 (i)).

### 11.5 Abstention
Withhold when the event or fill mechanism is unidentified; information is too thin (e.g. N_eff < 30 episodes, or any one-episode dependence); the calibrator is unstable across windows; the regime or costs lie outside validation (post-hoc calibration fails under even mild shift — Ovadia et al. 2019); the model was tuned on the same data; observations are overlapping/reused without dependence treatment; the interval spans the decision range. Show the reason and keep collecting cases. Where probabilities are withheld, show qualitative regime classes, ordinal buckets and stress P&L profiles instead.

### 11.6 Break-even threshold and how uncertainty moves it
`E = p·G − (1−p)·L − c − b`; `p* = (L + c + b)/(G + L)`, where G and L are gross gain and loss per episode, c ≥ 0 the full execution cost, and b the **incremental** matched-benchmark drag actually foregone (signed; not charged twice if G and L are already net; with b = 0 this reduces to the familiar p ≥ (L + c)/(G + L)). If p* falls outside [0,1] there is no interior cut-off. Act only when the *lower* bound of p exceeds the *upper* bound of p* under the pre-registered scenario set; with uncertain payoffs evaluate the joint net-excess expectation (a high p with negative expected excess is possible) against the hurdle and the path-funding limits; a straddle is inconclusive. Each component moves the threshold: cost uncertainty raises c (use the stress band); benchmark/rate regime moves b (1,036 vs 1,553 RUB per quarter above); model uncertainty is applied as a RUB haircut on the excess (interval per episode, DSR deflation at N_eff, cost-stress delta, funding wedge) — if the residual is positive with margin proceed, if its sign depends on N_eff or the cost cell it is inconclusive and the candidate stays in shadow. Scenario-conditional probabilities P(excess > 0 | base / moderate / severe) are more useful than one number.

## 12. Decision outcomes, gates and the pilot

### 12.1 Outcome vocabulary
| Outcome | Meaning | Typical criteria (all pre-registered) |
|---|---|---|
| **Invalid for the stated claim** | data/vintage/execution failure; pipeline leak; registry gap | null test positive; invariance test fails; search exposure unresolved |
| **Rejected** | the executable claim fails | upper bound of the excess interval ≤ 0 (or < hurdle) under the primary cost/delay scenario; fails a baseline; a mandatory constraint (cash/availability, funding path) is crossed; DSR/PBO indicate noise given the search. State *which* dimension failed — statistical, implementation or risk; a funding failure does not mean the signal is false |
| **Inconclusive** | the data cannot support a stronger statement (the most likely outcome) | interval spans the hurdle; corrections unstable; few episodes dominate; cost/quote/tax ambiguity changes the sign; prospective exposure too short; edge disappears under modest deterioration; "fail to reject ≠ no effect; positive point estimate ≠ pass". Next step: simpler hypotheses, more observation, or end — **not** more parameter search; continue shadow without parameter change |
| **Supported for a narrow claim / provisional candidate** | advance to the next gate only | proposal: one-sided 95% lower bound of the matched-LQDT excess > δ_H at the pre-specified review with family-wise control (a winner-only interval or calibration scores are insufficient; probabilities are not required for a deterministic policy); survives the pessimistic scenario set, selection correction and baselines; small sample and non-stationarity → shadow/pilot only |
| **Operationally validated, profitability unproven** | the honest pilot outcome | execution, margin, collateral, latency, incident handling verified |
"Validated" without qualifier is avoided; "accept" is always "accept for gate X, version Y" — accept-for-shadow is permission to spend time, not capital.

### 12.2 Evidence hierarchy
L1 data integrity (vintage data, executable prices, defensible episode count) → L2 genuine out-of-sample process → L3 search-adjusted inference → L4 dependence-aware uncertainty → L5 economic robustness (costs/delay/baselines) → L6 prospective behaviour. Proceed to a pilot with L1 plus a pre-registered L2 protocol and a defined holdout; scale only with L2–L6; abandon if L1 fails or L2 fails after the pre-registered trials. If vintage data, executable prices or a defensible episode count cannot be established, the outcome is inconclusive however attractive the chart.

### 12.5 The pilot and the delegated rules
The supplied rules are provisional operational controls: budget RUB 30,000 plus emergency funding ≤ RUB 15,000 cumulative gross (≤ 45,000 total; the account keeps 40M in LQDT); entry only with zero emergency funding drawn; internal access to core capital does not make an entry affordable; warning at D = 5,000 (audit latency, slippage, fills; trading continues); action at D = 10,000 **or** S = 10,000 (suspend new entries, de-risk to flat — flat-only state); outer limit S = 15,000 (liquidate, suspend pending review); mandatory review at day 90 with no reset of lifetime funding and loss records; unapproved broker use of core collateral is an incident. Evaluate D and S separately; the first applicable trigger governs; a later boundary never permits ignoring an earlier one; a favourable path never retro-justifies a breach. Triggers are tested with the information and reaction availability that actually existed; gaps, halts, partial fills and delays can overshoot the boundaries, so report the maximum funding need and the flattening latency; a collateral sale is not an equal economic loss — the thresholds are action rules, not guaranteed maximum losses and not evidence thresholds. Emergency funding used is a path fact to report, never evidence of a pass.

**What 90 days can establish:** manual-execution friction, both-leg execution, broker collateral treatment (including whether LQDT is silently liquidated for VM), VM tracking across the two clearings, the reaction-time distribution, incident handling, risk-limit behaviour, feed and roll logic — i.e. operational facts, with 5–15 completed trades or 5–10 episodes typical. **What it cannot establish:** profitability (power ≈ 7–17% at plausible edges); a calibrated probability; capacity at 10–20M. Zero trades in 90 days is an acceptable operational outcome. The pilot's P&L enters the lifetime record but is labelled an "operational test" and is not reused as backtest data; interim statistical verdicts occur only at pre-declared dates.

### 12.6 Three design scenarios and their likely verdicts
*A — minimal search* (2–3 families × 3–5 parameter sets, deterministic rules, no ML probabilities): mild multiplicity penalties; may reach "provisional candidate"; residual risk is dependence on pre-2022 data. *B — broad search with ML and probability outputs:* N explodes, every winner is downgraded, PBO high, calibration poor; expect "inconclusive" at best. *C — operational focus:* the pilot is process validation; the honest claim is "no obvious overfitting or leakage, plus operational robustness", never "statistical proof".

## 13. Minimal reproducible protocol (consolidated)
1. **Freeze the question and the decision rule:** horizon H, matched-LQDT definition, hurdle δ_H, interval level (95%) and family-wise α (0.05) as governance choices; secondary metrics descriptive only; the hurdle is not transferable to another size or horizon.
2. **Preregister** everything in Section 6.3 per family (one-sentence idea, instruments, labelling, inputs, ranges, costs, delays, benchmark, metrics, baselines, gates), with a timestamped artefact.
3. **Build the as-of data manifest** (what existed, what was tradable, what was published, which parameter version applied, what the quote state was): observation, exchange-event, first-publication, receipt and revision times; snapshots and checksums; contract map; market-state calendar; time-versioned parameters; if availability cannot be established, exclude or mark exploratory. Audit a provenance sample.
4. **Specify one feasible portfolio simulation:** deterministic disposition under finite capital and simultaneous-signal priority, lot rounding, shared-leg netting, legging exposure, treasury rules, manual availability.
5. **Validate the engine on trivial baselines** (B0–B3) and run the null tests before any candidate is scored.
6. **Register the complete search** (Section 6.1) — every evaluation appends a row before its result is inspected.
7. **Nested chronological development** (Section 5): inner selection per origin from the registered grid; outer frozen procedure; purge/embargo by actual overlap with a split-boundary label audit; calibration also precedes the test; three regime views; construction-sensitivity tests.
8. **Keep testing and research distinct** (Section 5.8); outer results that cause a redesign become development evidence for a new version.
9. **Selection-adjusted, dependence-aware inference** (Sections 7–8): error target declared in advance; RC/SPA or Romano–Wolf on the registered family; DSR at N and N_eff and PBO as diagnostics; block-bootstrap/HAC intervals in RUB; fold-by-fold and regime reports; no precise p-value where not defensible.
10. **Economic robustness:** scenario grid, baselines, breakeven cost, hurdle waterfall, falsification tests T1–T6.
11. **Freeze and evaluate the final holdout once** (lock registry version, candidate or declared family with its multiplicity; record the opening).
12. **Prospective shadow** with timestamped signals, zero-signal days retained, frozen efficacy review dates, risk interventions recorded.
13. **Pilot** under the delegated rules, declared in advance as operational; incident log; GO/collateral and tariffs settled beforehand.
14. **Outcome and change log:** window-level X, uncertainty assumptions, missing-data sensitivity, funding stress, negative findings; any redesign is a new version with new prospective evidence; failed versions are preserved; lifetime funding/loss records never reset.

### 15.4 What would falsify the methodology (and the strategy)
**Method-level:** appending future observations changes past decisions (→ rebuild vintage-aware, simpler, or with later decision times); null tests show an edge; the IS→OOS rank relation across candidates is not positive (stop searching); gates are met only in the most favourable cell; selection-adjusted uncertainty is uninformative or one episode dominates (reduce the family, add an economic prior, extend observation; never manufacture a sample from bars or resamples); calibration fails or skill vanishes or a cost change reverses the threshold (remove probabilities); operational violations (restrict opportunities, simplify the response, stop at shadow); a new regime breaks the relationship (mark the claim conditional/expired; restart versioned prospective evidence).
**Strategy-level:** fewer than ~10 episodes together with failing DSR/StepC diagnostics; failure against a baseline under the stress scenario; results vanish under truncation, raw-contract spreads, roll-window removal, ex-date removal, or pessimistic cost/delay/quote assumptions; the advantage disappears in the feasible single-portfolio simulation; the excess is concentrated in one regime or two episodes without a mechanism ("not yet identified"); the shadow result falls outside the pre-stated interval or is inconsistent with the backtest beyond its bootstrap interval; the reaction window is insufficient; the data-feasibility gate fails; the LQDT/flow accounting is inconsistent (ledgers do not reconcile). Where only specific episode types carry the effect, the claim is restricted to them (episode-specific) rather than generalised.

### 15.5 Changes to the project that follow
1. Shrink the hypothesis set to 2–4 economic themes; apply the 1/e stopping discipline; cap configurations.
2. Use episodes (chains) as the evidence unit; report all frames; no probability display by default; make "inconclusive" an explicit, expected outcome.
3. Reduce the universe to roots with the longest point-in-time history; build calendar spreads from explicit contracts; segregate perpetual and quarterly families.
4. Capture intraday data forward from live contracts during shadow mode; do not assume free minute bars.
5. Pre-commit the Feb–Mar 2022 treatment and the estimation-window rule; use documented breaks as boundaries and stress cases.
6. Extend observation beyond 90 days (6–12 months of shadow) if statistical claims are wanted; frame the pilot as operational with explicit objectives, failure triggers and the power arithmetic attached.
7. Decide the GO/collateral question, broker tariffs and the tax layer before the pilot.
8. Widen the episode count (more roots, periods, calendar structures, cross-root spreads) *or* keep a selective zero-trade preference — an explicit trade-off to record, not to resolve by accident.
9. Keep a lightweight append-only registry from day one; fix 2–3 cost/delay scenarios ex ante; run leakage audits on at least one candidate before any further search; treat DSR/FDR as filters, not badges; limit complexity (scenario A of Section 12.6).
10. Scale only after statistical *and* operational gates, in stages, with shadow periods for every design change.
