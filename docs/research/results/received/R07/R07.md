# R07 — Pilot risk, funding liquidity, stress and sizing

**Consolidated evidence report** (merge of nine independent research reports)

**Question.** Are the delegated pilot defaults internally coherent and feasible for integer futures spreads with 3–90 minute reaction, and what bounded sizing/risk rules should be tested? The defaults are not targets to validate at any cost. No-trade is a valid result.

**Evidence cut-off:** 2026-10-22 (current-facts cut-off; project pack prepared 2026-10-07; market figures from the 07.10.2026 ISS snapshot inside the supplied reports unless dated otherwise).

**Status.** Evidence-backed research. Not investment advice, not a trading recommendation, not a legal or contractual opinion, not a backtest. Every RUB ledger below is hypothetical arithmetic on supplied or published parameters. No broker account was accessed, no trade was placed, no broker was contacted, no legal verification was performed, and no book was read in full. Absent information is not zero cost and is not permission.

## 0. What this document is, and how it was assembled

This is a merge of nine independently produced reports answering the same R07 brief. They were written without mutual visibility, from the same brief but with different access to the project's dependency reports (R01 broker rules, R02 costs, R04 data, R05 LQDT benchmark and tax, R06 contract universe, R08 execution, R09 validation). The merge does three things:

1. **De-duplicates.** The eight-way overlap on definitions, the entry inequality, the state machine and the 90-day convention is stated once, in the strongest form any source reached.
2. **Keeps every distinct finding.** Where only one report caught something (evening limit-lock, FIFO/ЛДВ interaction, native calendar-spread order mechanics, WTI negative settlement, the two funding models, σ-per-pair calibration, capacity tables), it is retained with its arithmetic intact.
3. **Adjudicates rather than averages.** Where the nine reports disagree — the fixed 5,000 buffer, the 50% margin ceiling, whether the 5,000 warning should be tightened or kept, the Carver chapter map, the clearing schedule — the disagreement is shown, the better-supported position is adopted, and the losing option is kept visible as a test candidate. Section 21 lists these explicitly. Appendix A maps every section back to its source reports so nothing is silently dropped.

**Provenance conflict that must be read first.** One source report (`ar`) records that it read the dependency pack R01/R02/R04/R05/R06/R09 in full and cites project-specific figures from it (e.g. per-pair gross GO and per-pair daily σ at `[R06 §12.1]`, commissions at `[R02 §3.5]`). The other eight reports state that no dependency report was supplied and mark all project-specific conclusions conditional. Both statements cannot be true of the same session. The resolution used here: pack-derived numbers are reproduced **with their `[Rxx §y]` provenance preserved and labelled "pack-derived, single-source"**, never presented as verified account parameters, and no conclusion is allowed to rest on a pack-derived figure alone. Where a pack figure is load-bearing (contract GO, σ, fees), the public-source arithmetic that brackets it is given alongside, so the argument survives if the pack citation is wrong.

**Notation used throughout.** `V(t)` — strategy-enabled wealth. `B(t)` — matched no-pilot LQDT counterfactual with identical genuine external flows. `X = V − B`, `X(0) = 0` before incremental test costs. `H = max(0, past X)`. `D = H − X`. Both paths mirror genuine external flows and exogenous core actions, but `B` does **not** mirror pilot-induced core sales or liquidations. `E` — lifetime gross additional funding consumed (cap 15,000). `L` or `L_stress` — stressed incremental economic loss to fully flat over the exit window τ. `U` — strictly positive uncertainty buffer. `c_in` — entry costs. `E_t` — sleeve-own eligible equity (core excluded unless approved).

## 1. Executive verdict

**The delegated defaults are arithmetically coherent as a research-governance system. They are not proven feasible as a live risk-control system at RUB 30,000 with 3–90 minute manual reaction on a shared account.** All nine reports converge on "conditional / no live feasibility proven"; none found a contradiction that makes the framework unusable, and none could demonstrate that a minimum-lot spread currently passes every test without emergency funding. The operative decision is therefore **conditional NO LIVE TRADE** until the named account-level facts are verified — not because the numbers are wrong, but because the two binding constraints are not yet measurable.

Seven findings carry that verdict.

1. **The binding constraint is not margin and not commissions — it is the stressed VM/cash path against the LQDT counterfactual.** A 1–3 pair book fits a 25–33% margin ceiling comfortably on NCC gross GO (pack-derived per-pair gross GO: CNYRUBF/CRZ6 2,291 ₽; RGBIF 2,336 ₽; GLDRUBF/GLZ6 2,745 ₽; GAZR 3,481 ₽; IMOEXF 5,134 ₽; SBERF 10,210 ₽; USDRUBF/Si 26,684 ₽ [R06 §12.1], single-source). Yet one joint shock — price gap + spread-offset removal near expiry + reaction delay — moves required liquidity by 8–15k ₽ inside a morning (Ledger B, §12). Conversely, on the packages' own economics the carry of the flagship pairs does not reliably clear the matched-LQDT hurdle at 30k scale (Ledger A).

2. **The 5,000 ₽ gap between the action trigger (10k) and the outer boundary (15k) is not defensible as a universal buffer.** Per-pair daily σ in the post-23.03.2026 window is 688 ₽ (CNYRUBF/CRZ6), 531 ₽ (GLDRUBF/GLZ6), 1,614 ₽ (IMOEXF/MMZ6) [R06 §12.1]. The 5,000 gap therefore spans **7.3 σ-days on a single CNY pair but only 1.03 σ-days on three IMOEX pairs, 1.45 on five CNY pairs, 1.9 on five GL pairs, 1.5 on two IMOEX pairs**. With documented reaction chains of 18–105 minutes and no guaranteed broker grace period, a fixed 5,000 gap can be consumed inside one adverse session on any book at the top of a feasible profile. Replace the fixed gap with a computed uncertainty buffer; retain 5,000 only as a floor.

3. **The fixed 50% starting-margin ceiling is not justified for spreads whose offsets can be removed.** NCC gives intermonth spreads a semi-net ("big leg") discount; that benefit is cancelled on the last trading day of the near leg. Separately, NCC has raised base GO by 1.5×–2.9× in past volatility episodes. Combining a removed offset (≈2×) with a 2× hike gives ≈4×. With a hypothetical 5,000 ₽ reserved for adverse VM and 1,000 ₽ for cash, the defensible starting-margin ceiling falls to ≈**20% of 30k**; with a 3× hike, ≈13%. 50% survives only if there is no offset-removal risk and hikes stay at or below ≈1.6×.

4. **The shared account converts the risk of broker liquidation into the risk of recourse to the core holding. This is the single most important feasibility issue.** Under Bank of Russia Instruction 6681-U and VTB's unified-account (EBS) design, the broker sees the whole portfolio. With >40m ₽ of LQDT on the same EBS, the broker's sufficiency indicators will practically never trigger a closure of a 30k futures sleeve; losses instead draw on core value or create a negative balance. The internal 30k envelope is invisible to the broker. "No silent core bailout" can be enforced only by internal controls or by broker-side segregation that the user has not confirmed. Unless R01 shows that such a control exists, the result is **no live trade**, not an assumed core bailout.

5. **Emergency funding cannot be counted as a risk reducer — not at entry, and usually not during stress either.** Funding counts only if approval **and** transfer **and** credit all fit inside the broker's closure deadline. Under 6681-U that deadline is the same trading day, or the next day's cut-off if the breach occurs after the restrictive closing time. Arrival times are unknown; the default is zero. In hypothetical Ledger D a transfer approved at the trigger misses the deadline and the position is closed at a worse price — approval and availability are different facts.

6. **Some risks cannot be bounded by internal limits at all:** broker recourse to the >40m core; exchange halts and close-only regimes (March 2022); settlement outside the price band (MOEX WTI, April 2020, settled at −$37.63); evening limit-lock, because the automatic corridor expansion is not performed after 19:00; weekend sessions that generate P&L while the user is away. The 5k/10k/15k numbers are action rules, not guaranteed maxima, and realised loss can exceed the 15,000 boundary by execution and liquidation mechanics alone.

7. **Integer lots make the pilot binary.** At 30k the account is in Carver's "threshold / one-instrument" regime: the feasible size is 0 or 1 pair per idea, continuous risk scaling does not exist, and fractional-Kelly sizing is not merely unsupported but undefined without calibrated probabilities. The correct output when one minimum-lot spread fails the gates is **no trade**, not a fractional contract.

**Recommended test package** (to be tested, not adopted blind): minimum integer lots only; a per-profile σ-budget in ₽/day as the primary size control; gross GO ≤ 33% of the remaining envelope **and** stressed GO (offset removed) + `U` ≤ envelope; entry/continuation gate `X − L_stress − u ≥ −15,000` with a computed `U` floored at 5,000; a separate cash-path gate; no Kelly-family sizing; one idea at a time until an aggregate joint-stress test passes without diversification credit. Details in §10–§14.

## 3. Risk quantities that must not be conflated

The policy triggers on `X`/`D`; the broker triggers on portfolio solvency; the market triggers on price gaps. These are different state variables measured in different systems — one pack source states it directly: the exchange offset reduces the value of the position while the client indicator NPR2 triggers liquidation, "two different numbers in two different systems" [R01 §7.2]. Conflating them is the most common failure mode in the source reports, and keeping them apart is the first substantive requirement of the brief.

| # | Quantity | Definition | What it consumes | What it does **not** capture / how it goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Relative economic loss | `X = V − B`; `D = H − X`, HWM convention fixed in advance | The 15k outer boundary and 10k action authority | Liquidity to survive the next VM call; the broker's opinion. `X` can be negative while absolute P&L is positive and vice versa |
| 2 | Absolute P&L | Sleeve wealth change net of external flows, including fees and funding | Nothing directly; it feeds `X` | Benchmark drift. A +100 ₽ nominal gain against +300 ₽ of LQDT is `X = −200` |
| 3 | Peak drawdown of X | `D = H − X`, with `H = max(0, past X)`; `D⁺ = max(0, H − X)` if H excludes current X — one convention must be registered | 5k warning, 10k action | Cash path and margin. D ≥ 10k can fire while `X` is still positive |
| 4 | Cash / VM path | Free rubles + due VM + due fees + debt service, as a function of time | Available liquidity, not necessarily `X` | VM settles **in money only** (BCS App. 6 §2.3: "Вариационная маржа — сумма денежных средств"). A liquidity crisis can precede `X = −15k`. Cash debits lag the marks by up to ≈20 h (§4) |
| 5 | Broker margin / collateral | `M0 = GO × k`; `NPR1 = S − M0 − S_block`; `NPR2 = S − Mx`; `Mx = 0.5·M0`; `УДС = NPR2/(M0 − Mx)` (6681-U method) | Broker-recognised collateral `S`, which includes core LQDT at haircut | The internal 30k label. The pack source is explicit: the internal "30 000 ₽" balance does not replace the broker's `S` [R01 §8.3] |
| 6 | Gross exposure | Σ of absolute leg notional, integer contracts, pair notional ≈23–26k ₽ per flagship pair (pack-derived) | Capacity and concentration | Loss. A market-neutral spread can carry large gross notional; low GO from spread relief is not low gap risk |
| 7 | Temporary single-leg risk | One leg filled or part-filled while the other is stale or rejected; the hedge "cancels instantly when the legs desynchronise" [R01 §9] | Margin and outright price risk **without** offset | Invisible in pair-level marks until the second leg completes. Can exceed any trigger's ability to respond within 3–90 minutes |
| 8 | Account-wide recourse | The broker's right to use all client assets: VTB EBS portfolio unity with discretionary closure; BCS App. 6 §1.4 (all assets secure futures obligations) and §1.5 (transfers between accounts without separate notice) [R01 §0, §6.2, §9] | The account, not the sleeve | Anything about the internal budget. The 30k/45k labels do not bind the broker [R01 §0] |

### 3.1 Three cross-state violations that are live-trading blockers

- **Policy green, broker red.** The sleeve passes `X − L_stress − u ≥ −15,000` while broker NPR2 < 0 because the core LQDT haircut rose. On a KPUR→KNUR swing, the collateral credit of 30k units moves 27,600 → 24,600 ₽ [R01 §6.1].
- **X ≈ 0 while the cash path fails.** VM is due in money; an LQDT sale settles T+1; a negative balance is a separately paid product (VTB margin loan ≈ key rate + 6.9% = 20.9% p.a.; BCS deficit maintenance 0.1% per calendar day ≈ 36.5% p.a. simple). "No price found" is not "free" [R01 §8.3].
- **Naked leg dominates the spread.** A rejected second leg converts a hedged pair into directional exposure larger than any X trigger can address inside the reaction window (Ledger G, §12).

### 3.2 Why the budgets are drawn at different times

This is the mechanism that makes additive "best-case" stress tests wrong. Benchmark drag accrues daily, including weekends. VM hits the marks immediately but the cash at T+1 20:00. A margin hike consumes liquidity with no economic loss at all. On a shared EBS the core absorbs a sleeve deficit silently. A stress scenario must move price, margin and liquidity **together on one timeline** (§11), and no diversification credit may be taken unless a sourced joint-tail scenario shows otherwise.

## 4. Venue mechanics that set the stress path

### 4.1 Trading session, clearing and the VM timing lag

MOEX moved derivatives to a unified trading session on **23 March 2026**; the dated 13 March 2026 notice states that intermediate clearing is cancelled ("Промежуточный клиринг … отменяется") [S12]. The main clearing now runs **23:50–00:30**, and settlement is **T+1 at 20:00** without stopping trading [S12, S13]. The exchange's own presentation describes the 20:00 T+1 event as the actual debiting of obligations booked in the mark-to-market registers, and defines the settlement price as the **median of 12 snapshots** of bid, ask and last taken from 18:58 to 18:59 [S13]. From **14 July 2026** weekday trading runs 06:50–23:50; currency futures joined the weekend session from 18 July, trading 09:50–19:00 within ±3% bounds [S14].

**A conflict must be resolved here, and it matters.** Two source reports (`gr`, `cr`) model margin calls at intraday clearing **14:00–14:05** and evening clearing **18:50–19:05**, with shortfalls to be covered by 17:30 the same day or 12:30 the next day. That schedule predates the 23.03.2026 change and is used by both reports without a date. It is also still visible in places: the VTB KDS formula, an undated unified-session explainer and older contract pages retain intermediate-clearing fields [S5]. **Resolution adopted:** the dated MOEX notice and the current session-specific schedule govern; the twice-daily clearing model is treated as **stale**, and any calculation that depends on a 14:00 or 18:50 call is recalculated on the 23:50–00:30 / T+1 20:00 basis. Where a report's arithmetic used the old schedule (`gr`'s clearing-liquidity constraint, `cr`'s margin-call deadlines), the *structure* of the constraint is retained and the *clock* is replaced. This is exactly the failure mode the brief warns about: a later edited page does not prove what was known historically, and a stale page does not prove the current regime.

**Consequences for the pilot:**

- Cash debits lag the marks by up to ≈20 hours. A sufficiency breach therefore happens **on the marks, intraday**, before any cash leaves the account.
- Broker sufficiency checks use real-time indicative VM. VTB's KDS includes "Вар. маржа реал.", computed in real time [S5].
- The exit window τ must extend through clearing and settlement, not just the trading session.
- Weekend sessions create P&L while the user may be away; for one weekend session NCC widened the corridor for BR from 0.04 to 0.0792, TTF 0.06→0.16, SILV 0.06→0.1188, NG 0.08→0.12 [S15]. The weekend is part of the following working trading day [S12].

### 4.2 Price limits, limit-lock and evening risk

When a price reaches the monitoring zone the exchange may suspend trading to widen the limits, and this "is accompanied by an increase in the guarantee provision" [S4, S16]. Between **09:00 and 19:00** the limit widens automatically within 5 minutes if an order sits near the limit; **from 19:00 to 23:50 the automatic widening is not performed** [S16]. An adverse evening move can therefore lock a spread leg at the limit until the next session. **The exit window τ must include "limit-locked until morning".** No new risk should be opened after 19:00 unless `L` already contains an evening limit-lock scenario.

The ±40% figure that circulates as a "futures circuit-breaker" is not one: in the trading rules it concerns securities in the main trading mode, while for futures the limit is calculated from the basic initial margin and the administrator may increase trading limits by 1.5× [S33]. This is a substantive correction to a common assumption in the reviewed material and is retained.

### 4.3 Halts, close-only regimes and settlement outside the band

These are the events that make internal limits non-binding, and they are documented, not hypothetical:

- **March 2022:** equity and index derivatives traded only through negotiated "Закрытие позиций" (close-only) orders; currency and commodity contracts traded 10:00–18:45; on 5, 7 and 8 March there was no trading on any MOEX market [S20].
- **20–21 April 2020:** MOEX halted the expiring WTI contract, which settled at **−$37.63**; aggregate long losses were estimated above RUB 1 bn [S19]. A settlement outside any price band is a loss mechanism that no `X`-based stop can address.
- **24 February 2022:** the RTS index fell roughly **49.3% intraday** (MOEX index −45.5%) before partial recovery, with repeated suspensions [S49].

### 4.4 What VM timing does and does not do

One report (`mm`) argues that because variation margin is calculated once per day at the night clearing, intraday adverse moves during the 3–90 minute response window "will not by themselves generate margin calls if IM coverage is maintained", so the pilot needs two distinct stress lenses: intraday (economic `X` drawdown plus possible IM increase) and end-of-day (VM crystallisation plus IM re-parameterisation). This is correct as far as it goes and is retained — with the correction that broker sufficiency is tested on **real-time indicative** VM [S5], so a margin breach can precede the cash debit even though the cash moves once. The two lenses are therefore: *economic* (marks, continuous) and *cash* (T+1 20:00, discrete, with step changes if prices gap into the clearing window).

## 5. Margin mechanics: offsets, their removal, and hikes

### 5.1 Portfolio margining and spread relief

Risks on individual instruments are netted using known price relationships [S1]; NCC states that dynamic parameters are defined at each clearing and during the calculation period when price bands or risk-assessment ranges change [S3], and publishes lists of eligible inter-contract and inter-month spread groups — correlation observed by a researcher alone creates no exchange relief [S3]. NCC's methodology is a scenario/SPAN-like portfolio approach, and initial-margin benefits apply to inter-contract and inter-month spreads [S30, pp. 7–8, 26].

The mechanics that matter for sizing:

- For opposite positions in an intermonth group the **larger of the two legs' GO is blocked** under semi-net margining (the "big leg" rule: spread GO is computed "без учета положительных рисков") [S21], or only the interest-rate risk under net margining.
- **On the last trading day the benefit for the nearest contract is cancelled** and moves to the next expiry [S22].
- MOEX has a native **calendar-spread order** (limit, market or FOK) executing both legs at once, but at matching "Гарантийное обеспечение резервируется в таком же размере, как при сведении 2-х атомарных заявок" — GO is reserved as for two atomic orders [S24]. A spread order removes *legging* risk; it does **not** remove the matching-time collateral requirement.
- **No equivalent native order exists for inter-commodity pairs**, which must be legged and therefore carry temporary single-leg risk.
- NCC changes spread-group membership by notice; e.g. from 06.02.2025 it changed the `window_size` parameter and intermonth inclusion for T/TCSI [S23]. Eligibility is therefore not a stable property.

### 5.2 The prudent base assumption

**At entry the pilot must be able to fund the sum of both leg IMs, with any exchange spread benefit treated as upside, not as a sizing input.** This follows directly from the brief's own requirement to assume existing margin offsets may be removed, and from the fact that broker configurations cannot be contractually verified ex ante. Every ledger in §12 uses this no-offset base except where a stress step explicitly shows the offset disappearing.

### 5.3 Hikes, and the multiplier arithmetic

NCC has raised base GO by large multiples in volatility episodes:

| Episode | Change | Multiple |
|---|---|---|
| 2016, GBPU | base GO 7% → 20% "ввиду повышенной волатильности" [S17] | ≈2.9× |
| 2016, RTS | 10% → 15% [S17] | 1.5× |
| 22 April 2020, nearest BR and CL | GO ≈73% for sellers, 55–61% for buyers [S18] | order of 10× |
| 12 March 2026, Brent family MR1/MR2/MR3 | 12/18/26% → 18/24/32%, effective 19:00 that date [S35] | 1.23–1.5× |

Past parameter revisions also show how far a single parameter can move: the spread risk parameter `NCl` for natural-gas futures was raised from 50 to 500, and inter-month spread risk rules have been tightened in ways that raised calendar-spread IM directly. MOEX's volatility index (RVI) captures 30-day implied volatility from RTS options but does not supply intraday or single-day empirical quantiles, so it cannot substitute for the tail estimation the pilot still lacks.

The 12 March 2026 notice is important as a discipline point: it is a **dated risk-rate change, not a universal 1.5× broker-margin multiplier and not a statistically calibrated tail**. Exchange margin-rate changes, broker required-asset changes and mark-to-market shocks must be combined as scenarios, never quoted as a general multiplier.

The combined stress multiplier used in the source set is

    γ_margin = μ_rate_hike × 1 / (1 − ΔK_spread_reduction)

where `μ_rate_hike ∈ [1.5, 2.0]` is base-rate expansion and `ΔK` is the fraction of spread discount lost. With a 50% rate increase and spread relief falling from 80% to 40%: `γ = 1.5 × (1−0.2)/(1−0.6) = 1.5 × 2.0 = 3.0`. A spread needing 10,000 ₽ of IM in a normal market can need 30,000 ₽ during a dislocation. **This is a sensitivity parameterisation, not an estimate of probability.** Treating 2× IM as "a roughly 99.9% one-day tail bound" (as one source report does) is explicitly rejected here: it is not a statistical claim and must not be described as calibrated.

## 6. Broker recourse and liquidation mechanics

### 6.1 6681-U: when the broker may close, and to what level

Under Bank of Russia Instruction No. 6681-U (12.02.2024, in force 01.04.2025):

- If NPR2 < 0 **before the restrictive closing time**, the broker closes positions **within that trading day**; if the breach occurs after it, closure is due by the next trading day's cut-off [S25].
- Closure runs to **NPR1 = 0** for KSUR/KNUR clients and to **NPR2 = 0** for KPUR clients [S25]. A qualified investor's KPUR status therefore changes the depth of a forced closure — which is why broker recognition of the user's status is a live-trading gate, not an administrative detail.
- Closure is not permitted if NPR2 has turned positive again, unless the agreement says otherwise [S25].
- Futures enter portfolio value through VM computed under the clearing rules [S25].
- The broker's internal document defines the restrictive closing time (п. 17) [S25, S26].

NCC's side: on the futures market a clearing member's margin requirement arises when free collateral SZSC is negative, must be met within the time set by the applicable temporary regulations, and non-compliance may lead to settlement-regime, asset-transfer or closing actions [S7, arts. 33.7–33.11]. NCC requires clearing members to satisfy margin calls **no later than 45 minutes before the next intraday or evening clearing session**, failing which it suspends execution, cancels orders and **mandatorily closes positions** [S30, p. 26]. These are member-level rules, not the retail contract, but they remove any assumption of a discretionary rescue window.

### 6.2 VTB

- NPR2 < 0 means the broker will independently close part of the positions [S26].
- For derivatives, if KDS < X the bank closes positions until KDS ≥ Y. The published page states **X = 0 and Y = 0.2**, and says guarantee-asset requirements change immediately on an exchange announcement [S5].
- A VTB reply quoted on Banki.ru cites clause 24.19: positions are closed "до окончания Торговой сессии следующего торгового дня", including when the exchange raises GO [S27]. A separate copy of the regulation clause set gives the same next-session deadline [S31].
- In litigation VTB reportedly called KDS "индикативный"; the KDS formula was corrected only in the regulation of 07.04.2023 [S28].
- **The public KDS page is internally inconsistent with the current regime:** its formula still contains intermediate-clearing fields that no longer exist after 23.03.2026 [S5, S12]. Reconfirm the currently effective client formula, category and regulation rather than applying the page mechanically.
- VTB's EBS (August 2025) lets liquid securities from one section serve as collateral in another with a single portfolio result [S29] — which is precisely the mechanism by which core LQDT can support a futures deficit. No verified core isolation follows from any public document.

### 6.3 BCS (a later, not current, broker)

- The futures service agreement, Appendix 6 §§1.4–1.5: **all assets** serve as security, and BCS may transfer funds between client accounts and markets without separate notice to restore NPR1 [S8, from the version approved 17.07.2023 and marked 01.08.2023].
- §§5.5–5.10: NPR2 below zero permits closure or cancellation; KPUR closure runs until a broker-set УДС level; market-price orders are at broker discretion [S8]. The accessible version also states that any debt on the derivatives market lets the broker close positions forcibly [S8, S29].
- A reduced-GO service exists (2× lower, valid 10:00–18:30), and search snippets indicate minimum margin = 0.5 × IM from 01.04.2025 with closure to УДС = 0.5 for KPUR — **snippet only, page body did not render, unverified**.
- These are BCS terms for a *later possible* broker. They do not establish VTB client thresholds and must not be transplanted.

### 6.4 What this means for the internal policy

A risk monitor that watches only `X`, `D`, or "initial GO < 15,000 ₽" is incomplete. It must read the actual broker indicators and model the **earliest possible broker action**, which can precede every monetary trigger. If the account's terms and indicator behaviour cannot be obtained, the correct position is **no live trade on the assumption that the internal policy controls the account** — because it does not.

## 7. Collateral, the LQDT benchmark and tax

### 7.1 LQDT as collateral

LQDT is admitted collateral at both brokers: VTB's list of liquid property of 07.10.2026 (sheet 1, row 813) and BCS Appendix 6 §1.4. Haircuts differ by broker and by client risk category, and the difference is material at pilot scale: a KPUR→KNUR swing on VTB moves the collateral credit of 30k units from 27,600 to 24,600 ₽ (8% → 18%), and BCS's schedule (23/18/10 or 25/20/12) differs again — a broker choice changes the credit of 30k-equivalent units by ≈3,000 ₽ [R01 §6.1; S8].

The manager's product page (accessed 21.10.2026) identifies a RUSFAR-linked reverse-repo objective and expenses of "не более 0,29364% в год" [S37]. **Expenses are already embedded in the observed NAV**, so they are not an extra debit against the matched benchmark `B`; deducting them twice is an accounting error the merged report explicitly forbids.

Two unresolved conflicts are carried, not hidden:

- **NCC LQDT limit interpretation:** "limit 60%" read as a 7.5% discount with a 60% concentration limit, versus as "count ≤60% of value" [R05 §11.1]. Both readings make LQDT credit **less than par**; the report uses the weaker qualitative conclusion only.
- **One-day marketability of LQDT under stress:** the assumption that units can be sold within one trading day is not confirmed by the documents ("допущение о реализации паёв за один торговый день документами не подтверждено" [R01 §6.1]). Combined with T+1 settlement, this is why a sale cannot be treated as same-day cash.

### 7.2 Tax: the one interaction that can dwarf the pilot

Under Article 219.1 of the Tax Code, the long-term holding deduction (ЛДВ) applies to securities held continuously for more than three years on a taxable brokerage account, with a deduction cap of RUB 3,000,000 per year of ownership. Russian brokerage tax accounting applies **FIFO** to securities sales. If the broker liquidates part of the core LQDT to cover a futures VM call, the oldest lots go first — resetting the three-year holding clock for those shares and destroying accumulated progress toward the deduction. A forced sale of 100,000 ₽ of core to cover a transient futures call can therefore cause multi-year tax leakage far exceeding the entire pilot allocation [S47].

Two accounting rules follow, and both are retained in the merged ledger convention:

- **Sale proceeds are not the loss.** A broker-initiated core sale creates a cash transfer, not an `X` loss; the `X` loss is the VM plus costs plus, separately, the tax and foregone-return consequences.
- **The tax layer is calculated separately, never folded into the numeric defaults.** For a 30% embedded gain on a 10,100 ₽ forced sale the tax at 13% is ≈390 ₽; for old high-gain core lots it can reach thousands, and for large core sales the tax "может быть сопоставим с границами 10–15 тыс. ₽" [R05 §9]. The tax cash call arrives later (broker as agent, year-end or withdrawal) [R05 §9.2].

Per the brief, only the loss of the LQDT long-term holding deduction is excluded from the matched comparison; every other investor/tax-residence fact remains unknown and is treated as a deferred sensitivity, `X_after_tax = X_before_tax − (tax on V − tax on B)` with identical genuine external flows on both paths.

## 8. Adjudicating each delegated default

Each default is assessed on four questions: is it internally coherent, is it **sufficient** as a control, what does it fail to capture, and what must be true before it is used live. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is stated with the adopted position and the losing option's test role.

### 8.1 Initial RUB 30,000 all-in allocation — **retain as an authority ceiling, not as feasible capital**

All nine reports retain 30,000 and none could demonstrate that it is sufficient. The envelope must simultaneously cover entry margin, a stressed VM path, exit costs and a positive uncertainty buffer; it is not one fungible "risk budget". The pack-derived view is that the realistic minimum is 1–2 pairs of the two cheapest flagship families (CNYRUBF/CRZ6 or GLDRUBF/GLZ6, gross GO 2,291–5,490 ₽) with a VM reserve of 24–28k, and that any pair with GO above 10k leaves no reserve at all [R06 §12.1]. The public arithmetic in §5.4 reaches the same conclusion independently: mini index pairs ≈4.9–5.6k, full RTS pairs ≈45–50k.

**Adopted:** 30,000 is a hard sleeve envelope and a lifetime authority limit. It is not an equity-loss limit, not evidence that one lot fits, and not a broker-recourse limit.
**Entry filter (test candidate):** minimum-lot spread IM ≤ 25–30% of 30,000 (≤7,500–9,000 ₽) under the **no-offset** assumption, which is the range several reports converged on independently (one proposed ≤9,000 ₽; another ≤7,500 ₽; another ≤33% of the remaining envelope). If the minimum lot cannot satisfy this plus the stress and cash gates, **size is zero** — there is no fractional contract and no rounding up.

### 8.2 RUB 15,000 lifetime gross additional funding (45,000 ₽ gross total) — **retain, count it as zero everywhere**

The cap is an authority limit on **gross contributions**. It is not a loss limit, not a notional limit, not an equity limit and not a broker-recourse limit. The counting convention is coherent and must be preserved exactly:

- Count **gross-positive**: a sequence of +5, +4, −3, +7 consumes `E = 16` and breaches the 15 cap while gross contributions reach 46 [R05 §4.5] — i.e. returned funds and withdrawals do **not** restore the allowance.
- A newly earmarked cash or collateral amount counts once. A **collateral-to-cash conversion is not counted twice**.
- **Unapproved broker use of core collateral is an incident and counts toward the cap** — a forced 20,000 ₽ core mobilisation breaches the 15,000 lifetime allowance even though there is no immediate 20,000 ₽ `X` loss.
- A genuine external top-up raises `V` and `B` by the same amount and **leaves `X` unchanged**; it consumes the allowance if earmarked for the pilot, but "a matched external top-up must not improve X".

**Adopted:** retain as a capital-accounting cap; require per-event approval **and** a verified availability/credit timing; funding is never restart approval and never permission for fresh exposure or averaging down. Entry must pass with zero emergency funding.
**Sensitivity:** the 45,000 gross is not a notional, equity, loss or broker-recourse limit, and the 15,000 is only 50% of the original envelope — one gap plus exit costs can consume it before any transfer arrives (Ledger D).

### 8.3 RUB 5,000 warning (D ≥ 5,000) — **retain as a soft trigger; do not treat as a risk limit**

Positions diverge here and the merge keeps both sides:

- **Retain at 5,000** (`ar`, `ds`, `gs`, `ge`, `cg` with a scale-in pause): it is cheap, informative, and in one calibration it coincides almost exactly with where the continuous stress inequality forces de-risking to begin (`X ≥ −15,000 + 1.5·L ≈ −5,150` for a 6.5k-`L` idea, §10.4).
- **Tighten to 3,000–4,000** (`cr`, `gr` at 4,000): a 5,000 warning is 17% of the envelope, reached by ordinary adverse MTM plus normal exit costs on a 8–10k-IM spread with a ±2–3k daily VM range, so it will be noisy; and against the common "1–2% of capital per trade" norm the whole 5k/10k/15k ladder (17%/33%/50%) is aggressive by an order of magnitude.

**Adopted:** retain 5,000 as the delegated warning because it is the level at which the *continuous* gate already requires action for a maximum-size idea, and the stress inequality — not the monetary ladder — is the working control. Record the 3,000–4,000 variants as test cases in the same replay. Either way: a warning is not a stop, it caps nothing, and it does not reset `H`.

### 8.4 RUB 10,000 action (D ≥ 10,000 or X ≤ −10,000) — **retain as a backstop, subordinate to the inequality**

The two limbs are different rules and both are kept:

- `D ≥ 10,000` is a **giveback** rule: it can fire while `X` is still positive (H = 12,000 → fires at X = +2,000; H = 20,000 → fires at X = +9,200). Response: suspend entries and additions, cancel risk-increasing working orders, reduce risk in an orderly way, require user review before resuming — even if `X` remains positive.
- `X ≤ −10,000` is a **shortfall** rule: cease new risk, begin an orderly exit; any separately approved funding is only to reach flat, not to await recovery and not to average down.

The coherent critique, adopted here: **a monetary action trigger cannot be the main defence**, because gaps and halts let `X` jump past it, and broker indicators can force action first. One report summarises it as: the continuous inequality protects the boundary; the 10k and 15k numbers are backstops. A second shows the arithmetic: with a 6.5k joint stress loss, a minimum-lot idea at `X = 0` must start de-risking around −5,250 — near the warning, well before 10k.
**Test candidates in the opposite direction:** tighten action to 7,000–8,000 (`cr`, `gr`) to widen the action-to-boundary distance, and/or add a **liquidity pre-trigger** (`ds`): if broker УДС or free margin falls below a pre-agreed buffer, reduce risk **before** the economic trigger, because broker action overrides monetary triggers.

### 8.5 RUB 15,000 outer boundary (X < −15,000) — **retain as a breach line, never as a loss cap**

All nine agree. `X < −15,000` is a breach, not a permitted overshoot; the boundary must never be widened to fit a lot. It is simultaneously true that realised loss can **exceed** it — through gap-through, forced-liquidation slippage, spread break and fees — and the ledger set in §12 demonstrates overshoot twice (−16,200 in one, −17,000 in another). The correct reading is an ex-ante stop on authority, not an ex-post guarantee, and a breach is an incident to be recorded and reconciled, not smoothed away.

### 8.6 The 5,000 gap between action (10k) and boundary (15k) — **the most fragile default; replace with a computed buffer**

This is the one finding on which all nine reports agree in substance and which changes the design of the control system. The gap is a **path-dependent budget, not a margin of safety**:

| `X` at the moment of action | Remaining headroom to −15,000 |
|---|---|
| −10,000 | 5,000 |
| −11,000 | 4,000 |
| −12,500 (Ledger after delay + exit) | 2,500 |
| −14,000 | 1,000 |

A joint price/margin/liquidity shock can consume the entire distance **while creating the trigger**. Concretely: a gap (−9,000) plus a 30-minute delay (−2,000) plus exit costs (−1,500) takes `X` from −9,000 to −12,500, leaving 2,500; add one more adverse session and the boundary is gone. In σ terms (§1, finding 2) the gap is 1.0–2 σ-days for any book at the top of a feasible profile.

### 8.7 The 50% starting-margin ceiling and the 5,000 stress buffer — **test hypotheses, not requirements**

Neither survives as stated, and they fail for opposite reasons: the ceiling is too **loose** because it is calculated on a variable that can double overnight, and the buffer is too **small** because it is fixed while the thing it must absorb is not.

- **50% ceiling:** permits 15,000 ₽ of IM, leaving 15,000 for VM, exit costs and buffer. Tested against offset removal plus hikes it degenerates (table in §10.3). Fifty percent survives only in the cell where there is no offset to lose and hikes stay at or below ≈1.6×.
- **5,000 buffer:** 5,000 ₽ is below the plausible 90-minute adverse move plus exit costs for several candidate families and below a 30% one-day index move on a mini contract (≈7.2–7.5k ₽ of MTM on a 24–25k notional). One report puts it bluntly: a 5k buffer does not absorb a 30% one-day tail on a single mini contract, let alone a two-leg spread with basis risk.

**Adopted:** replace both with derived rules (§10.3, §10.4). Keep 5,000 as a **floor** on the uncertainty buffer and keep 50% as a **sensitivity grid point** (25%/33%/35%/50%) for the replay, not as a live rule.

### 8.8 Manual reaction 3–90 minutes — **a scenario input, not a measured distribution**

All reports treat 3–90 minutes as given and none could measure it. What is documented: a 15-minute delay on free public feeds, and a full monitor→notice→order→fill chain of 18–105 minutes in the pack measurements [R04 §1.6, single-source]. The **p90 case is the sizing constraint**, not the median. Model 3, 5–15, 30, 60 and 90 minutes, plus "user unavailable for one session", "connectivity down" and "market closed", and require the exit window τ to cover them. If no reliable upper bound exists, the pilot must not hold unattended exposed positions. One report's tightening suggestion — a 10-minute legging timeout after which the filled leg is flattened — is retained as a test candidate.

### 8.9 90-day review, no resets — **retain; pin the convention** (§17)

### 8.10 Emergency money only to flat at X ≤ −10k, never for fresh exposure — **retain**

Consistent with "entry must pass with zero emergency funding", and with the prohibition on funding averaging down. Funding approval is not restart approval; each event needs separate approval **and** an availability/credit check; absent approval the default is no funding.

### 8.11 Two funding models that must not be blurred — a 14% p.a. hurdle decision

The 30,000 label can be implemented two ways with identical `X` algebra but different economics, and the brief's comparison requires the choice to be explicit:

- **(a) Cash sleeve.** Sell ≈30,000 ₽ of LQDT, hold cash plus futures. `B` keeps the units, so `X` carries the full LQDT drift as opportunity cost: at RUSFAR ≈ the key rate of 14.00% since 27.07.2026 [S38], the hurdle is ≈**690 ₽ per 60 days ≈ 14% p.a. on the whole sleeve**.
- **(b) Pledged units plus a small VM cash buffer.** Keep the units as collateral (the haircut applies to credit, not to wealth) and sell only the VM buffer. `X` then excludes LQDT drift on pledged units (they grow in both `V` and `B`) and pays instead: VTB's fee on uncovered GO `(GO − rubles) × (КС + 2%) × T/365` = **16% p.a.** currently, plus LQDT drift on the sold buffer — a hurdle of ≈**2–4% p.a. on the sleeve**. But VM still needs real rubles and haircuts can bind.

**Consequence, and it belongs in the charter.** In model (a) the carry of the flagship pairs — CNY ≈ +3.1% p.a. on pair notional (≈13% p.a. on 30k with five pairs), GL ≈ +7.1% (≈27% p.a. on 30k with five pairs), IMOEX ≈ +13.7% [R06 §10] — does **not** dominate the LQDT hurdle uniformly: a CNY book is roughly break-even before noise, while a GL book is positive in expectation with a five-pair daily 1σ of 2,655 ₽ against a 690 ₽ hurdle. The pilot's excess therefore depends on **spread-timing alpha (z-entry/exit) and/or GL-family carry, not on the delegated risk arithmetic**. The evidence base for that alpha is inconclusive (141 daily observations, one regime, one expiry). A structurally mildly negative `X` is a plausible outcome of a correctly executed pilot, and zero-trade days are acceptable.

## 9. From a monetary ladder to a control system

The delegated defaults are a **ladder of monetary triggers**. A ladder has three structural weaknesses at this scale, and the source reports converge on the same remedy:

1. **A monetary trigger is discrete; the risk is continuous.** `X` can jump from −8,000 to −14,000 between two reliable marks. A rule that reads only the crossing level is therefore a backstop, not a control. The protection has to be a *forward-looking inequality evaluated at every reliable mark*, of which the ladder is the escalation overlay.
2. **Two budgets move on different clocks.** The economic budget (`X`) and the liquidity budget (free rubles + broker collateral) fail at different times and for different reasons. A single combined "worst case P&L" number hides that path dependence. The pack source states the requirement directly: two independent reserves, risk and cash [R01 §8.3]. So there must be at least two gates, and a trade must pass **both under the same joint scenario**.
3. **The account is co-mingled, so the policy cannot be the last line of defence.** If the broker's right to use the core is unbounded in practice, internal limits govern behaviour but not consequences. That is why a **recourse gate** is a hard precondition rather than a parameter.

**The adopted architecture therefore has one leading rule and four backstops:**

| Role | Rule | When it acts |
|---|---|---|
| Leading control | Economic stress inequality (C1) evaluated continuously | Every reliable mark, before any gap becomes a breach |
| Independent gate | Liquidity/cash inequality (C2) | Every reliable mark, on a T+1 cash clock |
| Entry discipline | Entry gates E1 and E2, tighter than C1/C2 | At every entry and increase |
| Backstop 1 | `D ≥ 5,000` warning | Escalation and reassessment |
| Backstop 2 | `D ≥ 10,000` / `X ≤ −10,000` action | Suspension, orderly reduction, review |
| Backstop 3 | `X < −15,000` breach | Incident, project stop pending review |
| Override | Broker / naked-leg / stale-data / delivery danger | Immediately, before any monetary rule |

The consequence is a design choice worth stating plainly: **"act at 10,000" cannot be the main defence.** For a maximum-size idea the inequality forces de-risking to begin near the warning level. That does not show the defaults are wrong; it shows which of them is the working control.

## 10. Gate architecture: the inequalities to register

All parameters below are proposals for the research protocol. None is approved, calibrated, or a validation result.

### 10.1 Terms

- `c_in` — entry costs (fees plus half-spread on both legs), already inside `X` after entry.
- `L_s` — stressed incremental loss under joint scenario `s` over exit window `τ_s`, comprising: adverse spread move including gap or limit-lock to the next session; legging or partial-fill outright loss; stressed exit costs on both legs; benchmark drag over `τ_s`. `L = max_s L_s`.
- `U > 0` — uncertainty buffer for model error, unmodelled tails, mark disagreement and timestamp error.
- `E_t` — sleeve-own eligible equity: sleeve cash + sleeve-own eligible collateral after broker haircut + indicative VM − pending T+1 debits. **Core LQDT excluded unless approved.**
- `IM_t`, `M_s` — broker initial requirement and margin-hike multiplier; `O_s` — offset-removal multiplier (≈2 for a semi-net intermonth spread with similar legs; 1 for pairs already margined as the sum).
- `R` — cash reserve for fees and T+1 timing.
- `F_s` — emergency funding counted in scenario `s`. **Zero at entry**; otherwise counted only if `t_approve + t_transfer + t_credit ≤ t_broker_deadline` **and** lifetime gross remaining suffices.

### 10.2 The four gates and the recourse precondition

**E1 — economic entry/increase gate (tighter than the continuation rule):**

    X − c_in − L − U ≥ −10,000

Rationale (judgment, not evidence): a fresh entry under its own stress scenario should not land at the action level, otherwise ordinary adverse noise forces an immediate exit.

**C1 — economic continuation gate (the default made continuous):**

    X_t − L_t − U_t ≥ −15,000    at every reliable mark

If it fails: begin orderly de-risking until it holds. **Broker, naked-leg, stale-data or delivery danger overrides C1.**

**E2 — liquidity entry gate, with zero emergency funding:**

    for all s:  E_0 − c_in − ΔVM_s − fees_s − IM_0 · M_s · O_s ≥ R

**C2 — liquidity continuation:**

    for all s:  E_t + F_s − ΔVM_s − fees_s − IM_t · M_s · O_s ≥ R

with `F_s = 0` unless approval **and** arrival timing are evidenced. If C2 fails, de-risk — that is **not** a trigger to request funds.

**R — recourse gate:** a verified mechanism must either prevent sleeve losses from reaching core collateral without approval, or detect it within the reaction time. **Otherwise there is no live trade.** This is the gate that is currently unverifiable, and the reason the overall verdict is conditional no-trade.

**Aggregate form:** `L_port = Σ_i L_i`, with **no diversification credit in stress** unless a sourced joint-tail scenario shows otherwise; `IM_port` summed with offsets removed.

**Action-trigger form** (equivalent statement of the same constraint, for use at the moment a trigger fires):

    L_exit,stress + U_remaining ≤ X_at_action + 15,000

At `X = −10,000` the right-hand side is 5,000; at −11,000 it is 4,000; at −14,000 it is 1,000. If exit stress plus uncertainty exceeds the remaining distance, either entry and de-risking must be earlier and tighter, or the strategy is infeasible at that size.

### 10.3 Deriving the starting-margin ceiling instead of assuming 50%

From E2: `IM_0 ≤ (30,000 − c_in − ΔVM_s − R) / (M_s · O_s)`.

Hypothetical inputs: `ΔVM = 5,000`, `R = 1,000`, `c_in ≈ 0` → numerator 24,000.

| Hike multiplier `M` | `O = 1` (no offset to lose) | `O = 2` (semi-net offset removed) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5× (RTS 2016 analogue) | 16,000 ₽ = 53% | 8,000 ₽ = 27% |
| 2.0× | 12,000 ₽ = 40% | 6,000 ₽ = 20% |
| 2.9× (GBPU 2016 analogue) | 8,276 ₽ = 28% | 4,138 ₽ = 14% |

**Finding:** 50% is coherent only in the top-left cell — no offset-removal risk and a hike at or below ≈1.6×. Use the formula with a per-family `M` taken from that contract's own GO history and an `O` of 2 for semi-net spreads; take `M ≥ 2` as a floor until history is studied. Against the public mini-contract arithmetic of §5.4 this still admits a one-pair RTSM/MXI/IMOEXF book (≈4.9–5.6k), which is why those families — and not full RTS or Si — are the only ones the merged report treats as arithmetically representable.

### 10.4 Calibrating the uncertainty buffer instead of assuming 5,000

Two candidate parameterisations appear in the source set, and the merged report keeps the second as the primary because it is anchored in measured per-pair σ rather than a flat judgement:

**(i) Proportional form** (judgment): `U = max(1,500; 0.5·L)`. Then E1 requires `1.5·L ≤ 10,000 − c_in`, i.e. `L ≤ ≈6,570` for a minimum-lot idea at `X = 0`; and C1 requires `X ≥ −15,000 + 1.5·L ≈ −5,150`. With a full-size idea, **de-risking starts at about the 5,000 warning level**.

**(ii) Delay-window σ form** (recommended candidate):

    U = max(5,000; 2.5 · σ_delay(book) + C_exit)

where `σ_delay(book)` is the 1-day σ of the book's spread P&L in rubles (per-pair daily σ: CNY 688 ₽, GL 531 ₽, IMOEX 1,614 ₽; GAZR ≈2.11% and SBER ≈3.83% log-σ scaled [R06 §12.1]) inflated for the reaction window (3–90 minutes plus the data chain) and for fat-tail days, with a 2.5 multiplier as an explicit **test candidate** to be validated by R08 replay; plus one-way exit costs (round trip 10.7–75.9 ₽ per pair, plus half-spread crossing — the GL family is quoted 100–108 steps wide, so limit orders only).

Illustrative outcome: a 2.5σ delay-window move on a 2-pair medium book is 3.4–8.1k ₽, i.e. **above 5,000**, which is precisely why the floor cannot be the whole rule.

**No Gaussian tail is assumed.** Use the empirical 141-day distribution plus the scenario multipliers of §11; no tail estimate is a guarantee, and a fixed "5k buffer" is neither necessary nor sufficient — its adequacy is set by `L`, which is set by the contract, the lot, `τ` and the halt/limit-lock regime.

**Testing both heuristics (pre-committed):**

- **Margin ceiling** ∈ {25%, 33%, 50%} of the envelope, always on **gross** GO, with a parallel stressed-GO column (offset removed). Report for each candidate pair set: minimum free cash, minimum (free cash − stressed VM), maximum `D`, trigger crossings. Falsifier: if any setting admits a path that breaches C2 in SC1–SC5 while passing the margin gate, the margin gate alone is rejected as a control.
- **Buffer** ∈ {5,000 fixed; computed `U`; `max(1,500; 0.5·L)`}. Falsifier: count path-days where the realised adverse move over the measured reaction window exceeded the buffer in force. If the fixed version exceeds computed `U` on ≥5% of stress days for a candidate book, retire the fixed value. Expectation: it fails for IMOEX-cluster books and passes for 2×GL.

**Threshold candidates that change the ladder, to be tested in the same replay:** warning 3,000/4,000/5,000; action 7,000/8,000/10,000; per-idea stressed loss cap 3,000–5,000; aggregate stressed loss cap ≤10,000; per-idea `L_stress` target ≤7,000–8,000 for any new position as headroom against the outer boundary.

### 10.5 What must be exercised on every intermediate timestamp

Cash and margin must be checked not only at the end state but **at every intermediate event `t ≤ τ`**:

    free pilot cash credited at t
    + contractually verified liquid pilot assets realisable by t
    − pending debits − locked orders
    − adverse VM/debit(t)
    − stressed broker/exchange margin increase(t)
    − stressed exit/hedge cash needs(t)
    ≥ broker-specific maintenance/settlement cushion(t) > 0

Account-level VTB KDS, maintenance, core haircuts and market-specific recourse are recomputed **separately**; the core is never inserted as available cash, and the unarrived 15,000 is never inserted as liquidity. Three failure modes must each be tested as their own scenario, because each can liquidate the position with `X` above −15,000: a margin rise, a benchmark-accrual-driven deterioration in `X` with no cash debit at all, and a VM drain on a position whose economic hedge is intact.

### 10.6 Integer constraint

    n* = max { n ∈ ℕ₀ : every relative, cash, margin, gross-exposure and leg-risk test passes }

`n = 0` is a valid answer. For a two-leg ratio a:b the whole-lot ratio is preserved and every intermediate single-leg state must pass the leg test on its own. A fractional solution of 0.4 lot is **not** rounded to one. If one-lot `L + U` exceeds `15,000 + X`, all `n` are rejected; rounding up is never permitted. At 30k the ideal weights are multiples of 7–17% of the envelope (pair GO / envelope), so integer rounding error is second-order relative to σ and the binding question is only ever "does one lot fit", not "how much to shade".

## 11. The joint stress scenario set

The brief requires testing joint price/margin/liquidity shocks, not additive independent best cases. The merged scenario library below is the union of the nine reports' sets, deduplicated to eleven scenarios. All magnitudes are hypothetical stress inputs pending R04/R06 data; none is a probability.

**How they must be run.** Each scenario is evaluated for the candidate integer package at `n = 1` and, if it fits, at the largest `n` all constraints allow — with the **aggregate** stressed loss and the **aggregate** stressed margin, never per-tranche. Each run reports: minimum free cash, minimum (free cash − stressed VM), maximum `D`, maximum `X` excursion, trigger crossings, whether the sleeve alone funded the path, and whether any core use occurred. A scenario that produces a realised loss above 15,000, a broker-initiated core use, or a forced liquidation before the user can act **falsifies the sizing rule for that instrument** — it does not justify widening the boundary afterwards.

**Falsification of the Gaussian shortcut.** Under a driftless Gaussian assumption — explicitly **not** a guarantee — a sleeve run at 25% annual vol (`σ_90d ≈ 3.75k` on 30k) touches −10k within 90 days with probability ≈ `2·Φ(−2.67) ≈ 0.8%`, and within a year ≈18%. Real spread tails, halts and margin jumps make those numbers optimistic, which is the whole reason the tail must come from the empirical distribution and the scenario set rather than from a normal approximation.

### 12.4 Ledger B — joint shock: the action state is reached with less than 5,000 ₽ of distance left

**Book:** 3 × GLDRUBF/GLZ6 + 1 × CNYRUBF/CRZ6.

| Step | Arithmetic | Cumulative state |
|---|---|---|

**Reading.** The book passed the 50% margin ceiling at entry and still reached the action state in one plausible two-session path; the 5,000 gap was partly consumed *by the move that fired the trigger*. This is the direct falsification of "5,000 is enough" for medium-volatility books.

**Tail variant (same book, harsher parameters).** Spread MTM loss deepens to −6,000 ₽ cumulative; NCC raises per-leg IM by 50% (4,866 → 7,300 ₽ per spread); applying the same `2 × IM` stress rule from the new level gives `L = 14,600 ₽`, and `−6,000 − 14,600 − 2,000 = −22,600 < −15,000`. Under those assumptions the position **cannot be justified at all** at that point — it had to be flattened before, or at latest when such a combined price–margin shock was suspected. A single mini spread nearly saturates the −15k bound once IM is shocked and losses accumulate.

**Reading.** A single severe gap plus forced-liquidation slippage breaches the boundary **before** any orderly exit is possible. The 15,000 is an authority limit that execution and liquidation mechanics can overshoot — which is exactly why a breach is recorded as an incident rather than treated as an impossibility.

### 12.6 Ledger C — gap through the trigger with an evening limit-lock

| Step | Event | `X` | `L` remaining | `U` | `X − L − U` | Status |

### 12.7 Ledger D — funding timing failure

| Time | Event | Cash / margin | `X` | Funding consumed | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | Shortfall trigger; entries suspended | 20,000 cash | −10,000 | 0 | Action |

**The ruble amounts of being wrong are small; the permission implications are not.** For a VM call of 8,000 ₽ due at the next cut-off with the user unreachable, the policy default is *no funding*, so positions must be reduced at market: through a 100-step GL book or a gapping CNY book that costs at least one round trip plus unmeasured slippage (slippage is not measurable on free data). If instead the deficit is carried as a negative balance, VTB charges ≈20.9% p.a. (≈13.7 ₽ for three days on 8,000 ₽) and BCS 0.1% per day (≈40 ₽ for five days) plus forced-closure fees — cheap in rubles, expensive in *permission*, because the core portfolio is what services the debt. The unknowns dominate the arithmetic: the arrival time of external money, and whether the broker waits at all.

**Variant — funding arrives and the breach happens anyway.** A six-step path from one source report: `V` 30,000 at day 0 (`X = 0`); day 14 `V = 34,500` against `B = 30,400` → `X = +4,100`; day 30 `V = 28,200` against `B = 30,800` → `X = −2,600`, `D = 6,700` (warning); day 45 `V = 20,100` → `X = −11,000`, `D = 15,100` (action); day 46 an approved +5,000 arrives (lifetime gross used 5,000 of 15,000) and `X` recovers to −6,000 with `D = 10,100`; day 60 `V = 15,500` against `B = 31,400` → **`X = −15,900`, breach**. The point of the path is not the amounts but the sequence: **a rescue payment is not a solution** — it converts a funding problem into a smaller funding problem while the economic position continues to deteriorate, and the boundary is breached 14 days later with 10,000 ₽ of the allowance still unused.

**The decisive point.** The 45,000 ₽ contribution cap and the 15,000 ₽ relative-loss boundary are **independent**. A pilot can be comfortably inside the funding cap and simultaneously one session from breaching the economic boundary, while the broker has already sold core. Internal limits do not ring-fence the account; only broker-side isolation (the R gate) or no live trade does. And the *economic* consequence of a forced sale can outlast the pilot: FIFO liquidation of the oldest LQDT lots resets the three-year ЛДВ clock (§7.2), a multi-year tax cost that can exceed the entire pilot allocation.

## 13. Sizing: what integer lots actually allow

All methods operate on **integer pairs** (1 pair = one 1:1 unit hedge where the family permits — CNY, GL, GAZR, SBER, IMOEX). Fractional hedging such as RI/MIX is excluded at this scale because the ratio hedge creates an unavoidable naked leg on a 30k account [R06 §12.1].

| Method | How it would work here | Verdict and test role |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Fixed minimum contracts** (1 pair per idea, cap on concurrent ideas) | `n = 1` per idea; concurrent caps low 1 / medium 2 / high 3–4 | **Baseline and control.** Matches tiny-lot reality; simplest to audit. Must still pass every gate — a small GO does not imply small spread or leg risk |
| **(b) Bounded risk-scaled sizing** | `n_i = min( floor(σ_budget / σ_i), floor(0.33 · Env / GO_i), cap_i )` with `σ_budget` in ₽/day per profile | **Recommended framework** once R06/R08/R09 inputs exist: it controls the binding constraint (the VM path) directly, and it doubles as a *reject* filter — if the 1-lot daily σ exceeds the budget, the idea is rejected, not downsized |
| **(c) Capped fractional-Kelly-like** | Requires calibrated `p(win)`, payoff ratio and edge per idea | **Defer / not approved.** 141 daily observations, one regime, one expiry cannot calibrate probabilities; "no 30-trade rule"; DSR/PBO are ceilings on optimism, not proofs. Errors in means dominate errors in variances and covariances about **20:2:1** [S43], and betting above Kelly lowers both growth and security. If reintroduced after a pre-registered forward record, cap at ≤ ½-Kelly — and note that Carver halves Kelly and halves again for negative skew, which mean-reverting spreads usually have |
| **(d) Scale-ins with precommitted caps** | Tranche 1 = 1 pair; tranche 2 only if the registry cap was precommitted ≥2, each tranche passes all gates, and no averaging down of a breached idea | **Allowed only as a variant of (b)** with hard aggregate caps, and stress computed on the **aggregate**, never the tranche. Functionally unavailable at 1–2 lots |
| **Cluster / correlation overlay** | Concurrent pairs sharing a factor (RUB carry; same underlying; shared leg) count as **one cluster**; cluster 1σ/day ≤ 60% of the profile budget | **Mandatory on any method.** Treat GL+CNY+IMOEX books as one cluster in stress, with correlation 1 and no √n diversification credit |
| **Discrete rounding** | Ideal weights at 30k are multiples of 7–17% of the envelope | Rounding error is second-order relative to σ; the real constraint is binary — if `n = 1` fails the gates, the idea is rejected |

### 13.1 Risk profiles (research labels, identical authority)

| Profile | σ-budget | Concurrent ideas | Other constraints |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low** (recommended starting mode) | 1,000 ₽/day 1σ | 1–2, entry only at z-extremes | Highest stress and liquidity cushions; `IM_sum ≤ 25%` of envelope (≤7,500 ₽); `U ≥ 2,000 ₽` |
| **Medium** | 2,000 ₽/day | ≤3 | Aggregate joint-stress and cash tests must pass with `Σ L`; human quota must support the exits |
| **High** | 3,500 ₽/day | ≤4 | **Research/sensitivity only — cannot enlarge this pilot's authority.** If a high-profile book needs more than the envelope under any stress, it is infeasible, not "high-risk" |

All profiles share the same `E` cap, the same outer boundary and the same no-trade triggers. The lower-profile variants proposed by other reports — 25%/35%/50% start-margin hypotheses (7,500 / 10,500 / 15,000 ₽) — map onto the same grid as sensitivity cases, with a tighter continuation inequality of −12,000 for the medium case; both are retained as test variants, not as recommendations. Choose the lowest profile that survives SC1–SC5 with margin.

### 13.2 Why the profiles cannot be scaled by leverage language

A single MXI contract is ≈80% of the 30,000 envelope in notional terms and several percent of equity in a plausible one-day stress move, which is materially above the ≈0.5–1% "risk per trade" convention Carver uses in small-account examples. By that standard **any equity-index spread is too large for the envelope** unless the risk horizon and the tail measure are redefined — and if they are redefined, saying so is part of the specification. A "high-risk profile" is a label describing research appetite; it is not a licence to exceed 30k/15k/15k.

## 14. Operating policy: state machine, stops, partial fills, emergencies, monitoring

### 14.1 Transitions and what each state permits

The state table in §12.1 is the reference. Operationally:

- **NORMAL** — only precommitted signals whose minimum integer size passes the relative, cash, margin, gross and leg-state tests. Zero emergency money assumed. All open positions coverable without core spillover.
- **WARNING (`D ≥ 5,000`)** — warn and reassess exposures, data, fees, margin and the path to flat. Provisional tightening: **pause optional scale-ins** until review. No reset of loss or high-water history; funding is not automatic.
- **DRAWDOWN ACTION (`D ≥ 10,000`)** — suspend entries and additions, cancel risk-increasing working orders, reduce risk in an orderly way, and require user review before resuming. Review is not restart approval.
- **SHORTFALL ACTION (`X ≤ −10,000`)** — cease new risk and start a controlled flat process. Approved funding may only avert a disorderly liquidation or complete the de-risking; it may not extend the holding period.
- **OUTER BREACH (`X < −15,000`)** — record a policy breach, suspend the live project pending formal review, reconcile every mark, flow, cost and any core use.
- **EARLIER OVERRIDE (broker, margin, naked leg, stale data, expiry/delivery)** — overrides every monetary threshold. Cancel entries and de-risk or flatten as actually executable; capture the incident and the slippage. **If no feasible controlled exit exists, the position was not eligible at entry.**
- **FUNDING EVENT** — freeze new orders; count newly earmarked cash or collateral (including broker-induced core use) against the 15,000 gross allowance once; a collateral-to-cash conversion is not double-counted; returned funds do not restore the cap.

### 14.2 Partial fills and single-leg states

- Prefer a **native calendar-spread order** where the platform genuinely executes it as one instrument; test whether it is a true spread execution or two atomic orders with atomic-order GO reservation [S24].
- For legged pairs, bound the naked-leg exposure **before** the first fill; a partially filled spread is a temporary single-leg position consuming more margin and more price risk than the intended spread.
- If the second leg cannot be filled within a pre-agreed window (candidate `T_leg = 10 minutes`), flatten the filled leg at a limit price unless the user explicitly accepts the naked risk. **The window must be defined before live trading.**
- Count only matched integer pairs; flatten any unmatched remainder. Avoid resting single-leg stops that can create a naked leg.
- Record the single-leg interval, the margin during it, and the cost of completing or unwinding. Virtual execution must never assume fills that did not occur.

### 14.3 Stop and exit policy

- **Economic stop:** the C1 inequality, continuous. The monetary triggers are backstops beneath it.
- **Liquidity stop:** the C2 inequality; if it fails in any scenario, reduce risk immediately even if `X` is far above −10,000.
- **Time/delivery stop:** exit or roll at least two sessions before the last trading day (offset cancellation, delivery exposure). This overrides everything else.
- **No new risk after 19:00** unless `L` contains an evening limit-lock to the next session.
- **No hold through a known venue closure** without a scenario that prices it.

### 14.4 Emergency funding

Zero at entry. Each event needs separate approval **and** a verified source, amount, availability and credit time. Only to avert a disorderly liquidation or to complete a de-risking; never for fresh exposure, averaging down, waiting for recovery, or resetting losses or the lifetime allowance. **Absent approval, the default is no funding.** Broker-initiated use of core collateral is an incident that counts toward the gross allowance, and the evidence (quantity, market value, timestamp) must be preserved for a broker query. Funding approval is **not** restart approval.

### 14.5 Monitoring, data and infrastructure

- **Flat-first bias on blackout.** If a data, VPN or VPS blackout exceeds the delay window and any position cannot be verified, exit at the next reliable opportunity. Do not build emergency exits on unverified VPN/VPS/API paths.
- **Stale-data rule.** A signal older than 20–30 minutes, or whose leg quote is stale, is **not** an entry: it is logged as stale/rejected. The finest free timeframe is a research question, not an assumed one-minute entitlement; a free feed is not assumed live enough for the strategy until R04 verifies timestamps, delay and interval [S4, S11].
- **Infrastructure reality.** The declared VPS (1 vCPU, 1 GB RAM, 2 GB swap, 20 GB disk, shared 250–350 MB background budget, active VPN and other workloads) cannot host full execution software, a local database and real-time order-book parsing; memory spikes will trigger OOM kills and silent failures. It can host a lightweight headless event listener that pushes Telegram alerts. The MacBook Pro is adequate for offline research. 24/7 monitoring does not mean all contracts trade 24/7 — signals and execution routines must be dormant outside actual sessions and per-contract trading windows.
- **Human quota.** Still undefined and must be defined before live use: a candidate cap is ≤2 entries per day plus declared availability windows. If the user's availability window is shorter than τ, there is no eligible position.

## 15. Criteria for no trade

Reject the idea, or the day, and **record the rejection**, if any of the following holds. This list is the merged union of the nine reports' criteria; several are hard blockers independent of price.

1. **No feasible whole-lot package** passes the relative-survival test and the cash/margin test simultaneously under the same joint path.
2. **Minimum-lot stress fails:** at `n = 1`, `X − L_stress − U < −15,000`, or one-lot `L + U` exceeds `15,000 + X`. There is no fractional contract and no rounding up.
3. **Margin fails:** required IM (no-offset base) exceeds the derived ceiling from §10.3, or stressed GO + `U` exceeds the envelope.
4. **Cash path fails:** no stable free-cash path exists across the price shock, VM, increased GO, exit costs and uncertainty — even if current initial GO is low — or current free cash is below (expected margin + the reserve).
5. **Recourse is unverified:** the R gate cannot be documented, actual KPUR/risk category is unconfirmed where it changes closure depth, or the broker's early-action conditions can occur before the operational response path.
6. **The only way to pass is an unapproved core transfer**, hoped-for rescue funding, an unauthorised asset sale, or assumed access to money inside the 3–90 minute window.
7. **The offset is necessary for viability** but the one-leg fill state, the margin-reset state or the offset-removal scenario fails.
8. **Contract facts are unknown, stale or not reproducible:** multiplier, tick value, settlement, expiry, current broker GO or category treatment.
9. **Costs are unavailable:** after-cost fees and plausible spread/exit cost cannot be established.
10. **Data are not defensible:** delayed or irregular at a level that invalidates the intended execution window; or the free feed cannot reproduce synchronised executable marks and settlement.
11. **Timing/venue:** the expiry falls inside the required buffer; the contract is subject to known close-only or halt conditions; the signal arrives inside a deadline window (e.g. minutes before a cut-off) where new risk cannot be serviced.
12. **The virtual ledger would be dishonest:** it skips eligible signals, omits partial fills, assumes guaranteed stop fills, looks ahead to settlement or revised data, or cannot reproduce the matched LQDT counterfactual.
13. **Human capacity:** no reliable response horizon, or an execution quota/exit market inadequate for the book.

**Zero-trade days and a zero-trade pilot are valid results.** Scarcity of opportunities is not evidence of safety: "not allowed: converting scarcity into certainty" [R09 §11]. Preservation of capital is itself a decision.

## 16. Falsification criteria

**Of the delegated defaults, for a given spread:**

1. Any admissible minimum-lot stress path realises a loss above 15,000 before the user can act.
2. A broker margin call or forced liquidation occurs **before** `X` reaches −10,000, because the broker's indicator falls faster than the economic loss.
3. A funding transfer approved at the 10,000 trigger cannot be credited before the broker's deadline in the relevant clearing session.
4. The minimum-lot spread needs IM above the derived ceiling after offsetting, leaving insufficient free equity for the stress loss and exit costs.
5. The action-to-boundary distance is consumed by ordinary exit costs plus a 30-minute delay, with no extreme gap at all.
6. Core collateral is used by the broker without explicit approval, even if `X` stays inside limits.
7. A 90-day review forces the exit of a position that still passes the continuation inequality — i.e. the review convention is mechanically destructive rather than risk-based.
8. An unpaired position lasts longer, or loses more, than the validated leg limit — falsifying the partial-fill control.
9. A positive pilot result disappears under matched LQDT flows, all fees, conservative fills or multiple-testing control — i.e. it fails the economics gate even though it is nominally profitable.
10. Frequent alert non-response, stale data, capital constraints or omitted signals falsify execution feasibility even if the theoretical spread is profitable.

**Of this report's own claims** (stated so they can be attacked rather than assumed):

- "The 5,000 gap is insufficient for medium/high books" is falsified if a replay of SC1–SC8 on the 141-day window with registered multipliers shows the maximum one-day adverse move inside the reaction window staying below 5,000 ₽ for those books on ≥95% of stress days — unlikely given an IMOEX 1σ of 1,614 ₽ per pair.

## 17. The 90-day convention and the peak-giveback rule

### 17.1 Exact convention to register

- **Anchor `t0`** = the earlier of (i) the timestamp of the first entry in the live pilot, or (ii) the first sleeve earmark or first incremental cost, in Moscow time. If no entry occurs, the review date is anchored at pilot start and the review reports a **zero-trade period** — a valid outcome.
- **Review date** = `t0 + 90 calendar days`, not 90 trading days (example: start 2026-10-14 → review 2027-01-12). One ±7-day logistics window; if the date falls on a non-trading day, resulting actions happen at the next session. Interim checks every 30 days.
- **The thresholds, `H`/`X`/`D`/`E` ledgers and incident log are continuous and do not pause for the review.**
- **At review:** re-run E1, E2, C1, C2 and R on fresh marks; re-estimate `σ_i` from the registered window plus new data; re-verify broker parameters (haircuts, GO tables, fee schedules — NCC LQDT parameters changed 2022→2026 and the VTB per-contract fee changes on 01.01.2027); publish `X`/`H`/`D`/`E`, the incident list, the reaction-time log and the stale/partial-fill counts.
- **Positions are not force-closed at day 90.** A position may continue beyond the review if its pre-registered idea horizon and exit conditions still hold and the continuation gate passes — including a dated convergence target such as GLZ6 expiry on 2026-12-17, which is only 64 days after a 2026-10-14 start. A **roll** (e.g. GLZ6 → GLH7) is a **new entry** subject to every gate and the event filter; GLH7 has different liquidity and basis.
- **No silent resets.** `X`, `H`, `D`, `E`, realised losses and cumulative underperformance are never reset by review, funding, restart, withdrawal or renaming. If records or reliable marks are missing, the portfolio is **not demonstrably compliant** and no added exposure is permitted until reconciled.
- **Three permitted outcomes** of the formal review: continuation under current parameters; modification (tighter parameters, lower frequency); termination. Profit alone is not a continuation case — the review re-underwrites risk, it does not ratify a P&L.

### 17.2 Peak giveback: what the `D ≥ 10,000` rule costs

The rule is a **high-water-mark ratchet**. With `H = 12,000` it fires at `X = +2,000`; with `H = 20,000` at `X = +9,200`. Two costs follow.

1. **The locked-out state.** If the book is cut to flat while `X = +2,000`, `X` can no longer improve, `D` stays ≥10,000 indefinitely, and resuming needs a human decision. This deliberately protects accumulated excess at the price of never re-risking automatically.
2. **Foregone carry and truncated right tail.** A 2-pair CNY book earns ≈263 ₽ of carry per 60 days (≈810 ₽ with GL). Being flat for 60 days forgoes that against `B`. At 30k this is second-order versus the 10–15k acceptance band; at 10–20m scale it becomes first-order, because de-risking a carry book is expensive. Rigid giveback stops also convert a naturally fat-tailed positive distribution into a truncated one, which lowers the long-run Sharpe of a mean-reverting spread strategy that oscillates around equilibrium.

**Counter-argument to the "just cut winners" critique.** With a per-idea `L ≈ 6.5k`, an `H ≥ 10k` implies substantial success, and a 10k retracement is ≈1.5·`L` — for mean-reverting spreads such pullbacks are typical before convergence, so the rule can cut winners. But the pilot's primary objective is bounded loss, not maximum expected return, and a tiny sample cannot distinguish a whipsaw from a regime break.

**Test design (pre-registered, in the virtual ledger, never selected after seeing outcomes):**

- (a) the rule as written (`D ≥ 10,000` → suspend and reduce);
- (b) a giveback tied to current risk: `D ≥ max(5,000; 1.5·L)`;
- (c) no giveback rule, C1 only;
- (d) a **two-stage** rule: at `D ≥ 10,000` reduce risk by ~50% and suspend entries; go to flat only if the cash/margin gates also fail or `X ≤ −10,000`.

Compare `X` distributions, breach frequency, missed-opportunity cost after cost, worst post-trigger overshoot, trigger frequency, and how many triggers were caused by ordinary VM noise. Until that test exists, **keep the conservative rule** (judgment, given the sample size). One source proposed allowing a single `H` reset after review — that is **rejected**: it reintroduces exactly the silent reset the policy forbids, and it would let a pilot that gave back 20k restart with a clean slate.

### 18.2 Promotion gates (all required; pilot profit is explicitly not one of them)

1. **Evidence and reproducibility:** universe and parameters specified before evaluation; frozen, point-in-time data lineage; realistic fills; **every** eligible signal; leakage audit; multiple-testing control under R09; pre-registered, out of sample, across ≥2 volatility regimes, with a minimum count of closed **independent ideas** (episodes, not fills) and ≥2 stress episodes included.
2. **Execution stability:** sampled or replayed 3–90 minute latency distribution (median ≤15 min, max ≤90 min as candidate bounds); partial/rejected fill and single-leg state counts; stressed close cost and liquidity by contract and time of day, including weekends only where that contract actually trades.
3. **Economics:** positive after-cost `X` versus matched LQDT with external flows matched; tax sensitivity under R05; stability by regime, not concentrated in one pair or one event; uncertainty reported. A small profitable pilot alone establishes none of this.
4. **Capacity at target size:** recalculate the whole strategy at 10m and 20m order sizes — displayed depth, fill ratio, market impact, book replenishment, simultaneous orders and correlated liquidation. CCP margin relief does not prove market depth.
5. **Controls:** current broker/client category and margin settings verified; usable funds and recourse known; alert and execution process tested; user availability and intervention quota specified; **no single stressed path requires unapproved core use**; zero unapproved core-use incidents during the trial.
6. **Re-derived limits:** E1/C1/E2/C2 recomputed with scale-specific `M`, `O` and `τ`, under a **new delegated envelope** decided by the owner. No linear extrapolation of 5k/10k/15k.
7. **Separate approval for any change of account structure** — a sub-account or a separate agreement changes the "same account" premise and must not be adopted silently.

The proposed quantitative overlay (≥30 round turns, `D_max < 5,000`, MAR ≥ 1.5, Sharpe ≥ 0.8) is retained as a **reporting template**, not as a statistical test: on a 30k sleeve with 1-lot positions neither MAR nor Sharpe is estimable with any confidence, and a pilot that earns +5,000 ₽ in ten days through directional leverage fails these gates by construction — which is the point of having them.

## 20. What cannot be bounded, and why the internal limits are not maxima

| Unbounded item | Why it cannot be bounded here |
|---|---|
| **Maximum gap size** | MOEX futures have gapped through price bands; the RTS fell ≈49.3% intraday on 24.02.2022 [S49]. A future gap can be larger and faster than any sample. No Gaussian and no historical-tail guarantee is valid |
| **Broker liquidation price and timing** | Forced closure occurs at market prices, at widened spreads, or through mechanisms the user does not control; the deadline is the next cut-off and the broker may act at any point after the threshold breach [S25, S30]. The user may have no time for an orderly exit |
| **Margin offset removal** | Existing relief may be reduced or withdrawn by NCC/broker parameter change or on the last trading day [S21, S22, S23]. The current published IM is not a guarantee of future IM |
| **Parameter regime change** | NCC defines dynamic parameters at each clearing and during the calculation period [S3]; the current methodology document version is itself in flux (see §21) |
| **Funding arrival** | Origin, amount and timing of any emergency money are unknown. The 15,000 is authority, not liquidity |
| **Account recourse** | Without written account-level confirmation, it is unknown whether core LQDT is eligible, whether the broker can pledge or sell it without instruction, and how that use would be accounted. The >40m account defeats economic ring-fencing |
| **Tax and ЛДВ consequences** | FIFO interaction, embedded gains in core lots, and the future cash call are not modelled in `X`; only the loss of the ЛДВ is excluded by instruction, and everything else is unknown |
| **Execution delay distribution** | 3–90 minutes is a brief, not a measurement; R08 must supply stale/rejected/unfilled distributions |
| **Legal and regulatory change** | The 6681-У project amendment (2026) is pending; tariffs change (VTB per-contract fee from 01.01.2027); fee promotions expire; NCC document versions are superseded with dated effect |
| **Single-leg and data outages** | Frequency unknown; consequence bounded only by the `T_leg` timeout |

**Therefore:** the 5,000/10,000/15,000 numbers are **action rules**, internally coherent as a graduated authority framework, and not a substitute for a separate liquidity path, a positive uncertainty buffer, and a no-trade decision when the minimum lot does not fit. A pilot that stays inside all of them has demonstrated *governance*, not *safety*.

## 23. Possible project changes (proposed, not approved)

Each of these changes a premise of the current plan. None should be adopted silently, and several require the owner's explicit decision because they alter the "same account" or "30k" constraints themselves.

1. **Charter amendment:** publish the funding-model choice — cash sleeve (≈14% p.a. hurdle) versus pledged units (≈2–4% p.a. hurdle plus a 16% p.a. GO-financing fee) — as the accounting basis of `X`, with the hurdle statement of §8.11. Without this choice, every performance statement is ambiguous by ~14% p.a.
2. **Replace the reliance on the 5,000 gap** with the computed `U` (floor 5,000) and the two-gate entry test; tighten the gross-GO ceiling from 50% to 33% with a stressed-GO twin.
3. **Two-stage `D`-rule trial** measured in the virtual portfolio before live use.
4. **Profile system** with σ-budgets and identical authority; choose the lowest profile that survives SC1–SC11 with margin.
5. **Narrow the universe** to what the arithmetic admits: mini index and CNY/GL families with a verified native spread order; exclude full RTS, Si and any family whose minimum lot fails the stress or liquidity tests.
6. **Pre-arrange a funding path or explicitly accept none:** a standing transfer instruction with a documented arrival time, or an explicit decision that no emergency funding is available (and size accordingly). Anything in between is an assumption, not a plan.
7. **Consider a genuinely ring-fenced arrangement** — separate sub-account or agreement — only if the broker supports it, and only with separate approval; a separate account reduces operational spillover but is **not** automatically a legal or collateral guarantee.
8. **A no-evening, no-weekend holding rule** unless `L` covers the lock and the gap.
9. **Start the virtual portfolio and data capture now** (daily snapshots ≈0.5 MB/day), with live 30k only after the R01 letters and the R08 replay, and 10–20m only after §18's gates. The expected outcome of a 90-day live pilot remains **operational validation, not profit**.
10. **Reject the pilot** if no integer spread can satisfy the zero-emergency-funding entry inequality with a positive buffer. This is a valid, evidence-backed outcome and not a failure of the research.

## Appendix B. The smallest next evidence package

Ordered by how much decision value each item carries. Until the first three exist, the correct state is no live trade regardless of what else is known.

1. **R01 written account-level answer:** is the core LQDT eligible for futures margin on this account; can the broker pledge or sell it without instruction; what is the recorded risk category (KPUR or other) and how does it change closure depth; what is the restrictive closing time; is there any per-sleeve control. This is the recourse gate.
2. **R02 all-in round-trip cost per candidate pair** and the exact GO-financing and negative-balance prices, to verify the 14% / 2–4% hurdle decision.
3. **R06 per-contract facts** — multiplier, tick, tick value, expiry, settlement, current GO, spread-group eligibility — for one or two candidate families, plus the expiry-adjacent offset behaviour. This converts "does one lot fit" from arithmetic to a number.
4. **R04 usable free data:** timestamped bid/ask/settlement history sufficient to reproduce synchronised executable marks, plus the actual delay and interval — otherwise the signal and the 90-minute window cannot be measured.
5. **R08 execution semantics:** what a native spread order really does on the chosen platform, and the stale/rejected/partial-fill distribution under a realistic delay.
6. **R09 pre-registered validation and selection protocol,** including how the giveback variants will be compared before any outcome is seen.
7. **R05 tax scenarios** and the investor's residence/account facts, since a forced core sale's FIFO/ЛДВ consequence is the only mechanism found in this research that can cost more than the pilot itself.
8. **User inputs:** real reaction-time distribution and unavailability windows; emergency-funding origin, approval protocol and credit time; availability calendar around sessions, weekends and holidays; the human intervention quota.

If items 1–3 establish at least one integer package that passes the relative, cash, margin and leg tests with a strictly positive buffer, a bounded pilot becomes a testable proposition. If they do not, the evidence-backed conclusion remains: **no trade**.
