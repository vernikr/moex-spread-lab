# R15 — Optional neural strategies under limited compute

**Decision report | English | Evidence cutoff: 2026-10-07**  
**Research access date:** 2026-10-22. Sources first published after 2026-10-07 were excluded. For undated live documentation inspected on the access date, I label it as current-page evidence only; it cannot establish what was available or legally permitted on the cutoff date.  
**Scope:** independent public-source desk research and proposed experiment design. No project repository, private data, trained model, notebook, executed backtest, account check, legal opinion, or hardware benchmark was available or used.

## Executive answer

**Could a neural model help?** In principle, yes: a tiny nonlinear model could capture interactions or shared structure across related contracts that a fixed rule, regularized linear model, and tree model miss. A large 2026 preprint on daily international futures reports neural gains in its own setting, while other time-series work finds simple linear models beat sophisticated alternatives on common benchmark datasets. Neither result establishes transfer to MOEX spreads or this pilot.

**Does the evidence justify adding neural complexity now? No.** The stipulated dataset has limited, delisting-affected histories and few independent market episodes; the outcome is highly path-, cost-, contract-, and execution-dependent; and the project has not established a lawful, reproducible free-data pipeline or realized execution costs. With only end-of-day inputs, manual response, integer contracts and RUB 30,000 all-in, adding a neural model before the deterministic, linear and tree baselines would enlarge selection risk without demonstrated net value. First establish the data and simulation gates below. A neural experiment is a small, optional challenger only if simpler baselines leave a robust, economically material residual opportunity.

**Recommendation for the research plan—not for trading:** keep the neural arm behind a gated experiment, not in the candidate production stack. If reached, test just one small tabular MLP, with a causal temporal convolution as a second and final architecture only if a pre-registered temporal-pattern hypothesis warrants it. CPU inference is the sensible default. Do not assume the Mac's Radeon GPU, the VPS, or free cloud GPU is a usable training resource. No model should take over execution, increase exposure, or use emergency funding.

## What is known, assumed, and still unverified

### Embedded assumptions used unchanged

I treat the prompt's definitions as project assumptions, not independent findings: the target data class is a free daily futures panel (OHLCV and OI where published) spanning tens to a few hundred contracts, with limited and delisting-affected histories; the required competitors are no-trade, one fixed deterministic spread rule, and simple linear/tree models; and validation is chronological walk-forward with training-only transforms, purging/embargo, a complete trial log and one untouched holdout. I use the same conservative/base/stress cost sets and the same RUB 30,000 integer-contract, finite-capital and 3–90 minute reaction scenarios for every model.

The matched LQDT counterfactual, actual flow matching, no double deduction of retained-collateral LQDT return, exclusion of only the lost long-term holding deduction, account treatment unknown, and all pilot loss/funding thresholds are retained exactly as provided. They are **not verified facts about the account or broker**. In particular: warning at RUB 5,000 excess-curve drawdown; action at RUB 10,000 excess drawdown from its high-water mark or cumulative LQDT shortfall of RUB 10,000; outer shortfall boundary RUB 15,000; formal review at 90 calendar days without reset; emergency gross funding allowance of RUB 15,000 cumulative for the whole pilot and only for de-risking/flat, never fresh exposure or averaging down. Entry must pass with no emergency funding. Core collateral is not silently treated as test capital.

## Proposed supervised problem and decision linkage

### One primary outcome, deliberately close to the economic decision

For each eligible, economically defined spread candidate at decision time \(t\), predict its **forward gross realized spread P&L per one integer contract package** over a pre-registered **five trading-session holding horizon**. The label should use the actual named contracts and their contract-specific economics, settlement/variation-margin accounting, and close/expiry/roll rules—not a return from a retrospectively back-adjusted continuous series. A spread package may require unequal integer legs; specify and freeze that mapping before fitting. Termination at the five-session horizon, an intervening mandatory close/expiry, and any stop/de-risk rule must be the same for every candidate model.

Five sessions is a proposed primary research horizon, **not a discovered optimum**. If a medium-term economic thesis requires another horizon, specify that as a separate, pre-registered experiment; do not sweep horizons and report the best. A short list such as 5 and 10 sessions can be an explicitly multiplicity-counted sensitivity set. Labels overlap when signals arrive daily and positions are held for multiple days, so the split purging must cover the maximum label/holding interval and any additional signal/exit dependencies.

Features at \(t\) may use only information demonstrably published and available by a fixed decision timestamp: lagged daily prices/volume/OI, contract/expiry/specification information known at that timestamp, and pre-defined transformations. End-of-day bars cannot be used before the final values are available. The simulation must timestamp signal creation, notification, the realistic 3–90 minute human response, first feasible order attempt, fill/partial-fill/rejection/stale outcome, and any subsequent management. If the source timestamp is after the relevant contract's trading window, execution moves to the next feasible session; do not infer an ideal closing-price fill from a post-close decision.

### Output and policy

A reasonable target is gross forward P&L scaled by a training-only, trailing ex-ante risk measure (for example, a volatility estimate calculated solely from information available by \(t\)); keep the scale frozen to its as-of decision value. The model outputs a forecast, while a common policy layer converts it to an order/no-order decision using expected value after parameterized costs and explicit risk/capital constraints. Freeze this policy layer across model families. Forecasting a positive return is not itself an order: the expected benefit must clear conservative costs, feasible integer sizing, finite margin/liquidity, and the chosen uncertainty hurdle. Do not train on future realized costs or use one model's more permissive threshold.

For each model's simulated portfolio, the primary result is not a predictive score but **net RUB excess against the matched LQDT counterfactual** under identical true external flows. Calculate daily full-account and strategy attribution carefully: retained eligible collateral's LQDT return is already present in the account/counterfactual comparison and must not be charged a second time. Separately report the stipulated excess-result curve, drawdown, cumulative shortfall, tail loss, margin/variation-margin liquidity, utilization, turnover, trade count and holding/roll outcomes. Prediction metrics (MAE, rank correlation, direction hit rate, calibration) are diagnostics only. Positive accuracy improvement with no robust improvement in net RUB excess/risk is no useful added value.

### Required fair comparators

At every split, use identical timestamps, universe eligibility, contract mapping, capital, position caps, opportunity-attempt logic, reaction-delay distribution, fill/stale/rejection treatment, costs and risk/funding rules for:

1. **No-trade:** no new spread exposure; matched LQDT account remains the comparator floor.
2. **Fixed deterministic spread baseline:** one economically specified rule with parameters frozen before the evaluation; do not tune it on holdout.
3. **Linear baseline:** regularized linear regression or logistic/linear score on the same features, with training-only tuning and a fixed decision policy.
4. **Tree baseline:** a small, regularized tree/boosted-tree model, fixed search budget; not a large ensemble search.
5. **Optional neural candidate:** only after the above and only with the same samples and rules.

“No discretionary omission” in the virtual portfolio means it attempts each eligible recommendation until the portfolio's finite capital/position limits bind. It must record what would have happened to each signal, including stale, rejected, unfilled, partially filled and infeasible signals; it must not assume every order fills. The manual quota, escalation/on-call availability, and actual execution assumptions are unknown and need a written, pre-test rule before a realistic simulation can begin.

## Validation: the hard part is effective episodes, not row count

### Effective sample size and panel structure

Hundreds of instruments observed on the same dates do not create hundreds of independent market episodes. Contracts share underlyings, macro shocks, trading sessions, price discovery, expiry cycles and sometimes near-identical exposures. The effective independent units may be a small number of **time/regime episodes** even when the raw panel has many rows. Count and describe episodes at the time level; cluster uncertainty by date/regime and, where relevant, linked underlying/market families. A large panel can help a pooled model estimate shared behavior but cannot make one common shock independent multiple times.

Before any neural model, create a data audit: actual distinct calendar/session span; eligible observations by instrument and date; non-missing-field coverage; listed, expired, delisted and newly introduced contracts; changes in specifications/contract multipliers/ticks/settlements; expiry/roll mapping; duplicate/revised observations; and source timestamp/availability. Show the number of disjoint training/validation/test windows after purging, and count non-overlapping outcome episodes and actual simulated trades. This is a prerequisite, not a deliverable already completed.

### Leakage, selection and regime protections

- Split on **global chronological date blocks**, not random rows. All instruments on a date belong to the same side of a split; otherwise a same-date market shock can leak cross-instrument.
- Purge training examples whose forward outcome/holding windows overlap a test interval. Set embargo by the full relevant maximum label/position interval plus an empirically justified dependence buffer; do not pick a short buffer merely to retain rows.
- Fit every scaler, imputer, winsorization threshold, feature selector, label transform, risk estimate, learned embedding, calibration step and hyperparameter on the training data available in that fold only. A cross-sectional rank at date \(t\) must use the universe and values actually available at \(t\), including eligible delisted contracts; do not rank only currently surviving contracts.
- If instrument embeddings or instrument-specific parameters are tested, they must be learned using training-only instruments/periods. Test new/unseen contracts with a defined pooled fallback. A test instrument's future returns or full-history normalizer must never influence a training embedding or scale.
- Use only as-of instrument identifiers and specification data. Continuous back-adjustment can create hindsight in price levels and erase roll/contract economics; features may use a deliberately constructed, point-in-time history, but P&L/labels must remain based on tradable contracts and valid settlement values.
- Chronological walk-forward fits must mimic the intended refit schedule. Log every feature, architecture, seed, horizon, cost threshold, stopping rule, seed retry and human choice. A final untouched holdout is opened once after selection and frozen; if it informs another design decision, it is no longer untouched.
- Report regime-by-regime outcomes and paired differences. Use block/episode-aware uncertainty methods, not IID standard errors on daily overlapping trades. Multiple-testing corrections should use the **complete** search ledger, including failed and abandoned trials. The result should survive conservative costs, reaction delays and reasonable data perturbations—not merely the base-cost case.

The proposed walk-forward/purged design is appropriate for the stated temporal question, but it cannot manufacture independence, eliminate structural breaks, make a small final holdout powerful, or remove model-choice bias by itself. Bailey et al.'s CSCV/PBO paper defines the risk as an in-sample-selected configuration underperforming the median configuration out-of-sample and describes combinatorially symmetric splits (2017 journal version, pp. 39–69; first published 2016). Its method is a useful **secondary diagnostic across a matrix of candidate backtests**, not a replacement for chronological deployment-like walk-forward: symmetric combinatorial partitions can train on later periods and so do not represent real-time learning. The project should keep chronological walk-forward primary and use PBO/CSCV only as a search-risk diagnostic where the candidate matrix supports it. [S1]

Harvey, Liu & Zhu's 2016 cross-sectional asset-pricing paper argues a new return factor should clear a t-statistic greater than 3.0 after accounting for the existing factor zoo (RFS 29(1), pp. 5–68). This is a warning about data mining, not a universal pass/fail threshold for this one portfolio's time-series RUB P&L, and the claimed factor count/hurdle cannot be imported mechanically. The more relevant action is complete-trial disclosure, paired chronological out-of-sample tests, dependence-aware uncertainty, and a decision hurdle tied to the user's tolerated LQDT shortfall. [S2]

## Neural families worth considering—and those not worth starting with

**Candidate A: shallow MLP.** A small feed-forward network on a deliberately compact vector of lagged spread, relative-price, volatility, volume/OI and known contract-state features. It is the least complicated neural challenger to the linear and tree models, and can test a concrete nonlinear-interaction hypothesis. Limit the design family to a few predeclared small widths/layers and regularization settings, not a broad architecture/feature/horizon search. Compare against a linear model on exactly the same inputs; otherwise the gain may come from feature engineering rather than neural nonlinearities.

**Candidate B: tiny causal 1-D temporal CNN, conditional.** Consider only if a pre-registered hypothesis says short local time patterns across synchronized spread/contract features matter beyond compact rolling statistics and the MLP. Keep it shallow and causal; model inputs must end at \(t\). This adds masking, padding, receptive-field, export and testing complexity. If the effective episode audit is weak, skip it.

**Defer:** LSTM/GRU, transformers, xLSTM, Mamba/state-space, large multi-task architectures, pretrained financial/foundation models, learned pairwise embeddings, reinforcement learning, hyperparameter optimization services and large ensembles. These options add state, tuning choices, dependencies, artifact and latency/maintenance risks; no evidence in the project establishes a sample-size or economic rationale. A model's being small on disk or possible to infer on CPU would not solve the statistical problem.

External results do not settle the question. Zeng et al. (AAAI 2023, “Are Transformers Effective for Time Series Forecasting?”, arXiv v3 2022) report that their one-layer linear LTSF models outperform then-current complex transformers on nine long-horizon time-series benchmark datasets. This supports demanding strong simple baselines; it does **not** imply linear models always win, nor does that benchmark establish P&L on traded spreads. [S3] A 2026-03-02 arXiv v1 preprint by Saly-Kaufmann et al. studies 2010–2025 daily, diversified international futures and reports selected recurrent/hybrid neural architectures outperforming linear baselines under its Sharpe-focused portfolio design. It is more domain-relevant than generic forecasting benchmarks, but is still a preprint, uses a much broader/longer international dataset and a different objective/capital/execution context, and explicitly says code is available on request. Its authors' abstract reports performance, but that is not an independently replicated result or evidence of MOEX transfer. [S4]

### Ablations (small, predeclared set)

1. Linear versus the identical-input MLP: isolates whether nonlinearity is adding value.
2. No cross-instrument pooling versus pooled model: tests whether the assumed shared panel signal helps; same dates and groups in each split.
3. Remove OI and other optional/sparse fields: test robustness to missingness/data-vintage issues rather than treating absence as zero.
4. Remove instrument identifiers/embeddings: test if IDs are simply memorizing contract or regime histories.
5. Compact lag summaries versus raw short sequence into the MLP: if no meaningful difference, the CNN is not justified.
6. Base versus conservative/stress costs; zero-delay idealization only as a diagnostic, not a decision case; full 3–90-minute response-delay scenarios and next-session execution.
7. Fixed seeds plus a small, fixed seed set: report dispersion, not best-seed performance.

Do not expand ablations in response to a disappointing holdout. Every added architecture, horizon, feature subset, seed-selection rule and policy threshold is a trial and consumes the experiment's selection budget.

## Reproducibility, artifact rights, uncertainty and maintenance

- Keep a manifest for each trial: data snapshot hash and source/date, universe eligibility, feature list and as-of definitions, labels, split boundaries/purge/embargo, fitted transforms, architecture/parameter count, optimizer/early-stop rule, seed, library/runtime versions, compute device, costs, policy, order assumptions, result and reason for stopping. Save all trials, not only the winner.
- Pin artifact files by immutable hash and record source revision, code/license, model-card information, training data provenance, preprocessing, intended-use limits, evaluation and known failure modes. Hugging Face's official model-card documentation recommends documenting model intent/limitations, training parameters, datasets and evaluation results; its license docs tell users to respect model/code/data licenses. A model-card “license” field is not proof that the weights' training data or upstream material is rights-cleared. [S14]
- Keep the project panel, positions, signal history and model artifact local/private. Hugging Face's Terms say users grant the service a license to use uploaded content to provide services; public repositories additionally grant other users broad rights, while private repositories are subject to the platform's stated confidentiality measures. Do not upload private MOEX-derived data, trades, account details, positions, credentials or the trained artifact to public repos, demo spaces or hosted inference. Private cloud still needs a separate contractual/privacy review; the no-cloud route avoids that dependency. [S15]
- Numerical checks: finite inputs/output; range checks and clipping only if trained/frozen in-fold; float32 CPU reference; compare exported outputs within frozen tolerances; reject decision changes under tolerated numerical variation; deterministic replay on the same pinned environment. Treat NaN, stale data, unknown contract, non-positive risk scale, file/version mismatch, timeout, memory pressure or invalid output as **no neural signal/fail to the already approved baseline/no trade**, not as permission to improvise.
- Uncertainty: compare paired daily portfolio excess differences across chronological folds and regimes; use block/episode-level resampling or dependence-robust methods with the block length justified by label overlap and observed dependence. Show intervals, number of episodes, number of trades and concentration in dates/underlyings. Calibration plots and coverage are only meaningful if fitted out of fold; with sparse episodes, state “not estimable” rather than claiming calibrated probabilities. A neural probability is not a confidence guarantee.
- Drift: monitor input/missingness/out-of-range/specification changes and forecast/action rates against training support. Because the project is not deploying now, drift thresholds and any monitoring operations are later gates. Initial safe response is disable the challenger and fall back to the fixed baseline/no-trade; no automatic retraining from live outcomes.
- Maintainability: the neural arm must demonstrate an incremental economic gain large enough to compensate for model packaging, CPU parity, reproducibility, monitoring and explanation burden. If only a few parameters matter, retain a simpler rule/tree. No implementation framework or architecture is selected by this report.

## Early discard and falsification criteria

A neural added-value claim is falsified for this project if any of the following holds:

1. The proposed signal depends on information not available by the real decision timestamp, future-fitted transforms, survivor-only universe, post-hoc continuous-series adjustment, test-set calibration, or accidental cross-instrument/time leakage.
2. Lawful point-in-time daily data/contract history or source licensing cannot be verified without assuming payment or accepting terms that do not permit the intended use.
3. The true number of independent regime episodes/trades is too small to distinguish the minimum worthwhile RUB improvement from noise; a large cross-sectional row count does not rescue this.
4. A neural model's apparent advantage vanishes after common walk-forward splits, overlap purging, costs, integer sizing, finite capital, manual delay, fills/rejections and stress scenarios—or it exists only under one seed, one instrument, one year/regime, or one selected horizon.
5. It beats predictive scores but not the no-trade/deterministic/linear/tree alternatives on net matched-LQDT RUB excess, or creates worse excess drawdown, tail loss, margin liquidity, shortfall or operational burden than the predeclared tolerance permits.
6. The outcome is not reproducible in a frozen offline CPU artifact or failure cannot safely revert to baseline/no-trade.
7. Model selection has already influenced the “untouched” holdout. In that case, the holdout is spent and a new future period is needed.

The claim is strengthened—not proven—only if one predeclared tiny neural model produces a positive paired net-RUB excess increment over **each** required baseline, with conservative costs and realistic delays, across multiple independent chronological episodes, stable across the fixed seeds/ablations, and uncertainty narrow enough to exceed a minimum economically meaningful RUB hurdle chosen **before** the holdout. That evidence would support a new review, not automatic deployment or scale-up.

## Bottom line

For this constrained MOEX project, neural value is a **testable possibility, not an evidence-backed expectation**. The decisive gates are lawful point-in-time data, a correct contract/execution/LQDT simulator, enough independent temporal episodes, and baseline results under identical costs and finite capital. If those gates pass and the simple models leave a durable residual signal, a tiny CPU MLP is an affordable research challenger. Until then, the likely marginal return is in validation and data/execution quality, not architecture complexity. No backtest, measured alpha, feasibility, legal conclusion, broker approval or investment recommendation is claimed.
