# R10 — Deterministic spread hypotheses, continuation and opportunity cost

**Research cutoff and access date: 22 October 2026.** The supplied pack date, 7 October 2026, is context—not the cutoff for this report.

**Deliverable:** a ranked hypothesis and validation plan. This is **not** a conducted backtest, a finding of trading feasibility, a selected strategy, or an investment recommendation.

## Executive conclusion

The project should begin with **five tightly bounded hypothesis families**, not a broad search for attractive historical pairs:

1. **Carry-adjusted financial calendar spreads.**
2. **Economically related, same-issuer equity-futures spreads.**
3. **Commodity calendar spreads with explicit tenor and seasonal controls.**
4. **Currency-identity baskets**, conditional on compatible settlement definitions.
5. **Dated-versus-daily-auto-prolonging futures**, conditional on complete historical funding records.

This is a **research-priority ranking**, not a ranking of expected returns. The first experiments should determine whether any minimum integer spread can satisfy the RUB 30,000 funding constraint, survive conservative costs and delayed execution, and offer positive net RUB excess over the matched LQDT counterfactual. **An empty feasible universe is a valid result.**

Three findings materially constrain the plan:

- **Calendar-spread execution is not an economic edge.** MOEX provides coordinated execution across maturities, including synthetic matching, but that does not establish favourable carry, sufficient liquidity, acceptable costs or an affordable position. MOEX describes the service as simultaneous opposite transactions in futures on one underlying with different maturities. [MOEX, “Календарные спреды,” service description, undated live page, accessed 2026-10-22](https://www.moex.com/ru/spreads)
- **Free access does not establish permitted trading use.** MOEX’s ISS page distinguishes delayed access without subscription from real-time subscription, but also states that information is for familiarisation and other use requires an agreement. Therefore, “free EOD research input” remains a conditional planning assumption, not established permission for a screener or trading workflow. [MOEX, “Программный интерфейс АПИ к ИСС,” access and use paragraphs, undated live page, accessed 2026-10-22](https://www.moex.com/a2193)
- **Current specifications cannot safely be projected backwards.** For example, MOEX’s GL launch notice names RUGOLD as the settlement reference, while its current GL product page names GOLDFIXME. The effective specification history must resolve that difference before an identity or convergence test can be trusted. [MOEX, GL launch notice, contract-parameter section, launch stated as 2023-06-28](https://www.moex.com/n56918) [MOEX, GL product page, “Цена исполнения,” accessed 2026-10-22](https://www.moex.com/ru/derivatives/gold)

**Continuation should be judged on conditional future value from the present state**, including remaining costs, cash requirements, exit-time uncertainty, expiry risk and realistic alternatives. Neither a long holding period nor a sunk loss is, by itself, a reason to exit or continue.

**Flexible sizing, scale-ins and opportunity-aware exits are optional experiments.** Missing evidence must not turn them into required architecture.

### Methodological evidence—and its limits

Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt distinguish carry from expected price appreciation. Their author-hosted **July 2012 preliminary version**, *Carry*, defines carry as expected return “assuming its price does not change”; its commodity discussion uses nearby and next-nearby futures. This motivates separating curve carry from a directional forecast, **not** treating a calendar-spread slope as assured profit. The institutional record for the **August 2013 NBER working paper 19325** also notes periods when carry strategies across asset classes perform poorly. These are different versions, not interchangeable historical documents. [Author-hosted paper, definition and commodity-carry discussions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/Carry.pdf) [CBS research record, abstract and publication metadata](https://research.cbs.dk/en/publications/carry/)

Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst’s *Pairs Trading: Performance of a Relative Value Arbitrage Rule*, **NBER working paper 7032, March 1999**, studies daily US equity data and explicitly acknowledges possible market-microstructure effects. It supplies a simple distance-based comparator and a warning about execution artefacts; it does **not** establish an edge in MOEX futures. [NBER, abstract and bibliographic record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7032)

Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu’s *The Probability of Backtest Overfitting*, **27 February 2015 version**, explains that a holdout does not account for the number of configurations tried. The relevant discussion is in **Section 1, “Comparisons to other approaches,” reported PDF p. 7**: “does not take into account the number of trials attempted.” Accordingly, chronological testing and an untouched final period must be accompanied by a complete trial ledger. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)

## 3. The common measurement framework

## 3.1 Primary outcome: matched LQDT excess in RUB

Let:

- \(W_t^A\): actual-policy account wealth, including retained LQDT, cash, futures variation margin and other assets, less liabilities and incurred costs;
- \(W_t^L\): matched counterfactual wealth;
- \(F_t\): true external cash flows, identical in both paths.

Define:

\[
X_t=(W_t^A-W_t^L)-(W_0^A-W_0^L).
\]

The counterfactual follows the stated core LQDT policy and keeps any LQDT diverted to the pilot invested. Approved emergency external contributions enter **both** paths at the same time; the counterfactual invests them according to its predefined LQDT policy. Internal sales of core LQDT are **not** external contributions.

This full-account comparison is necessary because the pilot and core holding share one account. The unchanged core position should largely cancel economically, but the ledger must retain any differences caused by collateral use, asset sales, fees, taxes or liquidation.

**Do not calculate excess as futures P&L minus interest on all posted collateral.** If eligible collateral remains invested in LQDT and continues earning its return, that return is already in \(W^A\). Deducting the same return again would double-count opportunity cost.

A useful reconciliation is:

\[
X_t =
\text{futures P\&L}
+\text{other actual-minus-counterfactual asset returns}
-\text{incremental costs}
-\text{incremental tax effects}.
\]

It is a reconciliation identity, not a replacement for the matched ledgers.

### Required accounting conventions

- Use identical valuation times and consistent marking conventions.
- Include the costs of opening, rehedging, rolling, closing, failed execution and forced de-risking.
- Report terminal results after liquidation.
- Track a separate **liquidation-adjusted excess** estimate for risk monitoring.
- Do not charge estimated closing costs twice—once in liquidation marking and again as an additional expense.
- Use market-price returns where the counterfactual could transact; NAV can be a diagnostic, not an automatic executable price.
- Fund-level costs embedded in observed LQDT returns must not be subtracted again.

**Tax limitation:** only the loss of the LQDT long-term holding deduction is excluded by instruction. Residence, account treatment, timing of taxes and loss-offset rules are unknown. Until resolved, label results **net of modeled trading costs, before unresolved investor-specific tax effects**—not after-tax excess.

## 3.2 Pilot authority and intervention rules

The simulation must enforce the supplied rules without treating internal budgets as broker ring-fencing.

| Constraint | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Initial allocation: RUB 30,000 | Includes initial margin, variation-margin liquidity and reserves. |
| Emergency allowance: RUB 15,000 cumulative gross | No replenishment after repayment; no netting of earlier emergency uses against recoveries. |
| Maximum gross contributed funding: RUB 45,000 | Not an entry budget. Entry must pass with **zero emergency funding**. |
| Emergency purpose | Only avert disorderly liquidation or complete de-risking of existing exposure; explicit approval required. |
| At RUB 10,000 cumulative shortfall | Any additional funding is solely to reach flat. |
| Core collateral used by broker | Counts toward gross emergency allowance; unapproved use is an incident. |
| Core asset sale proceeds | Funding/collateral event, not automatically an economic loss in \(X\). |
| Review after 90 calendar days | No resetting losses, high-water marks or lifetime emergency allowance. |

For the provisional rules:

\[
H_t=\max(0,\max_{u\leq t}X_u),\qquad DD_t=H_t-X_t.
\]

Apply:

- warning at \(DD_t\geq\) RUB 5,000;
- action at \(DD_t\geq\) RUB 10,000 **or** \(X_t\leq-\)RUB 10,000;
- outer cumulative-shortfall boundary at \(X_t\leq-\)RUB 15,000.

A faithful proposed action response is: **cancel new exposure, cancel unfilled additions, initiate orderly de-risking and seek required approval for any emergency funding.** The action sequence and emergency availability still require user confirmation.

These are intervention triggers, **not guaranteed bounds**. Gaps, locked markets, margin changes, delayed human response and partial fills may carry losses beyond them.

## 4. Common normalization, hedge estimation and execution rules

## 4.1 Separate the economic signal from the tradable P&L

For leg \(i\), retain:

- raw quote \(P_{i,t}\);
- quote unit and underlying unit;
- contract quantity \(Q_{i,t}\);
- tick size \(\tau_{i,t}\);
- RUB tick value \(v_{i,t}\);
- settlement and variation-margin formula;
- any conversion rate and its fixing time;
- effective specification version.

For an appropriate locally linear contract:

\[
\Delta\Pi_i \approx
n_i\frac{v_{i,t}}{\tau_{i,t}}\Delta P_i.
\]

This is only a diagnostic approximation. Actual P&L must use the applicable contract formula, including conversion, rounding and settlement adjustments. In particular, **a USD quote is not permission to model the contract as a simple USD cash asset**.

Signals may use normalized economic prices \(p_i\), log ratios or curve slopes. Accounting must use actual contract cash flows.

### Three distinct hedge concepts

1. **Physical-unit hedge:** match units of the same underlying across maturities.
2. **Value or delta hedge:** match local economic sensitivities.
3. **Statistical hedge:** estimate a residual relation from training data.

They are not interchangeable. Equal contract counts do not necessarily mean equal units, and a statistically quiet spread need not be economically neutral.

## 4.2 Robust residual and standardized signal

For a two-leg relationship:

\[
s_t=\log p_{A,t}-\alpha-\beta\log p_{B,t}.
\]

A simple robust standardized residual is:

\[
z_t=
\frac{s_t-\operatorname{median}_{\mathcal T}(s)}
{\max(1.4826\,MAD_{\mathcal T}(s),\sigma_{\min})},
\]

where \(\mathcal T\) contains **training observations only**. A residual scale floor \(\sigma_{\min}\) prevents stale or almost constant prices from generating enormous artificial signals.

For a log spread, local implementation sensitivities are proportional to:

\[
\frac{\partial s}{\partial p_A}=\frac{1}{p_A},
\qquad
\frac{\partial s}{\partial p_B}=-\frac{\beta}{p_B}.
\]

Therefore, \(\beta\) must be translated into economic quantities and then contract counts; it is **not automatically a contract ratio**.

### Why z-scores, correlation and stationarity are insufficient

- **Correlation** measures co-movement, not a force restoring a price relationship.
- **A z-score** measures historical unusualness, not the probability or speed of recovery.
- **A stationarity test** depends on sample length, breaks and specification. Repeated testing also creates selection opportunities.
- A stationary quoted residual can coexist with negative net excess after costs, slow exits, unaffordable integer positions or untradeable marks.
- A two-maturity spread does not necessarily converge to zero when the near contract expires: the far contract still has remaining maturity.

Statistical diagnostics are filters and falsification tools—not substitutes for an economic mechanism and executable cash-flow model.

## 4.3 Hedge estimation: deliberately restricted

Proposed baseline hierarchy:

1. Use an identity/physical hedge where justified.
2. Otherwise use **one robust static regression per training fold**.
3. Freeze the hedge during the trade.
4. Re-estimate only at scheduled training updates while flat, unless a separately registered rehedging variant is tested.

Do not begin with Kalman filters, continuously changing hedge ratios or pair-specific bespoke models. They add turnover, estimation choices and paths for fitting away a structural break.

### Integer mapping

Enumerate a small set of integer baskets \(n\), including **no trade**, and minimize:

\[
\|Dn-a^*\|_W^2+\lambda\,\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\Delta\Pi(n)),
\]

subject to:

- funding and stressed liquidity limits;
- residual factor-exposure caps;
- maximum contracts and concurrent positions;
- permitted settlement/expiry exposure;
- minimum hedge quality;
- human execution constraints.

Here \(D\) maps contracts into economic sensitivities, and \(a^*\) is the desired exposure vector.

**Reject the signal if no acceptable integer basket exists. Do not round up merely to obtain a position.** Carver’s small-account discussion identifies precisely the conflict between indivisible futures and diversification: “you can’t own less than one futures contract.” This is a methodological warning, not a MOEX capital estimate. [Robert Carver, “Diversification and small account size,” March 2016, fractional-contract discussion](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2016/03/diversification-and-small-account-size.html)

## 4.4 Shared proposed parameter menu

These are starting candidate ranges, **not a Cartesian grid to exhaust**:

| Component | Proposed candidates |
|---|---|
| Residual estimation window | 60, 120 or 250 joint trading observations |
| Entry magnitude | \(|z|\): 1.5, 2.0 or 2.5 |
| Residual exit band | \(|z|\leq 0.25\) or 0.5 |
| Maximum intended holding | 10, 20 or 40 joint trading sessions |
| No-entry expiry buffer | 5 or 10 applicable trading sessions, enlarged for broker cutoffs/closure risk |
| Statistical diagnostic | Robust fixed hedge; identity hedge where available |
| Structural-break checks | Residual displacement, hedge instability, reference-rule change and data-quality failure |

**Trial budget:** preregister at most **12 complete configurations per family** for the first pass, chosen for interpretable contrasts. Do not silently test every combination above. All discarded configurations, later changes and family variants enter the trial ledger.

Hard financial and expiry limits override signal thresholds.

## 5. Candidate hypothesis cards

All five cards inherit the common funding, cost, counterfactual, validation and execution requirements.

## H1 — Carry-adjusted financial calendar residual

**Research priority: 1.**

### Economic story and instruments

Investigate whether unusual deviations in the curve between two futures on the **same financial underlying** subsequently normalize enough to exceed costs and LQDT opportunity cost.

Initial leads: adjacent, compatible maturities of currency futures, or financial contracts with a clearly documented financing relationship. MOEX lists the relevant currency families, but listing alone establishes neither depth nor affordability. [MOEX, currency futures, available-pairs section, accessed 2026-10-22](https://www.moex.com/s3811)

This is **not** “sell contango because it must disappear.” Currency forward slopes may reflect interest differentials, segmentation and other persistent conditions.

### Data and calendar prerequisites

- Contract-specific daily prices, volume and OI where actually published.
- Overlapping history for both maturities, including expired contracts.
- Quote units, settlement formula, fixing source and rule history.
- Actual exchange calendars, settlement calendars and broker cutoffs.
- Point-in-time rate/financing inputs **only if** the model explicitly uses them.
- Synchronized quotes later, if execution sensitivity proves material.

### Formula and units

For comparable normalized prices:

\[
c_t=
\frac{\log(F_{f,t}/F_{n,t})}{(T_f-T_n)/365},
\]

in annualized log-price units.

Fit a parsimonious training-only curve benchmark \(m(\cdot)\), for example a robust median by tenor bucket:

\[
e_t=c_t-m(\text{tenor bucket, permitted state variables}).
\]

The first baseline should omit rate inputs rather than manufacture historical forecasts. A rate-adjusted version is a **separate logged variant**, using only rates and publication times available then.

### Selection, entry, continuation and exit

- Choose one shared configuration across eligible contracts where possible.
- Enter opposite the residual only if historical conditional forward outcomes—not slope alone—support positive conservative incremental excess.
- Match physical/economic exposure, not arbitrary one-for-one contract counts.
- Continue under the forward-value test in Section 7.
- Exit at the registered residual band, holding limit, expiry buffer or risk trigger.
- Treat each roll as a new, fully costed exposure decision.

### Invalidate and expected failure

Invalidate on fixing changes, broken unit compatibility, reference-market discontinuities, severe hedge residuals or absent usable overlapping history.

Expected failure: the “dislocation” is a persistent financing premium; observed daily prices are asynchronous; or the near-leg expiry does not deliver the supposed convergence.

### Validation and rejection

Use chronological folds separated by actual signal/holding overlap. Report contribution by maturity bucket and market regime.

Reject for the pilot if minimum integer sizing fails, the conservative cost frontier is below plausible execution costs, or performance depends on one expiry episode. **Insufficient independent events means inconclusive, not profitable.**

## H2 — Commodity calendar residual with tenor and seasonal controls

**Research priority: 3.**

### Economic story and instruments

Investigate deviations from a commodity’s own curve structure, allowing for storage economics, seasonal demand and benchmark-specific settlement.

Start with only one or two independently justified commodity families—not every available commodity. Gold and energy contracts are leads, subject to specifications and data.

The carry literature motivates studying nearby/next-nearby curve information, but does not establish that a particular MOEX calendar position earns it. [Koijen et al., *Carry*, July 2012 preliminary version, commodity-carry discussion](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/Carry.pdf)

### Data and calendar prerequisites

H1 inputs, plus:

- benchmark location, quality and settlement reference;
- contract month and season;
- benchmark publication calendar and time;
- point-in-time specification changes;
- adequate repeated seasonal cycles for a seasonal model;
- explicit treatment of non-positive prices if encountered.

Do not use logs through non-positive observations. An alternative difference normalization would require a separately frozen specification.

### Formula and units

For positive prices:

\[
s_t=\log F_{f,t}-\log F_{n,t},
\]

\[
e_t=s_t-
m(\text{tenor bucket},\text{contract-month pair}).
\]

Use a **no-seasonality robust baseline first**. Add a coarse seasonal effect only if training history contains repeated cycles sufficient to estimate it. Avoid twelve free monthly parameters on a short history.

### Selection, entry, continuation and exit

- Share parameters within the family.
- Enter only where residual normalization has a supported conditional forward payoff after costs.
- Carry slope is a state variable, not a guaranteed cash receipt.
- Continue only while the original benchmark relationship remains valid and forward value clears the continuation hurdle.
- Exit before the near-leg risk window unless settlement exposure was explicitly part of a later, separately approved test.

### Invalidate and expected failure

Invalidate on benchmark replacement, changed delivery/settlement economics, missing seasonal history, insufficient overlapping maturities or contract-rule ambiguity.

Expected failure: a shortage or inventory regime permanently changes the curve; a recurring seasonal premium is mistaken for mispricing; or the reference commodity does not behave like the MOEX contract.

### Validation and rejection

Hold out entire seasonal/expiry episodes where possible. Compare:

1. no seasonal adjustment;
2. one coarse seasonal adjustment;
3. no-trade LQDT counterfactual.

Reject if the seasonal variant earns its apparent advantage from one event, a foreign-market backtest, or an unverified settlement assumption.

**Specific GL gate:** the accessible historical launch notice and current product page disagree on the named settlement reference. Do not infer the transition date or assume equivalence. [MOEX launch parameters](https://www.moex.com/n56918) [MOEX current GL parameters](https://www.moex.com/ru/derivatives/gold)

## H3 — Robust same-issuer common/preferred equity-futures spread

**Research priority: 2.**

### Economic story and instruments

A same-issuer relationship provides a more defensible starting point than selecting unrelated securities for high correlation. A candidate example is matched-maturity Sberbank ordinary/preferred futures, listed separately by MOEX. [MOEX, equity-futures directory, Sberbank sections, accessed 2026-10-22](https://www.moex.com/s3809)

However, **common and preferred shares are not assumed fungible**. Their relative valuation can change persistently. The hypothesis is conditional relative-value mean reversion, not a contractual convergence trade.

### Data and calendar prerequisites

- Matched-maturity futures histories.
- Point-in-time underlying share definitions and contract multipliers.
- Corporate-action notices and dividend announcements with availability times.
- Settlement method, corporate-action adjustment rules and expiry cutoffs.
- Underlying share prices as diagnostics if lawfully available.
- No future announced dividend inserted into an earlier observation.

### Formula and units

Normalize futures into comparable underlying-share prices:

\[
s_t=\log p_{\text{ordinary},t}
-\alpha-\beta\log p_{\text{preferred},t}.
\]

Primary baseline: \(\beta=1\), robust center and scale.

One additional variant: robust training-only \(\beta\), frozen during the position.

Economic quantities must be translated into actual contract counts; any residual issuer-market exposure is measured separately.

### Selection, entry, continuation and exit

- Enter on a preregistered robust residual threshold only if conservative expected excess is positive.
- Compare including versus excluding predefined corporate-event windows. Neither treatment may be chosen after seeing the event outcome.
- Continue using current conditional value, dividend/event state and remaining risk.
- Exit on normalization, time limit, impending expiry or account trigger.

### Invalidate and expected failure

Invalidate on changed share rights, corporate restructuring, incompatible corporate-action treatment, unstable hedge or an event that removes the original economic story.

Expected failure: a permanent relative repricing is mistaken for temporary divergence; a missing dividend expectation drives the spread; the preferred leg is stale or costly.

### Validation and rejection

Compare the fixed economic hedge against the robust statistical hedge under identical splits and costs. Record how much “alpha” disappears when stale observations and event windows are handled.

Reject if profitability requires frequent hedge refits, one dividend event, continuous position averaging or an unverified corporate-action adjustment.

## H4 — Currency-identity basket

**Research priority: 4; conditional three-leg experiment.**

### Economic story and instruments

For consistent spot definitions:

\[
\frac{\text{RUB}}{\text{USD}}
=
\frac{\text{RUB}}{\text{CNY}}
\times
\frac{\text{CNY}}{\text{USD}}.
\]

MOEX lists USD/RUB, CNY/RUB and USD/CNY futures families. That supports investigating a three-leg relationship, but does not prove that their futures settlement references, fixing times or sizes form a tradeable identity. [MOEX, currency futures, available-pairs section](https://www.moex.com/s3811)

### Data and calendar prerequisites

- Compatible maturity dates and reference periods.
- Each leg’s fixing source, timestamp and fallback rules.
- Correct quote orientation and contract quantity.
- RUB variation-margin conversion rules.
- Synchronized executable quotes for later validation.
- An explicit account of differences between the futures and the underlying spot identity.

### Formula and units

After conversion into the stated units:

\[
r_t=\log F_{\text{RUB/USD},t}
-\log F_{\text{RUB/CNY},t}
-\log F_{\text{CNY/USD},t}.
\]

The zero benchmark is admissible **only if contractual compatibility is demonstrated**. Otherwise, fit a restricted training-only residual center and identify the remaining basis risks.

Use the basket Jacobian to derive local hedge quantities; three futures contracts do not automatically produce a currency-neutral basket.

### Selection, entry, continuation and exit

- Start with the identity hedge, not a regression search.
- Enter only outside a full three-leg cost-and-uncertainty band.
- Continue while compatible reference economics remain and expected forward value exceeds flattening/reallocation value.
- Exit on normalization, loss of a leg, fixing-window risk, expiry constraints or account rules.

### Invalidate and expected failure

Invalidate if settlement references are incompatible, one leg is unavailable, or integer mapping leaves excessive residual currency exposure.

Expected failure: daily timestamps create artificial deviations; a fixing basis is persistent; three-leg costs exceed the residual; manual delay destroys the opportunity.

### Validation and rejection

EOD data can reject grossly incompatible economics. It **cannot establish executable identity arbitrage**.

Reject from the manual pilot if the advantage is concentrated in short-lived discrepancies, coordinated execution is unavailable, or legging stress requires core collateral.

**Deferred alternatives:** MOEX/RTS/FX and RUB-gold/USD-gold/FX baskets belong to this structural class, but are not additional first-pass searches. ## H5 — Dated-versus-daily-auto-prolonging futures

**Research priority: 5; data-intensive conditional experiment.**

### Economic story and instruments

- Both price histories.
- Actual historical funding accruals/payments.

### Formula and units

For appropriately matched quantities:

\[
\Pi_{t\to u}
=
\Pi^{dated}_{t\to u}
+\Pi^{daily}_{t\to u}

-C_{t\to u}.

A basis signal may be:

\[

## 7. Entry versus continuation: a forward decision

## 7.1 The relevant comparison

At time \(t\), define feasible policies:

- **F:** flatten and follow the LQDT/cash policy;
- **H:** hold the current basket under its exit rules;
- **R:** flatten/reduce and allocate to a currently observable alternative;
- **W:** flatten and wait for future opportunities under a past-information arrival model.

All policies start from the **same present economic state** and are evaluated over a common horizon with a terminal liquidation convention.

For action \(a\):

\[
V_t(a)=
E_t[\Delta W^a-\Delta W^L]
-\text{risk/uncertainty penalty}_t(a).
\]

A proposal to continue must beat the feasible alternative:

\[
V_t(H) >
\max\{V_t(F),V_t(R),V_t(W)\}+\delta,
\]

where \(\delta\) is a preregistered switching/estimation buffer.

This need not be an elaborate optimizer. The first version can be a conservative lookup based on residual state, trade age and time to expiry.

### Costs must be compared consistently

- Paid entry fees and past slippage are sunk.
- Future close costs, funding, rehedging and liquidation costs remain relevant.
- Immediate flattening incurs close costs now.
- Holding usually incurs close costs later, potentially in a different liquidity state.
- If wealth is already liquidation-adjusted, reconcile that adjustment rather than charging it twice.
- A switch pays current exit costs **and** new entry costs.

**Do not subtract a round trip from continuation when the entry half has already happened. Do not ignore the exit half because the position already exists.**

## 7.2 What the continuation forecast must contain

At each scheduled decision:

1. Conditional expected remaining RUB payoff.
2. Remaining cost range.
3. Expected LQDT wealth difference from funds still diverted.
4. Distribution of time to exit.
5. Probability of loss-trigger exit, expiry-buffer exit and non-normalization.
6. Remaining VM liquidity needs and possible margin increases.
7. Residual unhedged exposures.
8. Value of feasible current alternatives.
9. Uncertainty due to small samples or changed economics.

Use coarse training-only state bins, for example:

- residual magnitude;
- trade age;
- days to expiry;
- normal versus stressed liquidity.

Avoid finely partitioned state tables containing only a handful of completed trades.

### Exit time is not just “average days held”

For remaining holding time \(T\), report:

\[
\Pr(T>h\mid\mathcal I_t)
\]

at selected horizons, plus:

- median and upper quantiles;
- probability of reaching the expiry buffer;
- probability of no economic normalization before forced exit;
- mark-to-market outcomes of positions still open at evaluation boundaries.

Right-censored trades must not disappear from the sample. If the evidence cannot estimate the tail reliably, retain the simpler fixed holding/expiry rules.

## 7.3 Long holding periods and sunk losses

A long-lived trade may still be worth holding if expected remaining excess is positive, liquidity needs are manageable and alternatives are weak. Conversely, a recently opened trade may deserve immediate exit after a structural change.

A sunk loss cannot justify continuation. However, past losses reduce the pilot’s **remaining lifetime loss and funding authority**. Thus:

- sunk P&L does not increase expected recovery value;
- the resulting account state legitimately restricts future actions.

This distinction prevents both “wait until breakeven” and “exit because it has been open too long” from masquerading as economic rules.

### Illustrative arithmetic—not a forecast

Suppose a position is down RUB 2,000. From now:

- expected additional gross payoff: RUB 900;
- remaining costs: RUB 250;
- incremental LQDT foregone return: RUB 200.

Expected continuation excess is RUB 450 before the uncertainty/risk penalty. The sunk RUB 2,000 does not change that arithmetic. Continuation may still be prohibited by the loss rules, expiry risk or insufficient confidence.

If a currently feasible replacement has greater net value over the same horizon **after switching costs**, switching can dominate. The replacement may not be chosen using its later realised return.

## 8. Fair comparison of sizing policies

The comparison must use the same:

- signal specifications and train/test boundaries;
- initial capital and maximum authority;
- execution-delay paths;
- cost scenarios and liquidity assumptions;
- loss rules;
- calendars and corporate-event information;
- signal-arrival ledger.

Use paired comparisons on the same market path. Do not give adaptive sizing better forecasts or additional capital.

| Policy | Definition | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| **S0: fixed minimum** | One minimum acceptable integer basket for each accepted opportunity | Does the economic hypothesis work without sizing sophistication? |
| **S1: capped adaptive** | Position size responds to a frozen forecast/risk rule, then maps to integers | Does forecast-strength information improve net excess after granularity and turnover? |
| **S2: preplanned scale-in** | Initial tranche plus at most one scheduled conditional addition | Does staged exposure add value beyond equivalent authority and timing controls? |

### 8.1 Fixed minimum sizing

Use the smallest basket satisfying hedge and risk constraints. If it cannot fit, reject the signal. It is not acceptable to take a badly hedged “one contract each” position merely because it fits the nominal allocation.

### 8.2 Capped adaptive sizing

A simple proposed continuous target is:

\[
k_t^*=
\min\left(
k_{\max},
\frac{B_{\text{risk}}}{L_{\text{stress,unit}}},
\frac{B_{\text{funding}}}{M_{\text{stress,unit}}}
\right)
g(|z_t|),
\]

where \(g\) is a preregistered capped monotone function.

Map \(k_t^*\) to acceptable integer baskets, with a no-trade region. Use a volatility floor and stress-loss constraint so quiet historical periods do not produce uncontrolled leverage.

Keep rebalancing coarse. A daily fractional target that repeatedly rounds between zero and one may add costs without useful risk control.

### 8.3 Preplanned scale-ins

The scale-in experiment may use one additional tranche, with:

- maximum final exposure defined at initial entry;
- total maximum funding and stressed liquidity reserved before entry;
- no emergency allowance included;
- no addition after a warning/action state;
- no addition after invalidation;
- the same expiry and intervention constraints.

Proposed comparison:

1. One-shot full authorized basket.
2. Initial smaller basket, never add.
3. Initial smaller basket, add only when the preregistered condition remains valid.

An adverse price move alone is **not** sufficient justification to add. If the rule deliberately adds on a larger residual, it must also require positive conditional marginal value, valid economics and adequate remaining loss capacity.

At minimum integer size, fractional staging may be impossible. That is a genuine reason to reject scale-ins for this pilot.

### 8.4 Equal authority, not artificially identical utilisation

The policies may use different amounts of capital at different times—that is part of the question. They must share the same **ceiling**, not be forced to hold equal exposure.

Report both:

- total net RUB excess;
- exposure, margin, funding and risk utilisation.

Additional capital has an LQDT counterfactual. It is not free merely because it was approved.

## 9. Opportunity arrival and reallocation without an oracle

## 9.1 Maintain an as-of opportunity ledger

Each signal should produce a record containing:

- detection and information-availability times;
- family, instruments and specification versions;
- forecast and uncertainty interval as known then;
- proposed integer basket;
- expected costs and capital requirement;
- signal expiry/staleness rule;
- contemporaneous rank;
- accepted, stale, rejected, unfilled or partially filled status;
- reason for rejection;
- human actions required;
- executable outcome where observable.

Include candidates rejected for insufficient capital, margin, stale data, expiry, human quota and execution constraints.

The realistic portfolio **processes every eligible signal**, but finite capital and rules determine which orders are attempted. It must not retrospectively omit inconvenient opportunities.

A rejected idea may be followed as a clearly labelled shadow observation where data permit. Its later outcome must not enter an earlier forecast or be mistaken for an executable fill.

## 9.2 Start with a ranked queue

The baseline queue is:

1. Remove invalid, stale and unaffordable candidates.
2. Rank currently visible candidates using a frozen conservative expected-excess score.
3. Apply a fixed tie-breaker.
4. Allocate within available authority.
5. Retain cash/LQDT when no candidate clears the hurdle.

A practical score can combine:

\[
\text{conservative expected RUB excess}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{committed funding-days},
\]

but expected excess divided by expected holding time should be **only a heuristic**. It ignores exit-time tails and interactions between capital release and future arrivals.

Use a no-signal/no-trade outcome as a normal state, not an operational failure.

## 9.3 Arrival model: add only if justified

Estimate arrivals using only prior ledger history. A first candidate is a rolling count model by family; test whether clustering and regime shifts make even that unreliable.

Forecast:

- probability of at least one eligible opportunity in a fixed future window;
- its likely capital requirement;
- conservative value distribution.

Include stale/rejected opportunities when defining the observable opportunity process. Separately distinguish:

- signals detected;
- economically eligible opportunities;
- executable opportunities;
- opportunities affordable to this portfolio.

Do not replace these with the ex-post number of profitable trades.

## 9.4 Opportunity-aware exit

For a current position \(j\):

\[
\text{switch only if}\quad
\widehat V_t(\text{feasible replacement})
-\widehat V_t(\text{continue }j)
>\delta_{\text{switch}},
\]

after all switching costs and uncertainty.

A forecast of future arrivals can justify a value for waiting, but **not an assumed future winner**. Test it against:

- no early reallocation;
- current-opportunity ranked queue;
- fixed holding/expiry rules.

If the queue performs similarly, reject the more complex arrival model.

## 10. Costs, delay and free-data adequacy

## 10.1 Explicit cost model

For fill \(j\) in leg \(i\):

\[
C_{ij}
=
|n_{ij}|
\left[
b_{ij}+e_{ij}
+v_{ij}(h_{ij}+s_{ij})
\right],
\]

where:

- \(b\): broker fee per contract;
- \(e\): applicable exchange/clearing fee;
- \(h\): adverse half-spread distance in ticks;
- \(s\): additional slippage in ticks;
- \(v\): RUB tick value.

If actual executable bid/ask prices are used, do not add the same half-spread again.

Add separately where applicable:

- settlement/execution charges;
- forced-liquidation charges;
- failed-leg unwinds;
- rehedging and rolls;
- funding cash flows;
- collateral/cash financing;
- incremental LQDT trading costs;
- unresolved tax effects.

MOEX’s tariff page describes calendar commission as the sum of constituent futures commissions. It does not verify the user’s VTB or BCS schedule. [MOEX, derivatives tariffs, calendar-spread section](https://www.moex.com/s93)

### Proposed synthetic scenarios

These are **sensitivity probes**, not estimates of MOEX spreads.

| Scenario | Half-spread \(h\), per side | Additional slippage \(s\), per side | Fees |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Base | 1 tick | 1 tick | Applicable \(b,e\), once verified |
| Conservative | 2 ticks | 2 ticks | Applicable \(b,e\), plus identified adverse tariff conditions |
| Stress | 4 ticks | 6 ticks | Applicable stressed charges, forced-exit and legging scenarios |

Observed worse conditions override these values. A wide or illiquid leg must not be capped at this synthetic stress.

Unknown fees remain symbolic or interval-valued. **No net-return result may silently set them to zero.**

## 10.2 Required survival frontier

For every family and sizing policy, report:

- fee range;
- half-spread/slippage range;
- delay conditions;
- fill/rejection assumptions;
- resulting net \(X\);
- the boundary at which the hypothesis ceases to pass.

For a simple sensitivity decomposition:

\[
X(\theta)=G-OC-C(\theta),
\]

where \(G\) includes the appropriate futures/funding cash flows and \(OC\) is the actual-minus-counterfactual LQDT effect.

The cost budget is:

\[
C_{\max}=G-OC,
\]

before any additional robustness hurdle. In practice, estimate the frontier over full portfolio paths because costs also change sizing, rejections and subsequent opportunities.

**No numerical survival range can be reported now:** no historical execution results or verified tariffs were supplied or measured.

## 10.3 What EOD can and cannot establish

EOD data can support:

- specification and unit checks;
- broad residual and curve hypotheses;
- low-frequency holding-time analysis;
- preliminary integer feasibility;
- rejection of effects too small to survive coarse costs.

EOD OHLC bars do not establish:

- simultaneous executable two- or three-leg prices;
- bid/ask spreads;
- fill probability or partial fills;
- the path between high and low;
- performance after a 3–90-minute reaction delay;
- availability of margin offsets during legging.

A defensible first proxy is: **form signals only after the required EOD information is available, then attempt execution at a predefined point in the next joint trading session.** A next-session open is still a proxy, not a demonstrated fill.

Do not construct an executable spread high from one leg’s daily high and the other’s daily low.

With later synchronized quote data, test fixed delays such as 3, 15 and 90 minutes. Do not assign probabilities matching the user’s reported median until actual reaction observations exist. A one-day delay test is **not a mathematical upper bound** on every intraday-delay outcome.

MOEX’s ISS documentation confirms the existence of candles and historical data types, but not the required free depth, expired coverage or permissions. Its subscription archive description must not be used to fill those gaps. [ISS documentation](https://www.moex.com/a2193) [Historical-data service](https://www.moex.com/a2863)

## 11. Chronological validation and falsification

### 11.1 Register the universe before outcomes

Admission requires a documented economic relationship. Retain expired, inactive and failed candidates where data permit; do not build the historical universe from current survivors alone.

Record all five families and all rejected leads, including:

- arbitrary correlation-selected pairs: excluded;
- foreign commodity backtests: methodological leads only;
- GL cross-currency identity: deferred pending specification reconciliation;
- index/FX identity: deferred pending current methodology and futures audit;
- China extension: not activated;
- funding spread without funding history: inadmissible.

### 11.2 Walk-forward procedure

1. Reserve a final untouched evaluation period.
2. Fit parameters, hedges, filters, cost calibration and queue rules using training data only.
3. Select among the preregistered configurations within training/inner chronological validation.
4. Freeze the configuration for the next test interval.
5. Purge observations whose outcome/holding intervals overlap the next evaluation boundary.
6. Size gaps using the **actual maximum signal and holding overlap**, including label construction.
7. Advance chronologically and repeat.
8. Evaluate the final holdout once, after design freeze.

If the available history cannot support meaningful independent folds and an untouched final period, report **insufficient evidence**. Do not shrink the holdout repeatedly until a favourable result appears.

Historical holdouts are imperfect when researchers already know the period. An explicitly prospective final evaluation is preferable where practical. The trial ledger remains necessary because holdout alone does not account for repeated configuration search. [Bailey et al., *The Probability of Backtest Overfitting*, February 2015 version, Section 1](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)

### 11.3 Position and boundary treatment

Predefine whether positions may cross retraining boundaries. A simple first choice is:

- retain the old frozen trade rule until exit;
- apply the new model only to new trades;
- prohibit retroactive hedge changes.

At evaluation boundaries, mark all open positions and disclose censored outcomes. Use a common terminal-liquidation convention for portfolio comparisons.

### 11.4 Required reports

Primary:

- terminal and time-path net RUB \(X\);
- excess high-water drawdown;
- cumulative LQDT shortfall.

Supporting:

- gross/fee/spread/slippage/funding/LQDT reconciliation;
- completed and censored trades;
- time-to-exit distribution;
- maximum margin and cash demand;
- emergency gross usage and incidents;
- stale, rejected, partial and unfilled counts;
- human interventions and leg tickets;
- results by fold, family, expiry and cost/delay scenario;
- contribution concentration;
- stress liquidation outcomes.

Do not make Sharpe ratio or trade win rate the selection criterion.

### 11.5 Proposed pass, reject and inconclusive categories

**Eligible for further research**, not live deployment:

- economically valid specifications;
- acceptable integer positions;
- positive net excess under base and conservative modeled conditions;
- no dependence on a single event;
- uncertainty analysis consistent with a genuine effect;
- no hidden core-collateral dependence;
- acceptable manual and cash demands.

For inference, preregister dependence-aware confidence analysis using blocks long enough to reflect holding/expiry clustering. Report sensitivity to block choice. A confidence bound is not a guaranteed future outcome.

**Reject for this pilot** if:

- minimum basket fails funding/risk constraints;
- edge survives only at implausibly cheap costs or instantaneous fills;
- a missing cash flow is essential;
- settlement economics invalidate the supposed relationship;
- complexity merely leverages one favourable episode;
- ordinary execution relies on emergency funding.

**Inconclusive** if sample size, rules, costs or permitted data are insufficient. Do not relabel inconclusive as successful.

## 12. Ablation plan: complexity must earn its place

Use common signal streams, cost paths and training/test periods.

| Comparison | Hold fixed | Isolated question |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed minimum vs capped adaptive | Entry/exit model, authority and execution | Does flexible size add net value? |
| One-shot vs staged exposure | Maximum authorized basket and reserved liquidity | Does staging improve timing rather than merely add exposure? |
| Smaller initial basket with/without addition | Same initial tranche | Is the addition itself valuable? |
| Fixed residual/time exit vs forward continuation | Entry and sizing | Does conditional continuation improve decisions? |
| Continuation vs current-opportunity switching | Forecast model and sizing | Does a visible alternative justify switching costs? |
| Ranked queue vs arrival-aware allocator | Eligible ledger and total authority | Is future-arrival modeling necessary? |
| Identity/static hedge vs refitted hedge | Entry family and costs | Does hedge flexibility add value after turnover? |
| No seasonal adjustment vs coarse seasonality | Commodity family and folds | Does seasonal structure generalize? |

Avoid launching a large factorial search. Test the simplest viable baseline first, then a small number of one-factor changes. Any later combined configuration is a new logged trial.

A complexity feature should be rejected if it:

- adds no stable net excess after costs;
- worsens cash/shortfall tails;
- relies on a few observations;
- increases human actions beyond capacity;
- cannot be distinguished from integer-rounding or exposure effects.

If adaptive size and scale-ins collapse to the same one-basket position under RUB 30,000, record them as **economically indistinguishable**, not as separate successful features.

## 15. Decision gates and possible project changes

The test plan should permit the project to become simpler—or stop.

| Finding | Appropriate project change |
|---|---|
| No acceptable integer basket within RUB 30,000 | Retain a virtual study; narrow the universe. Do not automatically enlarge capital. |

| Effects disappear under conservative costs | Reject those hypotheses; do not assume automation will repair them. |

| Funding/specification history is incomplete | Exclude the dependent family. |

| Ranked queue matches complex allocation | Keep the queue. |

## Bottom line

R10 supports a **small, economically admitted hypothesis set with strict accounting and falsification**, not a chosen strategy.

The decisive sequence is:

**specification and permission → integer/cash feasibility → simple delayed-execution baseline → net LQDT excess → continuation and sizing ablations → untouched evaluation.**

Until those tests are conducted, the justified conclusion is neither “spreads will beat LQDT” nor “holding capital is wasteful.” It is that **every new entry, continuation, addition and switch must earn its place against the same feasible LQDT alternative, under the same finite authority and without hindsight.**
