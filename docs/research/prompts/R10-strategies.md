## Investigate
1. Propose a compact family of simple economic/statistical baselines for distinct spread classes, e.g. justified carry/basis/calendar relationships and robust spread mean-reversion hypotheses. Explain why raw z-scores/correlation/stationarity tests alone are not an economic mechanism or a proof of tradeability.
2. Define spread normalization, hedge estimation, integer mapping, entry/exit, failure/structural-break checks, expiry handling and required point-in-time inputs. Keep thresholds as preregistered candidate ranges/hypotheses until validated. Different families may deserve different models but unlimited per-pair tuning increases selection bias.
3. Entry vs continuation: conditional expected incremental value from now, remaining costs, LQDT counterfactual, distribution of time-to-exit, loss/expiry risk and freed funds for competing opportunities. Do not make “holding long” automatically bad or sunk losses a reason to continue.
4. Compare fixed minimum sizing, capped adaptive sizing and preplanned scale-ins using the SAME data splits, execution delays, costs and total authority. New capital isn't free, and the emergency reserve cannot fund an averaging-down strategy in this pilot.
5. Opportunity-arrival modeling must be based only on past available opportunities, including stale/rejected ideas. Avoid an oracle that always reallocates to the ex-post best trade. Test whether a simpler ranked queue achieves similar results.
