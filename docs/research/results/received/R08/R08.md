# R08 (Consolidated) — Delayed manual execution, signal validity and the virtual portfolio

**Status:** Consolidated evidence-backed research report for a private MOEX futures-spread project. It is not software, not an investment recommendation, not authorisation to trade or to fund an account. No new sources were fetched for this consolidation and no new measurements were taken: it merges eight independently produced R08 reports into one text, resolving overlap, carrying conflicts forward explicitly, and marking what was actually checked versus proposed.

**Facts cutoff:** the newest source-report cutoff governs. Source cutoffs are 2026-10-15 (`ar`), 2026-10-21 (`gs`, `ex`), 2026-10-22 (`cg`, `ds`), and "October 2026" for `mm`, `gr`, `ge` (the worked examples in `gr` and `ge` carry 2026-10-21 signal ids; `mm` gives no date). The underlying task pack was prepared 2026-10-07. Where two source reports state a fact observed on different dates, the later observation is used and the earlier one is recorded as a vintage.

**Decision researched:** how a medium-term MOEX futures-spread idea survives a manual, delayed, human execution chain — free-data lag, Telegram delivery, a human who is unavailable for a large part of the trading day, two-leg order placement at VTB (later potentially BCS), finite RUB 30,000 pilot capital measured against a matched LQDT counterfactual — and how that chain must be simulated honestly in **one** realistic virtual portfolio before any live conclusion is drawn.

## 0. Executive conclusions

**0.1 The core methodological finding, on which all eight reports agree.** A signal observed at one time is not a fill at that time. A signal is an invitation to re-evaluate two executable legs at decision time, not an instruction to fill prices observed 3–90 minutes earlier `[cg]`, `[ex]`. The project therefore needs a second, timestamped execution layer that begins when a person can actually act, and every candidate must be revalidated against then-current executable prices, session state, contract state, minimum lot, fees, margin and finite cash. A good historical signal can legitimately become a rejected, stale, partially filled or unfilled trade; the simulator must preserve those outcomes instead of forcing every signal into the P&L ledger `[cg]`, `[ds]`, `[mm]`. The object to validate is not an abstract entry signal but a **delayed-fill, capital-constrained order lifecycle** `[mm]`.

**0.2 The binding delay is not the human reaction time.** It is the sum of three clocks the project does not control `[ar]`:

1. **The free-data clock.** ISS delayed data is "at least 15 minutes" by rule and was measured at 15 min 02–03 s `[R04 §1, §7.2]`; the documented event-to-action chain on free data is **18–105 minutes** `[R04 §1.6, adopted in R07 P9]`. Add the 3–90 minute reaction and the market input is roughly 18–105+ minutes old when an order arrives `[gs]`. On free data, **no strategy that lives shorter than ≈20–30 minutes is verifiable or executable in this project** `[R04 §1 point 6, §9]`. This makes the "stale winner" a structural risk, not an edge case `[gs]`.
2. **The human-availability clock.** The user is unavailable from 00:00 until 11:00–12:00 MSK `[USER 2026-10-15, via ar §3]`. That window covers the 06:50 opening auction, the whole morning session, and the 09:00 (or 10:00 — MOEX's own page conflicts, §2.1) margin-requirement issuance. A signal or margin event that lands in the blackout is not "delayed by 90 minutes"; it is in a different regime.
3. **The broker clock.** VTB's restrictive closing times of 16:00 MSK (Unified Brokerage Account portfolios) and 14:00 MSK (Derivatives portfolio), discretionary forced closure, and **no guaranteed grace period or pre-closure warning** `[R01 §0 point 4, §7]`.

**0.3 The pilot's defence cannot be "react quickly in the morning".** The human is present for the afternoon/evening risk-management window but absent for the morning risk-information window `[ar §3.4]`. The design constraint must be: *never enter a position whose stressed morning state requires a morning action* — R07's stress scenario S1 ("overnight gap + morning margin call + slow human") promoted from a stress case to a standing entry rule.

**0.4 Atomic spread execution must not be assumed.** Three routinely conflated things are separated in §2.3: exchange-level calendar-spread instruments (MOEX service, same underlying / different expiries only), clearing-level margin offsets (NCC MMS/MKS groups), and broker-level indivisible joint orders (a narrow documented exception, at BCS). MOEX documents the calendar-spread service with synthetic matching and limit / market / fill-or-kill order categories; inter-product spreads are described only as a prospective extension `[ar W2]`, `[cg S5]`, `[gs S5]`. Nothing in any source establishes atomic handling of two different economic underlyings, and nothing establishes that a VTB client interface exposes even the eligible exchange spread instrument `[cg]`, `[gs]`, `[ds]`. **Default for all VTB simulation: two independent leg orders with a priced, time-boxed unhedged interval** `[ar §7]`, `[gr]`.

**0.5 Exchange trading hours do not establish VTB client access.** VTB's public help page surfaced a derivatives window of 08:50–23:50 MSK, which conflicts with the MOEX schedule's 06:50 start; the page's update/effective date is unclear and a direct fetch returned HTTP 502 `[cg S4]`, `[ex]`. Until confirmed, early windows are classified `BROKER_SESSION_UNCONFIRMED` rather than either falsely traded or silently excluded `[cg §2.1]`.

**0.6 Public quote display is not execution-grade history.** MOEX contract pages state that displayed futures data are delayed 15 minutes and that real-time data require a subscription; ISS order books and historical data are subscriber-only (2016 wording; current scope to verify) `[cg S6]`, `[gs S9–S10]`, `[mm 4–6]`. Anonymous ISS provides **no order book and no bid/ask history at all**, and the trade tape only for the current day `[R04 §1 point 3]`. Historical bid/ask cannot be back-filled because it does not exist retrospectively `[ar §9.1]`. With no historical bid/ask available for free, the honest historical product is a bounded, conservative OHLCV replay — an interval, not a point estimate — plus a forward collection plan that starts manufacturing the missing evidence `[ar]`, `[gs]`, `[cg]`, `[ds]`, `[ex]`.

**0.7 The ISS licence may block a signal pipeline.** MOEX describes unsubscribed ISS information as "только для ознакомления … не может быть использована для извлечения прибыли", with any other use possible only by agreement `[gs S11]`, `[ex]`. Any programmatic (non-display) use, including recording and systematisation, is treated as contract-only `[R04 §1 point 4]`. An automated screener that systematically records ISS data therefore sits in a legal-risk zone until written clarification is obtained; the manual contour (human reads, human decides) is partly a consequence of this, not only of the manual-execution mandate `[ar §2.3]`, `[gs]`.

**0.8 The 2026 session regime changes the timing model.** The Unified Trading Session (ETS) started 2026-03-23; hours were extended from 2026-07-14; the main session start moved to 09:00 MSK effective 2026-09-14 `[cg S1–S2]`, `[gs S1–S3]`, `[ds]`, `[ex]`. Under the current regime: the intermediate (intraday) clearing session is abolished; mark-to-market clearing runs 23:50–00:30; variation margin, premiums and commissions settle at 20:00 on T+1; margin is recomputed intraday only when price limits change; participants must meet margin calls by 17:30 T+1 while the client deadline is set by the broker; orders stay live until the end of the trading day; the weekend session belongs to the next trading day, covers only a listed set of contracts, performs no clearing, and can be cancelled `[gs S2, S4, S7, S8]`, `[ds]`, `[ex]`. The routine weekday span 06:50–23:50 is 17 hours, not 24: a "dark-only" screener may monitor health around the clock but must only label a contract actionable when the exchange *and* broker accept that order type for that contract `[cg §2.1]`.

**0.9 Session documentation is internally inconsistent and must be versioned, not hard-coded.** The same MOEX ETS page lists 06:50–07:00 auction / 07:00–09:00 morning in one section and 08:50–09:00 auction / 09:00–23:50 trading in another `[ex]`, and its FAQ still describes the pre-expansion schedule with margin requirements issued at 10:00 while its schedule table says 09:00 `[ar W1]`. Disposition (§2.1, §15): the protocol consumes a *versioned calendar object* and treats the margin-issuance instant as a parameter `t_MT ∈ {09:00, 10:00}`; any result that flips between the two values is reported both ways. Three source reports (`ge`, `gr`, `mm`) still describe a 14:00–14:05 intermediate clearing and an 18:50–19:05 evening clearing; those windows belong to the pre-ETS regime abolished on 2026-03-23 and must not be applied to post-ETS dates, though they remain the correct historical calendar for any pre-ETS window and remain the origin of the still-valid "no entry near a clearing boundary" rule.

**0.10 VTB order mechanics add specific, dated failure modes** (§2.7): a market order that cannot be immediately placed is automatically cancelled `[ds]`; limit orders may be partially executed unless the client instructs otherwise, and a partially executed order is cancelled only as to the unexecuted part `[ds]`; telephone orders must arrive no later than 15 minutes before session end (paper/fax: one hour) `[ds]`; QUIK conditional/stop orders are checked on a server outside the exchange and may fail for technical reasons `[gs S12]`; since a 2026-07-22 release, app orders can be valid up to 90 days and orders entered while the market is closed are queued for the next session — a stale-fill hazard `[gs S13]`; one VTB page says orders can be changed or cancelled only by phoning traders, which conflicts with the app's "valid until cancel" feature and probably reflects a sanctions-era special regime `[gs S14]`, `[gs §11]`; backup channels are a web version, QUIK and voice orders, highlighted after the 2026-10-11 Runet outage `[gs S15]`.

**0.11 Entry economics are computable now; entry permission is not.** R02/R06 supply the full cost algebra and per-pair snapshots (full round-turn friction including exchange fees: CNYRUBF/CRZ6 10.7 RUB, GLDRUBF/GLZ6 33.0 RUB, IMOEXF/MMZ6 32.3 RUB per 1:1 pair, snapshot 2026-10-07; VTB broker fee 0 RUB per contract-side until 2026-12-31, then 1 RUB) `[R06 §10.1–10.3, §12.1]`, `[R02 §1.3]`. But live entry additionally requires the five written VTB confirmations listed in R01 §0 (margin-perimeter isolation, KPUR category, applied LQDT haircut, notification channel/timing under 6681-U §23, absence of contract clauses 15/19 derogations) — none obtained `[ar §0 point 7]`. **The virtual portfolio is therefore the only sanctioned next step; this report makes no claim of actual trading feasibility**, exactly as R01's verdict requires: public research and a capital-limited virtual test are "Допустимо"; a real test on the current VTB account is "Недопустимо до письменного подтверждения условий" `[R01 §0 table]`. Reports without those inputs correctly marked net-advantage thresholds as illustrative and `COSTS_UNKNOWN` as a valid rejection reason `[cg §5]`, `[ds §1]`.

**0.12 The pilot must fail closed on capital and unknowns.** RUB 30,000 is the full initial strategy envelope, not spare margin on the shared account. Entries must pass with **zero** emergency funding; prior rescue money or retained earnings do not enlarge the entry envelope `[cg §0.5]`, `[ar §8.4]`. Emergency allocations require separate approval *and* verified credit timing, are capped at RUB 15,000 lifetime **gross** (gross contributions therefore at most RUB 45,000), may only avert disorderly liquidation or reach flat, never fund fresh exposure, averaging down, restart or loss resets; returned funds and withdrawals do not restore the cap; broker-induced use of core collateral counts toward the cap and is an incident if unapproved `[ex]`, `[ar §8.5]`, `[gr §5]`. Gross contributions are not a notional, equity, loss or broker-recourse limit.

**0.13 The user's "one trade per day = one purchase or sale (can be a bit more)" cannot be applied literally to a two-leg spread without banning the strategy** `[ar §0 point 4]`. One spread entry is two purchases/sales; entry plus exit is four. Read literally as one *leg order* per day (Q-LEG), an entry would have to sit naked overnight, which the project's own naked-leg override forbids. §3.3 proposes — and does not silently assert — the counting unit the virtual portfolio will use (one *idea-level Manual Action Episode* per day, Q-IDEA), shows what the literal reading implies, and reports what Q-LEG would have blocked so the user's eventual choice is informed by simulation rather than pre-judged by it. This is the single user decision that most changes the simulation design.

**0.14 The delegated money triggers stay as governance, with R07's revision adopted as a test candidate.** Retain warning at excess-drawdown `D ≥ 5,000`, action at `D ≥ 10,000` or `X ≤ −10,000`, breach at `X < −15,000`, lifetime gross emergency funding `E ≤ 15,000` `[R05 §7.1; R07 §3.1 P2–P5]`, `[gr §5]`, `[ex]`. **Replace** the fixed 5,000 gap between action trigger and outer boundary with a computed buffer `u = max(5,000, 2.5 × σ_delay(book) + C_exit)` `[R07 §4.1 gate 1 — PROPOSAL]`, because the fixed gap is only ≈1.03 σ-days on a 3-pair IMOEX book and ≈1.45 σ-days on a 5-pair CNY book `[R07 §0 point 1]`. These are unvalidated action rules, not statistical bounds and not guaranteed maximum losses `[R09 §1 point 8]`.

**0.15 "Inconclusive" is a legitimate result.** If the conservative bound of the replay interval does not clear the matched-LQDT hurdle after the full cost stack, the correct outcome is *inconclusive*, which R09 explicitly legitimises: "Most sparse histories should be allowed to end 'inconclusive'" `[R09 §1 point 7]`. A 90-day pilot validates operations — signal arrival, both-leg execution, margin behaviour, reaction-time distribution, incident handling — not profitability (illustrative t ≈ 0.5 at annualised excess Sharpe 1) `[R09 §1 point 8]`.

**0.16 What this consolidation does not establish.** It does not establish that a single minimum-lot pair fits the RUB 30,000 envelope, that the intended broker route supports atomic spread execution, that the desired free historical data exists, that the signal has positive edge after delayed execution, that the user's reaction distribution matches any proposed scenario, or that live trading is feasible `[cg]`, `[gs]`, `[ds]`. Those require the missing confirmations in §15.

## 2. Dated evidence base

### 2.1 The exchange clock: sessions, clearing, settlement, calendar (MOEX, MSK = UTC+3)

**Current applicable schedule.** Under ETS (from 2026-03-23) the derivatives day is continuous: opening auction 06:50–07:00, morning additional session 07:00–09:00, main session 09:00–19:00, evening additional session 19:00–23:50; margin requirements to clearing members are issued at 09:00 and must be met by 17:30; expiries occur in trading at 14:00 or 19:00 depending on specification; settlement prices are determined at 19:00 (published 19:00–20:00); mark-to-market clearing runs 23:50–00:30; the settlement clearing session that actually moves variation-margin money runs at 20:00 on T+1; weekend additional sessions run 09:50–10:00 (auction) and 10:00–19:00 with **no clearing during the weekend** `[ar W1]`, `[R06 §4.1]`, `[cg S2]`, `[gs S1–S2]`, `[ds]`, `[ex]`. There is no intraday clearing: margin risk accumulates over ≈17 trading hours between evaluations `[R06 §4.2 point 1]`.

**Dated chronology of the 2026 changes** `[cg S1–S3]`, `[gs S2b, S3, S16]`, `[ds]`:

| Effective date | Regime |
|---|---|
| Before 2026-03-23 | Old session structure, including an intermediate (intraday) clearing session. Three reports still describe it and their figures differ slightly: `ge` gives main session 10:00–18:45, evening clearing 18:50–19:05, evening session 19:05–23:50; `mm` gives main 10:00–18:50, clearing 14:00–14:05, evening 19:05–23:50; `gr` uses the 14:00–14:05 and 18:50–19:05 windows as session-transition boundaries. All are a pre-ETS picture. |
| 2026-03-23 → 2026-07-13 | ETS: 08:50–23:50 (morning 08:50–10:00, main 10:00–19:00, evening 19:00–23:50); weekend additional session 10:00–19:00; intermediate clearing abolished; MTM clearing 23:50–00:30; VM/premiums/commissions settled T+1 at 20:00 `[gs S2b]`, `[ds]`. |
| From 2026-07-14 (announcement of 10 July 2026) | Extended to 06:50–23:50: auction 06:50–07:00, morning 07:00–10:00, main 10:00–19:00, evening 19:00–23:50; weekend 09:50–19:00; FX futures in the weekend session from 18 July; currency futures subject to ±3% price bounds in the morning session `[cg S1]`, `[gs S3, S16]`, `[ds]`. |
| From 2026-09-14 (announcement of 14 August 2026) | Morning 06:50–09:00, main 09:00–19:00, evening 19:00–23:50; weekend 09:50–19:00 including a 09:50–10:00 opening auction `[cg S2]`. This is the most recent published schedule. |

**Calendar discipline.** MOEX published a 2026 holiday schedule on 30 December 2025 and a 2026 weekend-calendar announcement on 25 November 2025; specific official holidays have special session formats, and weekend sessions have been cancelled (15–16 August 2026) `[cg S3]`, `[gs S4]`, `[ex]`. Use a dated exchange trading calendar including exceptional days; never classify dates by weekday alone `[cg §2.1]`, `[ds §3.3]`, `[gs §2]`. No dated VTB holiday/early-close schedule was supplied; the report does not assume every weekday is a full trading day `[ds]`.

**Session facts that shape execution** `[R06 §4.2]`, `[gs S2, S17]`, `[ex]`:
- Deliverable equity futures are exercised in the 23:50–00:30 clearing session with delivery trades on the stock market the next trading day, so a spread leg in such a contract must be closed before the *broker's* delivery deadline, which is earlier and account-specific.
- In the weekend session, requirements are only determined by Monday's clearing, funding is calculated only within the main session, and **exit from perpetual contracts is not provided at weekends**. A perpetual leg is therefore effectively closed for exit on Saturday–Sunday even where the quarterly leg trades — a leg-specific `MARKET_CLOSED_LEG` state, not a market-wide one.
- Weekend trades enter Monday mark-to-market and Tuesday settlement; no clearing or settlement sessions run during weekend trading `[ex]`.
- Price limits do **not** widen automatically in the evening session (19:00–23:50); MOEX may suspend trading to widen limits `[gs S17]`. A leg near its daily price limit is a revalidation failure, not a minor condition.
- Risk parameters are recalculated daily (published around 19:00 MSK) and can change intraday when price limits change `[mm 8, 48]`, `[gs S2]`, `[ex]`.

**Conflicts carried, not resolved.**
1. **Margin-requirement issuance and main-session start: 09:00 vs 10:00.** The same MOEX ETS page's FAQ block still describes the pre-expansion schedule (08:50–09:00 auction, 09:00–10:00 morning, 10:00–19:00 main) and states margin requirements are issued at 10:00, while its schedule table states 09:00 `[ar W1]`. R06 §4.1 records the same disagreement across MOEX releases (n98363 vs s3886 vs n101980). `ex` independently observed the same intra-page contradiction (06:50–07:00 / 07:00–09:00 in one section, 08:50–09:00 / 09:00–23:50 in another) and instructs: do not choose one silently.
2. **Weekend window: 10:00–19:00 (March announcement) vs 09:50–19:00 (July announcement).** The 09:50 start is most plausibly an opening auction `[gs §11]`.
3. **NCC's risk-parameter page still mentions the abolished intraday clearing** `[R01 §11.1 item 5]`.
4. **Broker window vs exchange window:** VTB's public page gives 08:50–23:50 for derivatives `[cg S4]`, `[ex]`, which does not match a 06:50 exchange open.
5. **Margin-call deadline: 17:30 same day vs 17:30 T+1.** The ETS page read on 2026-10-15 states that the requirement issued at 09:00 must be met by 17:30 `[ar W1]`; the ETS guide read on 2026-10-21 states 17:30 T+1 for participants `[gs S2]`. Both agree that the **client** deadline is set by the broker and is unknown. Disposition: treat 17:30 (whichever day) as a clearing-member-level parameter, and plan against the earlier of the broker's restrictive closing times (14:00/16:00 MSK), which dominate in every case.

**Consequence for the protocol.** Never hard-code one session table. Consume a *versioned calendar object* (`calendar_version` with `announcement_at / effective_at / received_at` `[R04 §10.2]`), parameterise the margin-issuance instant `t_MT ∈ {09:00, 10:00}`, keep exchange trading-day labels separate from wall-clock dates `[ex]`, and report both ways any result that flips between the two values. Classify unconfirmed early windows `BROKER_SESSION_UNCONFIRMED` `[cg]`.

| Sat–Sun 09:50/10:00–19:00 | Weekend session, no clearing, no perpetual exit, limited contract list | Unavailable until 11/12, then available | Perpetual-leg exits impossible regardless of availability; weekend signals for such pairs = `MARKET_CLOSED_LEG` |

Reports `ge`, `gr`, `mm` additionally propose "no new entries in the last 30 minutes before close"; retained as `[PROPOSAL]`.

### 2.3 Three kinds of "spread support" — execution atomicity ≠ margin offset ≠ broker risk indivisibility

### 2.4 Data: what "now" means on the free stack

- **Delay.** ISS real-time requires subscription; delayed data (15 min) is free `[ar W3]`, `[cg S6]`, `[gs S9]`, `[mm 4–6]`. The delay is defined as "не менее 15 минут" and was measured at 15 min 02–03 s by three independent probes `[R04 §1, §7.1–7.2]`. Free refresh minimum is 15 seconds for the delayed display; paid feeds refresh at 1 second `[ex]`, `[mm 4]`. Do not equate refresh rate with latency or permission `[ex]`.
- **No order book, no bid/ask history.** Anonymous ISS provides no order book and no bid/ask history at all, and the day's trade tape only for the current day `[R04 §1 point 3]`. ISS documentation lists candles, current order books and historical trading results, but that list does not establish a retrospective timestamped bid/ask tape, queue depth or a past free minute entitlement `[ex]`. ISS manuals state order books and historical data are only available to paid subscribers (2016 wording; current scope to verify) `[gs S10]`. MOEX's spread archive gives daily bid/offer, high/low, volume and trade counts — usable only as a daily sanity bound `[gs S21]`.
- **Fields and timestamps.** Candle fields are `open, close, high, low, value, volume, begin, end`, with `value = 0` for futures `[R04 §1 point 1]`. Candle interval codes include 1, 10, 60 and 24 (one day) `[gs S20]`. Timestamps must be kept separate: exchange event time (`TIME`/`TRADETIME`), record-update time (`UPDATETIME`), system time (`SYSTIME`), margin-calculation time (`IMTIME`), candle bucket bounds (`begin`/`end`), and local `received_at`/`ingested_at` `[R04 §7.3]`. A candle's `end` is a bucket boundary, not a trade time. Distinguish event timestamp, publication timestamp and collection timestamp `[ex]`.
- **`CLOSE ≠ SETTLEPRICE`** in all 16 checked SiZ6 days `[R04, cited in R07 §10 point 3]`; settlement prices are published 19:00–20:00 after determination and are **not** an executable quote at candle close. Booking settlement as a fill is leakage pathway 8 in R09 §4.2.
- **Rights.** Any programmatic (non-display) use, including recording and systematisation, is contract-only, and the ISS page restricts use to familiarisation without profit extraction `[R04 §1 point 4]`, `[gs S11]`, `[ex]`. **Until MOEX/broker written clarification, an automated screener that systematically records ISS data sits in a legal-risk zone.**
- **Finest defensible free structure** (the answer to the task's research question): M1 is the finest *free* timeframe and is diagnostics/raw material; the decision layer that free data can defend is daily, with an hourly/10-minute robustness layer; monitoring cadence 10-minute polling behind the 15-minute lag with an action threshold of ≥20–30 minutes; pre-action truth is the **broker's** bid/ask, not ISS `[R04 §9]`. `cg` and `ds` reached the same conclusion from the other direction: a delayed public quote screen cannot justify a 3–90 minute execution simulation, and the finest free defensible timeframe remains unresolved without a documented data-coverage check `[cg §2.3]`, `[ds §1]`. Any replay that fills signals off 1-minute candle prices at signal time contradicts the evidence base it is built on `[ar §0 point 2]`.
- **Analytical/demo products.** MOEX's analytical products deliver data at five-minute intervals with a delay and explicit demo-status disclaimers; the mobile app likewise shows delayed data `[mm 7–8]`. These are display products, not a licensed historical feed, and cannot be used to justify an executable replay.
- **Paid options noted, not endorsed.** ALGOPACK sells richer order-book statistics — real-time candles, order books and trades every 10 s, "Super Candles" for futures — and a MOEX open-positions page reportedly becomes paid at 600 RUB/month (year to verify) `[gs S25–S26]`. This is a candidate small justified one-off spend, to be decided under R04 `[gs §8]`.

### 2.5 Costs: the algebra the revalidation gate must run

All formulas from R02 Appendix B / §9.1, snapshot rates as of 2026-10-07 `[ar §2.4]`:

- **Exchange + clearing fee** per contract-side (taker, anonymous): `FutFee = Round(Round(|FutPrice| × Round(W/R;5);2) × (Base_MOEX + Base_NCC);2)`; maker = 0; scalper (open+close same day) = 0.5 × FutFee; calendar-spread anonymous = `T × F × (1 − 0.2)` during the marketing period. Taker rates by group: FX 0.00462%, index 0.0066%, commodity 0.0132%, interest-rate 0.0165%, equity 0.0198% of contract value `[R02 §1.1 point 3, §5.2]`.
- **Broker fee per contract-side:** VTB **0 RUB until 2026-12-31 inclusive, 1 RUB from 2027-01-01** `[R02 §1.1 points 1–2]`. A 90-day pilot started after mid-October 2026 straddles this boundary, so the cost model is **two-period** `[R02 §1.2 point 5]`. BCS "Trader" 1.20→0.08 RUB by assets/turnover tier + 299 RUB/month in months with trades; BCS "Investor" (0.1% of notional on each sale) is disqualifying for spread trading `[R02 §1.1 point 4]`, corroborated by secondary tariff comparisons `[mm 54–56]`.
- **Round turn and full cost.** For a two-leg pair with q contracts per leg (4q contract-sides): `C_RT = 2q × (b_A + F_A + b_B + F_B)`; `C_full = C_RT + execution friction + allocated incremental fixed fees + event costs (roll/expiry/forced closure) + financing + U(unknown, never zeroed silently)` `[R02 §9.1, Appendix B]`.
- **Friction is the same order as, or larger than, explicit fees:** crossing the full quoted spread on all four executions cost 137.14 RUB vs 38.64 RUB exchange fees per RTS pair round turn (3.5×), 80 vs 7.04 RUB for wheat (11×), but only 0.17–0.76× for Si, MXI, Brent, gold and Sberbank futures in the single end-of-evening-session snapshot of 2026-10-07 `[R02 §10.1]`. R02's measurement protocol — ≥20 trading days of spread/depth observation in the main session before trusting any friction number `[R02 §10.4]` — is adopted into §9.4.
- **Financing.** VTB charges for initial margin not covered by rubles: `(ГО − рубли)⁺ × (key rate + 2%) × T/365` = 16% p.a. at the 14% key rate in force since 2026-07-27 `[R02 §3.5, R07 §3.2]`. Hence R07's two funding models: (a) **cash sleeve** — the whole RUB 30,000 carries the matched-LQDT hurdle (≈14% p.a., ≈690 RUB per 60 days); (b) **pledged LQDT units + small cash VM buffer** — hurdle ≈2–4% p.a. on the sleeve, but VM still needs real rubles and haircuts bind `[R07 §3.2]`. The revalidation gate must state which model it is pricing under; mixing them is the double-count R05 §5 forbids.
- **Benchmark expense.** LQDT's TER is 0.29364%, with a lower expense cap effective 9 June 2025 `[ex]`. Use applicable dated fund valuations/market exit prices; do not deduct embedded fund expenses a second time or treat an indicative value as a guaranteed executable price `[ex]`.
- **Broker sufficiency coefficient.** VTB's KDS (коэффициент достаточности средств) for the futures market is documented with parameter Y = 0.2; forum reproductions indicate X = 0, Y = 0.2 for sufficiency thresholds `[mm 12–14]`. KDS directly constrains when additional positions may be opened or must be reduced and must appear in the capital gate; the forum values are secondary and need confirmation against R01/VTB regulation `[mm 49]`.
- **Where costs are unknown.** Reports without R02 treat exact rates as absent and make `COSTS_UNKNOWN` a valid rejection reason `[cg §5]`; net-advantage thresholds there are illustrative only `[ds §1]`.

### 2.7 Broker mechanics at VTB (and later BCS): order modes, cutoffs, failure modes

**Order types and their documented behaviour.**
- VTB's public help page lists market and limit orders via app, QUIK or trader, and **stop orders via app/QUIK but not via trader** `[ex]`. This is page-level evidence that a stop instruction exists in some channel; it is *not* evidence of reliable triggering behaviour on futures, and no supplied R01 material establishes it `[ar §7.2]`. Disposition: treat protective stops as an **unconfirmed risk control** — no protective-stop logic may be designed into the pilot until confirmed, because an unconfirmed stop is a fictional control that suppresses position-size discipline.
- A **market order that cannot be immediately placed in the trading system is automatically cancelled** `[ds §5.2]`. A market order is therefore an immediate-or-cancel instruction with unknown price, never a guaranteed fill.
- **Limit orders may be partially executed** unless the client instructs otherwise; a partially executed order is considered cancelled only as to the unexecuted part `[ds §5.2]`. A partially filled order creates a smaller unhedged position, not a clean cancellation.
- MOEX derivatives support DAY (in-queue), IOC (immediate or cancel) and FOK (fill or kill) `[mm 28–29]`. General mechanics — a market order's price is not guaranteed, a limit order's execution is not guaranteed, IOC cancels an unfilled remainder, FOK requires immediate full execution — are corroborated by the SEC's investor bulletin, cited as general mechanics only, not as evidence of MOEX/VTB support `[cg]`, `[ex]`.
- **QUIK conditional/stop orders are checked on a server outside the exchange and may fail for technical reasons** `[gs S12]`.
- Since a **2026-07-22** release, app orders can be set to stay live for up to **90 days**, including futures, and orders entered while the market is closed are **queued for the next session** `[gs S13]`. That queueing is a stale-fill hazard: a queued order could fill at the next open on information that is hours old. Such orders must not be used for entries in the virtual or pilot portfolio `[gs §4]`, `[gs §11]`.
- One VTB page states orders can be changed or cancelled **only by phoning traders** `[gs S14]`, which conflicts with the app's "valid until cancel" feature `[gs S13]`; this most plausibly reflects a sanctions-era special regime and is carried as a conflict `[gs §11]`. If cancelling really requires a phone call, the time to cancel must be added to the unhedged interval `[gs §4]`.
- Backup channels are a web version, QUIK and voice orders, highlighted after the **2026-10-11 Runet outage** `[gs S15]` — evidence that channel outages are plausible, not hypothetical.

**Acceptance cutoffs (broker-side constraints on order intake, separate from exchange session times)** `[ds §3.3]`. VTB's public regulation states that, unless otherwise agreed, the bank accepts orders only if sent **no later than 15 minutes before the end of the trading session**, except orders sent through remote access systems; telephone orders sent less than 15 minutes before the end may be accepted only by agreement with the bank's trader; paper/fax orders must arrive at least **one hour** before expiry and are passed to the trader within 15 minutes of receipt and verification. A signal valid at signal-creation may therefore be **unacceptable at order submission** even while the exchange is open → `BROKER_CUTOFF`.

**Risk, margin and forced action.**
- Margin can increase **during or outside trading**, and inadequate funds for margin/variation margin may cause involuntary close `[ex]`, `[gs S2]`.
- VTB's public page on insufficiency of guarantee assets and on a significant bid/ask spread (>10%) describes broker discretion in both situations `[ds §1]`.
- Forced closure chooses instruments "самостоятельно по своему усмотрению" (at the broker's own discretion); the reconstructed VTB closing order places spread components late but not last, and BCS's algorithm (instrument class vs margin-level ordering) is not determinable for a spread without a broker answer `[R01 §0, §11.1 item 16]`. Prices on forced closure are bounded by regulation to no worse than the min/max anonymous-trade prices of the preceding 15 minutes, executed in practice with market orders in possibly thin liquidity `[R01 §11.1]`.
- Restrictive closing times: **16:00 MSK** (Unified Brokerage Account portfolios) and **14:00 MSK** (Derivatives portfolio); discretionary forced closure with **no guaranteed grace period or pre-closure warning** `[R01 §0 point 4, §7]`.
- **Settlement mode:** many instruments including derivatives are in T+1 settlement — executed today, settled next business day `[mm 10–11]`. This affects when cash/variation margin actually hits the account, not matching-engine latency, and it is why LQDT sale proceeds arriving T+1 cannot be counted in the cash path.
- Reported KPUR/qualified status is not account-level confirmation `[ex]`, `[ar §15.2 U1]`.

**Latency components that are small but must still be logged** `[mm §2.1, §3.1]`, `[gr §1]`: exchange matching infrastructure is sub-millisecond (MOEX FAST reports average update latency <300 μs for some markets); QUIK-based routing is sub-second with anecdotal 2–3 s GUI/API delays; Telegram per-message latency is tens of milliseconds to seconds in low load and longer in large channels; brokers do not publish per-order manual latency, so broker internal delay is an opaque but comparatively small component. Rare but serious anomalies are reported on FORTS — "hung" market orders that fail to execute for up to an hour while the market trades `[mm 37]`; these are minority events that matter for extreme-scenario design. Manual order entry in non-finance corporate settings has been measured at roughly **6 minutes per order** and hundreds of hours per year in aggregate `[mm 61]`; trading-specific sources report similar orders of magnitude `[mm 20, 35, 62]`.

**Infrastructure controls that exist at the exchange level:** Cancel-on-Disconnect (COD) and kill-switch functions, sponsored-access pre-trade risk checks `[mm 32–34]`; cross-order control prohibiting self-crossing at the same TIN `[mm 59]`; order parameter restrictions and rejection codes `[mm 57–58]`; price limits and monitoring zones `[mm 39–40]`.

### 2.8 Accounting and risk rails (R05/R07 embedded provisional policy)

**Primary metric.** `V(t)` = strategy-enabled wealth; `B(t)` = matched no-pilot LQDT counterfactual; `X = V − B`, `X(0) = 0` before incremental test costs; `H = max(0, past X)`; `D = H − X`; shortfall `S = max(0, −X)` `[R05 §2.4; R09 §9.2]`, `[gr §5]`, `[ex]`, `[cg §7]`. Both paths mirror genuine external flows and exogenous core actions; **B does not mirror pilot-induced core sales/liquidations**; core-sale proceeds are a change of asset form, not the economic loss; retained eligible collateral keeps earning in both paths and must not be charged twice `[R05 §2, §4]`. If core LQDT is used as collateral for futures margin, its continuing yield is credited to V and B identically and must not be added as strategy income in X `[gr §5]`. VM is a transfer/realisation of the same P&L, not a second profit `[ex]`. An external top-up must be reflected symmetrically so a contribution cannot improve X `[cg §7]`, `[ex]`.

**Scale discipline.** Thresholds are evaluated in cumulative RUB, never as a percentage of the >40 million core (≈0.075% — a meaningless denominator) `[R05 §0 point 9, R09 §9.1]`. Reconcile the sleeve ledger to the whole-account wealth difference X through the core-impact bridge of R05 §4.3; never compare a 30k sleeve against the entire core `[cg §7]`, `[ex]`, `[ds §6.3]`.

**Marks.** One frozen price hierarchy; conservative flattening values (long at bid, short at ask) for trigger evaluation; settlement marks reported separately from fills; both legs and the LQDT counterfactual marked at the same timestamp; every open position marked net of estimated exit costs `[R09 §9.1–9.2]`, `[ds §6.3]`, `[ex]`.

**Action rules as delegated** `[R05 §7.1; R07 §3.1 P2–P5]`, `[gr §5]`, `[cg §7]`, `[ex]`:

| Trigger | Value | Mandatory action |
|---|---|---|
| Warning | `D ≥ 5,000` | Warn and reassess; re-calibrate signal parameters and slippage assumptions |
| Action | `D ≥ 10,000` | Suspend new entries/additions, reduce risk in an orderly way, require explicit user review before resuming — **even if X remains positive** |
| Action | `X ≤ −10,000` | Cease new risk, start an orderly exit; any separately approved funding at this trigger is **only to reach flat**, not to await recovery |
| Breach | `X < −15,000` | Boundary breach, not permitted overshoot; complete protocol shutdown |
| Emergency funding | lifetime gross `E ≤ 15,000` (gross contributions ≤ 45,000) | Separate approval **and** verified credit timing for every use; only to avert disorderly liquidation or reach flat; never fresh exposure, averaging down, restart or loss reset; returns/withdrawals do not restore the cap; broker-induced core use counts once and is an incident if unapproved |
| Formal review | 90 calendar days | Review without resetting X, H, losses or consumed funding; does not itself force every open position to expire |

Earlier broker, naked-leg, stale-data, delivery or communication danger **overrides monetary triggers** `[cg §7]`, `[ex]`. No instantaneous or guaranteed fills are assumed.

**Entry/continuation gates** `[R07 §4.1]`: `X − L_stress − u ≥ −15,000` under **joint** shocks (price gap + spread-offset removal + delay, not additive best cases), with `u = max(5,000, 2.5 × σ_delay(book) + C_exit)` `[PROPOSAL]`; plus an **independent cash-path gate** — free rubles ≥ stressed VM over the delay window + fees + settlement-lag reserve, because VM is money-only and LQDT sale proceeds arrive T+1 `[R01 §0 point 3]`; plus a gross-margin ceiling (33% `[PROPOSAL]`, delegated default 50%) and a **cluster gate**: pairs sharing a factor (RUB carry, same family, shared leg) count as one cluster capped at 60% of the book σ-budget `[R07 §4.1 gates 3–4]`. The 5,000 stress-buffer heuristic and the 50% starting-margin-use hypothesis are **test candidates, not approved requirements** `[cg §5]`, `[ds §13]`, `[gr §3]`. The 5,000 distance from action trigger to outer boundary is not proved sufficient for gaps and delays.

**Registered stress scenarios S1–S6** `[R07 §4.2]`, reused as replay scenarios in §9: overnight gap + margin escalation + slow human; LQDT haircut shock (KPUR 8% → KNUR 18% at VTB); one-leg liquidity evaporation; forced-closure path; funding-timing failure; market closure/suspension.

**Tax.** Numeric defaults are before investor-specific taxes, after trading/funding costs; tax sensitivity is shown separately; only the loss of the LQDT long-term holding deduction is excluded from the tax layer; investor tax residence/account treatment is unknown `[R05 §9]`, `[cg §7]`, `[ex]`.

### 2.9 Validation rails (R09)

- **Point-in-time** means *actually available* at decision time, with event / publication / receipt / vintage timestamps kept apart; a later-edited page does not prove historical availability `[R09 §4.1; R04 §7.3]`, `[ex]`.
- The evaluation object for overlapping, multi-day positions is the **daily portfolio ledger**, not per-trade P&L `[R09 §2.3]`. Purge width = actual label/holding overlap; embargo where dependence requires; every transform (hedge ratio, z-score moments, volatility thresholds, pair ranking) fitted only inside the permitted information set, verified by a truncation/old-signal invariance test `[R09 §5.3–5.4, §4.2 pathway 5]`.
- **Trials accounting:** the wide search is logged in full; the primary delay/cost scenario and decision threshold are frozen before the confirmation block is opened once; delay-scenario *selection* after seeing results converts scenarios into trials and must be counted `[R09 §6, §5.2]`. A large exploratory search increases selection-bias risk; the Deflated Sharpe Ratio corrects for selection bias under multiple testing and non-normality but **cannot compensate for false fills or missing market data** `[cg S10]`; conventional holdouts may be unreliable when many backtests are selected (Probability of Backtest Overfitting) `[ex]`.
- **No numerical win probabilities are displayed by default** `[R09 §11.1]`; confidence is communicated as evidence grades (§11).
- Sparse outcomes may legitimately end "inconclusive"; the 90-day pilot validates operations, not profitability `[R09 §1 points 7–8]`.
- General methodological caution: hypothetical performance may not adequately account for bid/ask spreads, margin calls, liquidity and actual trading conditions `[cg S11, CFTC advisory]` — cited as method only, not as governing the MOEX/VTB project. Simulated brokerage cannot guarantee real market conditions `[cg S13]`.

### 3.2 Two ambiguities that are flagged, not resolved

**Ambiguity A — timezone and the emergency carve-out.** The user is located in Gdańsk; the exchange clock is MSK (UTC+3 year-round), while Gdańsk is CEST (UTC+2) until late October, then CET (UTC+1). "Midnight to 11–12" in Gdańsk time is 01:00–13:00 MSK in summer — an even deeper bite into the main session. `[ASSUMPTION]` All user times in this report are read as **MSK**; confirmation is requested (§15, Q1). Separately, (a) and (b) are in tension: a hard blackout 00:00–11/12 cannot simultaneously contain a reliable early-morning emergency response. Two readings are modelled, with the conservative one primary:

- **A-hard (primary for all sizing and gates):** no human action is possible 00:00–11:00/12:00. Emergency alerts may be *sent* but are queued unseen; nothing in position sizing, margin planning or the virtual portfolio may credit a wake-up.
- **A-soft (sensitivity only):** an emergency push can interrupt the blackout "early in the morning" with an unknown, longer latency. Modelled in scenario F2 with wake + revalidation + action sampled from the slow tail; results under A-soft may never be used to pass an entry gate, only to describe incident outcomes.

**Ambiguity B — what counts as "one trade".** See §3.3.

### 3.3 Counting units, quota proposals, and the cost of the literal reading

Units used for all accounting and burden metrics `[ar §3.3]`:

| Unit | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Leg Order (LO) | One buy or sell order in one contract (one contract-side) | Buy 1 GLZ6 |
| Order Action (OA) | One submit / cancel / replace instruction on one order | Cancel an unfilled GLZ6 limit |
| Manual Action Episode (MAE) | One continuous human intervention serving one idea-level purpose, containing ≥1 OA/LO | "Enter CNY pair": submit leg-1 limit, submit leg-2 limit, one cancel/replace = 1 MAE, 2 LO, 3 OA |
| Idea Entry / Idea Exit | The MAE(s) that open / close one spread position | Entry MAE + (days later) exit MAE |
| Risk Action | Hedge completion, naked-leg unwind, margin-driven de-risking, forced-closure response | Never counts against the *entry* quota |

**Primary proposal — Q-IDEA** `[PROPOSAL, requires user confirmation]`: at most **1 new-idea entry MAE per calendar (MSK trading) day**, soft — an occasional second entry MAE is logged as `QUOTA_SOFT_EXCEEDED`, not concealed and not treated as a breach; leg orders inside the episode are burden-counted, not quota-counted. Risk Actions, hedge completions, cancels and exits are **exempt from Q-IDEA** but fully burden-counted; exempting them is what prevents the quota itself from manufacturing naked-leg risk. This matches "можно чуть больше" as a soft cap and implements the instruction to distinguish risk exits from new-idea quotas `[ar §3.3]`, `[mm §8.4]`, `[ds §6.1]`.

**Literal reading — Q-LEG (1 LO/day), costed honestly** `[ar §3.3]`: a two-leg entry needs 2 LO on 2 different days, so the first leg sits unhedged overnight, violating the rule that naked-leg danger overrides monetary triggers and R07's immediate hedge-or-unwind rule `[R07 §7]`. Entry + exit = 4 LO = 4 days minimum per round trip, during which the position cannot be exited in one day either. Under Q-LEG the only coherent entries are single-order instruments — the exchange CS instrument for calendar pairs — whose VTB accessibility is unproven. **Q-LEG therefore ≈ "no two-leg manual pilot at VTB".** If the user confirms Q-LEG literally, the recommendation flips to calendar-pairs-only via the exchange CS instrument, contingent on VTB access proof, with all other families virtual-only `[ar §13.2]`.

**Alternative quota designs proposed by other reports** (all `[PROPOSAL]`, none asserted):

- Sweep **0, 1 and 2** accepted new-idea episodes per day as alternatives rather than silently selecting one live quota; always report what the daily maximum means in every result `[cg §11]`.
- At most **2** new-idea entries per day; at most **1 idea being legged at a time**; risk exits, hedge completion and emergency de-risking never use the quota but are counted separately; no new entries in the last 30 minutes before close or in self-declared quiet hours `[gs §10]`.
- Limit the virtual portfolio to a maximum of **1 entry attempt per day** during the initial 30 days to keep manual error rates near zero before contemplating automation `[mm §10.2]`.
- Manual execution is sustainable for at most **1–3 interventions per day**; a daily "trade" defined as one complete round-trip spread intervention (entry and subsequent exit) or an active multi-leg adjustment `[ge §7]`.
- Count a "new trade" as one uniquely authorised spread entry, while separately capping and reporting individual order submissions and all risk interventions without suppressing necessary exits `[ex]`.
- Report **three separate metrics** — idea-level, order-level, intervention-level — and do not collapse them into a single "daily trade" number until the user defines which one is the constraint `[ds §11.2]`, `[mm §10.2]`.

The virtual portfolio is built under Q-IDEA as primary and additionally computes two shadow counters (not portfolios): entries Q-LEG would have blocked, and entries a no-blackout counterfactual would have taken — reported as counts and RUB opportunity-cost bounds, never as alternative P&L curves to choose from `[ar §8.6]`.

## 4. Timestamp ontology

Every event carries the following timestamps, stored separately and never overwritten by later revisions `[ar §4]`, extended with the field-level detail of `[cg §3]`, `[ds §2]`, `[gs §1]`, `[ex]`, `[gr §1]`, `[ge §1]`. Store both UTC and MSK rendering with clock uncertainty and source IDs; use a monotonic clock for measured durations and synchronise host clocks; keep exchange trading-day labels separate from wall-clock dates; enforce causal ordering (`information_available ≤ evaluation ≤ signal`; fills cannot precede acceptance); record manual reaction timestamps at the finest honest precision rather than falsely implying milliseconds `[cg §3]`, `[ex]`. The decision-relevant clock for availability is MSK `[ASSUMPTION §3.2]`.

| # | Timestamp | Definition | Source of truth | Failure it exposes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| t0 | `t_info_event` | Exchange event time of the last market observation actually used (trade/quote/candle bucket end) | `TIME`/`TRADETIME`, candle `end` | Using a bucket end as if it were receipt time |
| t1 | `t_info_published` | When the source published the observation | `UPDATETIME`/`SYSTIME` | Later-edited pages masquerading as historical availability |
| t2 | `t_info_received` | Local receipt/ingestion instant, with instrument, source, fields, sequence/version and quality flags | Screener log `received_at` | Makes the 15-min ISS lag measurable: t2 − t0 (on free ISS, `t2 ≥ t0 + 15 min`) |
| t3a/t3b | `t_eval_start` / `t_eval` | Model evaluation start / completion on the frozen input set (input hash, model version and data watermark recorded) | Screener log | Assigning a signal before its calculation finished; re-running evaluation on revised data and back-dating the signal |
| t4 | `t_signal` | Signal created and registered (`signal_id`, version, thesis/episode id, legs, bounds, expiry, exact data-snapshot ids) | Signal registry | **Signal birth. It cannot be refreshed by re-notification** |
| t5 | `t_notif_sent` | Bot API request sent; response result, message id, retry count, Telegram's returned send timestamp | Bot/API log | Screener→Telegram outage. Telegram's `Message.date` is the send time, **not** a user-read time; delivery is not guaranteed, especially when the user is offline; `retry_after` governs flood-control waits `[cg S9]` |
| t6 | `t_notif_delivered` | Delivered to the user's device (server ack / client receipt where obtainable) | Telegram receipt if obtainable, else `UNKNOWN_PENDING` — never assumed equal to t5 | Push outage, device offline, VPN failure |
| t7 | `t_seen` | User actually reads it | Explicit in-Telegram acknowledgement (button/tap or reply) | The gap t7 − t6 is unobservable without acknowledgement design; shadow mode must instrument it. Standard bot notifications must not be assumed to provide a read receipt `[cg §3]` |
| t8 | `t_decide` | User decision (accept / reject / defer) recorded, with self-report flag and uncertainty | User ack payload | Decision latency t8 − t7, separable from notification latency |
| t9 | `t_reval` | Fresh broker bid/ask for **both** legs captured with per-quote timestamp/age, plus session state, margin/cash, model state, recomputed costs and edge | Broker terminal/API snapshot | Executing against ISS-delayed prices instead of executable ones |
| t10 | `t_order_submit_i` | Leg order i submitted, with client/order ids, order type, price cap, quantity | Terminal log | Manual entry latency t10 − t8 (opening the app, selecting the contract, entering volume/price, authentication, submitting) |
| t11 | `t_broker_accept_i` / `t_exchange_accept_i` | Broker acknowledgement, then exchange registration | Order reports | Assuming acceptance from submission — **a click is not order acceptance**; missing acknowledgements mean `UNKNOWN_PENDING`, not an inferred cancellation `[cg §3]`, `[ex]` |
| t12 | `t_fill_i,k` (per partial) | Each partial fill of leg i with price/qty/fee | Trade reports | Partial-fill and queue realism |
| t13 | `t_hedge_complete` | Instant the spread becomes hedged (both legs at target ratio) or is unwound flat | Derived: last required t_fill | **Unhedged interval = t13 − first t_fill** |
| t14 | `t_cancel_req` / `t_cancel_ack` | Cancellation requested / confirmed; residual fills after a cancel request | Order reports | Trading on an order believed cancelled; a cancel request does not prove the order is cancelled |
| t15 | `t_mark` / `t_settlement` / `t_reconcile` | Synchronised mark instant for V/B valuation (price hierarchy + leg ages stored); official settlement; matched-counterfactual reconciliation | Valuation log | `CLOSE ≠ SETTLE` contamination; mixing mark-to-market, cash funding and realised P&L; double-counting VM |
| t16 | `t_funding_req` / `t_funding_credit` | Emergency funding approved / actually credited | Bank/broker evidence | Approval ≠ arrival (T+1 LQDT proceeds, cut-offs) `[R07 S5]` |
| t17 | `t_expire` | Idea validity end, fixed at t4/t_signal creation | Signal record | Countdowns started from delivery time instead of signal creation |

**Derived latencies reported for every signal:** data lag `t2−t0`; system/processing latency `Δt_sys = (t3−t0) + (t4−t3) + (t5−t4)` `[gr §1]`; notification latency `t6−t5`; **human reaction `t8−t7`** (the 3–90 minute quantity of the task — *measured*, not assumed); order routing `t10−t8`; exchange execution `t12−t11`; inter-leg unhedged latency `Δt_hedge = t13 − t12_leg1`; total event-to-action `t10−t0` (documented range on free data 18–105 min *before* blackout effects) `[R04 §1.6]`, `[ar §4]`, `[gr §1]`.

**Outages are separate states, not long delays** `[ar §4]`, `[ds §2]`, `[gs §1]`, `[ex]`, `[gr §1]`:

| State | Meaning | Distinct remedy |
|---|---|---|
| `OUTAGE_DATA` | Feed stale/disconnected; quote ages grow. Suspend signal generation if `(t_now − t_info) > Δt_max_stale` (candidate 3 min) `[gr §1]` | Feed redundancy; staleness gate |
| `OUTAGE_MODEL` | Screener down; no t3/t4 produced. On recovery, inputs are re-fetched fresh, never replayed from the outage window as if live. Detect via heartbeat pings (e.g. every 60 s) `[gr §1]` | Model redundancy |
| `OUTAGE_NOTIFY` | t5 without t6; API rate-limiting (HTTP 429) or timeouts with a retry cap (e.g. 180 s) before `DELIVERY_FAILED` `[gr §1]` | Notification escalation, alternate channel |
| `OUTAGE_HUMAN` | Blackout (§3); the alert is read only after the allowed limit, so the idea is unexecuted, not merely late | Availability calendar; generation-window design |
| `OUTAGE_BROKER` | Terminal/API unreachable, VPN failure, phone queue. VTB's own app guidance recommends disabling VPN and QUIK access is key-based, so an active-VPS/VPN stack is a named fragility `[R01 §2, R01-44]`; the 2026-10-11 Runet outage is a worked precedent `[gs S15]` | Backup channels (web, QUIK, voice) |
| `OUTAGE_MARKET` | Halt/suspension/closed session; MOEX suspended derivatives intraday as recently as 2025-09-11 `[R09 §3.1, cited in R07 S6]` | Session calendar, halt monitoring |

Each outage interval is logged with start/end; signals whose validity window intersects an outage are disposed by the expiry rules of §6.3, not silently extended. Model-down and data-down are separate health states `[cg §3]`. Conflating outages with human reaction time makes the delay distribution uninterpretable `[ds §2]`.

## 5. Delay scenarios

### 5.1 What is known and what is not

Given by the task/user: manual reaction 3–90 min, typical median ≈5–15 min; **distribution unknown**. Added by evidence: free-data lag ≥15 min before the human even sees an event; margin machinery runs from 09:00 regardless of the human; forced closure is discretionary with no guaranteed warning `[ar §5.1]`. **No probability distribution over reaction time is claimed anywhere in this report.** Everything below is a deterministic stress lattice plus alternative seeded designs and regime cases; probabilities enter only after forward calibration (§5.5). General manual-trading commentary suggests seconds to minutes for focused professionals, so the project's 90-minute upper tail is conservative but realistic for a side project `[mm §2.1]`.

### 5.3 Alternative seeded designs (menu, not a fitted law)

These are mutually exclusive candidate designs proposed across the source reports. All satisfy the stated 3–90 minute range and the 5–15 minute median band; none is asserted to be true. Freeze one primary plus a registered sensitivity set at preregistration.

**Mixture designs** (probabilities over reaction minutes):
- Centred: 3 min 5%, 5 min 20%, 10 min 45%, 15 min 20%, 30 min 9%, 90 min 1% — median 10 `[cg A]`.
- Heavier tail: 3 min 5%, 5 min 15%, 10 min 35%, 15 min 20%, 30 min 15%, 60 min 8%, 90 min 2% — median 10 `[cg B]`.
- A: 3/10/90 with 0.2/0.6/0.2; B: 5/15/60/90 with 0.35/0.40/0.20/0.05; C: 3/8/30/90 with 0.15/0.45/0.25/0.15 `[ex]`.
- D1 attentive log-normal truncated (median 6, p90 ≈20); D2 typical (median 10, p90 ≈40); D3 heavy-tail mixture 70% fast / 30% across 30–90 (median 14, p90 ≈75) `[gs]`.

**Deterministic grids and regimes:**
- Fixed stress grid: run every eligible idea at 3, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60 and 90 minutes, one delay at a time, showing outcome as a function of delay without probability weights `[cg C]`.
- Three-regime design: F (fast, 3–10 min), T (typical, 10–30 min with mass at 15–20), S (slow/distracted, 30–90 min). Evaluate every signal under at least F and T; S measures how often signals are invalidated before entry `[mm §3.2]`.
- Scenario bands by cause: calm fast read 3–7 (median 5); typical workday 5–20 (median 12); slow but valid 25–60 (median 35); late tail 60–90 (median 75); outage/no decision (no t8 at all) `[ds §3.1]`. With modest weights on the first three and tails on the last two, the overall median sits in the 5–15 band while the range reaches 90. The distribution is deliberately **not** assumed lognormal, exponential or uniform.
- Regime matrix by market state: normal flow (calm mid-session 11:00–16:00, tight spreads) 3–10 min; high volatility/news 15–45 min; session transition near clearing boundaries 30–90 min; operational absence 90+ min `[ge §2]`; and a five-row variant adding "volatility spike delay" (15 min in an extreme regime with the bid-ask widened ~300% and the book thinned) and "session boundary cross" (25 min intersecting a clearing session) `[gr §2]`.

**Correlated and clustered designs:**
- D4 regime-conditional: median 8 when calm, 25 in the top volatility quintile, the evening session, or the first 15 minutes after the open `[gs]`.
- D5 adverse ordering: same marginal distribution as D2, but the longest delays are assigned to the signals with the highest volatility / widest spread `[gs]`.
- D6 clustered: second and later alerts within 30 minutes of a previous one get +10–30 minutes `[gs]`.
- Correlated adverse regime: conditional on high trailing volatility and/or poor trailing liquidity, shift more trials to 15/30/60/90 minutes **and** widen the adverse-price and exit-cost stress at the same time; thresholds must use only information available at decision time `[cg D]`.
- Outage/no-response: signal-generation, Telegram-send, data-feed, broker-access and user-unavailable episodes are separate event types; a message delayed across a closed session may not become actionable until a later eligible session and must then be revalidated from scratch. **Do not truncate outage duration to 90 minutes just because normal reactions were described that way** `[cg E]`, `[gs]`, `[ds S5]`, `[ar F1]`.

### 5.4 Correlation structure (assumed mechanisms, to be calibrated)

Three mechanisms are pre-registered as hypotheses, not facts `[ar §5.3]`:
- **M1 Morning clustering** — gap/volatility events concentrate at the 06:50–09:00 open and the 09:00 margin issuance, i.e. inside the blackout.
- **M2 Attention competition** — stressed markets generate more alerts exactly when each alert takes longer to process (quota plus MAE serialisation).
- **M3 Liquidity co-movement** — the pairs with the widest standing spreads (GL legs 100–108 ticks) widen further in stress (R02 stress = doubled width), so the tail-delay scenario must also carry the tail-friction scenario. In replay, P-D/C1 pair a 90-minute delay with doubled spreads and, for GL, limit-order-only fills with explicit non-fill probability mass — delay and cost are never stressed independently.

A formal expression of the same intuition `[gr §2]`: expected executable price drift over human delay `Δt` is `ΔP_exec = μ_drift · Δt + γ · σ_t · √Δt + ε_t`, where `μ_drift` is the directional trend of the spread leg, `γ > 0` is the adverse-selection coefficient under informed order flow, and `ε_t` is order-book micro-noise. As `σ_t` rises, the probability that the executable spread breaches the minimum entry threshold increases non-linearly, producing a high rejection rate for delayed manual orders.

**Correlation design discipline** `[cg §4]`, `[ds §3.2]`, `[mm §3.3]`: classify market state at action time only from data already available then (trailing realised volatility, displayed spread, depth, quote update rate, recent traded volume). For high-volatility or illiquid states, run both longer reaction delays and adverse execution assumptions. Do not use future realised volatility to assign a scenario retrospectively. If best bid/ask/depth is unavailable, do not claim to have modelled liquidity — use an explicitly coarse stress proxy. A simple default is to stratify recorded delays by volatility tercile and quoted-spread tercile at t0 and sample within strata `[ds §3.2]`. Tag each signal with its session segment (pre-open, main, clearing-adjacent, evening, weekend) because fills may be impossible or constrained around clearing `[mm §3.3]`.

### 5.5 Seeding, closed sessions, and later calibration without look-ahead

- **Seeding.** A master seed is registered before any run (e.g. derived from the preregistration hash; the literal value recorded in the run manifest), with one delay draw per (signal, seed) and fixed seeds recorded per run `[ar §8.7]`, `[gs §2]`. Use **common random-number seeds across strategy variants** so a performance difference is not caused by different delay draws `[cg §4]`. Preserve both the deterministic grid and the stochastic scenario labels. Random delay must be drawn without looking at the signal's future return.
- **Availability calendars** (quiet hours, workdays) are separate inputs, not part of the delay draw `[gs §2]`.
- **Closed markets.** If `t6 + delay` lands after session end, inside clearing, or on a weekend for a contract not in the weekend list, the outcome is `MARKET_CLOSED`. It is **not** converted into a next-open fill unless a separate, declared next-session rule exists `[gs §2]`, `[ds §4.1]`. At an exchange closure, queue only if the particular broker permits it and the instruction remains valid; otherwise expire — never fill on reopening at Friday prices `[ex]`. Overnight/weekend gaps and next-session opening prices need separate stress treatment `[cg §9]`.
- **Calibration.** In shadow operation every signal logs t0…t17. The reaction-time model used to evaluate window *k+1* is fitted **only** on completed observations from windows ≤ k (truncation invariance `[R09 §4.2 pathway 5]`); blackout observations are fitted as a separate regime (availability at signal birth is known at t4, so conditioning on it is causal); the fitted object is a scenario *weighting*. If headline conclusions flip between the unweighted lattice and the calibrated weighting, both are reported and the difference is attributed to the delay model, not to the strategy `[ar §5.4]`. Fit only from prospectively logged notification/read/order events, split by session, weekday, volatility and outages, estimated on training dates only, with a frozen holdout `[ex]`. Report empirical percentiles by time of day and market regime with sample counts and uncertainty; if telemetry is missing, report bounds and "unknown", not zero delay `[cg §4]`. Report how much the estimate changes as data accumulates `[gs §2]`. The 20-trading-day spread/depth measurement programme of R02 §10.4 runs concurrently so friction calibration ages at the same rate.

## 6. Revalidation after delay

A signal is an *idea with an expiry*, not an order. Between t4 and any order, the gate below runs **in full, at t9, on broker prices** — and again immediately before each submitted leg if legging `[gs §3]`, `[ex]`. Cheap, decisive checks (session, expiry, funds) precede expensive market-data rechecks `[ds §4]`. Any failure produces a reason code (§10), never a silent retry. Recompute the whole idea, not just the signal timestamp `[cg §5]`.

| **G1 Freshness** | Both legs' broker bid/ask age ≤ `AGE_MAX`; leg timestamp skew ≤ `SKEW_MAX`. ISS-derived inputs are thesis context only, never execution price. | Signal TTL / max alert age | `min(model TTL, time to session boundary, time to registered event, capital-reservation TTL)`; candidate model TTL 30 min attended, expiring at blackout start if unattended `[ar §6.3]`; fail-closed 90 min from alert as an operational design candidate `[cg §5]`; `min(t_sig + 90 min, session end − buffer)` `[gs §3]`; 45–60 min tentative for this pilot, tested for sensitivity `[mm §4.1]`; strategy-family-specific values `[mm §12]` |
| Edge-decay invalidation | spread retraced > 50% of expected reversion `[ge §3]`; > 40% toward fair value `[gr §3]`; > x% of expected reversion or > y standard deviations against the idea, x/y calibrated `[gs §3]`; `Edge_exec < Edge_min` `[ar §6.2]`, `[ds §4.2]` |
| Gross-margin ceiling | 33% of remaining envelope `[R07 §4.1 gate 3 — PROPOSAL, tightened from the delegated 50%]`; 1.5× margin multiplier on each leg plus an unrealised-loss buffer `[gr §3]`; 20% VM buffer on top of required margin `[ge §5]` |
| Entry blackout before close | no new entries in the last 30 minutes of any session `[mm §4.1]`, `[gs §10]`; none within a declared buffer of the 23:50 clearing `[gr §1]` |
| No-entry price-limit rule | reject if either leg is near its daily price limit; limits do not widen automatically in the evening session `[gs S17]` |

### 6.2 Price/state invalidation (any one ⇒ signal dead in the current version)

Spread executable price beyond the signal's limit bound; `Edge_exec < Edge_min` at t9; funding sign flip or K1/K2/spec change since t4; either leg halted, unsynchronised, or quote age breached; margin parameters changed (GO snapshot differs from the signal-time snapshot beyond tolerance); session boundary crossed (revalidation would complete after 23:50 or inside a clearing window); capital state changed (another signal reserved the envelope); X/D state moved into suspension; event embargo entered; depth below the minimum executable quantity `[ar §6.2]`, `[ds §4.2]`, `[mm §4.2]`.

Three invalidation families, stated in executable rather than narrative terms `[ds §4.2]`:
- **Leg-price invalidation** — either leg's executable price at t9 is worse than the signal's worse-case bound for that leg. The bound is set at t4 from the then-visible bid/ask plus a pre-specified slippage allowance.
- **Net-advantage invalidation** — the revalidated net advantage falls below the LQDT threshold after costs, even if both legs are individually within bounds.
- **Depth invalidation** — visible depth at the bound is insufficient for at least the minimum executable quantity; the signal is invalid or resized.

Invalidation is a *state*, recorded with the triggering field's old and new values — the audit trail that lets later analysis distinguish "the idea was wrong" from "the idea was stale" `[ar §6.2]`.

### 6.3 Expiry and reissue (idempotency)

- Entry permission is **perishable**; the position's holding horizon must not be conflated with the alert TTL `[cg §5]`.
- Each signal carries `TTL = min(model TTL, time to next session boundary, time to registered event, capital-reservation TTL)`, fixed at creation, not counted down from delivery `[ar §6.3]`, `[ds §4.2]`. On TTL lapse → `EXPIRED`, reservation released.
- **A fresh notification, a Telegram retry, a re-run on the same input vintage, or a delayed read cannot resurrect it** `[ar]`, `[cg]`, `[ds]`, `[gs]`, `[ex]`, `[mm]`. A fresh signal timestamp cannot renew old quote timestamps `[ex]`.
- **Reissue** is permitted only as a *new version*: a new evaluation on inputs with t2 newer than the previous inputs' t2, a new `signal_id` version linked to its parent (`supersedes`), all gates re-run, and no inheritance of the old signal's queue priority or capital reservation `[ar §6.3]`, `[ds §4.2]`, `[gs §3]`. Reissue should additionally require a distinct strategy episode satisfying its prescribed neutral/reset condition and cooldown `[cg §5]`.
- **Trials accounting:** reissues of one parent count as one trial family by default, per the registry rule frozen at preregistration `[R09 §6.1]`, `[ar §6.3]`.
- Repeated notification of the same version is suppressed by idempotency key `signal_id + version` (§11, Example 6).
- A missed or expired entry remains recorded as such even if the pair later moves favourably; a separate later signal may qualify only under the pre-registered re-entry rule. No "the chart later came back" exception `[cg §5]`, `[ds §9.1–9.2]`.

## 7. Two-leg and partial execution

### 7.1 Mode selection (strict order of preference, each labelled in simulation)

1. `EXEC_ATOMIC_EXCHANGE` — exchange CS instrument, **calendar pairs only**, and only in the labelled sensitivity branch until VTB access is proven (§2.3). Order categories available at the exchange: limit, market, fill-or-kill. Even here, margin reservation equals the two-leg amount, so the stressed-margin gate is unchanged. Remaining risks in this branch: unfilled, partially filled (cross-leg behaviour not documented), stale resting order `[gs §4]`.
2. `EXEC_JOINT_BROKER` — broker indivisible joint order. Documented only for BCS (Appendix No. 6 §5.4.9; special calendar-spread application). Not available to the VTB-first design; reserved for the later BCS stage, again only after account-level confirmation `[ar §7.1]`, `[R01 §5]`.
3. `EXEC_SEQUENTIAL` — **the VTB primary mode**: two independent orders, placed and acknowledged separately, with a real unhedged interval and real partial fills.

Where a native spread is claimed, model its actual accepted order types, net-price increments, reject conditions, expiration rules, available quotes and fees; if any of those is unresolved, the native-spread branch is exploratory only `[cg §6]`.

### 7.2 Sequential protocol `[PROPOSAL — test candidates to be calibrated, not approved rules]`

**Which leg first.** Default: the **less liquid leg first** (for GL: either leg — both quote 100+ ticks wide; for CNY: CRZ6 at 4 ticks vs CNYRUBF at 2 ticks quoted width), because it is the harder fill and determines whether the hedge is feasible; if it fails or slips severely, the transaction aborts before any position is taken in leg 2 `[ar §7.2]`, `[gr §4]`, `[ds §5.2]`. The liquid leg is then placed as a **marketable limit** bounded by the worst acceptable spread. Harris's framing applies (§17): a standing limit order grants a free trading option to the market, and on a 100-tick-wide book that option is expensive — which is why R06's verdict for GL is "limit orders only" *inside* the spread, accepting non-fill risk as the price of not crossing 100 ticks. A stale resting leg is filled exactly when the market has moved against it, which is why resting orders need short lives `[gs §4]`. `ex` adds the caution that leg-first alternatives should be compared using immediately executable depth, expected unhedged stress and liquidity, and that a universal "liquid-leg-first" rule must not be asserted.

**Unhedged interval.** The clock starts at the first `t_fill` and ends at hedge completion or explicit abandonment. Test-candidate tolerances across reports: target hedge completion ≤ 2 min and hard deadline ≤ 5 min attended `[ar §7.2]`; maximum 120 s before an abort protocol `[gr §4]`; abort if the second leg cannot be completed within 3 minutes `[ge §4]`; a named timer `T_h` with a 2-minute worked example `[gs §4]`. These are *attended-execution* parameters, meaningful only once the user is at the terminal. On deadline or bound breach the filled leg is unwound with a marketable limit at the best available price; the loss taken is whatever it is. **Averaging into a naked leg or "waiting for it to come back" is prohibited** — naked-leg danger overrides monetary triggers, and R07's rule is immediate complete-or-unwind `[R07 §7]`, `[ar §7.2]`. A one-leg exposure is a naked-leg danger and overrides the money-based triggers `[gs §4]`.

**Price bound.** Execute against a *spread* limit, not two independent price limits: leg 2's limit = leg 1 fill price ± the maximum acceptable spread from G2. If leg 2 cannot fill inside the bound, leg 1 is unwound. Independent leg limits that can both fill at a jointly unacceptable spread are a design error this rule exists to prevent `[ar §7.2]`. On `T_h` expiry or bound breach, choose: (a) cross the spread on leg 2 if the remaining edge after crossing is still ≥ 0 and no price-limit or stale-data flag is set; otherwise (b) unwind the filled quantity of leg 1 with a marketable limit `[gs §4]`.

**Partial fills.** Hedge (or unwind) **only the filled quantity** of leg 1; scale leg 2 as `Qty₂ = ⌊Qty₁,filled × HedgeRatio⌋`; cancel the unfilled remainder of leg 1 before adding to leg 2; await `t_cancel_ack` before closing the episode `[ar §7.2]`, `[gr §4]`, `[gs §4]`. Each partial tranche re-checks G3/G4 before any additional tranche `[R07 §7]`. A partially filled spread is marked at conservative flattening values and carries a `PARTIAL_OPEN` state until completed or unwound. A partial fill is **not** a clean cancellation; the unhedged exposure is real `[ds §9.4]`. A partially filled second leg may leave a residual that must be explicitly closed or rebalanced `[cg §6]`.

**Asynchronous acknowledgements.** Submission ≠ acceptance ≠ fill, per leg. The state machine forbids placing leg 2 on the *assumption* leg 1 was accepted; it keys off leg 1's fill report. A missing leg-2 acknowledgement within the attended window triggers cancel-and-verify, never a blind duplicate — a duplicate buy is the classic manual double-fill `[ar §7.2]`. Until a response is known, represent status as `BROKER_STATE_UNKNOWN`; do not presume a click succeeded or failed `[cg §6]`. If leg 1's status is unknown, assume it filled and hedge to the most conservative known position; do not send duplicate orders until the position has been checked `[gs §4]`. Pause secondary leg submissions, issue an order-status query, and if the timeout exceeds ~30 s assume unexecuted and verify via the trade report `[gr §4]`. Broker/exchange timestamps are used where available, not the user's phone notification time `[ds §5.3]`.

**Cancel races.** A cancel request remains live exposure until cancel acknowledgement or a confirmed terminal state; handle the race where a fill arrives while cancellation is pending; apply every broker event in order and recompute quantity from the final state, never from the last UI status alone `[cg §6]`, `[cg §10]`, `[ex]`. Reserve worst-case funds for overlapping live orders and do not release them on a cancellation request alone `[ex]`.

**Missing quotes / worse-than-expected exits.** No quote on the hedge leg at the decision instant ⇒ treat as unfilled-market: unwind leg 1 if its quote exists; if *neither* quote exists (halt), the naked leg is an incident — log, alert, exit at the next reliable opportunity (the "flat-first" blackout bias) `[R07 §7]`, `[ar §7.2]`. If the second leg has no quote or violates the bound, do not fabricate a hedge: record a naked-exposure incident and a conservative exit path; even flattening can slip or fail `[ex]`. Exit slippage worse than the G2 bound on a *risk exit* does not block the exit — risk exits are price-insensitive by design; the excess cost is booked to implementation shortfall, not debated in real time `[ar §7.2]`. An inability to compute a safe executable exit is not "zero loss"; it is a separate failure mode that may falsify the live feasibility of the pair `[cg §6]`.

**Order-type hygiene.** Entries: limit or marketable-limit only ("вход только лимитными заявками" adopted from R02 §10.4); market orders reserved for emergency exits (forced-closure approach, naked-leg unwind past deadline) `[ar §7.2]`. Never assume a market order fills at the signal price — at VTB an unplaceable market order is auto-cancelled `[ds §5.2]`. Stop-style conditional orders at VTB are **not** assumed to exist in a usable form: QUIK conditional orders are checked off-exchange and may fail for technical reasons, and no source establishes VTB stop behaviour on futures, so no protective-stop logic may be designed into the pilot until confirmed `[gs S12]`, `[ar §7.2]`. "Valid until cancel" and closed-market queued orders must not be used for entries `[gs §4]`.

### 7.3 What the broker may do while the human is mid-episode

Forced closure selects instruments at the broker's own discretion; the reconstructed VTB closing order places spread components late but not last, and BCS's algorithm is not determinable for a spread without a broker answer `[R01 §11.1 item 16]`. The episode protocol therefore assumes a broker closure can interleave with manual action: before submitting any order, the terminal position table — not the user's memory — is the state; after any broker-action alert, all working orders are cancelled and re-verified. Prices on forced closure are bounded by regulation to no worse than the min/max anonymous-trade prices of the preceding 15 minutes, executed in practice with market orders in possibly thin liquidity. None of this is controllable; all of it must be *reconcilable* in the ledger (§8.5) `[ar §7.3]`.

### 7.4 Execution failure taxonomy (modes → recovery)

| Failure mode | Root cause | Recovery protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Leg 1 partial fill | Depth exhausted before the desired lot size | Retain the filled fraction; scale leg 2 proportionally; cancel the remainder of leg 1 `[gr §4]` |
| Leg 2 rejection | Margin limit reached or broker rejection (e.g. price-collar breach) | Emergency market liquidation of leg 1; do not hold single-leg exposure `[gr §4]` |
| Asynchronous ack delay | Broker API latency delays status confirmation | Pause secondary submissions; query order status; on timeout assume unexecuted and verify via trade report `[gr §4]` |
| Clearing-adjacent submission | Order submitted right before a clearing boundary | Orders/queue state reset; re-evaluate portfolio state after the boundary `[gr §4]` (re-based on the post-ETS 23:50–00:30 clearing) |
| Fill during `CANCEL_PENDING` | Race between cancellation and matching | Compute the final position from ordered broker events and actual filled quantities `[cg §10]` |
| Hung order | Rare FORTS anomaly: a market order fails to execute for an extended period while the market trades | Treat as `ACK_UNKNOWN`/`UNFILLED`, verify, and stress-test as an extreme scenario `[mm 37]` |

## 8. One realistic finite-capital virtual portfolio

### 8.1 Identity and ledgers

Exactly **one** virtual portfolio (`PORTFOLIO_ID = VP-R08-01`) exists for the evaluation, with event-sourced cash, positions, working orders and reservations — not an independent backtest per signal `[ar §8.1]`, `[cg §7]`. It holds: the cash sleeve (RUB), open spread positions in whole contracts, reservations, the emergency-funding ledger E (gross, never restored by returns/withdrawals), and the accounting triple V/B/X/H/D computed exactly as in §2.8. The sleeve ledger reconciles to the whole-account X through the core-impact bridge of R05 §4.3, so a 30k sleeve is never compared against the >40m core `[R05 §2.1]`. The funding model (R07 §3.2 a: cash sleeve, or b: pledged units + cash VM buffer) is declared at preregistration; the headline run uses one and the other is a registered sensitivity. The primary layer is pre-investor-tax, with tax sensitivities separate; only the loss of the LQDT long-term holding deduction is excluded from the tax layer.

### 8.2 Signal intake: attempt every eligible signal

Every signal that passes the *model's* eligibility (universe, event embargo, data sufficiency) is presented to the portfolio. "Attempt" means it enters the state machine of §10 and receives a disposition — it does **not** mean forcing a fill `[cg §7]`. There is no discretionary omission and no ex-post deletion; rejected, stale, unfilled, partial, quota-deferred and capital-blocked outcomes are first-class records `[R09 §5.1]`, `[R02 §10.4]` ("in one virtual portfolio … not improving the result by selecting successful executions"). Zero-signal days and zero-trade days are valid portfolio days `[ar §8.2]`, `[ex]`.

### 8.3 Deterministic priority (frozen at preregistration; no ex-post reordering)

At each evaluation instant, process in this order:

1. **Risk Actions and position repair** on open positions — broker margin/restriction events, hedge completion, naked-leg unwind/flatten, cancel races, margin-driven de-risking, expiry-forced exits — earliest deadline first. These never consume the new-idea quota.
2. **Existing working orders and position updates** — fills, cancels, acknowledgements, recalculated reservations.
3. **Exits** of open positions whose exit rule fired.
4. **Revalidations** of queued signals near TTL lapse (revalidate-or-expire before admitting new work).
5. **New entries**, in the frozen order below.

**Entry ordering — primary rule:** earliest signal timestamp (t4), then stable `signal_id` lexicographic; ties beyond id are impossible because ids are unique `[ar §8.3]`, `[gs §5]`, `[ds §6.1]`, `[ex]`. **Registered alternative:** highest precomputed conservative net edge after costs and uncertainty buffer per unit of stressed capital, tie-broken by earlier timestamp then stable id; if that score is not itself validated, fall back to oldest-eligible-first `[cg §7]`, `[gs §5]`, `[ex]`, `[gr §7]`, `[ge §5]`. `ge` proposes a three-key rank (highest net expected return relative to margin utilisation, then lowest historical pair volatility, then oldest valid signal). Whatever is chosen is frozen before outcomes and **never** ranks on future returns, eventual fills or later volatility — ranking by anything known only later is selection leakage `[R09 §4.2]`, `[cg §7]`. A signal that loses an allocation contest must not be retrospectively treated as if it had been independently funded. A lower-priority idea freed by another idea's failure is re-checked at its own simulated time against then-current quotes, and does not inherit its earlier state `[gs §5]`, `[ds §9.3]`. The priority rule is part of the evaluated procedure: the deployment procedure *is* the evaluated procedure `[R09 §5.2]`.

### 8.4 Capital reservations

At gate-pass (t9) and before t10, the portfolio places a **reservation** = stressed margin (the offset-removed arm of G3) + stressed VM buffer over the hedge/exit window + `C_exit` estimate, earmarked against the envelope `[ar §8.4]`. Reservations:
- block later signals (`CAPITAL_RESERVED` dispositions show *which* reservation blocked them — required by Example 3);
- expire with the signal TTL and are released on unwind, expiry or rejection;
- convert to actual margin accounting at t13;
- are held for pending orders until the state is known, so simultaneous signals can never double-spend the same free funds `[cg §7]`, `[mm §8.2]`;
- are never released on a cancellation request alone `[ex]`.

Previous rescue money or retained earnings never enlarge the entry envelope; entries must pass with zero emergency funding `[R07 §4.1 gate 6]`, `[ar §8.4]`. The engine must never oversubscribe margin even if OHLCV-based orders happen not to fill `[mm §8.2]`.

### 8.5 Funding approvals in simulation

The virtual portfolio **cannot approve funding**. Any path requiring emergency funding branches: with no approval event in the scenario script, the position is de-risked to flat (`FUNDING_APPROVAL_ABSENT`) and the counterfactual "funding arrived in time" is reportable only as a labelled sensitivity `[ar §8.5]`. Approval, when scripted, still carries an arrival timestamp (t16) — bank hours, T+1 LQDT settlement, broker cut-offs — and funding ≠ restart approval; X/H/D/E never reset on funding, review, restart or withdrawal `[R05 §4.5–4.6]`, `[ds §6.1]`. Broker-initiated use of core collateral, if simulated as an incident scenario, counts once into gross E (collateral-to-cash conversion not double-counted) and forces sleeve de-risking `[R07 §7]`. Core LQDT is not silently available to fund the sleeve: the shared-account setup does not establish that the broker recognises core assets as eligible collateral or that the account provides the intended recourse controls `[cg §7]`. Any NCC/broker margin re-rating is tracked on its effective timestamp, and a discount for a specific recognised spread group applies only if the actual two contracts and account route qualify `[cg §7]`.

### 8.6 Quota and availability inside the portfolio

The portfolio enforces §3 as registered: blackout regime F for signal births 00:00–12:00 (worst case), Q-IDEA = 1 entry MAE/day (soft), Risk Actions exempt. For transparency it also computes two shadow counters (not portfolios): entries that Q-LEG would have blocked, and entries that a no-blackout counterfactual would have taken — each reported as counts and RUB opportunity-cost bounds, never as alternative P&L curves to choose from (choosing the best-looking counterfactual after the fact is the multiple-comparison failure R09 §7 exists to prevent) `[ar §8.6]`. An existing position's exit or hedge does not count as a fresh-idea slot `[cg §7]`.

### 8.7 Seeded delay experiments

The master seed is registered before any run. Each eligible signal is evaluated under the **primary** availability + delay model (P-B human latency inside attended windows, F-regime in blackout, C1 correlation switches on registered volatility/liquidity flags). The full lattice of §5.2–5.3 is then replayed as *paired sensitivities* on the same signal set with the same seed: identical signals, identical priority, only the delay/friction regime varies `[ar §8.7]`. Paired reporting (same signals, Δ outcome by regime) is what makes "how much does delay cost this strategy?" answerable without inventing a delay distribution. Scenario count and the identity of the primary are frozen in the trial registry; exploring scenarios and then promoting the kindest one to "primary" is prohibited `[R09 §6]`.

Per signal version and per day, retain `[ar Appendix B]`, `[ds §6.2]`, `[cg §7]`:

### 8.9 What the portfolio must not do

It must not assume that every signal fills; that a market order fills at the signal price; that a partial fill is a clean cancellation; that broker credit or core collateral is available; that a repeated notification is a new trade; that losses reset on review, funding, restart or withdrawal; that core-asset sale proceeds are external flows or economic loss in X; or that a rejected signal becomes a free option later `[ds §6.4]`, `[ds §9.3]`, `[mm §8.3]`.

## 9. Falsifiable delayed-fill evaluation protocol

### 9.1 Three evidence tiers — claims may only be made at the tier the data supports

| Tier | Data | What it can prove | What it cannot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E0 — Research replay** | Daily ISS history (OHLC, SETTLEPRICE, OPENPOSITION from 2001-09-07; contract listing map) `[R04 §1 point 2]` | Existence/persistence of spread dislocations, convergence behaviour, seasonality, opportunity counts over long horizons | Any fill price, synchrony, capacity, margin path `[R04 §8]` |
| **E1 — Conditional execution replay** | Candles M1/M10/H1 (ISS, from listing; futures-minute floor 2011-03-14) `[R04 §1 point 1]` + the R02 cost stack + the §5 delay lattice | Bounded execution outcomes (interval estimates); timeframe suitability; delay-regime sensitivity | Point-estimate returns; queue position; partial-fill truth |
| **E2 — Observed execution** | Forward self-collected BBO + sizes, trade prints, order/fill reports (§9.5) | Actual implementation shortfall, reaction distribution, one-leg incident rate | History before collection start — backfill is impossible for bid/ask, it does not exist retrospectively |

The strategy's timeframe claim is itself falsifiable: if E1 bounds at the H1/M10 layer are too wide to separate edge from zero while E0 shows structure, the honest conclusion is "medium-term daily strategy, intraday dynamics unresolvable on free data" — not a retreat to M1 point estimates `[ar §9.1]`.

### 9.2 Protocol phases

**A. Data and timestamp audit.** For each data source and contract, document timestamp semantics, receive latency, actual history horizon, update cadence, session coverage, survivorship/contract-roll treatment, missing bars, quote-staleness distribution and licensing. Reconstruct what was knowable at every signal evaluation and delayed decision — not merely what appears on the final chart. Align both legs and the LQDT comparator; forbid any field timestamped after a decision from affecting it `[cg §9]`.

**B. Freeze the experiment.** Lock the instrument/contract universe with integer ratios, signal formula, feature availability cutoffs, universe-selection and pair-formation rules, signal threshold, invalidation/expiry, fees, spread/impact model, delay grid, response regimes, quote-freshness caps, edge/stress buffers, leg sequence and limits, calendar version, VM treatment, capital rules, risk limits, allocation and rejection taxonomy, and ranking policy **before** the final out-of-sample run. Maintain a full record of all parameter variants tried `[cg §9]`, `[ex]`, `[ds §7.1]`. If the rule is changed after seeing results, the evaluation is invalid `[ds §7.1]`.

**C. Generate every signal, then replay decisions.** Recreate signal creation from point-in-time data. For each eligible event, replay each delay design from t4 to the simulated decision instant; then obtain only the first valid market state available at that time. If the needed quote is missing/stale or the market/broker is closed, record failure rather than forward-fill. Use common seeds across variants `[cg §9]`.

**D. Revalidate and submit.** Recompute executable net edge and all account/capital gates at the delayed time. Preserve rejects, expired signals, no-fill and partial-fill outcomes. Avoid midpoint execution. Model the two legs independently unless the exact native spread path is evidenced `[cg §9]`.

**E. Mark and close conservatively.** Mark using a synchronised, explicitly selected source; add realistic exit costs. Test adverse exits, missing quotes, partial fills, cancel races, session closes, contract expiry/roll, changing margin and one-leg exposure. Report both ordinary and tail/stress scenarios. Apply the X/H/D policy without resetting it at review, at funding or on restart `[cg §9]`.

**F. Separate discovery from confirmation.** Use training/validation/test chronology or walk-forward folds; avoid random shuffles that leak future regimes; purge overlapping holding horizons near boundaries. Report results by pair family, contract, session, volatility/liquidity state and delay bucket as well as in aggregate. The test must include rejected and unfilled ideas; selecting only completed or winning trades is invalid. Report uncertainty intervals and the number of independent signal episodes, not just a headline Sharpe ratio `[cg §9]`, `[ex]`.

**G. Compare to explicit, pre-registered falsification criteria.** Set thresholds before results are reviewed: minimum net edge after adverse execution, acceptable rejection/partial-fill and manual-burden rates, worst credible loss/one-leg stress, required sample size. If no defensible thresholds have been selected, report the distributions and call the result exploratory — do not invent a pass `[cg §9]`, `[ds §7.1]`.

Additional protocol requirements from `ds §7.1`: use only information available at t0 (any post-t0 information is leakage); model leg risk explicitly in the leg-by-leg case; reserve finite capital and reject what cannot be reserved; report a **distribution**, not a point estimate, including the worst case; compare against the matched LQDT counterfactual using the same external flows and valuation times.

### 9.3 Conservative OHLCV fallback fill rules (E1)

With OHLCV only, do **not** claim that a bid/ask, order-book queue, fill probability or actual fill was observed `[cg §9]`. The fallback must be conservative by construction — designed to overstate costs rather than understate them `[ds §7.2]`. Two outputs, neither an executable backtest `[cg §9]`:

1. **Primary OHLCV study (idea survival).** Test signal age/decay and whether the strategy's theoretical pair state survives until the first *complete bar strictly after* the simulated order-ready time. Bars overlapping order submission are excluded. Keep event-time alignment and the bar timestamp convention explicit. This is an idea-survival study, not a fill backtest.
2. **Pessimistic execution sensitivity.** For a hypothetical marketable buy use the adverse high of the first wholly post-arrival bar plus a declared impact buffer; for a sell use the adverse low minus that buffer. Calibrate the buffer only from independent forward fills/quote evidence where possible; otherwise sweep fixed tick/ATR-linked stresses as declared assumptions. These extreme prices are stress proxies, not assertions about actual execution.

**Detailed rules (consolidated):**

| Situation | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tradable instant | A signal computed on bar *k*'s close can act no earlier than the first bar whose *end* exceeds t4 + data lag (15 min ISS) + scenario human latency, rounded up to whole bars; in blackout, the first attended revalidation instant. Never fill before arrival; never use bars that were not yet available. Fresh signal timestamps never refresh stale inputs | `[ar §9.2]`, `[gs §8]`, `[cg §9]` |
| Synchronous spread construction | Spread bars use only buckets where **both** legs have a bar with volume > 0 and identical bucket bounds. `High(A) − Low(B)` and any cross-bucket extreme is forbidden as an executable spread | `[R04 §1 point 5]`, `[ar §9.2]` |
| No midpoint, ever | Entry/exit valuation uses a *bound pair*: a favourable bound (best synchronous closes) and a **conservative bound** — entry at the worst synchronous close of the spread within the action window, plus full friction: quoted-spread crossing where a dated quote exists, else a cost band of at least ±1 tick per leg (GL's dated 100–108-tick widths set the scale — a 1-tick band on GL would be fabricated precision), plus `C_RT` under the two-period broker fee. Headline results use the conservative bound; the favourable bound is reported only to show interval width. Midpoint fills `(H+L)/2` are strictly prohibited | `[ar §9.2]`, `[R09 §9.6]`, `[gr §8]`, `[ex]` |
| Marketable orders | Buy at the adverse extreme of the arrival bar — `max(open, high)` — plus the declared impact buffer; sell at `min(open, low)` minus the buffer. In stress scenarios use the conservative bound price, not the bar close | `[gr §8]`, `[ds §7.2]`, `[cg §9]`, `[mm §7.1]` |
| Limit orders | A limit-priced leg fills in a bar **only if the bar's synchronous range trades through the limit** — at least one tick beyond it. A touch alone proves only that a trade occurred somewhere in the bar's range; it does not prove the order was accepted, displayed, ahead in the queue, or filled. The primary conservative record for a touch-only bar is `UNFILLED/UNKNOWN`; an optional touch/penetration scenario must be separately labelled, use no midpoint, and never be merged into observed fills | `[ar §9.2]`, `[gs S18]`, `[cg §9]` |
| Filled quantity | ≤ a registered participation cap (candidate: ≤ 10% of the bar's volume per leg). Do not infer a partial-fill quantity from candle volume without a validated capacity/queue model. Where volume is unknown, assume at most the minimum lot rather than a full fill. Legs with 111 contracts/day (RIH7-type) will correctly fail the cap | `[R09 §9.7]`, `[R02 §10.1]`, `[cg §9]`, `[ds §7.2]`, `[gs §8]` |
| Both legs | Both must satisfy their fill conditions within the hedge window; otherwise `UNFILLED`/`PARTIAL` per §10 codes | `[ar §9.2]` |
| Unhedged interval | Mark the unhedged leg at the **worst** of the bar high/low in the adverse direction, not at the bar close — the close may be the best point in the interval | `[ds §7.2]` |
| Forced exit | Exit at the worst adverse bar extreme plus an additional slippage charge; forced exits are not patient executions | `[ds §7.2]` |
| No trade in the bar / no eligible post-arrival bar / closed session / missing leg bar / ambiguous time zone | **No fill evidence.** Do not carry the last close forward as an executable quote; do not infer a fill from a quote that was not traded. Overnight/weekend gaps and next-session opening prices need separate stress treatment | `[ds §7.2]`, `[cg §9]`, `[gs §8]` |
| Marks vs fills | Positions are marked at synchronous closes (research) and at conservative flattening values (trigger evaluation); settlement prices are a third, separately-labelled series used only for clearing reconciliation. Booking settlement as a fill is leakage | `[R09 §4.2 pathway 8]`, `[ar §9.2]` |
| Margin path | Daily (E0/E1) margin is modelled from *forward* GO snapshots only; historical GO does not exist in the free contour, so historical margin-breach claims are scenario claims (offset-removed ×2–3), labelled as such | `[R04 §10.3]`, `[R06 §12.1]`, `[ar §9.2]` |
| Calendar-spread archive | MOEX's spread archive (daily bid/offer, high/low, volume, trade counts) is used only as a daily sanity bound, never as intraday fill evidence | `[gs S21]` |
| Labelled optimistic branch | A touch-fill variant priced at `min(L, close)` for a buy limit (and symmetrically for a sell) exists in one source report `[mm §7.1]`. It is retained **only** as a separately labelled optimistic sensitivity, never merged into headline results, because it contradicts the trade-through rule adopted above | `[mm §7.1]`, resolved against `[ar]`, `[cg]`, `[gs]`, `[ex]` |

**Comparison requirement.** Only quote/trade history with defensible timestamps can move the project toward an execution-grade replay. OHLCV sensitivity can falsify a fragile idea, but a favourable OHLCV result cannot establish the fills necessary for live feasibility `[cg §9]`. On genuine timestamped paired quotes/depth/trades, inject delay before order arrival, use side-specific bid/ask and sufficient depth; marketable orders consume price levels, resting limits require plausible queue-ahead clearance, and missing exchange sequencing yields **bounded** fill outcomes rather than certain fills `[ex]`. If a re-run on more granular data (1-minute vs 5-minute) consistently yields materially worse P&L, the bar-based fill model was too optimistic `[mm §7.2]`.

### 9.4 Metrics (all pre-registered; definitions per R09 §9)

- **Primary:** terminal and path `X(t)` vs matched LQDT in RUB; `D(t)`; shortfall `S(t) = max(0, −X(t))` (distinct triggers — D can fire while X > 0); capital-time (RUB-days split margin / VM liquidity / reserve / LQDT diversion); stress-funding path (max `IM + cumulative VM deficit`, time-to-meet, gross E usage) `[R09 §9.2–9.4]`, `[ar §9.3]`.
- **Execution:** full implementation shortfall vs the t4 decision spread (decision-price vs achieved, including the opportunity cost of the unfilled), decomposed into **data-lag, human-delay, spread-crossing and unfilled-opportunity** components — this decomposition *is* R08's research question in one number set `[ar §9.3]`, `[Perold]`. The framework splits costs into delay costs and the opportunity cost of unexecuted trades `[gs S23–S24]`, `[mm §11]`.
- **Delay-decay evidence:** an edge "half-life" — net edge as a function of delay — reported per scenario and seed, with signal count, eligible count, order count and fill count by reason code; the legging-loss distribution; the X path and maximum D `[gs §8]`. Documented external anchor (not MOEX evidence): waiting one day cut average excess returns by about 30–55 bp (fully invested) and 20–35 bp (committed capital) in US equity pairs trading `[gs S22, Gatev–Goetzmann–Rouwenhorst 2006, RFS 19(3), p. 12]`.
- **Operational:** disposition mix (§10), stale rate, one-leg incidents and unhedged-interval distribution, hedge-deadline breaches, quota exceedances, MAE/LO per day (§13), blackout-born signal outcomes.
- **Uncertainty:** dependence-aware (block) resampling on the daily portfolio ledger; Gaussian tails are not assumed `[R07 §4.3; R09 §8]`.

### 9.5 What must be collected forward (starts before any live claim)

Priority order — "collect backwards what exists, record forwards what does not" `[R04 §10.4]`:

1. **Daily snapshots from day one** (after rights clarification): contracts/specs versions, GO/price limits (`INITIALMARGIN, HIGHLIMIT, LOWLIMIT, PREVSETTLEPRICE, STEPPRICE, MINSTEP, LOTVOLUME, LASTTRADEDATE…`), dated tariff/broker-parameter snapshots, session-calendar versions, recognised spread-group state, daily history and LQDT executable prices. ≈0.5 MB/day class volume; laptop-feasible, VPS-feasible for snapshots `[R04 §11]`, `[ar §9.4]`, `[cg §9]`.
2. **Signal/order journal** with t0…t17 and input hashes (§4, Appendix B) — the calibration asset for §5.5. Capture **all generated signals and all revalidation failures**, not just alerts the human accepted `[cg §9]`.
3. **Live shadow BBO** (after channel/rights clarity): both legs' bid/ask + visible size at t4, t9, t10, t12, t13 with `received_at` in UTC, connection/reconnect flags, trading status, quote update/sequence timestamps, session state, recent trades/volume; gaps recorded as gaps, never back-filled `[R04 §10.4 point 2]`, `[ar §9.4]`, `[gs §8]`. Resource envelope: a narrow sub-universe (near + next series of the flagship families), not the 496-series universe; the existing VPS (1 vCPU / 1 GB + a shared, unverified 250–350 MB budget) is a *snapshot and alerting* host only until a measured load test says otherwise — no production probing now.
4. **Friction series:** ≥20 main-session trading days of quoted spread/depth for the candidate pairs before any friction number graduates from "snapshot" to "estimate" `[R02 §10.4]`.
5. **Human-factors log:** t7 acknowledgements (or their absence), t8 decisions, outages, ignored and missed alerts, no-response episodes — without it, the reaction distribution stays assumed forever `[ar §9.4]`, `[cg §9]`.
6. **Broker and account evidence:** every notification send attempt/API response/message id and read/decision time (manually marked if necessary); every broker order request/ack/reject/cancel/partial/full fill with prices, quantities and timestamps; VTB displayed free funds/blocked collateral and changing risk limits; fee schedule version; actual hedge/exit action and unhedged interval; VM/settlement cash movements; synchronised LQDT counterfactual marks and true external flows `[cg §9]`, `[ds §7.3]`, `[ex]`. Retain raw immutable events alongside cleaned derived data.
7. **Counterfactual delay probe:** for each paper signal, a "would-have-submitted" price and a counterfactual outcome at +5, +15, +30, +60 and +90 minutes from public trade data `[gs §8]`.

**A note on one collection target.** `gr §8` specifies top-5 L2 order-book depth at 1-second resolution for all monitored pairs, timestamped execution reports with matching-engine arrival times, and broker API ping logs. That specification is a legitimate *target* but exceeds what the free contour provides (ISS order books are subscriber-only; ALGOPACK's advertised cadence is 10 seconds) and exceeds the stated VPS budget for a wide universe. Disposition: adopt it only for a narrow sub-universe and only after a paid-source decision under R04; otherwise record top-of-book snapshots at the timestamps listed above and label the coarser resolution explicitly.

### 9.6 Literature anchors used in the protocol

Implementation shortfall (decision price vs achieved, with delay and opportunity-cost components) `[Perold 1988, JPM 14, 4–9]`, `[gs S23–S24]`; selection bias under multiple testing and non-normality (Deflated Sharpe Ratio) `[cg S10]`; unreliability of conventional holdouts when many backtests are selected (Probability of Backtest Overfitting) `[ex]`; bid–ask spread and order-book mechanics in market-liquidity overviews `[ex, BIS Papers no. 2, pp. 3–4]`; general caution that hypothetical performance may not account for spreads, margin calls, liquidity and actual trading conditions `[cg S11]`; simulated-brokerage caution on slippage assumptions `[cg S13]`. None of these repairs execution-data defects.

## 10. Lifecycle and reason-code taxonomy

### 10.1 States

**Signal/portfolio level** `[ar §10.1]`, merged with `[cg §3]`, `[ds §8.1]`, `[ex]`, `[mm §6.1]`:

```
MODEL_ELIGIBLE → SIGNALLED(t4) → NOTIFIED | DELIVERY_FAILED
  → SEEN | USER_UNAVAILABLE → DECIDED → REVALIDATING
  → { READY | REJECTED_* } → CAPITAL_RESERVED
  → ORDERING(leg states) → { HEDGED_OPEN | PARTIAL_OPEN | NAKED_INCIDENT }
  → OPEN → { EXITING | DE_RISKING } → CLOSED / UNWOUND
```
with side exits to `EXPIRED`, `STALE`, `SUPERSEDED`, `ABORTED`, `CANCELLED` from any pre-order or open state. Broker-forced transitions (`FORCED_CLOSING`, `FORCED_CLOSED`) can interrupt any open state and are reconciled, never edited away. Eleven named stages make the same path explicit `[cg §3]`: observe → evaluate → create idea → notify → wait/human unavailable → revalidate → admit to portfolio → submit orders → hedge/abort/work remaining quantity → manage/exit → reconcile; each transition records a timestamp, the actor (model, Telegram, user, broker, exchange) and a reason code `[ds §8.1]`.

**Leg-level states** (per leg order) `[ar §10.1]`, `[mm §6.1]`, `[ex]`:
```
NOT_PLACED → SUBMITTED → BROKER_ACCEPTED → EXCHANGE_ACCEPTED
  → { PARTIALLY_FILLED → FILLED, REJECTED, CANCEL_PENDING → CANCELLED, EXPIRED_SESSION }
```
plus `ACK_PENDING` / `BROKER_STATE_UNKNOWN` / `UNKNOWN_PENDING` wherever an acknowledgement is missing. The pair state is a function of leg states plus quantities; there is no pair state in which leg quantities are "assumed". Every outstanding broker order is marked independently until acknowledged terminal `[ex]`.

**Position-level states:** no position, half-leg open, full spread open, exiting, closed `[mm §6.1]`. Exit resolution classes: profit-taking, stop-loss, time-based (expiry/roll), forced by margin/KDS, manual discretionary close, or no entry `[mm §6.1]`.

### 10.2 Unified reason-code taxonomy

Every disposition carries at least one code; codes are data, not commentary. Never replace a failure with a blank "no trade" `[cg §3]`. Retain the causal reason plus timestamp and observed-versus-imputed status, and distinguish a rejected idea, an unsubmitted order, an exchange-unfilled order and a late cancellation `[ex]`.

## 11. Signal communication

### 11.1 Separation of layers

An **idea signal** (research object) states what the model saw and what would still have to be true at execution. An **order instruction** (execution object) is produced only *at the terminal, after the gates pass*, and contains exact orders `[ar §11.1]`, `[ds §10]`, `[gs §6]`. Telegram carries idea signals and risk alerts — never standing order instructions, because by the time a human reads them their prices are stale by construction. This separation is also the control that makes "repeated notification ≠ another trade" enforceable by a human, not just by software. An alert must be concise enough to act on but complete enough to reject without reconstructing its premise, and must say **IDEA — NOT AN ORDER** unless a later, separately authorised system actually generates broker-order instructions `[cg §8]`.

### 11.2 Mandatory signal card (idea layer)

Required fields, consolidating `[ar §11.2]`, `[cg §8]`, `[ds §10.1]`, `[gs §6]`, `[ex]`, `[mm §2]`, `[ge §6]`, `[gr §6]`:

- **Identity and vintage:** immutable `signal_id`, version, strategy/model version, thesis/episode id, creation time (t4), `t_mkt` of the newest input, data age at creation, `valid_until`/TTL, and an explicit statement that a fresh Telegram timestamp does **not** refresh the market data.
- **Legs:** full exchange contract codes, expiries, direction per leg, target quantities in lots and the integer ratio, tick values and minimum lot, hedge-ratio basis (base-asset units). No ambiguous shorthand.
- **Thesis:** the economic rationale in one or two sentences — why the spread should converge/diverge — so the user can reject an idea they do not understand.
- **Economics:** price-snapshot timestamp and age **per leg**, displayed bid/ask and visible size where observable, data source/status and any delayed/stale qualification; estimated executable entry spread using the correct sides; acceptable maximum entry prices/net spread; expected net advantage versus matched LQDT after itemised fees, funding, benchmark foregone return and exit costs, expressed as a **range from the §9.3 bounds**, not a point and not a gross "target"; minimum edge hurdle and uncertainty buffer.
- **Risk:** stressed loss and exit cost, one-leg failure risk, current margin estimate with its date, free funds reserved, VM-liquidity requirement, unhedged-leg risk, maximum holding/decision uncertainty, and whether pair execution is native-spread-verified or independent-leg only.
- **Evidence confidence:** evidence grade, sample size, out-of-sample status, and the specific falsifier ("what would kill this idea") — **never an invented win probability** `[R09 §11.1]`. Grades available from the current evidence base: A = primary-document link + multi-regime observations; B = spec link + single-regime 141-observation window; C = hypothesis with a named explanatory variable. Only B/C can honestly be issued for flagship economics, since the aligned window is one regime `[R06 §2]`. Useful wording: "Evidence status: exploratory; spread quote history incomplete; net-cost range depends on unconfirmed R02 rates; no calibrated probability of profit" `[cg §8]`.
- **Executable bounds (re-checked fresh at the terminal, not from the card):** enter only if broker BBO on both legs ≤ the freshness cap; executable spread ≥ the bound; `Edge_exec` ≥ hurdle; both legs in a tradable session; no event before the stated instant.
- **"DO NOT ENTER IF" clauses:** blackout or quota exhausted; quote stale or missing; alert age beyond expiry; spread beyond bound; edge below floor; funding sign flipped; GO snapshot changed; session/broker route unconfirmed; margin or cash fails; contract rolls or changes; model/data health fails; existing overlapping exposure or no capacity; a leg near its price limit; X/D in suspension; expiry/delivery guard.
- **Order block (only if authorised):** executable bound per leg and for the spread, order type and lifetime (day order, no carry-over), leg sequence, unhedged timer `T_h`, cross/unwind rule, slippage tolerance.
- **Decision capture:** a button/reply records accept, decline or ignore as a decision event and never directly creates a second trade on retry; any eventual order workflow revalidates again immediately before submission.

Worked example of the card format `[ar §11.2]`:

```
SIGNAL  CNY-2026-…-0147  v1            [IDEA — NOT AN ORDER]
Born (t4): 2026-… 13:05 MSK   Inputs: ISS candles to 12:50 (age 15m, delayed feed)
Thesis: CNYRUBF/CRZ6 funding+basis convergence to 17.12.2026 expiry [family MKS-3]
Legs: SHORT 1× CNYRUBF  |  LONG 1× CRZ6   (1:1 in CNY 1,000; integer, no residual)
At signal closes: spread 247 RUB/pair; model fair …; edge after full costs ≈ … RUB
  (cost stack: round-turn 10.7 RUB snapshot 07.10 + crossing band …; VTB fee period 0 RUB)
Risk if entered now (snapshot params): gross margin 2,291 RUB; stressed (offset off) …;
  1σ/day ≈ 688 RUB; stressed loss L_stress …; cash-path need …
Holding uncertainty: days–weeks; hard deadline: exit/roll before 17.12.2026 14:00/19:00
  expiry instant (per spec); NOT a promised holding period.
Evidence grade: B — spec-confirmed link, 141 obs post-23.03.2026, funding sign 151/151
  positive historically [R06 §10.2]. NO win probability is stated (policy [R09 §11.1]).
EXECUTABLE BOUNDS (checked fresh at the terminal, not from this card):
  Enter only if: broker BBO both legs ≤ 60 s old; executable spread ≥ … RUB;
  Edge_exec ≥ …; both legs in main session; no event before …
DO NOT ENTER IF: blackout/quota exhausted; quote stale; funding sign flipped;
  GO snapshot changed; X/D in suspension; either leg near expiry/delivery guard.
Expires (TTL): 13:35 MSK or session boundary, whichever first. Reissue needs fresh data.
```

A second, more compact card format is also on record `[ge §6]`: a signal id and `t_sig`, an explicit freshness limit with an expiry clock, pair identification with quantity per leg, target entry spread versus executable limit ask, net expected profit after TCA and the LQDT hurdle in RUB per lot, a minimum executable spread bound, estimated initial margin, a required cash buffer (1.5×), a maximum holding duration, and three numbered "DO NOT ENTER IF" lines. One field in that template must be changed before adoption: it states confidence as a *statistical cointegration z-score over a 60-day lookback*. A z-score describes the current deviation, not out-of-sample validity, so it must be accompanied (or replaced) by the evidence-grade, sample-size and falsifier convention above; on its own it invites exactly the invented-confidence reading the policy forbids `[R09 §11.1]`.

### 11.3 Risk alerts (separate, higher-priority channel semantics)

Margin-state, naked-leg, broker-action and outage alerts carry: position, trigger value versus threshold, **deadline instant** (e.g. today's VTB restrictive time), and available actions. They are exempt from the entry quota and from the "one idea/day" rhythm; during blackout they are sent for the record and for A-soft, but the system state must assume no reader until 12:00 `[ar §11.3]`.

### 11.4 What the message must not do

It must not present a single target price as a promise; must not present a win rate or probability unsupported by out-of-sample evidence; must not hide the unhedged-leg risk in the leg-by-leg case; must not say "enter now" without the revalidation checklist; must not treat a notification retry as a new opportunity; must not hide costs inside a gross spread `[ds §10.2]`, `[cg §8]`.

## 12. Worked examples

All examples below are **illustrative constructions**, not observations, forecasts, fee estimates or backtest results. Where a source report used dated R06/R02 snapshots (2026-10-07), the contract parameters are real snapshots and the intraday paths are invented to exercise the protocol `[ar §12]`, `[cg §10]`, `[ds §9]`, `[gs §9]`, `[gr §7]`, `[mm §9]`, `[ge §7]`, `[ex]`.

**Example 1 — Stale winner.**
- *Blackout variant* `[ar]`: 08:20 MSK, an ISS-based screener (data to 08:05) flags a GLDRUBF/GLZ6 dislocation implying edge ≈ +180 RUB over the 33.0 RUB round-turn snapshot cost. The human is in blackout. TTL lapses at 08:50 → `EXPIRED_TTL` + `HUMAN_BLACKOUT`; no reservation is placed. At 12:05 a fresh evaluation shows the dislocation has converged: executable edge ≈ +12 RUB < `Edge_min` → `EDGE_GONE` on the *new* version. A naive OHLCV backtest that "filled" at the 08:20 candle close would book ≈ +147 RUB of profit that the realistic portfolio correctly records as **forgone opportunity cost** inside implementation shortfall, with zero position and zero margin use. Lesson priced by falsifier F-2: if most signals are born in the blackout, the *generation* schedule is wrong, not the market.
- *Attended variant with numbers* `[cg]`: at signal time the estimated advantage is 700 RUB per minimum pair; estimated two-leg costs 300 RUB and uncertainty buffer 100 RUB, so the candidate initially clears a 400 RUB net hurdle. At the user's 18-minute revalidation the current executable advantage is 250 RUB; 250 − 300 is negative before the buffer → `EDGE_BELOW_FLOOR`/`STATE_INVALIDATED`, no order. A later favourable price path cannot retroactively turn this into a fill.
- *Variant with quote widening* `[ds §9.1]`: signal at 14:00 with a 0.30% net advantage over LQDT after costs; delivered 14:01; read 14:18; the far leg's bid/ask has widened from 1 tick to 4 ticks and the revalidated net advantage is −0.05% → `NO_NET_ADVANTAGE`. The model later shows the spread reverted and the original signal would have been profitable; that is irrelevant — counting the hypothetical profit would be look-ahead bias.
- *Decay-threshold variant* `[ge]`, `[gs §9.1]`: a 75-minute delay leaves the book 40 ticks against the entry; 70% of the expected reversion has already happened; the decay check scrubs the signal and records it only in the opportunity-cost log, not in X.

**Example 2 — Missed opportunity.**
- *Data lag decisive, attended* `[ar]`: 13:00, signal born on ISS data 15 minutes old; the human reacts in 9 minutes (P-B). At t9 (13:11) the broker BBO shows the executable spread already 6 RUB worse and `Edge_exec`, after the 10.7 RUB round turn and the crossing band, is below `Edge_min` → `EDGE_GONE`, no orders. The E1 conservative bound agrees; the favourable bound would have shown a fill — which is exactly why the favourable bound is never headlined. Recorded: data-lag component of shortfall ≈ the full edge; human component ≈ small. **The scarce resource was the feed, not the human.**
- *Session-end variant* `[gs §9.2]`: alert at 23:31, user unavailable, trading closes at 23:50 → `USER_UNAVAILABLE` → `MARKET_CLOSED` → expired; no next-open fill; the contract is not on the weekend list.
- *Expiry-before-read variant* `[ds §9.2]`: signal created 18:40 with expiry 19:10; delivery delayed to 18:55 by a retry; read at 19:20 → `SIGNAL_EXPIRED`. No "second chance" on the same t4; a new signal requires a new evaluation on fresh t0.
- *Boundary-crossing variant* `[gr §7 B]`: 95-minute latency against a 90-minute maximum → `EXPIRED_TIME_LIMIT`, logged as a missed opportunity for benchmark tracking, no order routed.

**Example 3 — Simultaneous signals competing for cash.**
- *Capital + quota interaction with dated snapshots* `[ar]`: 13:05, three signals pass model eligibility in the same cycle — CNY pair (gross margin 2,291), GL pair (2,745), IMOEX pair (5,134). Priority: no risk actions pending → entries by (t4, id): CNY first. Reservations at stressed terms (offset removed, ≈×2 test value + VM buffer + exit cost) earmark ≈2×2,291 + buffer for CNY; GL reserves similarly; by IMOEX's turn the remaining envelope cannot satisfy the stressed margin arm and the cash-path gate simultaneously with both reservations live → `CAPITAL_RESERVED (blocked_by: CNY…, GL…)`; the quota is also exhausted after the first entry MAE, so the other two carry `QUOTA_ENTRY_DEFERRED` and expire on TTL. No human "picked the best one"; tomorrow, yesterday's losers get no priority compensation. Gross margins alone (sum 10,170 RUB) would have fitted 30,000 easily — the binding constraints were *stressed* margin, VM reserve and quota, in that order.
- *Score-ranked variant* `[gr §7 C]`: two signals at 11:00, margin 18,000 vs 15,000 and expected returns 850 vs 450; free cash 22,000; expected edge per margin unit 0.0472 vs 0.0300 → signal 1 executed, signal 2 `INSUFFICIENT_MARGIN`.
- *Re-check-after-failure variant* `[gs §9.3]`, `[ds §9.3]`: free envelope 9,000; ideas A (14:02) and B (14:03) each need 6,000 margin plus 3,000 reserves; A is reserved first, B gets `LOWER_PRIORITY_CASH`. If A fails revalidation at 14:15, B is re-checked at 14:15 against 14:15 quotes — it does not inherit its 14:03 state, and its released reservation is not a free option.
- *Reserve-then-release variant* `[mm §9.2]`: three ideas each needing ~10k margin against 22k free; the engine reserves for A and B; C is rejected for insufficient funds; A fills, B never fills and its reservation is released at cancel, with no new signals arriving in the interval. End of day: only A is held, and the opportunity cost of B and C is visible in the logs.
- *Envelope-arithmetic variant* `[cg §10.3]`, `[ex]`: pair A needs 18,000 margin plus 7,000 stress/VM/exit reserve; pair B needs 16,000 plus 8,000; each passes alone but together they would reserve 49,000 against a 30,000 envelope. Apply deterministic priority, admit the frozen-rank winner, record the other as `OVERLAP_CAPACITY`/`INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`, and never give both independent hypothetical capital.

**Example 4 — One-leg fill.**
- *GL, sequential* `[ar]`: attended, 14:20. Leg 1 (sell 1 GLDRUBF, limit inside its 100-tick-wide book) fills after 6 minutes. Leg 2 (buy GLZ6) is submitted as a marketable limit bounded by the maximum acceptable spread; GLZ6's ask runs away beyond the bound within 3 minutes (`UNFILLED` remainder). The 5-minute hedge deadline breaches → `LEG1_ONLY_NAKED` incident; the filled GLDRUBF is bought back with a marketable limit at +7 minutes, crossing a wide book. Illustrative arithmetic: adverse move 22 ticks × 0.1 RUB = 2.2 RUB price loss plus crossing/friction on two extra executions ≈ tens of RUB — small money, *maximum* process severity: the incident, not the RUB, is the output. It counts in F-3, forces same-day review, and — had it occurred near the 14:00/16:00 broker restrictive times with the user absent — is exactly the scenario the morning-sizing constraint G7 exists to prevent. Had leg 1 filled zero, the episode closes as `UNFILLED` with zero risk: the accepted price of limit-only discipline on this pair.
- *Cancel-race variant* `[gr §7 D]`: buy limit for 2 lots of an illiquid leg; 1 lot fills at 11.45; the trader cancels the remainder and places a 1-lot limit on the liquid leg; at the 120-second timeout the leg has not filled; the trader cancels leg 2 and sells the filled leg at market at 11.41. Realised slippage 0.04 (≈40 RUB) plus fees (≈12 RUB) = −52 RUB booked to X with `LEG2_TIMEOUT`.
- *Partial-both-legs variant* `[ds §9.4]`: far leg limit for 2 lots fills 1; the near leg fills only 1 after 90 seconds; the portfolio records a 1-lot unhedged position for that interval, marks the unhedged leg at the worst adverse price in the interval, and either hedges the second lot at a worse price or closes the residual (`ABORTED`), charging the abort cost to the strategy.
- *Hedge-slippage-accepted variant* `[mm §9.3]`: the pre-defined hedge band allows +0.2 versus the modelled spread but the actual best ask is +0.5; the engine either accepts directional risk within a pre-specified cap and places a limit, or treats it as a hedge-condition violation and flattens. If it hedges at +0.5, the spread exists with reduced expected profit (`P4`) and X is logged accordingly.
- *Required simulator outcome* `[cg §10.4]`: mark `ONE_LEG_FILLED`/`PARTIAL`, reserve exposure, process any late fill, then follow the predeclared bounded hedge/flatten policy; report the exact unhedged duration and worst price excursion. A cancel request alone does not clear risk.

**Example 5 — Margin escalation.**
- *Morning blackout, IMOEX* `[ar]`: 2× IMOEXF/MMZ6 pairs (gross margin 2 × 5,134 = 10,268 RUB snapshot; book 1σ/day ≈ 2 × 1,614 ≈ 3,228 RUB, correlation-naive). Overnight the spread moves −2σ (≈ −6,500 RUB VM direction) and NCC/broker parameters tighten; at 09:00 the margin requirement is issued while the human is unavailable; a hypothetical offset removal near the quarterly's expiry would roughly double-to-triple the pair's requirement. The user returns at 12:00 to a risk alert whose *real* deadline is VTB's 16:00 restrictive time (EBS) — or 14:00 if the account sits in the Derivatives portfolio — not the 17:30 clearing-member deadline. Pre-registered response: entries already suspended if D/X triggers fired; de-risk to the size at which the margin and cash-path gates pass again; funding requested only to reach flat and only with explicit approval — in simulation, absence of a scripted approval ⇒ `FUNDING_APPROVAL_ABSENT` and flattening. This is R07 S1 instantiated; a book for which this morning is *not survivable by construction* fails G7 at entry and must never be opened — which is why the realistic book at 30k is 1–2 cheap pairs, not the 5-pair IMOEX maximum that gross margin alone would allow.
- *Parameter-change variant* `[gs §9.5]`: a price-limit widening raises margin intraday; the requirement rises by 4,000 against 1,500 free; no approval → orderly de-risk, `ADDITIONAL_FUNDS_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. Even if approval arrives, a bank transfer credited after the broker's deadline gives `FUNDS_NOT_CREDITED_IN_TIME`. Any broker use of LQDT collateral is an incident and counts toward the gross cap.
- *Mid-episode variant* `[ds §9.5]`: the first leg fills and the exchange raises the guarantee provision for the far contract; the reservation was based on the pre-fill parameter; the broker's free-funds check may already have passed but the post-fill margin is higher, putting the hedge at risk → `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` for the hedge, then reduce or abort. The stress buffer must be sized to cover this escalation; the 5,000 RUB heuristic is a test candidate, not a validated requirement.
- *Overnight parameter update* `[mm §9.4]`: risk parameters update at 19:00; the open spread requires more margin; KDS drops toward Y = 0.2; if `X − stressed loss − buffer < −15,000` or the KDS threshold is violated, the engine simulates a forced partial close, potentially at unfavourable prices in an illiquid evening or weekend session.
- *Arithmetic variant* `[cg §10.5]`: a pair initially requiring 12,000 margin faces an 18,000 requirement with only 15,000 available for that purpose → block new risk, run orderly reduction, do not assume core LQDT funds the gap, record `MARGIN_STRESS_FAIL` and any unauthorised core-collateral use as an incident.

**Example 6 — Notification retry (idempotency).**
- `[ar]`: a 13:05 signal `…-0147 v1` is delivered; Telegram retries at 13:07 and the user, seeing it twice, opens the card twice. The idempotency key (`signal_id + version`) suppresses the second presentation as a trade candidate (`NOTIF_DUPLICATE_SUPPRESSED`); the user's single acknowledgement at 13:09 starts *one* MAE. At 13:40 the screener, on genuinely fresher inputs, issues `v2 (supersedes v1)` after v1's TTL lapsed: a new decision object with the full gate run and a registry link — not a resurrection of v1. If instead the 13:40 message were v1 re-sent, the correct disposition remains *expired*, however attractive the remembered prices look. In the ledger, notification count (3) and trade candidates (1) are different metrics; conflating them is how manual systems double-enter.
- `[ds §9.6]`: retries at 14:05 and 14:10 after a delivery failure; the user sees three messages; the portfolio treats them as one signal record — no new trade, no expiry reset, no reservation reset.
- `[gs §9.6]`: a send fails at 10:05, a retry delivers at 10:07, a duplicate arrives at 10:08; the same `idea_id` and data-state hash mean one idea and no second trade, and age is measured from t0/t4, not from 10:07.
- `[cg §10.6]`, `[ex]`: a send attempt times out after the server might have accepted it; the alert is resent and appears twice. Keep one `signal_id` and one idea/episode id, deduplicate on id/version, record the retry separately, and create no second admission, reservation or order. Telegram's message send timestamp is not proof of user receipt or read.
- `[mm §9.5]`: retries at 16:05 and 16:15 while the user is offline; the decision occurs at 16:30 within a 45-minute TTL; the engine ignores the retries as new ideas. If the user postpones to the next day, the 10:00 re-check fails TTL and `V2`/`EXPIRED_TTL` is logged.

**Additional edge cases for automated tests.**
- An order fills while `CANCEL_PENDING`, after which the second leg is rejected. The final position is computed from ordered broker events and actual filled quantities, never from the last UI status alone `[cg §10]`.
- A margin-escalation alert with an 85% margin utilisation caused by an unhedged leg triggers the abort inside the registered window; leg 1 is closed at market with a small friction loss that does not breach the 5,000 warning threshold `[ge §7]`.
- A genuine fresh quote alone cannot transform a retry into a second trade `[ex]`.

Include a conservative "no response until the next available window" scenario for existing-position emergencies. A claim of 24/7 monitoring is not a claim of 24/7 human execution or of 24/7 market access `[cg §11]`. All metrics are reported against the *confirmed* quota once §15 Q2 is answered; until then against the Q-IDEA proposal, labelled as such `[ar §13.1]`.

### 13.2 Per-idea time budget

Non-finance studies of manual order entry report several minutes per order; one case study found ≈6 minutes per manual order and hundreds of hours per year in aggregate `[mm 61]`. A working budget for this pilot `[mm §10.1]`: 1–3 minutes to read a signal explanation and check context; 1–2 minutes to input orders (spread or two legs) and verify; additional time for monitoring exits if not automated. Manual execution is sustainable for at most 1–3 interventions per day `[ge §7]`.

### 13.3 Where this leaves the "one trade per day" question

The word *trade* could mean an idea, a user decision, a broker order, an individual fill, a complete two-leg entry, an exit, or a full round trip. These units differ sharply for a manual two-leg spread and cannot be combined into one quota without distorting workload and risk `[cg §11]`, `[ds §11.2]`.

- The user's formulation is honoured as a **burden budget**, and §3.3 shows it cannot simultaneously be a leg-order count and a viable two-leg pilot.
- `[PROPOSAL for the later requirements gate]` adopt Q-IDEA (1 entry MAE/day, soft, occasional 2) as the entry quota; measure LO/OA but do not cap them, because capping them mid-episode manufactures naked legs.
- Risk Actions unlimited by quota, always logged; a day with > 3 Risk Actions triggers next-day review as an attention-overload indicator `[ar §13.2]`.
- Soft-exceedance budget: `[PROPOSAL test value]` ≤ 4 soft-exceeded days/month; beyond that the *strategy's signal rate*, not the user's discipline, is redesigned (fewer families, longer TTLs, generation only in attended windows — §16).
- Report the idea-level, order-level and intervention-level counts **separately** and do not collapse them until the user defines which one is the constraint `[ds §11.2]`, `[mm §10.2]`.
- The key unresolved human requirement is: **what should one daily "trade" cap count, and what emergency response time can actually be guaranteed?** Until both are answered, do not claim realistic human operability even if the simulated edge is positive `[cg §11]`. Emergency-response availability (who acts, how fast, at night) still needs definition; until it exists, any position that needs overnight intervention is ineligible `[gs §10]`.
- Longer-run questions to resolve later: how to scale from "one daily trade" in the pilot to a sustainable workload given other commitments; and whether complex exit logic (partial take-profits, staggered hedges) imposes a disproportionate manual load relative to expected excess X `[mm §10.2]`.

## 14. Falsification criteria and decision gates

### 14.1 Execution-design falsifiers (R08's own; thresholds are `[PROPOSAL]` test candidates to be frozen at preregistration)

The delayed-manual design is falsified — triggering redesign or abandonment of the manual channel (§16) — if, in E1-conservative or E2-shadow evaluation over the registered window `[ar §9.5]`:

- **F-1:** Conservative-bound `Edge_exec` at revalidation is ≤ 0 for ≥ 50% of signals that were positive at signal-time closes (the delay destroys the typical idea, not just the tail).
- **F-2:** Blackout-born signals that survive to revalidation with edge intact are < 10% of blackout-born signals (signal generation is systematically mistimed versus availability → fix generation windows before judging the strategy).
- **F-3:** One-leg incidents exceed 5% of sequential entries, or any unhedged interval exceeds the registered deadline without unwind.
- **F-4:** Realised adverse move over measured reaction windows exceeds buffer `u` on ≥ 5% of stress days.
- **F-5:** The E1 interval width (favourable minus conservative terminal X) exceeds the interval's own conservative point — free data cannot distinguish the strategy from zero at any delay scenario (→ E2 collection period or a paid sample; do not narrow the interval by assumption).
- **F-6:** Manual burden (§13) exceeds the confirmed quota/attention budget on > 20% of active days in shadow (the human channel, not the market, is the binding constraint).

### 14.2 Additional pre-registered falsifiers proposed across reports

| # | Criterion | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F-7 | Realised profit / model-expected profit < 0.50 across N ≥ 30 trades because of human execution delay (slippage-decay ratio) | `[gr §9]` |
| F-8 | More than 15% of initiated multi-leg trades end in a leg-2 timeout requiring single-leg market liquidation | `[gr §9]` |
| F-9 | Cumulative net deficit versus **zero-delay theoretical execution** exceeds 5,000 RUB over a 30-sample window → the manual-execution hypothesis is falsified, requiring automated execution or termination. *Note:* this reuses the number 5,000 with a different meaning from the `D ≥ 5,000` governance warning; the two must be kept distinct in the registry | `[ge §7]` |
| F-10 | After charging bid/ask, commissions, fees, funding and conservative slippage, the distribution of X has a median ≤ 0 over the evaluation period | `[ds §14.1]` |
| F-11 | The worst-case delay scenario produces `X ≤ −10,000` or `D ≥ 10,000` without a plausible recovery path under the entry rules | `[ds §14.2]` |
| F-12 | Leg-by-leg unhedged-interval losses, aggregated, erase the spread edge even when the atomic case is positive — the strategy would then be conditional on unproved atomic execution | `[ds §14.3]` |
| F-13 | A significant fraction of signals is rejected for `STALE_DATA`, `EXPIRED_TTL`, `NO_NET_ADVANTAGE` or `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`, so the attempted portfolio is not actually attempting the strategy | `[ds §14.4]` |
| F-14 | The conservative OHLCV fallback reverses the sign of the result relative to a midpoint-fill assumption — the apparent edge is an artefact of optimistic fills | `[ds §14.5]`, `[mm §7.2]` |
| F-15 | The matched LQDT counterfactual outperforms the strategy after the same external flows and costs, including in scenarios where the strategy is positive | `[ds §14.6]` |
| F-16 | The median-delay pass rate falls below a pre-set level; the edge half-life is shorter than the 5–15 minute median; net edge under the correlated/adverse regimes is not above LQDT carry; legging loss at p75 wipes out the median edge; forward-collected bid/ask is systematically worse than the OHLCV proxy | `[gs §8]` |
| F-17 | VTB cannot confirm the intended session/order route or cannot enter/close the eligible pair in the stated way; the minimum one-lot pair fails cash, stress-loss, VM-liquidity or one-leg flattening tests inside 30,000 without emergency funding; an inter-underlying pair has no proven atomic route and credible one-leg stress breaches the risk boundary; the free data source cannot supply timestamped inputs and enough market evidence to revalidate both legs; reasonable joint stress requires assuming core collateral or unlimited emergency funding; event accounting cannot reconcile `X = V − B`, external flows, VM, core-collateral incidents and LQDT opportunity return without double-counting; human workload, overlapping alerts or unresolved one-leg states exceed a pre-registered availability bound | `[cg §12]`, `[ex]` |

If none of these fires, the result is **not proof of live feasibility**; it is evidence that the strategy survives the specified delayed-fill stress. Live feasibility still requires the dependency reports, broker confirmations, account checks and a separate production gate `[ds §14]`.

### 14.3 Inherited accounting, risk and method falsifiers

- Buffer `u` is retired if the fixed-5,000 variant breaches on ≥ 5% of stress days for a candidate book; margin ceilings {33%, 50%} are rejected as sole controls if a passing book breaches the cash-path gate under S1–S5 `[R07 §4.3]`.
- Construction sensitivity: a spread result that flips sign between explicit-contract accounting and any adjusted continuous series is an artefact `[R09 §4.2 pathway 2]`.
- Settlement-as-fill or `High − Low` spread constructions invalidate a run on sight `[ar §16]`, `[R09 §4.2 pathway 8]`.

### 14.4 Decision gates

(i) E0/E1 protocol frozen → replay → (ii) shadow E2 with journal + BBO collection → (iii) burden/quota review against §13 → (iv) *only then*, and only with unknowns U1–U3 (§15.2) resolved, may a live-pilot question be posed. A 90-day clock, if a pilot ever starts, validates operations, not profitability, and its 5/10/15k rules never reset `[ar §16]`, `[R05 §4.6]`, `[R09 §1 point 8]`. A larger allocation needs separate prospective evidence rather than scaling pilot percentages `[ex]`.

## Appendix A — Formula sheet

- **Excess accounting:** `X = V − B`; `H = max(0, max past X)`; `D = H − X`; `S = max(0, −X)`. Convention: current X is included in H so that `D ≥ 0`; frozen in the journal before the first entry `[R05 §2.4]`.
- **Counterfactual growth:** `B(t) = B(0) × Π(1 + r_LQDT,k) + Σ ExternalFlows`; `V(t) = CoreLQDT(t) + TestCash(t) + Σ UnrealisedVM + Σ RealisedProfit − Fees_total` `[gr §5]`.
- **Round turn:** `C_RT = 2q × (b_A + F_A + b_B + F_B)`; `FutFee = Round(Round(|FutPrice| × Round(W/R;5);2) × (Base_MOEX + Base_NCC);2)`; calendar-spread anonymous fee `T × F × (1 − 0.2)` in the marketing period; scalper 0.5 × FutFee; maker 0 `[R02 Appendix B]`.
- **Full cost:** `C_full = C_RT + friction + Δfixed fees + events + financing + U` `[R02 §9.1]`.
- **Quoted spread in RUB:** `(OFFER − BID) / MINSTEP × STEPPRICE`; conservative four-execution crossing `= 4 × spread_RUB × q` `[R02 Appendix B]`.
- **Financing charge:** `(ГО − рубли)⁺ × (key rate + 2%) × T/365` `[R02 §3.5]`.
- **Entry/continuation:** `X − L_stress − u ≥ −15,000`, with `u = max(5,000, 2.5 × σ_delay(book) + C_exit)` `[R07 §4.1 — PROPOSAL]`, plus the independent cash-path gate.
- **Executable edge:** `Edge_exec = (FairValue − ExecutableSpread) signed to the trade − C_full(q)`; require `Edge_exec ≥ Edge_min`.
- **Synthetic spread quote:** `Spread_ask(t) = P_ask,A(t) · Ratio_A − P_bid,B(t) · Ratio_B`; the entry test is `G_raw(t) − TCA_total − r_LQDT · Δt_holding > Threshold_min` `[gr §3]`.
- **Delay–volatility drift:** `ΔP_exec = μ_drift · Δt + γ · σ_t · √Δt + ε_t`, `γ > 0` under informed flow `[gr §2]`.
- **Latency decomposition:** `Δt_sys = (t_eval − t_info) + (t_sig − t_eval) + (t_tg − t_sig)`; `Δt_human = t_user − t_tg`; `Δt_route = t_broker − t_user`; `Δt_exec = t_fill1 − t_broker`; `Δt_hedge = t_fill2 − t_fill1` `[gr §1]`.
- **Implementation shortfall (per signal):** `(achieved − decision)` signed to the trade over filled quantity, plus opportunity cost over unfilled quantity, plus explicit costs; the decision price is the synchronous executable spread at t4 where obtainable, else the §9.3 conservative-bound convention — the convention is part of the registered method `[Perold]`, `[ar Appendix A]`.
- **Priority score (if the ranked variant is registered):** `Score = (expected net edge after costs and uncertainty buffer) / (stressed capital requirement)` `[cg §7]`, `[gr §7]`.
